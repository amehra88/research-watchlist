## 1. Headline read

**Confirm — emphatically.** ANET's first-ever $3B quarter (revenue +37.7% YoY, ~6% above Street, ~7% above its own $2.8B guide) with 49.9% operating margin validates the AI-networking-leader thesis across the board; management raised the FY26 revenue outlook for the **third time this year to ~$12.6B (~40% growth)**. The signal that matters for the thesis is that **supply — not demand or competition — is now the governor of the model**: the guide raises track the supply chain "ecosystem gaining traction," multi-year purchase commitments jumped to $9.7B, and product deferred revenue built ~$600M sequentially (demand booked but not yet recognized). The one thesis line to watch is customer concentration, which the print intensifies rather than eases (potential new 10%+ AI-lab customer in Q4).

## 3. Forward guidance

- **FY2026 revenue raised to ~$12.6B, ~40% growth** — the third raise this year (up from ~$11.5B in May 2026 and the ~$10.5B Analyst Day goal; +$2.1B vs Analyst Day, +$1.1B vs May). Management explicitly attributes the raise to improving supply, "expected to apply across all our product sectors" — backend AI fabrics, core data-center front end, and campus/routing. (md_6_0)
- **FY26 component targets:** campus ≥$1.25B; AI fabrics ≥$3.5B (CFO, md_7_5). CEO separately referenced an "overall AI target of at least $3.6 billion in 2026" (md_2_2) — both figures cited as stated; not reconciled here.
- **FY26 margins:** gross margin range maintained at 62–64% (inclusive of memory/silicon cost increases); operating-margin target **raised to 48–49%**; tax 21.5%.
- **Q3 FY26 guide:** revenue ~$3.3B; GM ~63%; operating margin 48–49%; diluted EPS $1.06–1.08 on ~1.279B shares; tax ~21.5%.
- **Tone / philosophy:** point-plus-range framing, unusually confident ("energized… well-positioned for this AI super cycle"). Notably, management raised the total-year number substantially while **holding the AI target and lifting campus** — i.e., the raise is not purely an AI-fabrics story and the AI line may be supply-gated / conservatively held (see Q&A flag, David Vogt).

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Etherlink AI switches now >100 cumulative customers (from "four to five" in 2024); management frames white box as "a tactical solution… in simple scale up or scale out where software and system requirements are low," while an Evercore analyst's "opposite of the white-box question" drew agreement that rising AI-network complexity increases the value of the integrated EOS platform. On NVIDIA: "we live in a NVIDIA world"; Arista participates little in NVLink scale-up but "does better in the scale out or scale across domain," with enthusiasm for non-NVIDIA accelerators (AMD MI series, Google TPUs, inference silicon). (md_2_0, qna_1/2, qna_23)
- **Implication:** The software-plus-merchant-silicon moat is intact and widening as topologies get more complex; the competitive risk remains the NVLink-anchored scale-up domain, which management concedes will take "two, three years" to shift to Ethernet — a known, slow-moving cap, not a new threat.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Third FY26 raise to ~$12.6B/40% growth; multi-year purchase commitments "almost tripling from a year ago at $3.6 billion to approximately $9.7 billion"; scale-across switching/routing TAM cited at "$15 billion to $20 billion in 2030"; product deferred revenue +~$600M q/q. (md_4_1, md_2_1, md_6_0, md_7_3/4)
- **Implication:** ANET remains a direct, high-beta beneficiary of the AI capex super-cycle, now capacity-constrained rather than demand-constrained — a higher-quality problem that shifts the risk from "is the demand there" to "can supply be converted to revenue on schedule."

### hyperscaler_revenue_concentration

- **Status:** Confirm (broadening at the margin, but concentration remains the structural feature — the risk is not easing)
- **Evidence:** A potential **new 10%+ customer expected in Q4** is an AI lab (Ben Reitzes cited the press-release Anthropic/Palo Alto webinar) — i.e., *additional* concentration. International revenue jumped to 23% of revenue (from 15.5%), but explicitly from "a shift in the geographic mix of sales to our large global customers" — the same titans booked abroad. Scale-across "is dominated by the cloud and AI titans." Genuine diversification (campus ≥$1.25B, enterprise, neoclouds) is real but ~10% of the $12.6B guide. (qna_14, md_7_0, qna_45)
- **Implication:** Do not let a blowout quarter soften the risk thesis. Revenue is broadening into neoclouds/enterprise/campus at the margin, but the incremental dollars still concentrate in a handful of titans, and a new 10%+ AI-lab customer would *raise* single-customer-cluster exposure. Concentration remains the primary structural cap on the name — confirmed, arguably intensified.

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Clear scale-up / scale-out / scale-across taxonomy; 7800 "flagship spine for distributed scale-across applications" with SRv6 multi-plane forwarding, contextual routing, traffic engineering; scale-across "expected to be approximately 30% of our overall AI target"; new 7060XE7 (100 Tbps capacity, 1.6 Tbps throughput) plus first liquid-cooling options; EOS innovations for multi-model / diverse-accelerator networks; UEC and "MRC" work; forward-looking take on CPO/NPO/XPO. (md_2_0/1/2, qna_15, qna_16)
- **Implication:** ANET is defining the vocabulary and the reference architecture for distributed AI networking (scale-across across power-/space-constrained sites) — a durable topology-leadership position that extends the TAM beyond single-site back-end fabric.

## 5. AI positioning signal

- **Current score:** 4 (watchlist note already flags "Trending 4+")
- **Evidence from this call:** Etherlink >100 customers; AI fabrics guide ≥$3.5B; scale-across leadership with a cited $15–20B 2030 TAM; explicit multi-accelerator strategy (AMD MI, Google TPU, inference silicon) reducing NVIDIA-fabric dependence; a marquee AI-lab customer (webinar with Anthropic) trending to 10%+; liquid cooling and 100T/1.6T switching launched. Offsetting: no participation in NVLink scale-up, and Ethernet displacement there is a 2–3 year story.
- **Recommendation:** drift to 4+
- **Reasoning:** The AI back-end ramp the score anticipated is now printing at scale with product, customer, and TAM breadth all confirming; the scale-up gap and supply constraint are the only reasons not to go to 5. This formalizes the existing "trending 4+" note.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: 4)
  - Evidence: 7060XE7 (100 Tbps / 1.6 Tbps) + first liquid cooling; SSU (Smart System Upgrade), SRv6 multi-plane, deep analytics, MRC, UEC contributions; R&D $278.1M (9.2% of revenue) within a pure-play model; "best-of-breed… open, interoperable standards" (CTO Ken Duda).
  - Recommendation: revise to 4+
- **Distribution** (current: 4)
  - Evidence: still heavily hyperscaler/titan-concentrated (new 10%+ AI-lab customer; international gains are the same global customers booked abroad); genuine broadening into neoclouds/enterprise/campus is real but small; new global operations executive (Eugenia Corrales) hired to harden supply/fulfillment.
  - Recommendation: hold 4
- **Overall** (current: 4)
  - Recommendation: hold 4 (with an explicit 4+ trajectory)
  - Reasoning: For this business model the two dimensions interact asymmetrically — the EOS software + merchant-silicon **innovation** moat is precisely what converts the AI opportunity and defends against white box, and it is strengthening. But **distribution** is the binding cap: demand is not the question, yet revenue still concentrates in a few titans and, this quarter, the true constraint moved to the supply chain (memory/silicon/optics) and Arista's ability to fulfill — not its reach. Innovation earns a 4+; concentration plus the supply-conversion risk hold the overall at 4 for now. A 4+ overall becomes defensible if the neocloud/enterprise/campus mix keeps compounding and supply normalizes.

## 7. Potential investor interest signal

- **Current score:** 4
- **Evidence from this call:** Third guide raise to 40% growth; ~15% EPS beat (though below-the-line-flattered — see §2); operating-margin target raised to 48–49%; index/prestige tailwinds (entered Fortune 500 and Russell 50 in 2026); $13.3B cash, ~$1.1B quarterly operating cash flow. Tempering: **no buyback executed this quarter** (capital return paused; $817.9M authorization idle), a premium multiple, and the concentration cap.
- **Recommendation:** drift to 4+
- **Reasoning:** Positive earnings-revision momentum (three raises), margin expansion, and index inclusion strengthen the interest case; the paused buyback and premium valuation are the only reasons to stop short of 5. Operator note: the EPS beat's below-the-line composition means the revision signal is cleaner at the revenue/operating-margin line than at EPS.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

1. **David Vogt (UBS) — why raise the total year substantially but hold the AI target and only lift campus?** Management: the raise is broad-based (front end, core data center, campus all expected to grow), not purely AI. Emphasis flag — implies the AI-fabrics ≥$3.5B target may be conservatively/supply-gated and the upside surprise came partly from non-AI sectors. (qna_4)
2. **Ben Reitzes (Melius) — progress landing AI labs / the potential 10%+ Q4 customer** (prompted by the press-release Anthropic + Palo Alto webinar). Confirms a large AI-lab customer forming — read as concentration risk, not just an accolade. (qna_14)
3. **Scale-up vs. NVIDIA (Karl):** unusually candid — "we live in a NVIDIA world," little Arista participation in NVLink scale-up; Ethernet displacement will take "two, three years… like InfiniBand." Realistic sandbag on the scale-up domain. (qna_23)
4. **Core data-center front end "flat" (Simon):** management conceded "we couldn't answer that question in Q1," now expects front-end growth. A candid acknowledgment of prior-quarter uncertainty now resolving positively. (qna_42)
5. **Supply chain — recurring emphasis:** CEO "quite vocal and candid" about industry-wide supply tightness and rising component costs; CFO flagged inventory and deferred-revenue volatility ahead of deployments. The dominant risk narrative of the call — supply conversion, not demand. (md_4_0, md_5_0, md_7_3)
