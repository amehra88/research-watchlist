# [AI: Anthropic Paces the Frontier, and Floors the IPO. AI-RTZ #1210](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=215611136&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNTYxMTEzNiwiaWF0IjoxNzg5NDQ4ODAyLCJleHAiOjE3OTIwNDA4MDIsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.6_iE35zJ90R5e1VRt7VmnZCysARSJkMwJF7MeOHtpyA)

### ...two profitable quarters, 80%+ margins, & turbo charged revenue growth

Two days after [Dario Amodei asked the whole AI industry to pace](https://substack.com/redirect/06e79789-b13b-4d35-9124-ff041ad1c03a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) AI growth, three pieces of news show what Anthropic itself is doing: sprinting toward its mega-AI IPO in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cccbb5ca-51e4-4027-96a4-cd0eeabf5e8a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The Financial Times reports in [‘Anthropic tells investors it will be profitable for second straight quarter](https://substack.com/redirect/08fc8c0b-9717-42c6-b4bd-daa400b51261?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)’ that the prospectus expected last week went instead to a small group of shareholders, who get to ask their questions before it goes public. The headline they were given:

> “The company has told a small group of shareholders that its adjusted operating income will be positive for the second consecutive quarter... The measure strips out costs including stock-based compensation.” ([Financial Times](https://substack.com/redirect/08fc8c0b-9717-42c6-b4bd-daa400b51261?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), September 13, 2026)

The first of those two quarters was the second quarter, in the documents Bloomberg surfaced in August that I covered in [#1181](https://substack.com/redirect/98901b4f-e5d6-40b4-869f-7f9756450a65?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). The second is the current quarter, the one still running. On margins:

> “Anthropic’s gross margins are above 80 per cent before accounting for revenue shared with distribution partners, including Amazon, and the cost of training its models, according to two of the people.” ([Financial Times](https://substack.com/redirect/08fc8c0b-9717-42c6-b4bd-daa400b51261?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA))

First, it is preliminarily telling select investors it will be profitable for a second straight quarter, with investors modeling $120 billion of revenue by December.

Second, it picked Nasdaq for a listing that could value it at $2 trillion or more. Finally, Anthropic signed a $13.7 billion, six-year compute deal with a Trump-linked neocloud that does not yet have the money to build the data center.

That’s in contrast to the AI industry ‘pace setting’ I laid out yesterday on [ARD 162](https://substack.com/redirect/d2fa8baf-4855-482f-8bd0-cd85c2ee5c7d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): Anthropic is playing the pace-pausing game and the pace-accelerating game at the same time, for its own IPO, ahead of arch-rival OpenAI. Both games at once, pedal to the metal.

The revenue line underneath is worth understanding. Second-quarter revenue of $11.5 billion, up fourteen-fold from a year earlier, with an adjusted operating profit in that quarter. An annualized run rate of $65 billion at the end of July, from $9 billion at the end of last year. The last private mark was $965 billion.

Then the forward numbers, from the SemiAnalysis analyst the FT quotes:

> “If you continue to operate at these margins and growth rates, it will be so hard to compete [with Anthropic] because they have so much [computing resource],” said Joey Brookhart, an analyst covering AI labs at SemiAnalysis. Investors are forecasting that Anthropic will end the year with $120bn in annualised revenue and close 2027 with almost triple that, he added. ([Financial Times](https://substack.com/redirect/08fc8c0b-9717-42c6-b4bd-daa400b51261?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA))

Those two forecasts are the new element of note. $120 billion of annualized revenue by year-end 2026, and almost triple that, call it $350 billion, by the end of 2027.

Both are higher than the industry ‘guesstimates’ that were circulating as recently as last week: $100 billion for this year, $200 billion plus for next. I carried those on [ARD 160](https://substack.com/redirect/3bfc68cd-e68e-42c2-a1b7-afb983eede6c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) on Thursday:

> “Anthropic caught up with a laser focus on the enterprise market and on AI coding, a white-hot market, and it is running ahead on revenue at $65 billion plus as of mid-July, on a trajectory toward $100 billion this year and $200 billion next.” ([ARD #160](https://substack.com/redirect/3bfc68cd-e68e-42c2-a1b7-afb983eede6c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), September 10, 2026)

They are also well above what the company itself put in front of the banks a month ago. In [#1181](https://substack.com/redirect/98901b4f-e5d6-40b4-869f-7f9756450a65?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) I laid out the Reuters reporting on the projection underneath the $2 trillion valuation talk:

> “The arithmetic explains the approach. On current-year numbers, any mega valuation looks over-ambitious. On the 2028 projection, a $2 trillion outcome is roughly ten times forward revenue, a multiple the Street can say out loud. The entire question becomes whether the $190 to $200 billion is real.” ([AI-RTZ #1181](https://substack.com/redirect/98901b4f-e5d6-40b4-869f-7f9756450a65?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), on [Reuters](https://substack.com/redirect/20f9b0d4-5cf5-4789-9984-b779c29b64dd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), August 17, 2026)

It’s all fluid and moving fast indeed.

Read that against the FT. The company’s own deck said $190 to $200 billion of revenue in 2028. Investors are now modeling a $350 billion run rate a year earlier, at the end of 2027.

One note of clarification needed. A $120 billion run rate in December is of course not $120 billion of 2026 revenue. So take all these ‘guesstimates’ with adequate lumps of salt.

On the quarterly path so far, $4.7 billion in the first quarter and $11.5 billion in the second per [Bloomberg](https://substack.com/redirect/527a077b-e726-49d1-9ca1-e709665f20d2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), the calendar year lands at roughly half that.

Exit rates and fiscal years are apples and oranges, and both will be in the S-1.

The FT’s phrase to underline is ‘before accounting for revenue shared with distribution partners, including Amazon’.

That is the revenue recognition difference between Anthropic and OpenAI, the one I took apart on [ARD 156](https://substack.com/redirect/c495bac8-9ff2-4dc2-8a30-fa2cc5cb7c2c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) ten days ago, off [Axios](https://substack.com/redirect/0a7c3638-4c3b-45d8-8b0e-b1bcb4084e3f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and The Information’s March reporting.

Anthropic records the full value of Claude sales through its cloud partners, AWS, Microsoft and Google, and books the partners’ cut as an expense. OpenAI records only its share of certain sales through Microsoft. If a customer pays $100 through a cloud provider, Anthropic books $100 and an expense; OpenAI books its slice.

Per Axios, going to net would take Anthropic’s number down 6 to 10 percent. Both follow GAAP. It comes down to who is the ‘principal’ in the transaction. And I have seen this movie before:

> “I saw this one up close. When I helped take eBay public at Goldman in 1998, there was a key differential from an accounting point of view: whether you count the total gross amount of products bought and sold on eBay, or the amount eBay kept as its net revenue. Gross merchandise value versus net revenue.” ([ARD #156](https://substack.com/redirect/c495bac8-9ff2-4dc2-8a30-fa2cc5cb7c2c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), September 4, 2026)

So two things to read carefully in the FT’s paragraph of numbers. ‘Adjusted’ operating income excludes stock compensation, which at a five-year-old company hiring at this pace is not small. And an 80 percent gross margin ‘before’ the Amazon revenue share and ‘before’ training costs is a gross margin on inference, not on the business.

OpenAI, for its part, has gone the other way and thrown [the kitchen sink of costs](https://substack.com/redirect/8dca8600-ff63-4727-a3c8-ce7eb42c5b43?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) into its own numbers ahead of its filing. The public S-1 will show both lines for Anthropic. That is where the bears will go first.

Separately, Business Insider broke the venue in [‘Anthropic has chosen the Nasdaq for its IPO listing](https://substack.com/redirect/03aedd84-5596-4ea8-9c36-13e144b863e8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)’ and the FT confirms it: Nasdaq, following SpaceX’s listing there earlier this year at $1.75 trillion. October remains the target. A $2 trillion figure is being discussed but is not finalized.

It’ll be interesting to note if Nasdaq cut the same deal with Anthropic as it did with SpaceX/xAI, to [accelerate the entry of its public equity](https://substack.com/redirect/c3fab3c0-33ce-4b8a-bcee-2c51233606e0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) into the Nasdaq 100 index. It would meaningfully accelerate the need for hundreds of billions in passive index investment to buy low-float IPOs like Anthropic, as it did for SpaceX/xAI.

One date to note: Anthropic has to release its financials at least 15 days before the roadshow begins. If October is real, the public S-1 is days away, not weeks.

Finally, The Information reports in [‘Anthropic Strikes $13.7 Billion Compute Deal With Trump-Linked Rum Group](https://substack.com/redirect/fa7cf385-9a12-4e0e-95c6-b26699877e20?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)’ that Anthropic is the unnamed customer behind a $13.7 billion, six-year contract Rum Group disclosed in an August filing.

Rum Group is the former Rumble, the video platform that hosts Truth Social and the White House livestreams, with Peter Thiel and J.D. Vance among its early investors.

In June it bought Northern Data, a German neocloud, for a data center site under development in Maysville, Georgia, with 120 megawatts of grid power. The site is still being built, and Rum said in its own filing it does not yet have the financing for the construction or the chips.

The sweetener reads like a Nvidia deal in reverse: Anthropic gets an option on as many as 51 million Rum shares at one cent each, against a $7.17 stock and a $2.8 billion market cap, vesting on how much compute it buys.

Anthropic’s compute agreements already total at least 14.8 gigawatts and as much as $517 billion over the next decade, the ledger I laid out last week in [Anthropic’s $517B vs OpenAI’s $750B](https://substack.com/redirect/3c38a3b2-dcf6-4b55-ac66-bde3f686dd6e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Rum adds to it.

My take, in three parts.

One, pacing the frontier is accretive. The FT says it plainly: slowing the pace of new models could save the company billions in training costs, while also letting rivals close the gap. Those training costs are exactly what sits between the 80 percent inference margin and the operating line.

So add this to the list of reasons why Anthropic may be calling to pace the development of new frontier AI models.

Former Administration ‘AI Czar’ and returning VC David Sacks [made the same point](https://substack.com/redirect/6701edc4-7a45-4a2c-b4e5-a76dbac09026?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)Saturday night on the way to a harsher conclusion: after the Hugging Face episode, trading raw capability for reliability is just good business. Call it alignment if you want. It also bolsters the quarter.

The public pause and the private profitability are the same decision, seen from two sides.

Two, the IPO clock is the real race, and Anthropic is ahead. OpenAI confirmed Saturday it will stay private this year, with the safety moment as the stated reason. Anthropic is on for October on a $65 billion run rate, against OpenAI’s last reported figure of over $40 billion, and with investors modeling $120 billion by December.

That is the gap I have been tracking since [the tight IPO clock](https://substack.com/redirect/958c198f-7eec-4594-befc-8e5ee2f6d3e7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in June and the [mega-IPO math](https://substack.com/redirect/98901b4f-e5d6-40b4-869f-7f9756450a65?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in August.

The company that asked for the pause is the one going first, and it is going first with two profitable quarters and Nasdaq 100 eligibility. The pause narrative and the growth narrative go into the same roadshow deck. The S-1 has to reconcile them on a single page, gross and net, and that page is the one to read.

Three, the compute ledger keeps growing while the capability pace slows. Pacing capability is not pacing compute, the point I made yesterday about [Jensen Huang’s 70 percent guide](https://substack.com/redirect/9a07cf9a-4810-4bf9-aba7-13488f28bfab?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Inference, agents and Claude Code demand do not pause because model releases do. A $350 billion run rate by the end of 2027 needs every megawatt on the ledger, and then some.

Rum is also of note in these ‘clever’ arrangements: a $13.7 billion commitment to a site that is not built, from a connected counterparty that has not raised the money, secured by options at a penny a share.

A continuation of [Anthropic’s rapid ramp](https://substack.com/redirect/3c38a3b2-dcf6-4b55-ac66-bde3f686dd6e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) of its AI Compute infrastructure.

That structure says Anthropic is the anchor tenant with all the leverage, a continuation of the ever convenient circular, [boomerang deals](https://substack.com/redirect/f6fefef7-8efb-4255-9cab-6516d70da8e3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). The customer is now the kingmaker too.

And a Trump-linked provider, three months after [the government cleared Anthropic](https://substack.com/redirect/b6b79f58-a91f-499c-8c2f-9f37ab770320?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) to ship again after The Blip 2.0, is a relationship as much as a data center. Going into the pace pause of ‘[The Blip 3.0’.](https://substack.com/redirect/d2fa8baf-4855-482f-8bd0-cd85c2ee5c7d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)

Anthropic asked the industry to pace the frontier on Saturday. By Sunday night it had two profitable quarters to show, investors modeling a $120 billion run rate by December, an IPO exchange (and passive index) picked, and another gigawatt-class deal signed.

Multiple moves at once, all the way to accelerating this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cccbb5ca-51e4-4027-96a4-cd0eeabf5e8a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)’s first $2+ trillion IPO. Stay tuned.
