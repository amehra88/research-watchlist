# [AI: Physical AI w/ Nvidia’s Long Game in Robots, Cars & More. AI-RTZ #1195 (Part 2)](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=213300935&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxMzMwMDkzNSwiaWF0IjoxNzg4MTUyOTQxLCJleHAiOjE3OTA3NDQ5NDEsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.g4BUdrFlYpClX531squqERpKFwm1iEDrIwjJIICXb9Q)

### ...the global, horizontal player: $10B of physical AI revenue today, Jensen’s 10x+ decade, the family, the investments, & the synthetic data engines.

[Part 1 yesterday](https://substack.com/redirect/7544585d-4a3b-4a85-93cd-4f7b8e3c0269?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) of the Physical AI update mapped the global field: China building the robot bodies at ecosystem scale, the near-term money in the unglamorous lanes, and timelines longer than the market’s clock.

Today, the featured player.

Because one company in my view, is positioned across the whole board, and it is running a playbook we have seen before by its founder/CEO Jensen Huang.

## 1. The $10 Billion ‘Physical AI’ Business ‘Hiding’ Inside Nvidia

The WSJ’s numbers [frame it](https://substack.com/redirect/ac2b6cad-7521-48ba-aa58-e659ced3f59e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Physical AI, spanning robots, cars and drones, already generates about $10 billion a year for Nvidia. Small against roughly $303 billion in trailing revenue.

But Jensen Huang expects it to grow ten-fold within a decade. His logic, from a San Francisco stage in July: if AI can generate video of a [hand picking up a glass](https://substack.com/redirect/ff8d3a67-710f-45d2-8925-8ffd507d15e2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), “why can’t I cause a robot to do the same?”

The playbook is [CUDA,](https://substack.com/redirect/364a3162-5b52-47c4-9bce-a5a6f5f049d8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) Nvidia’s open source ubiquitous software/hardware layers [all over again.](https://substack.com/redirect/fba55289-fd36-409c-8861-ec6ced14eb41?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) Two decades ago Nvidia paired chips with software until developers were committed to the stack, and grew into a company valued in the trillions.

Today the physical AI version pairs the Jetson robot computers, Thor at the high end, the new entry-level Jetson Orin Nano 2 released August 25, with open-weight models developers can freely download and modify. GR00T for robot brains. Cosmos for world models, the thread we covered in [Beyond LLM AIs to ‘World Models’](https://substack.com/redirect/15f9727e-8ca1-450f-a2e7-2e7b08ac0bdb?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). We mapped the full roadmap from GTC 2026 in [Nvidia’s dense announcements](https://substack.com/redirect/c2e29be8-01fc-462e-b724-6c4562aae121?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in March.

And note who is buying.

China, the world’s largest robot market and shipper of 97% of the world’s humanoids, is Nvidia’s most eager physical AI customer, in the WSJ’s framing.

Washington bars Nvidia’s most powerful AI chips from China. The robot and car chips still trade. AgiBot, Unitree and Fourier machines populate Nvidia’s own labs, in the US and China, for data collection and experiments. In cars, the same horizontal logic: Nvidia counts Waymo AND Tesla as customers, plus the Chinese robotaxi fleets. It does not need to pick the winner. It supplies the grid.

## 2. The Family Is on the Mission

Here is the detail in the WSJ piece that says the most about how seriously Jensen takes this. He has put his children on it. The next generation, for the next generation of the [AI Tech Wave.](https://substack.com/redirect/ff53507a-8c13-40a3-b510-ddb3821ca0aa?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)

Daughter Madison Huang oversees marketing for Nvidia’s physical AI division. In August she visited LG Electronics’ robotics data factory in Seoul, the day after LG announced a humanoid built on Nvidia’s robotic model. The next day she toured the Beijing robot exhibition, drawing onlookers the way her father does on his Asia trips, watching robots do flying kicks and stopping by the booths of Chinese companies built on Nvidia technology. Son Spencer Huang is a director of product management on the same effort.

My take: founders signal priorities with their calendars and their families. Jensen has been saying physical AI is the next wave in every keynote for two years. Assigning the next generation of Huangs to the physical AI business says he means it as a decades-long franchise, not a side bet. For a company whose founder-led continuity IS the strategy, that is worth noting.

## 3. The Investment Web: Arming Every Side. Again.

Nvidia is not just selling to the robot ecosystem. It is funding it, especially the US side of it.

The roster of Nvidia’s US and Western robotics bets is getting long. Figure AI, the Bay Area humanoid maker that just produced its 1,000th robot, took Nvidia investment back in its 2024 mega-round. Skild AI raised [$1.4 billion for its general robot foundation model](https://substack.com/redirect/8859bc92-9dc8-4183-be1a-3dfa92816113?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) with Nvidia alongside SoftBank and Jeff Bezos.

Field AI, building robot brains for unstructured industrial environments, hit a [$2 billion valuation](https://substack.com/redirect/7fc6f1be-c6ed-40da-aa23-e231e27a2ab0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) with Nvidia and Bill Gates on the cap table. Neura Robotics, the German humanoid maker, raised [up to $1.4 billion](https://substack.com/redirect/b6fbe789-7d8b-46ba-99b9-f624b7fa5c94?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) with Nvidia and Amazon participating. Add this week’s $12.9 billion Hugging Face acquisition, with its Pollen Robotics team and that $399 Microduck teaching millions of developers reinforcement learning, and the pattern is clear: Nvidia funds the robot brains, sells them the compute, and owns the developer front door.

Big tech is arming up around it.

Meta acquired robotics-AI startup [Assured Robot Intelligence in May](https://substack.com/redirect/94867c81-31f9-4be2-b1c1-f69f4dad795a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) to bolster its ‘Metabot’ humanoid software platform ambitions, the expansion we covered in [Meta expands Humanoid AI robots race](https://substack.com/redirect/23a0d50e-471d-4791-926d-06233cb411ee?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Google backs Apptronik. Amazon deploys its own warehouse fleet and invests alongside. OpenAI has robotics hires and stakes of its own.

And Meta is also this section’s cautionary tale. A [Reuters special report this week](https://substack.com/redirect/3b64b850-8cfc-488c-ad1b-c991216c5b91?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) detailed how Mark Zuckerberg’s ‘Project OT’ plan to make Meta ‘AI native’ imploded. The scenarios explored cutting some teams by up to 60%, in two waves. Staff revolted. The May wave cut 10%; the November wave was called off hours before the first one fired. The internal data is the telling part. AI-generated code changes were up 220% year over year, but user-facing feature changes only 36%, while technical and security incidents spiked 40%. Zuckerberg conceded to employees that AI agent technology had not accelerated as quickly as he anticipated. All this while Meta plans $130 billion of AI capex this year.

My take: the same gap, in software, that we just watched in Beijing in hardware. The demo is spectacular; the deployed, reliable, paying version runs years behind the keynote. Meta ran the experiment on its own workforce and blinked. That is a notable piece of evidence this month for the longer timelines in Part 1.

And it sharpens the platform contrast: this is the ‘[Kingmaker,](https://substack.com/redirect/9da0c806-1826-48e3-bf4b-2f5aa27bda93?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) uber investor and banker’ pattern from the [data center story](https://substack.com/redirect/e197ac31-902d-4ec2-86bb-66d33cd6259d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), transplanted into the physical world. Nvidia funds the category, supplies the category, and sets its software standards, and it gets paid whether each rival’s AI transformation lands on schedule or not. Every robot program, Meta’s included, makes the picks-and-shovels player stronger.

## 4. The Synthetic Data Engine: Jim Fan’s Simulated Worlds

Now the deepest layer, and the one I find most interesting as a technologist. Robots are data-starved. The internet trained the LLM AIs. There is no internet of touch, balance and friction. The IFR’s Susanne Bieller called “the lack of training data for AI” the industry’s huge challenge, in the FT this week.

Nvidia’s answer is to manufacture the data. Jim Fan, who leads Nvidia’s GEAR embodied-AI research group and whose work we have followed in these pages since the [Perfecting ‘Synthetic Data’](https://substack.com/redirect/902fd1a0-9b1e-4d6c-b10b-539a84bdbcec?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and [World Models](https://substack.com/redirect/15f9727e-8ca1-450f-a2e7-2e7b08ac0bdb?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) pieces, frames the endgame as a [‘Physical Turing Test’](https://substack.com/redirect/1976d9a9-ad8c-439f-9cdb-a9f4ab1e0e5a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): machines doing physical tasks indistinguishably from humans, which he pegs within two to three years for narrow domains. His formula for how: “Compute now equals environment equals data.”

Train robots by trial and error inside simulated worlds, neural simulators and world models generating the physics, so a robot can fail a million times in silicon before it touches your kitchen. GR00T’s newest versions train exactly this way. Egocentric human video, tens of thousands of hours of people simply doing things, transfers dexterity to robots with teleoperation shrinking toward a rounding error. The FT’s report of cameras on textile workers’ heads is the industrial version of the same idea, privacy questions and all.

My take: this is the quiet moat. China wins the hardware iteration loop. But if the scarce input is training data, and the data is increasingly manufactured in simulation, then the advantage flows to whoever owns the simulation stack and the compute underneath it. It’s part of the reason Nvidia is [focused on AI Data company Mercor](https://substack.com/redirect/efe8fabd-8b08-4b34-b621-d9d4359c8cb6?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)with its recent partnership and investment.

That is Omniverse, Cosmos, GR00T, and CUDA. It is also why the ‘embodied AI as the road to more general intelligence’ argument, which the FT airs and I take seriously, lands on Nvidia’s doorstep either way.

## 5. The Risk: One Governmental Policy Away

The honest counterweight, in both directions. Nvidia’s China physical AI franchise runs on a trade lane both capitals keep threatening. Washington could extend export restrictions to robot and car chips. Beijing’s makers are already testing Huawei, Black Sesame and Horizon Robotics alternatives, just in case; a former Horizon researcher told the WSJ the homegrown day is not top of mind, but he can see it “coming eventually.”

And the FCC’s new import bans on Chinese robots cut the other way, shrinking the US market for the very machines that run on Nvidia inside. The balkanization we wrote about in [‘Fumbling the Ball’](https://substack.com/redirect/77bb9189-1da0-4775-a702-a9353861cf88?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) is the single biggest threat to the horizontal position. Much hangs on the second meeting in DC between the Presidents of China and the US at the White House September 24. And this time, Air Force One won’t have to pick up [Jensen Huang in Alaska.](https://substack.com/redirect/cb37d2af-565a-4617-8e89-c66591330450?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)

## The Bottom Line

Put both parts together. China builds the bodies, at ecosystem scale, led by AgiBot and Unitree. The US builds the best robot brains, and the prize for the standard robot operating system is enormous. The near-term money is industrial, vehicular, and airborne. The Jetsons run a decade-plus late, on the research houses’ own numbers.

And one player sits across all of it. Chips, open models, simulation, developer distribution, and capital, in every lane and on both sides of the Pacific, with $10 billion of physical AI revenue today and a founder aiming at ten times that, family aboard.

That is why [Nvidia has been on my ‘Call Your Shot’](https://substack.com/redirect/d946ec22-2db5-4a1b-bc1e-ead2297b2304?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)list since 2024, and why this pair of pieces extends the [open-source champions thesis](https://substack.com/redirect/edaadb99-8436-4ed1-b10d-fe7b85f88ebc?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) into the physical AIworld.

The robots beat Usain Bolt last week. They still cannot stop, steer, or plug in a charger with their hand. It’s early days for all that in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/ff53507a-8c13-40a3-b510-ddb3821ca0aa?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Yet the physical world is likely the bigger opportunity, and paradoxically a slower one than Mr. Market currently expects. Watch for mainstream utility, not backflips. Stay tuned.
