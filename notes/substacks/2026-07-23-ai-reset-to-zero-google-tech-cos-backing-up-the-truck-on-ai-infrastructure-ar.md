ARD #125](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=208250013&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIwODI1MDAxMywiaWF0IjoxNzg0ODM4NTcwLCJleHAiOjE3ODc0MzA1NzAsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.kjWXrWqsLTy1lToNJOiFzYa4bTM28GE-iOeNRJuy3Yo)

### ...Google ramps AI capex post Q2 2026, Meta/Microsoft/Amazon report next week, & Big Tech’s debt tops $1.65+ trillion.

Today’s theme: the big tech companies are ‘backing up the truck’ on AI-infrastructure spend. Google is the latest example this earnings season — ramping AI capex hard even as its free cash flow goes negative for the first time in decades — with Meta, Microsoft and Amazon up next week on their own earnings calls. Meanwhile, Big Tech’s combined on- and off-balance-sheet AI debt and equity now tops $1.65+ trillion. This is the [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/3c88fd0d-1af5-45d7-8c27-8cdf1fc5c553?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) at its most capital-intensive. Three events, each with my Take, then my Overall Take. Plus a Gadget AI on Ford running Apple Maps in a new EV, and two questions. Let’s get started.

## (1) Google — Ramping AI Capex Even as Free Cash Flow Turns Negative

MP TAKE: Google’s quarter again came in above expectations on all the key businesses and metrics. Even the worry over AI Search being a touch weaker was overdone, given the current success of Google Gemini AI Mode I discussed recently. The company raised its 2026 AI-capex guide to as much as $205 billion, up from $190 billion-plus — even as its free cash flow went negative for the first time in a long while (FT clocks a $6 billion cash burn on the quarter), and it’s bolstering its balance sheet with a recent $80 billion-plus equity raise. This from a company still throwing off $112 billion-plus in profits — no dearth of cash; the spend is simply running ahead of the balance sheet. The demand signal underneath it is real: Bloomberg reports the cloud backlog ballooning to $514 billion. As the WSJ puts it, Google has [the muscle to overpower spending worries](https://substack.com/redirect/47946666-d668-45bc-9e7b-b889687cf9fd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Its approach is different from its frontier-lab AI peers. Google isn’t competing as aggressively on front-of-the-line benchmark models. It’s instead shipping a slate of Gemini models that are fast, efficient and relatively cheap to run at mass-market scale — this week’s [new, cheaper](https://substack.com/redirect/e803b109-eb20-4890-8464-c8f76f4ceb73?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)[Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite and 3.5 Flash Cyber](https://substack.com/redirect/e803b109-eb20-4890-8464-c8f76f4ceb73?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) are the latest. That’s a ‘good enough’ AI strategy the market hasn’t fully absorbed yet. It worked for Microsoft with Internet Explorer browser vs Netscape in the Internet Wave. And likely will work for Google in the [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/3c88fd0d-1af5-45d7-8c27-8cdf1fc5c553?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). In my view, Google remains the best-positioned global consumer-AI firm — along with Apple (Not stock advice).

## (3) Global AI Capex at Unprecedented Levels — the $1.65+ Trillion Question

MP TAKE: Cumulative debt at the US tech giants is up roughly 8x in four years, to an estimated $1.65 trillion, as AI capex keeps ramping quarter by quarter. The latest Nikkei Asia study looks at Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Oracle: the debt on their actual balance sheets comes to about $1.35 trillion — but the off-balance-sheet piece is the eye-opener. Meta’s off-balance-sheet debt alone is pegged at ~$420 billion — about 3x its recorded debt — largely through SPVs (special-purpose vehicles) that PE and VC firms set up so institutional investors from around the world can pour money into AI data centers.

It’s framed as ‘hidden, opaque and scary,’ but these SPVs are well understood institutional investment vehicles — the kind BlackRock and the big PE/VC funds run all the time — not the 2008 CDO shenanigans the comparison implies. If I’d draw one real parallel to ‘08, it isn’t the balance-sheet debt; it’s the [NASDAQ index fast-tracking SpaceXAI’s IPO](https://substack.com/redirect/8a40b962-ce37-4383-872c-801ccea1e5b1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)[](https://substack.com/redirect/8a40b962-ce37-4383-872c-801ccea1e5b1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)into the index in weeks rather than the usual seasoning — and maybe doing the same for freshly-minted Anthropic/OpenAI IPOs. Investors are currently flashing green lights on this kind of multi-year funding. We’ve seen versions of it in prior tech waves — but never at this scale. Alarmists will, of course, point to the swelling debt. But for now, most of the investors leaning in are calculating the risk-reward as positive.

## MP OVERALL TAKE

Google and the rest of Big Tech are ‘backing up the truck’ on AI-infrastructure capex — into the trillions. The markets are talking about all of it, especially through this week’s and next week’s quarterly results, but they’re still leaning into the spend. My view: the risk/reward analysis favors continuing to ‘step on the gas’ after ‘backing up the truck.’ Even with cumulative industry investment running into the $1.6-trillion-plus range, the enterprise and institutional demand — globally — is there for all the AI-data-center infrastructure being rolled out, right down to the earlier generations of chips (just look at the spot and long-term contract markets).

And here’s the counter-intuitive part that keeps the whole thing relatively intact: the flow of new, more efficient open-source AI models doesn’t shrink demand — via the ‘[Jevons Paradox](https://substack.com/redirect/d2a20f42-0e78-4ee2-aa98-23adb3036fdb?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA),’ cheaper compute expands and accelerates the global market for AI compute. It sounds backwards, but it’s a fundamental, bottoms-up market trend for now — which is also why barring or slowing the best open-source models, many now from China, would be detrimental to US long-term interests. There will be volatility and ‘air pockets’ — stocks are down today — but the comparison with previous tech ‘bubbles’ — like the internet — is premature. I know a little about that one; I was at the front tables then.

# GADGET AI

## Ford Puts Apple Maps in the Driver’s Seat — a First

### MP TAKE — Gadget AI (take-first, Ep 103+)

Here’s a notable first: Ford is building Apple Maps into a new, ‘skunk works’-driven ~$30,000 EV for next year — at the operating-system level. That’s different from the usual Apple CarPlay or Google Android Auto, which run off your smartphone inside the car; this bakes Apple’s mapping into the vehicle itself. Both Apple and Google have been working for years to get their software deeper into cars, with limited success — GM has moved away from CarPlay entirely, as has Tesla with its full vertical stack. So Ford’s move is particularly notable for Apple, which has spent years as the #2 in maps behind Google Maps and Waze. A sub-$60 billion Ford leaning on a ~$5 trillion Apple underlines the software age we’re in. The car operating-system race worldwide is still in its early innings — with Chinese players like Xiaomi, Geely and others racing ahead too.
