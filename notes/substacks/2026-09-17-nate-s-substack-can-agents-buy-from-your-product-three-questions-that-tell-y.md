### What I took away from my conversation with Emily Sands, Stripe’s Head of Data & AI.

Some of the decisions that determine whether agents can use your product are being made as fraud decisions, pricing decisions, and onboarding decisions. They may never show up in a meeting about AI strategy.

Cursor came to Stripe with a complaint that Emily recalled this way: “Radar is world class, but it is not solving my problems.” Radar is Stripe’s fraud protection product. It had spent years learning to recognize bad payments. Cursor needed help with people who could cause substantial losses without making a payment at all. They were creating accounts, consuming free credits, running through trials, or accumulating usage they would never pay for. By the time a conventional payment fraud system had something to evaluate, the compute had already been spent.

I asked Emily about trust because that’s what I keep hearing about from people working with agents. We can see their capabilities improving. We’re much less certain about how to let them act on our behalf, especially when those actions cost money or affect somebody else. The Cursor story gave us a way into that question through an actual business problem, and following it led somewhere I think deserves more attention than it gets: a company’s ability to distinguish good users from abusive ones can determine which customers it can afford to serve.
