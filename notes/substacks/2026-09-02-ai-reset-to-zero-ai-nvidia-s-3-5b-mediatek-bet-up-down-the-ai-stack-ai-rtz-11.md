# [AI: Nvidia’s $3.5B MediaTek Bet, Up & Down the AI Stack. AI-RTZ #1197](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=213316286&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxMzMxNjI4NiwiaWF0IjoxNzg4MzI1NjY0LCJleHAiOjE3OTA5MTc2NjQsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.6SjE2slxvyWYrR5NtD8-6H3DEf14jVrjrgPefMPMRbQ)

### ...a $3.5 billion bet that keeps AI running on Nvidia’s platform, from giant data centers to laptops, phones & cars.

Nvidia keeps extending its AI advantage up and down the vertical AI stack. The latest move: a $3.5 billion investment in Taiwan’s MediaTek, its largest direct investment outside the US.

MediaTek provides chips for local smartphones and computers in particular, competing with Qualcomm and others.

It extends the ‘Kingmaker’ thread I have tracked from [Nvidia as AI Computing Kingmaker](https://substack.com/redirect/7ede3f77-501d-485c-902f-5addfa6edf15?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) to [‘Uber AI Investor & Banker’](https://substack.com/redirect/18b8de47-ca04-41a6-bc94-b03be3c06cd7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

And this weekend’s [Nvidia long-game Part 2](https://substack.com/redirect/d5e8de06-0f91-421a-8eb4-80da92f221c3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), right as this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cf75df5b-a825-4bc2-8912-f2593c07a302?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) pushes into laptops, phones, cars and robots.

Bloomberg lays it out in [“Nvidia to Invest $3.5 Billion in Chipmaker MediaTek”](https://substack.com/redirect/c568da41-39fa-4862-a424-fe786d93c966?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA):

> “Nvidia Corp. is investing $3.5 billion in MediaTek Inc. to deepen collaboration with the Taiwanese chipmaker.The investment will see MediaTek using Nvidia’s NVLink Fusion and NVHBM technology to help components communicate more seamlessly in data centers.The agreement is part of Nvidia’s effort to ensure the future of artificial intelligence is built around its hardware platform and standards.”

The financial mechanics are as follows. Nvidia is buying bonds convertible into MediaTek shares, part of a $3.9 billion convertible sale that Alphabet/Google and others also joined.

MediaTek adopts Nvidia’s NVLink Fusion and the newly announced NVHBM technology, so its chips plug cleanly into Nvidia-standard data centers. The two will also collaborate on AI platforms for personal computers and automobiles. So local AI and devices I’ve been talking about [for both Nvidia](https://substack.com/redirect/33780884-cb37-493d-93bc-2dd1c3654af5?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and [Apple.](https://substack.com/redirect/f4b4c8ea-9e18-481a-92d5-d6a4e726e6b2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)

The important takeaway here is the scope.

This is a multi-generation collaboration between Nvidia and MediaTek. And it spans AI infrastructure in the cloud, what I’ve been calling ‘mainframe AI’, and ‘local AI computing’ on computers and smartphones. And extends to ‘Physical AI’ chips needed in robots, cars and other AI devices.

Nvidia’s chunky capital commitment and Alphabet/Google’s participation affirms MediaTek’s custom silicon capabilities and positioning within the broader AI ecosystem. They’re an important custom silicon provider like Broadcom and others.

Additionally, the adoption of Nvidia’s networking ‘NVLink’ technologies expands MediaTek’s participation in rack-scale AI systems. It gives them the ability to capture new value across connectivity, AI memory architecture, and advanced packaging. All hot areas in AI chip production. From Asia and elsewhere.

This partnership strengthens MediaTek’s longer-term AI [ASIC](https://substack.com/redirect/edaac6c3-d7c9-4cd9-8ced-b1e6506a878f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)(application specific integrated circuit) positioning.

It can potentially enhance MediaTek’s ability to win more projects from other customers down the road.

And it bolsters MediaTek’s custom HBM (high bandwidth memory) base-die design capability as a less appreciated, potential new business category, extending its role into memory subsystems.

So it’s a ‘win-win’ for both companies.

NVLink is Nvidia’s high-speed wiring that lets many AI chips talk to each other fast enough to behave like one giant computer. [NVLink Fusion](https://substack.com/redirect/1df133d9-8eae-465c-b146-dc3a18eb8e35?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) opens that wiring to chips Nvidia did not design. A company’s custom AI chip can now slot into Nvidia-style racks and data centers, instead of building all that plumbing from scratch.

NVHBM, [announced by Nvidia days before this deal](https://substack.com/redirect/1df133d9-8eae-465c-b146-dc3a18eb8e35?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), is Nvidia’s new take on the specialized ‘high bandwidth memory’ stacked next to every AI chip. It moves the memory traffic controller off the AI chip and into the memory stack itself. Per Nvidia, that frees up to a quarter of the chip’s area for actual computing, with up to 30% more memory bandwidth at lower power.

And MediaTek’s part, per [the joint announcement](https://substack.com/redirect/7fd2c695-4c92-4058-bc84-08026abc5dad?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): it is one of the world’s top custom chip design houses, with deep expertise in power-efficient chips, packaging and connectivity. It gives customers a faster, lower-risk path from a custom chip to a full AI factory. That is what Huang means by the two supply chains merging.

Jensen Huang calls it a massive engineering alignment, on a road map he says runs a decade.

The Kingmaker play, again. The pattern is by now familiar. Nvidia invests in customers, suppliers and even would-be rivals to keep the AI buildout organized around its platform and standards. MediaTek is trying to challenge Broadcom and Marvell in helping hyperscalers design their own custom chips. Rather than fight that tide, Nvidia is wiring itself into it.

The Huang line that matters:

> “Nvidia’s networking ecosystem is now part of MediaTek’s supply chain”,

With MediaTek’s XPUs part of Nvidia’s in return, both companies expanding their supply chains in one deal. Amazon just made a similar pledge, deploying 2 million more Nvidia components and using Nvidia’s interconnects with its in-house chips.

This is the ‘frenemies’ dynamic I have written about since [Nvidia vs its top customers on AI GPUs](https://substack.com/redirect/9dd768b6-b173-4801-86d0-767c320c6df5?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), and across [the AI Coding space](https://substack.com/redirect/e94e9e04-3c46-49ca-a8a8-c765e417c85f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Even when the biggest customers build chips to replace Nvidia’s, the connective tissue stays Nvidia’s.

The circularity question follows these deals around, and investors keep asking it. Huang’s answer here: MediaTek runs its own profitable business, and this is alignment, not manufactured demand. Both sides of that debate are worth holding onto, with Nvidia forecasting a 70% sales increase next year.

Down the stack: local AI in laptops, phones and cars. Here is the leverage that interests me most. MediaTek is one of the quiet powers of low-power edge silicon, now pivoting hard into AI. It already helps build Nvidia’s RTX Spark push into laptops, part of the [local AI computers](https://substack.com/redirect/f153df99-7971-45d2-ac59-6e904532a2b8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) thread with Microsoft.

We have discussed MediaTek before in the context of [OpenAI’s own OS-driven AI smartphone](https://substack.com/redirect/19c00aa8-6887-40d5-b403-effcb356bff1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), and around [Apple’s AI camera sensors for its device ecosystem](https://substack.com/redirect/65626980-62bb-4509-9339-87d986b69bd7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The local vs cloud AI split is where the next billions of AI devices get decided, and MediaTek gives Nvidia additional leverage on the local side, to go with its data center dominance in the cloud. MediaTek’s own AI chip business is guided to roughly $2 billion this year, aiming at 15% of an $80 billion segment next year, and its market value has roughly tripled in recent months.

The Taiwan and geopolitics layer. Huang was born in Taiwan, and this is Nvidia’s biggest direct bet there outside its TSMC manufacturing dependence. It lands while Jensen keeps [balancing US and China](https://substack.com/redirect/c68feb13-7ec6-4c0d-97d1-d1c584de5623?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), from [hundreds of billions in US investment pledges](https://substack.com/redirect/6cb25aab-4247-4cf1-a7e9-aeda5a84c2cc?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) to [riding Air Force One to China](https://substack.com/redirect/7c602985-6961-48fb-8879-9f0e51395142?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

And it strengthens the case I made for [Nvidia and Apple as the US’s global open source AI champions](https://substack.com/redirect/0d9f7f25-9880-434e-be42-c98bbdaaf9f0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), with the open hardware-and-standards stack doing the work, the same thread as [‘Rockets Are Hard’](https://substack.com/redirect/e5482fd7-f0c8-40ab-a6a7-6f123d5b671d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) on open source AI vs China. A deeper MediaTek tie pulls more of the world’s edge silicon into the US-led stack.

My take: the stack is the strategy. Nvidia’s accelerators won the cloud layer. NVLink and networking are winning the standards layer. And now the MediaTek bet works the edge layer, where laptops, phones, cars and robots will run their AI locally. Each investment looks circular if you squint at the money flows alone. Viewed as a stack, each one adds a floor to the same building.

The check on the enthusiasm: standards leverage invites regulators and rivals, Broadcom and Marvell will not stand still, and the edge is a market Nvidia has tried and stumbled in before, from phones a decade ago to its slow laptop entry now. A decade-long roadmap is a promise, not a result. But as the [long game](https://substack.com/redirect/d5e8de06-0f91-421a-8eb4-80da92f221c3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) I outlined this weekend argued, Nvidia is playing longer clocks than most, and this deal fits that arc precisely.

This is Nvidia solidifying its hardware and software options at both the cloud and local end for [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cf75df5b-a825-4bc2-8912-f2593c07a302?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) opportunities, up and down the stack. Stay tuned.
