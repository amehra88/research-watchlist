# [‘Coming from Behind’. Cognition, ElevenLabs & Amazon AWS. ARD #158](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=214810412&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNDgxMDQxMiwiaWF0IjoxNzg4OTEzNjAzLCJleHAiOjE3OTE1MDU2MDMsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.NC6HP1jlDYCtoI6VzNKWhUmXJ3xjJbUk7yoJMn2dd60)

### ...Cognition doubles to $48B off Acqui-hire leftovers. ElevenLabs eyes 2028 IPO, profitably. #1 AWS rebounds in AI.

Every day in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/eb0fd6da-cc31-4f0a-8670-b4e5d03fed5f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) we talk about the leaders. The frontier models, the trillion-dollar compute commitments, the mega-IPOs coming in the next few weeks. Today is about the other side of that spectrum. Three companies gained ground on the leaders and they did it three completely different ways.

Two are AI startups. One is the biggest incumbent in the cloud business. All three were viewed as being behind the eight ball not very long ago, and all three are coming from behind, and shining in their own right. That is what the opportunities are in technology waves of all stripes, and I have watched them for over fifty years now. With the AI tech wave it is bigger than ever, and the zeros, as usual, come on faster than ever before.

One bought the leftovers of somebody else’s collapsed deal. One walked through a door the giants were not standing in front of. And one rebuilt its own broken plumbing with six engineers. Not one of them won by having the best model. Plus a Gadget AI on Meta finally launching its personal AI agents, and the trust problem it has to solve first, ‘coming from behind’ vs peers like Apple, Google et al. My takes on all below, as discussed on the show.

## (1) The Latest AI Coding Phenom Raises at $48 Billion

First up. Cognition, the company behind the Devin coding agent, has raised more than $2 billion at a $48 billion valuation, nearly double where it sat in May, per The Information.

Andreessen Horowitz and Accel led the Series E. Founders Fund, General Catalyst and Avenir came along from the existing table, and Benchmark, Bessemer, Kleiner Perkins, Greylock, Lightspeed and Altimeter all put money in. That is most of Sand Hill Road in one round.

The business underneath: roughly $900 million of annualized revenue, about $75 million a month, up from $492 million in May. It roughly doubled in four months. Customers named in the reporting are Nvidia, GE Aerospace, Citi, BNY, Santander and Mercedes. Those are production deployments, not pilots. And the company is not yet three years old. The other number matters just as much: cash burn could reach $800 million this year, because the specialized servers to train and run this are expensive.

My take: start with where this company was. A few hundred million dollars two years ago. Forty-eight billion tonight. And I have already talked about [Cursor being bought by Elon’s SpaceX right before the IPO for over $60 billion](https://substack.com/redirect/cabf3ff3-bde7-4ba8-b3b2-a69fca4632e0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), the largest tech private company payout in the history of Silicon Valley. We may have another one not very far behind it.

Now look at how Cognition got the pieces. When the Windsurf deal with OpenAI fell apart, it fell apart because Microsoft said that if OpenAI buys Windsurf, we get all the IP under our deal. OpenAI said no, we cannot do that, and the deal collapsed. Google came in, took the intellectual property and hired the core founders and part of the R&D team. I covered that here in [the ‘Frenemies’ piece](https://substack.com/redirect/20646eb0-f7db-402e-8e84-cb42efbb4b1d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) at the time: $2.4 billion, about thirty times Windsurf’s annualized revenue. What was left over, the stub, went to Cognition. That stub is now inside a business doing $900 million a year.

That’s a first in acqui-hire strategies kicked off by Microsoft only two years ago with the [$650 million Inflection AI deal](https://substack.com/redirect/18c37f7b-bbce-4245-8cd9-c9eb66ba2cbf?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)as a [backup to its OpenAI partnership.](https://substack.com/redirect/10e531f6-c9c1-4e3c-8c32-64d811130d44?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)

This is the first example in this wave of a tech AI startup where the acqui-hired founders left behind a stub that ended up being worth massive amounts on paper, at least for now.

And it tells you something about this wave that the headline valuations do not. The assets that get discarded in a scramble are not always the worthless ones. Often they are just the ones that were inconvenient to whoever was doing the scrambling.

It also summarizes the white-hot nature of the AI coding space, the same heat driving Anthropic toward a mega-IPO in the coming weeks on the strength of Claude Code and Cowork, with [OpenAI coming fast as number two](https://substack.com/redirect/ed200788-0982-47fe-9393-34b7fa04f1b0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Cognition coming from behind, from a failed acqui-hire, as a stub, is a remarkable story, and that is why I wanted to lead with it.

Now the discipline, because it cuts both ways. Forty-eight billion on $900 million of revenue is roughly fifty-three times. And the burn is most of the revenue. This is a company growing beautifully and consuming nearly everything it makes to do it. Both are true at once, and the second one gets repriced faster than the first.

## (2) Voice AI Gets an Accelerating Startup Against the Majors

Story two. ElevenLabs, the voice AI company, has hired a chief financial officer and is eyeing an IPO in 2028, per The Information.

The hire is the big reveal. Ethan Tandowsky, who was CFO of Adyen, the European payments company, since 2023, and who was in the finance seats through Adyen’s 2018 public offering. You do not hire that person unless the date is getting real.

The company itself is London-based, founded in 2022 by two Poles, Mati Staniszewski and Piotr Dąbkowski. Its earliest customers were individuals making voice-overs for YouTube videos. Where it is now: enterprise voice agents at Stripe, Klarna and Deutsche Telekom, handling customer support and sales screening calls. It also just hired its first chief revenue officer, Ashley Kramer, who ran enterprise sales at OpenAI. It says it is on track for roughly $600 million of annual recurring revenue, more than half of that from large enterprises, up from about $350 million at the end of last year.

And this is the line to absorb. The company is profitable. It was valued at $11 billion in February on a $500 million round led by Sequoia, and raised again in May from Nvidia’s venture arm, BlackRock, Wellington and Santander at an undisclosed valuation.

My take: I have been writing about voice as an AI interface for a while now. The [multimodal piece in 2023](https://substack.com/redirect/1509939b-68dc-4e9b-9f61-d162ded80b80?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). [‘Voice AIs are back’](https://substack.com/redirect/9fabc425-5928-41d1-b7c6-b48a36bb2f5b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in the spring of 2024, when OpenAI had its moment with a voice modeled after Scarlett Johansson because Sam Altman really loved the sci-fi movie Her. Despite its dystopian ending. And the episode that followed. [Google and Apple trading rounds](https://substack.com/redirect/3a904ab8-ca01-40c6-a316-1b8c3ca7da79?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) through that summer. And [‘Voice stirring AIs far beyond a mouse’](https://substack.com/redirect/b8ab3404-a175-4b7f-be77-386c02cd5bdf?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) at the end of last year.

Through all of it, the assumption was that voice is primarily a consumer-facing service and gets won by whoever owns the consumer. Think Google with Gemini. Think OpenAI. Think Apple, eventually, with its Siri AI launching this week, which we will get to later in the week.

That is the assumption ElevenLabs is rapidly re-framing. Rather than pursuing a consumer-facing strategy against the big boys, it decided to go the enterprise route, providing voice as an underlying enterprise service, much like what Stripe did for payments, Twilio for cloud communications, and countless others in the software-as-a-service (SaaS) space. Now modified for AI. It walked through a door the giants were not standing in front of.

And put that profit line next to what we have been covering all week. Two frontier labs walking into their IPOs having committed more than a trillion dollars between them on compute, burning tens of billions a year to get there. A four-year-old company out of London is profitable selling voice agents, and just hired the CFO who took a European fintech public.

Now the vulnerability, because it is real.

ElevenLabs’ agents run in part on Anthropic’s and OpenAI’s models, so some of that margin flows straight back to companies it competes with.

The IPO advisers quoted in the piece flag exactly that, along with the question of whether the product stays sticky as the labs keep shipping at the same customers. It also competes with Sierra and Decagon on one side and with OpenAI and Google on the other.

And it is not alone in the IPO timing; Perplexity has said it wants 2028 as well. But coming from behind in enterprise looks nothing like coming from behind in consumer. Enterprise buyers switch slowly and pay reliably. Consumer attention is winner-take-most. They picked the harder market to enter and the relatively easier one to hold.

## (3) Six Engineers Rebuild Amazon’s Bedrock

Story three. Six AWS engineers rebuilt Bedrock, Amazon’s service for running AI models, and Amazon is pulling AI spending back from Microsoft’s Azure, per The Information.

Start with how bad it was. Bedrock shipped in the fall of 2023, built in a hurry by engineers working sixty-hour weeks, and it showed. Customers got error messages. Capacity requests took up to three weeks. Anthropic’s models ran as much as sixty-three percent slower on Bedrock than going direct, measured in tokens per second. An internal sales report said the problems were costing customers, and senior leaders called the capacity situation a disaster.

Then Anthony Liguori and five other senior engineers built a new inference layer called Project Mantle. And they built it using AWS’s own AI coding tool, Kiro.

The insight underneath is the good part. One of the people who built it put it this way: at the heart of inference, this is not really a web service, it is a scheduling system. Bedrock version one had been designed with twenty years of web-service assumptions. AI workloads are spiky, agent runs are long, and none of that fits the old shape. So they stopped patching a web service and built a scheduler. Priority levels per task. Workload isolation, so one customer’s spike does not eat another’s capacity. Checkpointing, so a long agent run does not start over. And native compatibility with OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s APIs, where version one made you write custom code.

The results: capacity waits went from three weeks to two days. More customers were added to Bedrock in the first half of this year than in its first two years. Second-quarter spending on it exceeded every prior quarter combined. Andy Jassy called Mantle the backbone of the service in his investor letter. And Bedrock now runs mostly on [Amazon’s own Trainium chips](https://substack.com/redirect/4e6d4ebd-c9d7-4d86-b11a-05e31ea81ffe?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), capacity that does not sit in the Nvidia queue behind everyone else. Then the strategic move: a few months after Mantle, AWS added OpenAI’s models to Bedrock, which ended the exclusive that was Azure’s whole argument to enterprise buyers.

My take: in the last couple of years I have written a lot about how [Microsoft Azure as number two](https://substack.com/redirect/0848e3a9-db4f-42f4-b0c6-3988d36bdb53?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and [Google Cloud as number three](https://substack.com/redirect/afd20ea3-51ee-4c18-995b-ee2fc67e665e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) have actually run ahead of Amazon AWS in providing AI-oriented services for businesses large, medium and small. I called Amazon AWS the [AI underdog](https://substack.com/redirect/00a136c9-4d7b-4be1-8e9f-d3a5be721869?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) back in August of 2023, and wrote about [those scaling problems](https://substack.com/redirect/8318e3bd-3867-474c-8bab-33c32bc83fc9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in the spring of 2025, so this is not hindsight.

Well, Amazon addressed that issue as an incumbent and as a big behemoth. They did it with half a dozen engineers on what was effectively a code-red basis. They did not call it that, but that is what it was. And this is the purest come-from-behind story of the three today, because nobody rescued them. There was no acquisition and no pivot. They had to fix it themselves.

Hold onto the detail that six engineers did it with an AI coding tool. The company used AI coding to rebuild the service that sells AI models. The Information’s story is worth reading in full for how they did it, what tools and technologies they used, how they approached customers and got them to try the revised Bedrock. It is a fascinating story for anyone interested in how AI technologies get built, deployed, redeployed and sold at massive scale.

And although Amazon AWS still has a lot of catching up to do to Azure and Google Cloud on AI services, the number one company in the cloud space is putting up quite the royal fight, and working like a startup to do it.

That is notable in terms of coming from behind. Even incumbents, the biggest one in the business, are coming from behind in AI for their business customers.

My overall take: three stories, and three different ways to come from behind. Cognition bought the wreckage. A deal collapsed, the founders went to Google, and what nobody wanted turned into part of a nearly fifty-billion-dollar company, with a lot to go before they sleep and see the rewards of their turnaround. ElevenLabs picked the open door. Not consumer voice, where the giants are fighting, but enterprise voice agents, and it is finding a way to do that profitably and grow fast enough to hire a CFO and point at a 2028 IPO. Amazon fixed its own plumbing, rebuilding Bedrock with half a dozen senior engineers.

What ties this all together is just the will to do it and the execution. Systematic, step by step. Do the hard work, focus on the problem, reframe the problem, build things. These are blocking-and-tackling things that apply to everything in life and to every business. And here in the AI business, if you do this fast enough and with considered effort, the rewards are bigger than anything we have seen in prior tech waves.

Notice also that not one of them won by having the best model. Cognition does not have a frontier model. ElevenLabs runs partly on Anthropic’s and OpenAI’s. Amazon’s edge is a scheduler and its own chips. In a wave where the whole public conversation is about which model tops which benchmark, today’s three winners were decided by distribution, by focus, and by the engineering underneath the model.

And that is what I wanted to highlight, because there are no default winners and losers, or losers and winners.

That is very easy to assume in today’s days of very active headlines: this one is behind, that one is ahead, that one is behind again. We have seen this for every one of the big tech companies, from Google to Meta to Elon to the top leaders. Anthropic became the Coke to OpenAI’s Pepsi, and who knows if that will switch around. All these valuations are fragile.

All these businesses are fragile. These are Cambrian explosion times, and things can change on a dime. But with concerted focus, it is nice to note the ones that do come from behind, on this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/eb0fd6da-cc31-4f0a-8670-b4e5d03fed5f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

## Gadget AI: Meta Launches Muse, and Points It at Your Glasses

Today’s Gadget AI is the agent Meta has been promising for a year. Meta is finally launching its personal AI agents, called Muse, built on its latest models under chief AI officer Alexandr Wang, who came to Meta through the Scale acqui-hire, per Axios and Bloomberg. Support for Meta’s AI glasses is coming soon.

What it is: a chat interface, like a text thread, but proactive and long-running rather than turn by turn. You name it, give it an avatar, set how it talks to you. Underneath, it runs on a dedicated virtual machine in Meta’s cloud, with a built-in browser you can actually watch work. The pricing tells you what it costs them: a free tier, then $20 a month, then $100. Wang says most people will stay on free and the paid tiers help cover the compute. And there is no advertising inside it. Meta, charging a subscription.

Then the part that decides whether any of it works, which is trust. There is a separate system called Sentinel sitting between the agent and the world. Muse proposes an action; Sentinel allows it, blocks it, or sends it to you. Read access is separated from write access, scoped to a service, a task, or a window of time. And Meta said out loud the thing most vendors will not: if you ask people to approve everything, they stop reading and click yes. A confidential version where Meta cannot see inside your workspace is targeted before year end.

My take: Meta has been very focused, especially Mark Zuckerberg leading the charge, on providing AI services and AI agents to consumers. I wrote in July of last year that [Zuck was focused on personal AI superintelligence for billions of people](https://substack.com/redirect/d71568c6-faa7-4a36-ae9f-88f1113b9965?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) while his peers sold to developers and enterprises. Muse is that thesis shipping.

They will distribute them through the smart glasses I have talked about a lot, where Meta has over seven million out there and hopes to grow them to seventy million and higher. And they will distribute these personal AI agents via Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp and Messenger, which reach billions of users globally. Nobody else in this race has that.

Add the glasses, and Meta has something neither Anthropic nor OpenAI has: a personal agent with a camera and a microphone already on the customer’s head, sold through channels it owns.

Notice it belongs in today’s theme too. On models, Meta has been behind. I covered [Muse Spark entering the arena](https://substack.com/redirect/548738df-92fd-421a-b8f5-d9ac839c7d99?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) as a closed model in April, and it has sat in the best-of-the-rest tier since. So Meta is coming from behind on capability and going straight at distribution instead.

They are also trying a different twist on another element, and I have written a lot about this: Meta has a deficiency on trust.

Consumers do not trust Meta as much, because they think Meta will take their data, optimize it and sell advertising against it, which is all true. Meta is better at doing that than almost everybody, having surpassed Google on ad revenues in the last year or so. So trust is an issue. Here Meta is trying something different, interjecting a new element between the agent and the consumer: Sentinel, which monitors whether what the AI is proposing is good for the user and blocks it or allows it. We will see if this works. It is an interesting twist on Meta technically trying to fix the trust problem. The proof is going to be in the execution at scale.

We have also seen how this fails. I wrote about [OpenClaw showing both the promise and the fear](https://substack.com/redirect/670bbb3f-ebd6-47ea-9503-636e038aa595?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) of AI agents, and about [the fragility of Cowork-type agent apps](https://substack.com/redirect/573d6641-1780-4ee5-9f78-c79f1338191b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in April. The example in today’s coverage is Meta’s own researcher, who gave an agent access to her email and watched it delete files off her Mac. Restaurants are already deleting the accounts of people who send agents to book tables.

So the right question is whether consumers will trust it, and my answer is that they will trust it right up until the first bad incident, and then the permissions model has to hold. This is [not one Skynet](https://substack.com/redirect/0ef8e984-9d94-498c-b5ab-0aabb7389c75?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). It is billions of small agents carrying credentials, and the real fight of the next two years is over what they are allowed to touch. It will be interesting to watch Meta come from behind here too, in a space being executed profitably and successfully by so many others: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Apple now, and soon Meta.

Cognition bought the stub of a $2.4 billion acqui-hire, and is now raising over $2 billion at about $48 billion.

MP Take: This is the first example in this wave of an acqui-hire leaving behind a stub that ended up worth massive amounts on paper, at least for now. If it goes on the track of Cursor, which Elon’s SpaceX bought for over $60 billion right before the IPO, it could meet or beat that number. It also tells you how white hot the AI coding space is, the same heat driving Anthropic toward its IPO with OpenAI right behind it. Coming from behind, from a failed acqui-hire, as a stub, is a remarkable story.

### Three Ways to Come From Behind in AI

Bought the wreckage, picked the open door, fixed its own plumbing. Three routes, one afternoon.

MP Take: What ties this all together is the will to do it and the execution. Systematic, step by step. Do the hard work, focus on the problem, reframe the problem, build things. These are blocking and tackling things that apply to everything in life and to every business. And in AI, if you do this fast enough and with considered effort, the rewards are bigger than anything we have seen in prior tech waves.

### AI’s Cambrian Explosion: No Default Winners

Anthropic became the Coke to OpenAI’s Pepsi. Who knows if that switches around.

MP Take: All these valuations are fragile. All these businesses are fragile. These are Cambrian explosion times, and things can change on a dime. There are no default winners and losers, which is very easy to assume in days of very active headlines: this one is behind, that one is ahead, that one is behind again. But with concerted focus, it is worth noting the ones that do come from behind. That is why I picked these three stories today rather than another round of who is ahead.

### Meta’s Muse: AI Agents on Your Glasses

Meta finally ships its personal AI agents, and puts a permission layer called Sentinel in front of them.

MP Take: Meta has a deficiency on trust. Consumers do not trust Meta as much, because they think Meta will take their data, optimize it and sell advertising against it, which is all true. Meta is better at doing that than almost everybody, having surpassed Google on ad revenues in the last year or so. Sentinel is an interesting twist on Meta technically trying to fix the trust problem. The proof is going to be in the execution at scale, and it will be interesting to watch Meta come from behind here too.
