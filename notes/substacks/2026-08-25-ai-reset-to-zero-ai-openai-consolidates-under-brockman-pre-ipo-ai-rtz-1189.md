# [AI: OpenAI Consolidates under Brockman, pre-IPO. AI-RTZ #1189](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=212247445&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxMjI0NzQ0NSwiaWF0IjoxNzg3NjM0NDg2LCJleHAiOjE3OTAyMjY0ODYsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.EtGs89zdq3b9EAU11QPRIzYnC_SFoBtMMuvsoXJx_o0)

### ...Simo, Lightcap & Dresser out, Brockman runs day-to-day, & the 2027 IPO re-grounding vs Anthropic.

So many flows and cross-flows at OpenAI, in so many directions.

Yesterday we looked at Anthropic’s bankers pitching the largest IPO in history. Today, the other half of the mega-IPO cadence in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/a89a8d57-6ee2-4ded-a162-561710bec43a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): OpenAI, and who is actually running it on the road to [its own public offering targeted for 2027](https://substack.com/redirect/b15051ea-9b18-43fa-9a42-9cbbd2d432d7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The Verge lays it out in [“It’s Greg Brockman’s OpenAI now”](https://substack.com/redirect/1607004d-d14e-47c1-b3e5-83ce1210e452?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA):

Sam Altman remains CEO, but in day-to-day operations, the president and co-founder has quietly become the big boss, his purview expanding with every departure in a year that included the Elon Musk jury trial [we covered from opening arguments](https://substack.com/redirect/b8118b0b-10e2-4eef-bba9-567baf0d64c0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), an Apple trade-secrets suit, and a steady exodus of the company’s biggest names. The key items to build on: the exits, the consolidation, and what it says about the 2027 IPO strategy.

The people churn is an ongoing OpenAI topic [discussed here](https://substack.com/redirect/bf75bd84-c91e-4b8a-98da-f74502fc2b97?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) for months, and the Verge assembles the full casualty list. April alone saw the Sora chief, the science VP, and the B2B applications CTO exit, with the CMO and [Fidji Simo](http://xn--openais%20simo%20tasked%20with%20keeping%20chatgpt%20ahead%20of%20bot%20rivals-hx53c/), OpenAI’s [CEO of AGI deployment we profiled when she arrived](https://substack.com/redirect/2d4ab411-0f51-40c9-8ccd-5f724ac1ac1f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), [went to work](https://substack.com/redirect/96467605-208d-43b6-a6dc-59a7273a60d4?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), and [got the close-up treatment](https://substack.com/redirect/c1ff8f0d-9156-47ec-bdfc-3037fadbfcb7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), both departing on medical leaves; Simo officially stepped down in July, weeks after the IPO filing. This month, chief revenue officer Denise Dresser left after just eight months, and longtime COO Brad Lightcap, latterly on ‘special projects’, announced he is leaving to start something new, [the churn we mapped as OpenAI pre-markets its IPO](https://substack.com/redirect/26bf7941-1d51-4313-b2da-fcec85a42a58?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Each wave expanded Brockman’s authority. When Simo went on leave, he took product, including the super app effort. The revenue-focused reorg then handed him the entire ‘scaling’ arm, virtually every part of the commercial operation. When Dresser departed, the press release quote came from Brockman, not Altman. The engineering workhorse of the founding team, the man who resigned in solidarity within hours of Altman’s 2023 ouster, now answers only to Altman himself. On CNBC he waved off the red flags: “I’m a constant, Sam [Altman] is a constant”, pointing to eras of different leaders as a strength.

Consolidation collapses decision layers and trims some of the most expensive salary lines ahead of a listing. Standard pre-IPO housekeeping, though the clustering in so short a window is unusual.

It fits the repositioning tracked all summer. CFO Sarah Friar telling investors [enterprise is now bigger than consumer](https://substack.com/redirect/52d631d6-11a5-4ca8-9650-76990bcc8a50?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) for the ChatGPT company. The [kitchen-sink costs thrown in ahead of the mega-IPO](https://substack.com/redirect/cf63d428-18b7-4e5e-b76d-f5dab4b0307b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The [rolling internal Code Reds](https://substack.com/redirect/bb07faea-af3d-4dcd-9b89-3769b467dafd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) that preceded this consolidation. And the pricing lead OpenAI keeps pressing, having [led the industry into the price wars](https://substack.com/redirect/bed16b99-fc0b-456b-ba8f-26cffa93a222?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and [a la carte, metered pricing for long-running agents](https://substack.com/redirect/cc0fbc81-6e70-4da2-86e0-aaf5363fff43?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), the volume play against [Anthropic’s premium-priced enterprise machine](https://substack.com/redirect/57d4252f-e592-47c0-be14-2d76e7360643?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The directional results landed this weekend in the FT’s [“Anthropic’s best AI model struggles to attract users as cheaper tools thrive”](https://substack.com/redirect/1047dd85-149d-4a8c-80e9-1c050bc44a77?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): GPT 5.6, priced significantly below Anthropic’s Fable 5, has jolted OpenAI’s annualized revenue up 35% this quarter to date, to over $40 billion, after a sluggish start to the year.

The same Ramp spending data across 70,000 companies shows Anthropic’s priciest model plateauing at about 11% of customer outlay on its tools, with its smaller, cheaper Opus 5 passing it in business spending: the Tokenmaxxing-to-Tokenbudgeting shift among business buyers we cover in [Monday’s mega-IPO piece](https://substack.com/redirect/759d514e-7e23-4bda-9c4b-f72f60ce63d4?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). The gap to Anthropic’s $65 billion run rate is still wide, but for the first time this year it is closing from OpenAI’s side.

My take: executive churn at this scale is usually read as a warning sign, and the experts quoted duly wave the flag.

But the pattern here reads more like a deliberate re-planting of responsibilities and assignments.

OpenAI is reorganizing itself from a research lab with celebrity executives into an operating company with one operator, pointed at revenue, an initial enterprise/AI Coding focus, then consumer products and hardware, and beyond.

The places it can differentiate to take back the [Coke seat Anthropic now occupies](https://substack.com/redirect/f41bf2ba-17e2-48b9-ad05-c3324f2aa716?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). That is what you do when the IPO window is 2027 and the company ahead of you is pitching $2 trillion valuations this fall.

The risk is the one the analysts land on: key-person concentration, squared.

The 2023 board crisis was about one indispensable man; the 2026 org chart now runs through two. For public investors, [the safety poses](https://substack.com/redirect/cde019bd-959f-414b-b462-489c4105ce61?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and the growth math will matter, but the institution question remains.

Can OpenAI operate without depending so heavily on Sam and Greg, is the one the 2027 prospectus will have to answer. Two mega-IPOs, two opposite playbooks: Anthropic selling premium discipline, OpenAI selling reach and reinvention this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/a89a8d57-6ee2-4ded-a162-561710bec43a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) through their mega-AI IPOs.

The public markets get to see the result of flows and cross-flows at OpenAI soon enough. Stay tuned.
