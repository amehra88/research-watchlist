# [AI: Nvidia Buys the ‘Home Shelf’ of Open AI, Hugging Face. AI-RTZ #1202](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=214188660&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNDE4ODY2MCwiaWF0IjoxNzg4NzU3NjA4LCJleHAiOjE3OTEzNDk2MDgsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.7TPrg1DhPkFyKqYTE6PGYyhI3UtPOJEhJ0XTHflNVmQ)

### ...$12.93B for three ‘deeply nerdy’ founders, Jensen’s two-path bet, & why open & closed AI co-exist & thrive globally.

Nvidia agreed on Thursday to [buy Hugging Face,](https://substack.com/redirect/e72bd22d-2b8c-44c0-9a5d-4d96be087375?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) the home shelf of the [world’s open AI models](https://substack.com/redirect/a25f5270-859d-4412-b1a7-48cd7222dff7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), for [roughly $13 billion](https://substack.com/redirect/0ae2bcb8-011f-4a1e-ac4b-014593f9295a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). I flagged the deal a few days ago on [ARD #150](https://substack.com/redirect/e3f44966-41ce-4567-86b2-47cbca5068cc?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), as the chess move tucked in behind [Nvidia’s $96 billion quarter](https://substack.com/redirect/e3f44966-41ce-4567-86b2-47cbca5068cc?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), as Nvidia otherwise ‘[reigns on’](https://substack.com/redirect/048760fe-fe56-4141-8ca3-b2a413d98680?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Now the fuller picture is in, from two directions: who built the thing, and why the world’s most valuable company wanted it. Both matter for where this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/3736879c-960d-4614-9f92-da5ff2821a71?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) goes from here.

The WSJ profiles the founders in [“The ‘Deeply Nerdy’ Hugging Face Founders Behind Nvidia’s $13 Billion AI Bet”](https://substack.com/redirect/4b97013c-5d1b-492e-9e8d-af35858a9666?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). And CNBC has the [full transcript](https://substack.com/redirect/db1f14ad-8f4b-4f18-93e2-aef16debe000?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) of Jensen Huang and Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue on Squawk Box the morning after. Read together, they explain the deal better than the price does.

The founders, and the accidental name. Three Frenchmen came to New York in the summer of 2016 for a Betaworks startup program, per the WSJ, to build a general-purpose chatbot that was not boring like Siri.

Seven years before ChatGPT. The demo promise from Delangue at the time: it will not always give you the right answer, but it will always give you a funny one. The name was a placeholder on the application form, their favorite emoji. They meant to change it. They never did.

Delangue sold motorbikes on eBay as a teenager and later worked there. Julien Chaumond, the CTO, is a mathematician who had worked for startups and the French government. Thomas Wolf, a physicist turned patent lawyer, joined as chief science officer the following spring. All three took an online Stanford class on natural language processing and formed a study group. Deeply nerdy, as their first investor puts it, and deeply into the science.

As [they say](https://substack.com/redirect/849a3f16-10c4-4a8c-b373-2ba6b449b2ab?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), life happens while making plans.

The turn came in 2018, when Google released a new version of the transformer. Wolf took a weekend to make an open-source version accessible in the language AI researchers actually use. By Monday it had gone viral; six months later it was the business.

As models grew, developers needed a place to host and share them, and Hugging Face built it: the GitHub of AI models, as the WSJ frames it. Today: 3 million models, 18 million developers, 200,000 enterprise customers, per Huang on CNBC. The last funding round, in 2023, valued the company at $4.5 billion, with Google, Nvidia and Salesforce in it.

And the price, $12,930,300,000, has what Wolf called nerdy meanings hiding in it. The first six digits translate to 1F917 in hexadecimal, the Unicode designation for one specific emoji. The hugging face.

Why now, in Clem’s words. Delangue went to Nvidia over the summer, not the other way around.

The catalyst, in his telling on CNBC, was the July incident I covered in [‘Frontier Models Slip Their Cribs’](https://substack.com/redirect/bd4ba245-a5a2-4828-bcf5-74e4f3a8680b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): an autonomous swarm of AI agents, escaped from an internal OpenAI cybersecurity test, breached Hugging Face’s production infrastructure. When the company went to dissect the attack logs with the proprietary models, the security guardrails would not let it. It turned instead to an open-weight model from a Chinese lab.

> “When that happened, what we realized is that we needed open models. Why? Because if you remember, we couldn’t defend ourselves with proprietary closed source APIs, so we had to use open models to defend ourselves.”

Then the fork in the road, as he sees it, with the Anthropic IPO a few months out:

> “I think there are two paths, where there’s a path where proprietary APIs are dominating the field and everyone is kind of like outsourcing their AI to them, and there’s a path where open-source AI is available to everyone, and everyone can actually become like an owner, a builder of AI and not just renting it or using it from other people.”

His target: from 18 million AI builders to 100 million in the next few years. The three founders and the whole team join Nvidia, running what he calls an independent, neutral platform inside it. Reports put the talent retention plan near a billion dollars; asked if that was the case, Huang answered with one word. Yes.

Why now, in Jensen’s words. This is the fifth lever. In [‘Open Source AI Rockets Are Hard’](https://substack.com/redirect/21ed982b-aad9-4c75-ad86-e7dced04eea8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in July I counted four that Huang was pulling at once for open models: he funds (Reflection, Thinking Machines, Mercor), he convenes (the Nemotron Coalition), he lobbies (the July open-weight letter, his first post ever on X), and he allies (the [Open Secure AI Alliance](https://substack.com/redirect/d55c2228-3efa-4ab7-8d53-26dededfdf2b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), the AI ‘Avengers’ he assembled, with Hugging Face a founding member). Now he owns the shelf.

His framing on CNBC is the one to hold onto, because it is not the either-or the headlines want:

> “I recommend that people use closed models as much as they can, you know, because it’s off the shelf. It’s incredibly good. It’s advancing very quickly. We use Cloud Code here. We use Codex. We use Perplexity. We use Cursor.”

And then the other half of the ledger:

> “Cloud service providers represents only half of Nvidia’s business, and yet the other half of our business are really largely driven by open models. And so Nvidia’s growing in both directions.”

The companies in that other half: science and drug discovery outfits with proprietary data, regulated industries, sovereign programs, anyone who needs control, ownership and the continued advancement of their own IP. Open frontier models, Huang argues, are now good enough for them, and so are the agent harnesses that let a company build its own AI on top. That is the customer base Hugging Face serves, and it is the half of Nvidia’s business the cloud narrative forgets.

He also answered the question every acquisition like this raises. Nvidia’s blog that morning said Nvidia compute will not be required to build on or deploy through Hugging Face.

Asked why not press the advantage, his answer was that it is just not the open ethos: Nvidia contributes more open models, datasets and training scripts than anyone, Megatron Core and Dynamo included, and they run on everybody else’s platform. And on the other bidders: it does not matter who they were. It only matters who wins.

The shelf, the router, and the meter. Here is the frame I keep coming back to for this wave, and this deal fits it precisely. Hugging Face is where developers find and download models. OpenRouter, which Stripe bought for a reported $7.5 billion last month, is where they run them, routing requests across 400-plus models from 80-plus providers, open and closed, on price, speed and task. I covered the Stripe deal on [ARD #142](https://substack.com/redirect/89765f42-ecbb-47ed-a08e-c02eb468c70e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and [ARD #145](https://substack.com/redirect/17781a10-5221-43ea-a77e-80699736cf8e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), with the standing disclosure that I am an early Stripe investor.

Between the two deals, in three weeks, two of the quiet plumbing companies of the AI developer economy changed hands: the shelf to the chip company, the router to the payments company. Neither buyer makes a frontier model. Both get paid on volume, whichever model wins. That is [the meter, not the seat](https://substack.com/redirect/8eae4005-8426-42e8-9675-b6e017403201?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), showing up as M&A strategy.

And the shelf’s biggest tenants are not American. Hugging Face’s own [summer census](https://substack.com/redirect/af0f204c-b9e6-475c-a036-c9a23d95b01d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), which I walked through in August, had Alibaba’s Qwen family [crossing three billion downloads](https://substack.com/redirect/209a9154-3f7e-4e9d-ac78-6ff693e67442?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), past Meta’s Llama and Google’s open models. In almost every month of 2026, the largest open model from a Chinese lab was bigger than anything American labs released.

The four leading open models in the world are from China, [as I have tracked since June of last year](https://substack.com/redirect/9d5ae589-a8e1-4cd4-9087-2bb020abc669?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): the [‘Six AI Tigers’](https://substack.com/redirect/77e72547-af75-4daa-8ecf-97c8f844056e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) of Z.ai, Moonshot, MiniMax, Baichuan, 01.AI and StepFun, plus DeepSeek and Alibaba, second-act founders shipping on their own schedules. Meanwhile America’s top open source publishers are now its chipmakers, AMD and Nvidia, each with 200-plus model repositories this year. Most US releases above 100 billion parameters are built on top of Chinese open weights.

So the stack is interleaving, not decoupling: Chinese weights on American silicon, American chips running Chinese models, developers everywhere fine-tuning whatever works. Nvidia just bought the neutral ground where that interleaving happens. Delangue said as much on CNBC: open source AI comes from anywhere, Mistral in France, Cohere in Canada, the labs in China, and the plan with Nvidia is to push more sovereign AI, giving any country the ability to build and own its own.

The co-existence, and the check on it. I made the case in May that [Nvidia and Apple could be the US open source champions](https://substack.com/redirect/c9b08466-d87e-4fc4-9ecd-318d93fc6eaa?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), for structural reasons: neither needs a proprietary frontier model to pay for its compute, so both can afford to grow the whole market rather than protect a margin. Anthropic and OpenAI cannot, which is why they sat out the July letter and have been [lobbying Washington to restrict Chinese open source](https://substack.com/redirect/f78723ac-e256-4618-bd53-9552e6ae6dbf?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) ahead of their IPOs. Both positions are rational. How a company makes money predicts where it stands on open weights.

The check runs both ways. First, the neutrality question: a shelf owned by the company that sells the chips underneath it has to keep proving it is neutral, and ‘Nvidia compute not required’ is the first test, not the last. Second, the price: roughly 80 times forward revenue, per The Information, in a company whose 2023 round was $4.5 billion. Third, and this is the one that matters most: buying the shelf does not build the rocket. [Rockets are hard](https://substack.com/redirect/21ed982b-aad9-4c75-ad86-e7dced04eea8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Nvidia’s own Nemotron 3 Ultra still sits at the bottom of the open leaderboard the Chinese labs top, and Reflection, the lab Huang personally steered toward open frontier models, has yet to ship one. Owning where the models live is not the same as building a Kimi K3 or a Qwen3.8-Max. Fourth, Washington: the same administration that just [waved Anthropic back through the gate](https://substack.com/redirect/fedce759-94f9-480d-a578-fac4a38e0b55?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) is still debating whether to restrict Chinese open weights, and the [balkanization](https://substack.com/redirect/4a9d8df9-d9ca-47d9-a509-18c2068e4c68?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) risk I keep flagging runs straight through the shelf Nvidia now owns. The second US-China White House meeting is September 24.

My take: the Kingmaker just bought the town square. I called Nvidia the AI computing [‘Kingmaker’](https://substack.com/redirect/89e5ed5d-2e72-47c7-995c-bdccc3c8be27?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in March and its [‘Uber AI Investor and Banker’](https://substack.com/redirect/c74df970-b85b-4515-83f2-afc7d63ba66c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in August, and this is that pattern at its most literal: Nvidia funds the open-model category, supplies it, sets its software standards, and now owns the place where the world goes to get the models, whichever lab built them. The [MediaTek bet](https://substack.com/redirect/0ba695da-643e-4a42-b2c6-4073d1782a2a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) last week worked the local AI layer; Hugging Face works the model distribution layer; the [physical AI long game](https://substack.com/redirect/a84a95b8-5dd5-4d28-bef9-70bbfd6906cc?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) works the robots. Each floor gets added to the same building.

The bigger point is the one both men made in different words, and it is the one I have been making since [‘Open Source by the Numbers’](https://substack.com/redirect/aa363959-77dd-4505-bb8f-f7eafd585323?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in 2023: open and closed models are not a contest with a winner.

They are two halves of one market that grow together. The closed frontier labs sell the best off-the-shelf intelligence, priced by the token, and are about to price it for public investors. The open models serve everyone who needs to own their AI rather than rent it, and that half of the world is at least as large. Nvidia sells to both, and now hosts one. Huang is talking his book, as he always is, but it is a book that wins whichever half grows faster, which is exactly why he can afford to argue for the outcome that grows both.

For the Chinese labs, open weights are not a philosophy but the only route to global usage they have. For the American labs, they are a hedge they can afford.

For Hugging Face’s three deeply nerdy founders, who put an emoji in their purchase price, it is a ten-year bet on distribution paying off inside the one company whose interest is distribution itself. A wave, not a race as I’ve been saying for this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/3736879c-960d-4614-9f92-da5ff2821a71?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). With the open source AI home shelf now in the Kingmaker’s hands. Stay tuned.
