# [AI: Data Centers become the Midterms’ Political Football. AI-RTZ #1191](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=212249218&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxMjI0OTIxOCwiaWF0IjoxNzg3ODA3MTk1LCJleHAiOjE3OTAzOTkxOTUsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.LTdwzmruE0hti4_yQm5WQDEKU39M7lmy04ef3U7FWZ8)

### ...WSJ maps the reversals from DC to the States, the GOP on the backfoot, & the AI supply-demand reality.

We have been tracking the growing [resistance to AI data centers](https://substack.com/redirect/dd0c4cd0-c4bd-4300-aaf5-1165d6654506?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) for over a year in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/b617777b-a451-479a-bd47-d3b1337c520e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), from [local water fights to power-bill anger](https://substack.com/redirect/422df422-beee-48e3-9720-6195a0a44a84?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

This week the story went fully electoral, with the November midterms turning the buildout into a veritable political football for both parties.

The WSJ lays it out in [“Politicians Who Once Championed Data Centers Are Now Bashing Them”](https://substack.com/redirect/6c01e267-e33a-4313-b742-8b8ffbea6616?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA):

In November, Texas Governor Greg Abbott declared his state the epicenter of AI development while announcing Google’s $40 billion investment; less than a year later he halted approvals of some 1,800 new data centers over power and water concerns, drawing the ire of President Trump, who defended the centers as an economic engine. The key items to build on: the reversals, the races, and the party now on the backfoot.

The races tell the story. Abbott’s challenger Gina Hinojosa has climbed within a few points while running a chatbot ad that blames rising electric bills on his data center approvals and his AI-tied donors, Elon Musk included.

In Michigan’s Senate race, the Democrat says voters flatly hate the facilities while his GOP opponent now backs a sector ‘pause’. In Ohio, Sherrod Brown brands Senator Jon Husted ‘the face of data centers’, and the Senate GOP’s own campaign committee warned in a memo that “data centers are the anchor hanging around Husted’s neck”, a toxic brand no single candidate can fix. Vivek Ramaswamy pledged an Ohio approvals pause, Nevada’s Democratic governor candidate would end tax abatements, and Pennsylvania’s Josh Shapiro, a 2028 hopeful who hosted Trump at a data center summit last summer, just signed an executive order letting the state block the facilities outright.

The weekend brought the sharpest turn yet. Axios lays it out in [“Data centers ‘dug their own grave,’ says Texas Gov. Greg Abbott”](https://substack.com/redirect/c142ac2a-83e1-43f8-bcf6-0095b1f0f7ff?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): the governor who declared Texas the epicenter of AI development told ABC’s This Week that developers moved into communities without winning support first. And got some backlash.

He has since ordered data centers to pay the full cost of the electrical infrastructure they require, called for phasing out tax incentives, and directed regulators to audit projects before they connect to the Texas grid, noting that fewer than 10% of data center companies even answered the state’s power-demand information request. New York’s Kathy Hochul has gone further with a one-year moratorium on hyperscaler data centers, while President Trump has taken direct aim at Abbott’s turn, arguing that for Texas to say no to data centers is a mistake for an industry that could be bigger than oil.

But the [feedback from local communities](https://substack.com/redirect/422df422-beee-48e3-9720-6195a0a44a84?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) across the country show a different narrative.

The polling explains the panic: more than 60% of Americans now oppose new data centers in their area, up from 49% in March per a University of Pennsylvania poll, and Democratic strategists are calling it their best wedge issue in decades. The Evercore ISI analysts flag the volume of bans and pauses across dozens of states as an investor risk running into the 2028 presidential cycle, and Chamath Palihapitiya warns the opposition could shave 2 to 3 points off annual economic growth if it metastasizes, calling it a powder keg.

Note who is squeezed hardest. The Trump administration has been all-in on the buildout and the builders: big tech broadly, Elon in particular, and Sam Altman’s Stargate, [whose SoftBank scaling story](https://substack.com/redirect/eed63134-79cb-433a-b9cd-c130ea9bff8b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and [growing pains we have covered](https://substack.com/redirect/6b25ddc2-bc3c-4b77-940f-742c7cdd6e74?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), down to [the $500 billion Ohio fine print from last week](https://substack.com/redirect/d8a8e081-952d-457b-9e45-8f4a5e4cf61e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and the administration’s new [tens-of-billions AI project with Japan](https://substack.com/redirect/d8a8e081-952d-457b-9e45-8f4a5e4cf61e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) we just wrote up.

That leaves Republican candidates running against their own president’s signature economic push, [the tech-under-Trump alignment visible from the start](https://substack.com/redirect/6af11cdc-7311-43ee-bf1b-703dfbaaceba?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). The president underlined the stance again over the weekend, [telling his former fixer Michael Cohen in an interview](https://substack.com/redirect/ab95dca0-1d05-42c4-9d18-b8b04ebce535?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) that communities turning away data centers are making a mistake, and that the builders are putting up their own power plants rather than draining the grid, a claim Axios notes is only partly true.

None of this is new to longtime readers. We covered [the giants boosting data centers despite the headwinds](https://substack.com/redirect/a58c73c7-bc42-4589-a27d-aa481fe600e0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), [the haste-makes-waste risks of super-sizing](https://substack.com/redirect/10ede1b7-dc2e-4310-986c-f016e68fe7a2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) through [Elon’s Memphis experience](https://substack.com/redirect/fe2f6d7a-e59a-472f-b2cd-7095f7726c29?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), Meta and Zuck [proactively courting the local communities around their sites](https://substack.com/redirect/02ab6ceb-5b9a-4250-aac5-dcad59070313?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), and the grid mechanics underneath, from [the AI power grab](https://substack.com/redirect/7cfbb7fb-d144-4029-b4d7-4ebd7b9c0199?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) to [power ramps and bad harmonics](https://substack.com/redirect/60be4e74-28e0-4fdc-8f55-4e6895b6eb98?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Axios has a useful companion explainer this week in [“Data center fight heating up: How they work and how much power they use”](https://substack.com/redirect/d3887082-97ae-48ad-887c-c791578adef4?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

And the other side of the ledger is the one we laid out from technology fundamentals in [our four-part AI supply and demand series](https://substack.com/redirect/435a0b10-9272-4230-b5e4-419e166ec72b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): [the supply ambitions (part 1)](https://substack.com/redirect/fe2f6d7a-e59a-472f-b2cd-7095f7726c29?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), [the demand bull case (part 2)](https://substack.com/redirect/817f6565-c4cd-4d61-b80d-c5666b4f94c1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), and [the compute crunch at ground level (part 3)](https://substack.com/redirect/4c44ba88-a227-43b0-9460-56decf712c9e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). The demand for AI compute is real, compounding, and already supply-constrained. These investments get built somewhere; the question the midterms will start answering is where, on what terms, and who pays for the grid.

My take: a wedge issue born in barely four years, from ChatGPT’s launch to 60% local opposition, is a remarkable political artifact of this wave. The industry earned a real share of it: haste made waste, bills went up, and trust arrived late, exactly the pattern we flagged. But moratoria do not repeal the supply and demand math; pausing the buildout while demand compounds just moves the crunch, and the price spikes, a few years down the road, or to the states and countries that kept building.

The likely landing zone is the one both parties are converging on from opposite directions: guardrails, pay-your-own-way power and water, local taxes paid in full, and community benefits up front.

Watch November as the first electoral referendum on the AI buildout in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/b617777b-a451-479a-bd47-d3b1337c520e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Stay tuned.
