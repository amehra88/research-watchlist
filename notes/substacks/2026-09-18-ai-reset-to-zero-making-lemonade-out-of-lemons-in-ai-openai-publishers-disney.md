# [‘Making Lemonade out of Lemons’ in AI. OpenAI, Publishers & Disney. ARD #166](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=216363475&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNjM2MzQ3NSwiaWF0IjoxNzg5NzY1MTM1LCJleHAiOjE3OTIzNTcxMzUsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.z36oUIpymOq17yYibfyoMl7M7UWNxizA_DclMOzI_cw)

### …OpenAI raises privately while trailing Anthropic. Publishers get AI’s own words in a court filing. Disney hires its first CTO.

It is Friday, and today’s theme is “Making lemonade out of lemons”. AI versions.

Three sets of companies today, and in each one somebody is holding something that did not go their way. You have got to make good things out of things that may not run as well as you would like.

That is the whole idea, and it is worth saying plainly at the top: the interesting thing in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/e453fc22-d2a7-4307-a50f-b7023b6de8cf?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) are the under-current and realities behind the announcements.

Three things today, my takes on each, and an overall take. Then the Gadget AI, where Apple’s Siri AI is starting to reach the mainstream.

## (1) OpenAI’s pre-IPO round. A who’s who.

OpenAI was anticipating going public this year. They pushed it back, for safety reasons and for a bunch of business reorganization reasons. I have written a lot about all of this.

In the meantime, Anthropic has rushed ahead of them in terms of its focus on AI coding and AI enterprise. They are running over fifteen to twenty billion dollars ahead of OpenAI on revenue run rates.

Anthropic is tracking over a hundred billion, a hundred and twenty billion in revenues this year, versus maybe eighty plus billion for OpenAI.

And OpenAI is not taking any of this lying down. They are saying, maybe we will not be able to do our IPO this year, but we are shooting for next year.

In the meantime, their last valuation was about eight hundred and fifty billion dollars. They are now talking to people about a one point two trillion dollar private valuation raise.

A few tens of billions more for additional funding, for all that AI compute, all that reorganization, everything that continues to go on. The company is investing aggressively on AI compute and on its frontier models, the latest being Astra. All of that requires additional dollars.

There is a lot of detail in the new reporting on which existing investors could come into this last round before the company does its IPO. It is all the usual suspects. Partners like SoftBank. Investors like Fidelity, BlackRock and Coatue. And also a lot of the suppliers to the company, companies like Amazon and others, who are lining up too.

MP Take: this particular item is essentially OpenAI continuing to make lemonade out of lemons. They are now the Pepsi behind Anthropic’s Coke, whereas they basically occupied the Coke spot not too long ago. But they are being positive and proactive to address what they have to address.

We will get a lot more detail on some of what Anthropic is doing as and when their S-1 files, imminently, in the next few days or maybe a week or two. So we will see where all of that shakes out.

## (2) Publishers worry about a broken internet. New discoveries.

I have talked a lot about this concern that AI is going to break the internet, as the frontier models and Google and others now surface AI results in a lot more detail from the content, leveraging all the AI at their disposal.

That has a potential negative impact in the short term on the publishers of content, whether it is text or video.

Well, in the discovery actions around the lawsuit that the New York Times and other publishers have brought against OpenAI and others, there are some documents coming out. Some of them are potentially embarrassing as the trial rolls on, assuming it is not settled.

There are quotes from senior executives at Microsoft and OpenAI, who are the targets of the lawsuit, saying things that show they understood that AI results surfacing big swaths of content from publishers was potentially going to have some negative impact on publishers in the short term.

There was one quote in particular, where a Microsoft document noted:

‘Our AI content strategy has started a “doom loop” that will hurt the performance of our models and the entire web at the same time. It is highly unusual that an end-product threatens the economic foundations of its essential suppliers.’

So a bit of an acknowledgement. Yes, in the near term there was going to be an ebb to the flow of dollars to the publishing industry because of AI.

There is also a quote by an OpenAI executive cited in the filing. In fact this is the president of the company, a co-founder, Greg Brockman. He apparently responds to a query on some of the impact analysis, basically saying:

‘we are excellent at news btw.’

So all of this, again, these are small details. But they will be coming out in this lawsuit.

MP Take: the broader point here is that AI is going to change the technology around how the internet works, and as a result it will impact the business models for publishers. That will have both negative and positive consequences.

But I continue to believe that technology generally expands the range of opportunities that are possible. And I dare say we are going to see the publishers find this down the road as well, and better ways to leverage that technology and that data.

We are going to see more of this, not less. So again, lemonade out of lemons.

## (3) Disney adds Silicon Valley tech execs. Incumbents anticipating AI.

Number three is a content publisher in its own right. Disney, in this case, which just hired its first chief technology officer, reporting directly to the chief executive.

This CTO, Karandeep Anand, comes from Character AI, which is one of the big prior hot AI startups. Its founders and chief executive and other executives were hired by Google, acqui-hired I should say, for two point seven billion dollars.

Karandeep was an operating executive who became the chief executive there. He is now moving to Disney as an operating executive, focusing on all things AI around technology, around streaming, and the rest.

Disney is also notable because they made another hire from Google and YouTube, so from Silicon Valley, a fellow by the name of Adam Smith, who is going to be running its streaming business. Obviously Disney and Hulu is a huge streaming business as well.

MP Take: all of these companies, the incumbents in this case, a hundred-year-old plus Disney, are just focused on how they can leverage the positives of AI as it impacts their long-standing businesses.

And I dare say there will be more net positives out of this than negatives. These new executives, who obviously have a lot of background in technology, could add a lot of positive elements to the mix.

So wish them well. And we would love to see more lemons making more lemonade for all of them, and for us, out of all this wonderful content.

## Gadget AI: Siri AI reaches the mainstream, and the reviews are in.

I wanted to talk again about Siri AI, which I have been very positive on for Apple. It just got introduced to the mainstream audience, as Apple released iOS 27 to everybody. Hundreds of millions of people are now downloading it and trying it.

I have been very positive on Siri, as you all know, saying it could make the iPhone again the AI device we have all been waiting for.

And now the reviews are coming out, including what I always love, the tricks and tips types of discussions. One has come forth from the Wall Street Journal, which is in the source notes. Do take a look. A lot of good useful tips.

Siri AI is indexing, or training on, your private data, your personal data, locally on the device. Your messages, your email messages, your photos. And it is surfacing content that is not accessible to the OpenAIs and the Anthropics and generally the Geminis of the world.

That is Apple’s edge. And we are starting to see this come through in the reviews of the professional reviewers.

MP Take: do take a look at some of the tricks and tips. There may be some positive things you may discover in terms of how to use these new capability sets of Siri AI.

## Q&A

Q1: What is my favorite feature of Siri AI?

ANSWER: I just find the serendipitous discovery of content that I may have long forgotten, things from years ago in my Gmail and in my messages, being surfaced by Siri AI at the right moment. That is an unusual plus.

Q2: What could make Siri AI better?

ANSWER: Here I would ask for something that both Apple and Google have an advantage in potentially, and that is surfacing the visual content in photos.

All of these products, Siri AI and Google Gemini, do a good job harvesting the photos, in terms of finding family members, friends and the rest. What they could do better, in my view, is harvesting the screenshot information.

The statistics show that over ten to twenty percent minimum of people who take photos take screenshots of things they find of interest. Receipts, documents and the rest. For me personally it is a number probably north of fifty percent, because I have a habit of screenshotting everything.

I would love for my AIs locally, whether it is Siri AI or Google Gemini or anybody else, to harvest a lot more contextual information about things that got my attention. Kept private to my interests, and leveraged by AIs like Siri AI, like Gemini, like any other AI model, to better train and inform the models that I use locally, and as private and as secure as possible.

That is one of the core opportunities for new amounts of data that are sitting in billions of people’s phones, whether iPhones or Androids. There are over five billion phones out there, out of eight billion people. So there is a lot of new data to harvest and leverage for users.

Again, more lemonade out of a lot of lemons.
