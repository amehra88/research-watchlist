### ...Anthropic’s $30 trillion TAM, buying galaxies, mining Slack archives, & GTA 6’s Netflix moment.

‘Beyond their Wildest Dreams.’ Especially with AI, this time around. Yesterday was the need for speed, what everyone is racing FOR. Today is about what the racers tell their backers the race is worth, and everywhere you look this week, the answer runs beyond their wildest dreams. Three stories, each a dream stated without a precedent, plus a Gadget AI on the most anticipated video game going. Expanding definitions, expanding imaginations: that is how this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cb6bb9c9-bc8f-4f12-ba61-7714bb11ca29?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) finances itself, and the addressable markets are just a start. My takes below, as discussed on the show.

## (1) Anthropic’s $30 Trillion TAM Tops Elon’s

The WSJ’s exclusive: Anthropic is likely to tell IPO investors its potential revenue opportunity is above $30 trillion, roughly the GDP of the United States, topping the $28.5 trillion SpaceX printed in its prospectus in May, of which $26.5 trillion was attributed to AI. Elon’s rocket maker called its TAM the ‘largest actionable’ market in human history; Anthropic goes a step further, sizing the market as the full scope of work that could be completed with AI models. And Elon has kept topping his own filing since: the S-1’s mission language runs to extending “the light of consciousness to the stars,” and when questioned this summer about the Anthropic compute deal, he posted that SpaceX “will be worth more than the rest of Earth if we accomplish our goals.” The TAM escalation has left the planet, literally. Some context on the goalposts: Uber went public in 2019 citing a $6 trillion market, essentially the mileage value of all ground transportation on Earth, and Wall Street thought that was aggressive; WeWork cited $3 trillion in its abandoned IPO. NYU’s Aswath Damodaran said of the SpaceX number that it was “reaching the end of what’s plausible and pushing beyond.” For scale, the 191 technology companies in the S&P 1500 brought in about $2.4 trillion in revenue last year, combined. Anthropic’s printed numbers are considerable in their own right: revenue more than doubled to $11.6 billion in the second quarter, the offering could aim to raise as much as $100 billion against SpaceX’s $86 billion, at a valuation around $2 trillion against SpaceX’s $1.77 trillion at listing, with disclosure documents expected within weeks and a listing potentially in September or early October.

My take: I spent years at Goldman taking internet companies public in the 1990s, and TAM slides have always been a directional storytelling device, not a forecast. What is new this cycle is the definition itself: when the market is ‘all work that could be done with AI,’ the TAM is less a market estimate and more a worldview with a number attached. Investors will not pay for the $30 trillion; they may pay for the $11.6 billion quarter, the growth rate, the compute commitments, and a believable path to profits, the things we walked through in Monday’s IPO piece. But the number does real work anyway: it frames the raise, it anchors the valuation debate, and it forces every competing prospectus, OpenAI’s included, to tell a bigger story. We covered the SpaceX print day at ARD 96 under the theme ‘the Sky is NOT the Limit.’ Anthropic just proved that a second time. For now, the market is whatever investors can be brought to dream.

## (2) Leopold’s Galaxies, and the Boom-Bust Inside One Month

The WSJ’s detailed profile of Leopold Aschenbrenner opens with the purest wildest-dreams anecdote of this entire AI wave: at San Francisco dinner parties, the young investor kept telling friends about his favorite idea. He wanted to buy galaxies. Not the private-plane kind, his friends had to ask. Actual galaxies. The logic: AI would soon unlock resources on a cosmic scale, so save now, spend on galaxies later. The arc is a compressed history of this era: Columbia at 15 from Germany, valedictorian at 19, a stint at Sam Bankman-Fried’s FTX Future Fund in the Bahamas, the OpenAI alignment team, and then the 165-page ‘Situational Awareness’ manifesto two years ago, which he parlayed, with no prior investing experience, into a hedge fund that grew to $45 billion in assets, backed by some of the best investors in the world, Jane Street and the Stripe co-founders among them. The unwind came in July on a concentrated AI book levered roughly 3-to-1: down 67% through July 30, with Jane Street taking about a $15 billion monthly loss, its worst on record, and Ken Griffin’s Citadel buying most of the public portfolio at a discount, days before Leopold’s wedding to the chief of staff of Anthropic’s CEO, with much of Anthropic’s leadership in attendance. At the toasts, one guest noted the groom’s standing promise: he would still buy his wife a galaxy.

My take: we covered Leopold’s rise at ARD 132 earlier this month and the broader bust debate in the AI-RTZ pieces, and this is a familiar cautionary tale told at this wave’s scale: very charming, very charismatic, very brilliant people telling amazing stories, and stories well told, galaxies inclusive, converting into capital. The lesson from three decades of cycles: leverage converts conviction into fragility, and a thesis can be right while the trade fails. The dot-com generation was right about the internet and still got carried out on timing and leverage. Notably, his stature in Silicon Valley rose through the blowup, because there the belief itself is the asset. Both scorecards are constantly flashed. Takes being awake to realize the reality from the ‘out there’.

## (3) The Infinite Ambition Around Data

The third story is the infinite ambition applied to data. Per another WSJ piece, executives are pushing staff to move conversations out of private DMs and into public Slack channels, so AI agents can crawl everything: products, earnings, strategy. Zapier’s CEO runs a friendly competition among his executives over whose share of messages is public; meetings get recorded and posted to channels; many messages now exist primarily to be read by bots. And the tell inside the story: Google’s $10 million bid for the digital archives of bankrupt Spirit Airlines, roughly 100 million emails, personnel records and Teams messages, intended to improve its AI models, delayed by a judge last week after the flight attendants’ union objected. A dead airline’s inboxes are now a training asset in bankruptcy court.

My take: this is the thread we have been on since ‘Our Data Exhaust’ back in 2023, through the synthetic data series, the enterprise-data integration battle, and the OpenAI vs Google ‘Data Dance’ last summer: corporate data in the countless messaging systems of companies large and small, worldwide, is new data that is not on the internet, and mining it to train and run ever bigger models is a truly infinite ambition. A new race is forming around how to value that data, how it earns a return, and what investors would pay for it. Expect data rights to show up as standard assets in every bankruptcy and M&A deal from here, the way patents did, and expect the pushback to grow with it. The constraint on this dream is not technical. It is consent.

My overall take: step back, and the three stories are part of a whole. This wave finances itself on narratives without ceilings: a TAM defined as all work, a portfolio thesis that ends in galaxies, a data ambition that ends in every message ever sent. As someone who took companies public through the internet cycle, I recognize the pattern: the narrative expands first, and the numbers either grow into it or they do not. That gap is where both the fortunes and the blowups live, and Leopold just demonstrated you can harvest both sides of it in the same month. This is not a bubble call. It is how frontier technology has always raised money: the imagination IS the collateral in the early innings, and the early innings is where we are in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cb6bb9c9-bc8f-4f12-ba61-7714bb11ca29?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). The discipline is holding two thoughts at once: the technology may yet deliver beyond the wildest dreams, while your entry price, your leverage, and your time horizon must stay wide awake. The prospectuses ahead, Anthropic’s within weeks, will be the aspirational reading of the year on exactly that tension.

## Gadget AI: GTA 6, Rockstar’s Universe of its Own

The Gadget AI today is the wildest-dreams story from entertainment: Grand Theft Auto 6, which The Verge’s Stepback column argues now exists in its own universe. Full disclosure and no secret to longtime listeners: GTA 5 is my favorite video game. The record so far, per the Verge: GTA 5 has sold more than 230 million copies across PC and three console generations, placing the franchise among the most successful media properties yet measured. The sequel’s road has included a 2022 leak of 90 gameplay videos that ended with a life sentence in a secure hospital for the hacker, multiple delays, and fresh gameplay leaks this month drawing hundreds of millions of views, with Take-Two subpoenaing Microsoft and Discord. None of it has dented the fervor: the first trailer drew nearly 290 million YouTube views even after leaking a day early, next week’s gameplay reveal premieres on Netflix six hours before YouTube, and Take-Two’s CEO calls preorder numbers unprecedented. Launch is November 19, digital only, into a games industry that is otherwise struggling, with most publishers simply clearing out of its way.

My take: Rockstar is the counterexample that proves the theme. It spent a decade hand-building a simulated world at a fidelity with little precedent: hand-built worlds, extraordinary amounts of detail, hundreds of characters inhabiting thousands of places, and the market rewards it with demand that looks effectively unlimited. Now note the AI angle: those hand-built worlds are exactly what the AI labs’ world models are racing to generate on demand. GTA 6 was largely built before the AI tooling era; I would expect the next one, and the wave of games over the next two to three years, to carry considerable AI in their construction, with the craft moving up a level to taste and direction. Two bets on the same belief, that human appetite for rich simulated worlds has no visible ceiling. And note the week’s symmetry: Elon tells investors SpaceX could one day be worth more than the rest of Earth. Rockstar already built a universe of its own, and sells the rest of us tickets. Both are dreams beyond wild. One of them ships November 19.

### Q&A

Q1: What makes GTA 5 MP’s favorite video game?

ANSWER: Losing yourself in the world, and the open-ended nature of playing its characters, or none of them, and just roaming around. The detail holds up wherever you point the camera, and you can lose yourself not just for hours but days and weeks, and more if you let yourself. Rockstar ran the same play in the Wild West with Red Dead Redemption, parts one and two, but GTA is the gold standard.

Q2: How does AI change games like this?

ANSWER: Considerably, over time. AI will help make these worlds more real and more dynamic. It is not in GTA 6 so much as it will likely be in the next one, and in the games coming over the next two to three years, with considerable AI tooling, authorship, and aspirational worlds to dream in.

The dreams beyond wild imaginations, a $30 trillion market, galaxies for sale, every message ever sent, a hand-built universe, are how this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cb6bb9c9-bc8f-4f12-ba61-7714bb11ca29?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) tells its story to the people funding it. Thanks for sharing in all these dreams. And Nvidia reports after the close today: the next reality check arrives tomorrow. Stay tuned.
