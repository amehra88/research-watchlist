# [AI: Big AI Turns to Healthcare, for Cures & an Image Fix. AI-RTZ #1199](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=213634067&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxMzYzNDA2NywiaWF0IjoxNzg4NDk4NDY2LCJleHAiOjE3OTEwOTA0NjYsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.qbW9vgxO9e0Dx9gmHCc7hgTzQo7IurNt9p7dNtxDpng)

### ...the labs bet on cures, the gadgets bet on wellness, and the industry bets both fix AI’s image problem.

Healthcare keeps coming back around as one of the biggest long-term promises of this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/fba1c612-1dde-404a-95ed-30144e3ca59c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

I made that case early, in [‘The Holy Grail of Healthcare’](https://substack.com/redirect/a1d3e867-94bb-4d81-89e7-4bb115f54f11?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) back in 2023, on an industry approaching 20% of US GDP. And in [‘Connecting the Dots’](https://substack.com/redirect/8bd98bcd-9da7-43da-a5c3-859f4d433202?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), on how AI can see across mountains of complex data and disparate disciplines in medicine. Now the industry is leaning on that promise for a different reason as well. It needs a positive AI story, badly.

Axios lays it out in [“AI turns to health care for an image fix”](https://substack.com/redirect/2fbbc53a-a5de-450e-8b95-7fd3a06ad67d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA):

The setup is blunt. AI has an image problem in the US. Saving the world with AI-designed cures reads a lot better than being blamed for rising utility bills and strained power grids. So the top labs are diving headlong into health care, right as they prepare to go public.

The cures frame. The moves are real money. Anthropic is talking up a harder push into biology and medicine ahead of its IPO, per the WSJ reporting Axios cites, with Dario Amodei pointing to hoped-for early glimmers in the coming months.

Nvidia and Eli Lilly are building a $1 billion drug discovery lab in San Francisco, pairing life science researchers with AI model builders.

And Isomorphic Labs, Google’s AI drug discovery spinoff, recently raised $2.1 billion, with research collaborations at Novartis, Lilly and Johnson & Johnson.

The other side of that ledger, per Axios: breakthroughs may still be years away.

The same tools raise biosecurity concerns, which is why Anthropic wrapped strict safeguards around its latest frontier model after concluding it could provide meaningful uplift to a bad actor.

And so far, AI in health care has also made care documentation more expensive, even as it speeds drug development and scan analysis. One digital health advisor in the piece sums up the tension: real advances could transform AI’s public image into an essential utility, but the material, AI-led drug breakthrough is not here yet. AI is a player on the field, not the whole team.

Then there is the other aspect of AI and healthcare, blended into the popular topic of ‘wellness’. Made up close and personal, with [devices all over mainstream bodies](https://substack.com/redirect/dea17a28-6fa3-4f71-801e-7a137f6430df?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The wellness frame, on your body, in particular for the data.

Here is the part I would underline. Most of the AI gadget and wearables aspirations today revolve around ‘wellness’ healthcare, driven by AI. Google just went screenless with the [Fitbit Air](https://substack.com/redirect/1d5042b0-1ab3-47ea-9869-923fff102178?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), built around its AI health coach. Watches from Apple, Google and Samsung all lead with health features. And smart rings, led by Oura, which I profiled in [‘Apple, Oura, and AI Smart Rings’](https://substack.com/redirect/140bcfa9-db9f-4904-8e6c-e3542cdbe4b0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), have grown into a category of their own. Oura has now [filed confidentially to go public](https://substack.com/redirect/5c4c30d3-d4bd-4b19-bf2a-0d6b9a61420e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), effectively an AI wearable IPO, with reports pointing to a valuation well past $10 billion.

This is the mainstream on-ramp.

I wrote in [‘Apple Watch & AI Healthcare’](https://substack.com/redirect/63ddd039-a1ad-45ff-949e-d6af6a193700?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) that these devices matter most before we get sick: the quiet, daily prevention layer on billions of wrists, fingers and ears. The caution flag stays up too, from [‘The Glass Half Empty on AI Devices’](https://substack.com/redirect/7bb2a854-4b3c-48be-9db9-c2850da79500?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): the wearables flood is long on promises and pitches, and mainstream utility has to be earned device by device.

The image problem underneath. The second frame is the one Axios leads with. US sentiment on AI is historically negative, the [sentiment gap](https://substack.com/redirect/9ac49d7a-6e88-4572-81b7-f616c4fed55c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) I have tracked all year, with data centers now the [midterms’ political football](https://substack.com/redirect/5cfc8006-a739-4b96-904a-839d1244febf?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), complete with moratoriums, lawsuits and job fears. Big tech and the AI labs are understandably eager, even desperate, for a positive AI story that lands with Main Street. As the digital health M&A advisor in the Axios piece puts it, “It’s a bit of a hearts-and-minds play.” That thread connects straight to [the go-direct echo chamber](https://substack.com/redirect/2d85737e-7a60-47c3-8149-7921d98f0a7a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) I wrote about yesterday: the industry has been telling its story to itself, and health care is its clearest shot at telling one to everyone else.

And big tech CEOs of every stripe are betting heavily on [AI Smart Glasses](https://substack.com/redirect/1d9c7475-7d5b-4d4e-8fff-caf1692ce936?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

My take: the healthcare promise is real, and it was real before the backlash made it convenient. The reason I called it the holy grail three years ago still holds. Healthcare is a fifth of the US economy, tangled in complexity that AI is unusually suited to untangle, from drug discovery to diagnostics to the paperwork in between.

But the cures story runs on a long clock.

Years, likely, before an AI-led drug lands as a fact rather than a forecast.

The wellness story runs on a shorter clock. The watches, rings and trackers are already on millions of bodies, quietly nudging sleep, fitness and early warnings, and now anchoring an IPO of their own in Oura.

With iterative progress, and a lot of the health gadgets tossed into drawers after short tries.

If the industry wants hearts and minds before the midterms and the mega-IPOs, the daily wellness utility is the story people can potentially feel.

But they have to vend their way to a complex healthcare bureaucracy, and ‘[RAMageddon’](https://substack.com/redirect/9ae877ad-08b5-4a24-933b-3b2eae31b490?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) om the device/gadget end.

The cures are the story people are being asked to believe. Both sides of that gap are worth watching honestly.

An important vertical for the [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/fba1c612-1dde-404a-95ed-30144e3ca59c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). On both the long clock for healthcare systems, and the short one for device leveraged ‘wellness’. Stay tuned.
