ARD #153](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=213729627&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxMzcyOTYyNywiaWF0IjoxNzg4MjkzMDc3LCJleHAiOjE3OTA4ODUwNzcsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.p3m9Weg7Z3RtUBqf9hb3KVlANUY4IQ5Z7ZKjDkwzhVE)

### ...$35 billion here, $45 billion there. Pennsylvania brakes. Apple and OpenAI feud. TV Streamers cost more.

‘It All Adds Up’, in an AI-driven era. Senator Everett Dirksen reportedly said it decades ago: “A billion here, a billion there, and pretty soon you’re talking real money.” Even the Senate’s own historians can’t confirm he said it. But it stuck, because it’s true.

And on this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/17916873-5e32-4e99-a702-3b92a331a19d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), it’s not just the money you have to add up. It’s the slowdowns, the legal filings, even the price stickers on the gadgets. Today, three ledgers: Anthropic’s $80 billion cloud sprint, Pennsylvania braking $100 billion-plus of data centers, and the Apple/OpenAI charges piling up. Plus a Gadget AI on ‘RAMageddon’ reaching the humble TV streamer. My takes below, as discussed on the show.

## (1) Anthropic Plays Hard Catch-Up on AI Compute, the Nvidia Way

The big one first. Anthropic just signed a $35 billion cloud deal, backed by Nvidia, per the WSJ’s exclusive. The mechanics are a wonder of financial engineering. Anthropic rents the compute from Lambda, a young Nvidia-backed cloud provider. Nvidia itself holds the lease on the data center. Hut 8, a bitcoin miner turned data center developer, builds the campus in Nueces County, Texas: a $20 billion facility, 700 megawatts, 15-year leases.

Now do the addition. Three weeks ago, Anthropic signed a $45 billion deal with Nscale, another Nvidia-backed neocloud, in West Virginia. That’s $80 billion of cloud capacity in roughly three weeks. A billion here, a billion there indeed.

My take: the significance here is Anthropic diversifying its AI compute to include much more Nvidia technology. Anthropic has been historically tied to Amazon and Google chips and infrastructure, driven by those early financing partnerships. Amazon’s Trainium, Google’s TPUs. I covered [Anthropic’s compute constraints throttling its growth](https://substack.com/redirect/562ced29-27ac-4787-b918-d928f367debc?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) earlier this year in #1080. When your products catch fire and your compute can’t keep up, you buy capacity wherever it lives.

And Nvidia is being particularly proactive to make sure it has every frontier lab covered. Look at its fingerprints on this one deal: investor in Lambda, holder of the data center lease, seller of the chips that fill it. And separately, running a credit-support program for these young neoclouds in exchange for a cut of revenue, per prior WSJ reporting. That’s the [AI Computing ‘Kingmaker’](https://substack.com/redirect/099d3723-768f-456d-bba9-4064cc937515?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) playbook I’ve tracked since #1036, now with [revenue-share deals](https://substack.com/redirect/4e0055d4-1881-4a7c-8e72-22ccb1f8c996?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) attached.

One more wrinkle worth savoring: the same developer, Hut 8, is also building data centers for Anthropic that will house Google’s TPUs, with Google providing the financial guarantees. Same builder, competing chips, everyone’s money on the same table. For the mechanics of what these campuses actually are, megawatts to gigawatts, my [Data Centers 101](https://substack.com/redirect/8b90ebbc-414b-4fa2-825a-65dd2d61e7b5?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) reference in #1192 has the full 101. The tally so far: $80 billion, three weeks, one AI lab. Keep that number in mind for story two.

## (2) Pennsylvania Pumps the Brakes on $100 Billion-Plus

Story two is the other side of the same ledger. Pennsylvania is slowing down over $100 billion in data center momentum. The NYT has the ground report from Pittsburgh, and the WSJ editorial page fires back. The setup: a year ago the President stood in Pittsburgh celebrating more than $90 billion in pledges from Google, Amazon and Anthropic for the region. Governor Shapiro was all-in, fast-tracking approvals, with more than 120 data centers planned statewide. Amazon alone pledged $20 billion. The Homer City project is converting the state’s biggest old coal plant into a $10 billion, 4.4 gigawatt gas-powered campus. Enough power for Pittsburgh, twice over.

Now the second thoughts. Two weeks ago the same Governor Shapiro called data center developers ‘predatory’ and signed what he calls the nation’s strictest guardrails. No more fast-tracking, local approval first. New York’s governor already paused new data centers statewide in July. A Pennsylvania state senator wants a three-year moratorium. And per the NYT, the environmental math is part of the backlash: seven proposed gas plants alone could emit as much CO2 as 15 million cars a year.

The WSJ editorial board adds up the other column. Data centers as tax base: without them, one Virginia county’s homeowners would pay $5,800 more a year in property taxes. Teachers near a Meta data center in Louisiana got bonuses up to $50,000. Their argument: the panic is misinformation meeting election-year anxiety, and the strategic loser in a US slowdown is not big tech. It’s the US against China.

My take: even slowdowns add up quickly. This matters because the [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/17916873-5e32-4e99-a702-3b92a331a19d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) remains on a supply-constrained slope for the next few years. Every gigawatt delayed lands on the same constrained curve that story one is about. Anthropic isn’t signing $80 billion of deals in three weeks because capacity is easy to find. It’s doing it because capacity is scarce. Pause projects in one state, and the demand doesn’t vanish. It moves to Texas, West Virginia, or abroad, and the prices go up for everyone still in line.

Both columns are worth reading in full. The jobs and tax receipts are real. So are the gas plants and the grid anxiety, in a region that has lived through these booms before. That’s exactly why I covered data centers becoming [the midterms’ political football](https://substack.com/redirect/65c8a328-8184-4323-8452-fc8df78ca638?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in #1191. The politics now compound as fast as the capex. It all adds up, on both sides of the ledger.

## (3) The Charges Add Up in the Apple/OpenAI Trade-Secret Feud

Story three. The Apple versus OpenAI legal fight escalated on both sides in one day. Quick recap: Apple sued OpenAI and two former employees in July, claiming stolen trade secrets around hardware design and supply chain as OpenAI pushes into consumer devices. Roughly 400 Apple people have moved to OpenAI’s hardware effort, per the filings of both companies. I covered the fight’s PR dimension in [ARD #133](https://substack.com/redirect/f674982a-7cbb-458e-b4bf-ca1125f83ac7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) last month.

The additions on Apple’s side, per 9to5Mac: Apple told the court an early forensic pass on one ex-engineer’s MacBook shows a confidential Apple circuit schematic used in his work at OpenAI. A simulation run in March, in a standard electrical engineering tool. Instructions to a colleague about destroying evidence. And a detail for our times: Apple argues that once a trade secret is fed to an AI agent that learns from it, the harm may become irreversible and continually propagating. Trade secrets, meet machine learning.

And OpenAI’s same-day response, per Reuters. Their filing calls the dispute “a mess of Apple’s own making.” Their argument: Apple’s own practices blur personal and company data, employees use personal iCloud for work files, departing staff get walked out the same day, and California law protects moving between rivals. The ex-employees say the file access was helping former colleagues, and the 24-year veteran says he returned his prototypes.

My take: the lists of legal charges back and forth are likely to add up for months between these two. Neither side is inclined to back down, because this is two fights running concurrently. A long legal fight in a San Jose courtroom. And a PR fight in the eyes of mainstream customers, investors and regulators, especially for OpenAI ahead of its highly anticipated mega AI IPO. Discovery fights like this are how each side runs up the other’s tab. Legal bills, executive depositions, and headlines, all compounding. Charge by charge, filing by filing, it adds up.

## Gadget AI: ‘RAMageddon’ Comes for the Humble TV Streamer

Today’s Gadget AI is under your television. The news, per 9to5Google: Google quietly raised the Google TV Streamer by $50, to $150. It launched in 2024 at $100, and used to see $20 holiday discounts. Google’s own statement points at rising component costs. Nest cameras went up the same day.

My take: this is [‘RAMageddon’](https://substack.com/redirect/56552470-9ba2-4d97-a42e-4ed6e7775920?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), which I covered in #1145, reaching the cheapest shelf in consumer electronics. These used to be $25-and-up gadgets, from Amazon, Google, Roku and the rest. Apple’s Apple TV is already at $200, from $129. And that’s before an anticipated upgrade, and before the HDMI cable, which isn’t in the box anymore. All driven by RAM and storage memory prices, the same memory the AI data centers in story one are hoovering up.

To paraphrase the good senator at the other end of the spectrum: $50 here, $50 there, and pretty soon you’re talking serious money. For a mainstream family, seriously enough to postpone the upgrade. Multiply that hesitation across the planet and it’s enough to seriously slow the consumer electronics market globally. The AI buildout’s bill is starting to show up at the checkout counter. It’s all adding up, and fast.

### Q&A

Q1: What’s MP’s favorite TV streamer?

ANSWER: still the Apple TV, with the Google TV Streamer a close second, for the AI controls. The Apple TV also carries the most local storage in the category, which of course adds up to more of those price increases. You get what you pay for. These days, you pay for what you get, too.

Q2: How could these streamers be better?

ANSWER: far better AI discovery. Help me find the right thing to watch, at the right time. Address the ‘tyranny of choice’ in today’s TV environment: countless networks, channels, programs and content, now increasingly AI-generated, no less. The choices keep adding up. The help finding them hasn’t.

None of the majors, Apple, Google, Amazon, Roku and the rest, have really begun addressing this. They’re all too busy [bundling](https://substack.com/redirect/a56d4f5a-57c7-49d1-8264-52f2fbb5dc4b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) the old cable TV bundles back together again, each one selling everyone else’s networks and channels. Customers are a priority further down the list. There’s the AI gadget opportunity, hiding in plain sight, under the TV.
