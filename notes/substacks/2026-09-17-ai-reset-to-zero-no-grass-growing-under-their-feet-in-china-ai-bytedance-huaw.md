# [‘No Grass Growing Under Their Feet’ in China AI. ByteDance, Huawei & Manus. ARD #165](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=216206124&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNjIwNjEyNCwiaWF0IjoxNzg5Njc1NjU2LCJleHAiOjE3OTIyNjc2NTYsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.cHSIkJ4-p-5alWxmER2Q7slnnzgY2aGvd6lWBAPDOTI)

### …ByteDance matches Meta’s revenue at a third the value. Huawei pulls its chip up nine months. Manus doubles to four billion.

It is Thursday, and the contrast the [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/a6f24033-e28e-4924-a665-505dfcfb5874?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) US vs China this week is hard to miss.

Today’s theme is ‘No grass growing under their feet.’

While we here in the US are debating existential questions about AI frontier models, hand-wringing over the risks, debating what the government should do and should not do, and whether we should ‘pace our progress’, China is just pramatically plodding on and executing.

China matters in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/a6f24033-e28e-4924-a665-505dfcfb5874?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), as I have said so often, because they are at least half the global AI market. And they are at least half of the AI talent, in terms of AI researchers, AI entrepreneurs and founders.

Their entrepreneurs learned a lot from us, and maybe copied a few things from us. But now they are innovating across the board, up and down the AI tech stack, from chips all the way to services.

Over the last few years they have executed ahead of what the US companies are doing. Not on frontier models. On applications, on the things people want at mainstream scale.

Three examples today, my takes on each, and an overall take. Then the Gadget AI, where Meta is trying AI smart glasses without cameras, and Snap is trying the high end.

## (1) ByteDance, China’s Meta. At a fraction of the price.

ByteDance is an extraordinary company, still private, valued at over 630 billion dollars. This is the company whose founder gave us TikTok, which had its own brouhaha here. Eighty percent of the US entity was sold to non-Chinese investors because of the geopolitical issues, in a saga that ran across months and years.

Per [The Information](https://substack.com/redirect/23ec9a27-ac85-4f09-8519-6059ee320218?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), ByteDance’s revenue over the last six months rose about 30 percent, to 120 billion dollars. Net profit came in near 20 billion, down a single-digit percentage, weighed down by AI spending.

Hold that against Meta. Meta’s revenue for the same six months was 117 billion, up about 30 percent. Meta’s market capitalization is about 1.7 trillion dollars. ByteDance is marked around 630 billion in the private market.

Both are founder-led companies. Mark Zuckerberg here, Zhang Yiming there.

MP Take: there is a valuation arbitrage here, private versus public. I know it is apples and oranges. But it is important to note, given the rate of innovation and the scale of investment.

And it matters well beyond the numbers. Given the adversarial US-China competition, most US and Western investors have been withdrawing from the China market. As a result, we are at risk of not being in on a lot of investment opportunities.

Some of the biggest investment opportunities of the last twenty to twenty-five years for non-Chinese investors have been in companies like Alibaba and Tencent. Specifically for SoftBank in Japan, and for Naspers in South Africa. Hundred-billion-plus fortunes created on that basis.

ByteDance does not get much visibility here. A lot of innovation on a bottom-up basis, from a mainstream perspective.

The core insight, which the founder has executed against for a decade or so, is that social media can be organized around people’s interests. Algorithms running on what each person actually does, their taps and their non-taps on a screen.

That turns out to be far more valuable than a social graph, which is the underpinning of Meta and Facebook and YouTube and essentially everything we run here.

Everything runs in China on ByteDance’s interest algorithms. Douyin, their version of TikTok, their e-commerce business there.

And one more thing I admire about them, because they also understand the value of marketing. They spent billions making TikTok TikTok, by advertising on Facebook itself.

At one point, for several years, ByteDance as a private company was ten, fifteen, twenty percent of Meta and Facebook’s annual revenues, because they were promoting TikTok in the US market.

Just think about that.

We do not do that. US companies do not do that kind of aggressive marketing. That is a theme I want to come back to in a later post. Chinese companies have figured out how to do this aggressively, and ByteDance is the prime example.

## (2) Huawei, ramping to be China’s Nvidia.

Huawei is officially ramping up to be China’s Nvidia. Their chairman said exactly that in an official transcript, [per the Wall Street Journal](https://substack.com/redirect/86a20cea-f290-4c99-aec7-4c049ff33825?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

They are behind, yes, in terms of capability versus Nvidia’s Vera Rubin and the roadmap Nvidia has. But the Ascend 960DT, which was due late next year, is now due in the first quarter of 2027. They say it delivers twice the performance of the one before it.

They are creating extraordinarily dense AI computers with their own universal bus linking technology, somewhat analogous to Nvidia’s NVLink. [Per The Information](https://substack.com/redirect/71ba8fd9-2b55-4012-8e3f-02b933fb8789?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), that system links as many as 4,096 Ascend chips so they work like one machine.

They are also leveraging China’s preponderance of open-source AI software by supporting all of those architectures on their chips.

MP Take: Nvidia has had a huge advantage on the training side with CUDA over the last decade or so. But that is not as strong an advantage on the inference side of the market.

And Jensen Huang has repeatedly said that we ignore the China opportunity at our own risk.

If we allow an opening for companies like Huawei to build three, four, five years of robust presence and market share, not just in China but globally, we lose a big portion of the foundation of US technology leadership.

Not in terms of the next few quarters. Nvidia is fine on that basis; it is a supply-constrained market. But in the context of the next four, five, six years and beyond.

Morgan Stanley has a report laying out that China’s AI chip market is probably worth close to 70 billion dollars by 2030, over 80 percent of which goes to Chinese companies.

In a different universe, that number was flipped. Nvidia would have the majority of that market share. Indeed they did a couple of years ago.

In the quarter coming up for Nvidia, and the next couple at least, there is no China, or a very nominal China revenue.

And of course Nvidia is trying to balance the interests between the US and China. Jensen is going to be invited to the state dinner, along with senior executives from Google and Meta and everybody else, when President Trump and President Xi hold their second US-China geopolitical, tech, trade and AI meeting at the White House on the twenty-fourth.

But in the meantime, Huawei is executing, and moving forward with a lot of momentum, while our US companies are debating whether we should pause and slow down our pace.

They are the leading Chinese company on AI agents.

[Meta One](https://substack.com/redirect/707f04ef-e562-4bd6-88ea-f9ddff9b3bb5?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), which we covered yesterday, is the subscription wrapper around it.

## My overall take

You put all of this together and you compare this whole week. Every leading newspaper in the US is on existential issues around AI, and my God, the hand-wringing around all of it. And in China, they are just getting the job done. Pragmatically, step by step. Building the chips. Building the results in terms of mainstream consumer services. Building AI agents for consumers.

MP Take: I just wanted to highlight and underline that stark contrast. China is not a fast follower anymore. They are not copying your IP. In fact, if anything, we need to be inspired by their IP, their innovations, their approaches.

And that is a dynamic that is good for the global market. It expands the overall AI tech wave and the AI pie, the overall addressable market.

If we continue down this road of us-versus-them competition, we are balkanizing AI on a global basis, and we are all going to be relatively poorer for it. That is my earnest view on this.

I keep underlining this because the dialogue right now is so navel-gazing. My God, what do we do about AI and singularity and AGI, and my God, this stuff is going to take over the world.

We are scarcely at a level where the AIs can barely remember what they do for us today versus yesterday. These systems are basically early 1980s level PC computing.

We are so far from these all-knowing AIs that can take over the world, take over PhD jobs, doctors and the rest. That is decades in the future, despite the exponential progress of these technologies.

Part of the reason is that the infrastructure needs to be built, in the trillions of dollars. That capital does not come overnight. And the infrastructure is going to take years to build. That is a slowing gap for everybody.

China has an advantage on power versus the US, because of the different way they run their economy relative to us.

This is a global opportunity for the US to take the leadership, not as a race, but as a joint project. And to be practical about the stuff we can reshore and the stuff we cannot.

Especially as AI moves into physical AI. Robots, drones, automated cars and vehicles. A lot of that ecosystem exists around the world, and so do the supply chains.

We cannot realistically bring that over here in two or three years. It is decades. It took decades for China to build this, and it will take decades for us to build it back.

And that is the thing to hold onto in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/a6f24033-e28e-4924-a665-505dfcfb5874?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

## Gadget AI: Meta tries AI smart glasses without cameras. Snap tries the high end.

Meta is trying smart glasses without cameras. I actually talked about this a couple of days ago, that it could be an interesting product for Meta.

[Per The Information](https://substack.com/redirect/23c33cd2-0b49-4220-ab94-cb784937ab85?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), the model is code-named Luna. Six built-in microphones so you can talk to Meta AI and to Muse, a button on the side, open-ear speakers, and slimmer temple arms so they look like ordinary eyewear. Two styles, targeting October.

They have a whole host of smart glasses models releasing later this fall. And Meta’s Connect conference is in a few days, where they will also exhibit their artificial reality glasses, which have been a science project for a while. Individual pairs have cost tens of thousands of dollars because of the diamond-like screens on them. They will have more commercial versions to show.

There are also products from Snap, which got revealed this week. [Per Bloomberg](https://substack.com/redirect/c61e0d30-c6ce-452a-95c5-11ccffc88896?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), the Specs are 2,195 dollars, or 2,395 with a carrying case that has its own cellular connection, on a Verizon deal. [The reviews](https://substack.com/redirect/2d53d1c1-1df2-455d-be8a-c5d19a87bc10?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) are mixed.

Apple is working on glasses for next year. They are also working on AirPods with cameras and sensors that do not take pictures.

So this business of privacy and trust, and the creepiness that mainstream consumers feel about cameras on glasses, is the thing Meta is recognizing.

MP Take: we do not have the pricing on Luna yet. But with RAMageddon it will probably be four or five hundred dollars. Not cheap.

These are all experiments, and it is interesting to watch how the founder CEOs of Snap and Meta stay laser focused, still spending tens of billions of dollars on AI smart glasses. It is still a very nascent area, but a lot of money is going to be spent by these and other companies.

## Q&A

Q1: What is my view on the flood of AI smart glasses this fall?

ANSWER: I still think it is all technology looking for a product and an application. I do not think there is any real product market fit for AI smart glasses yet.

I have talked about how I use them for Bluetooth headphone functionality, a little bit, and occasionally for talking to Meta AI. But I do not use the cameras that much, again because of the creepiness. We will see if these glasses without the cameras are interesting.

Q2: What is the biggest hurdle for these things?

ANSWER: RAMageddon. These things are up in price.

A year ago these were three hundred and fifty dollars. Today it is probably five or six hundred. And if you try to buy two or three styles, sunglasses versus prescription, you are talking about seven or eight hundred dollars for these things. Like most people with multiple pairs of sunglasses, you are easily into a two, three, four thousand dollar collection of smart glasses.

So these are early adopter nerd products. And the companies are spending billions of dollars to explore this. OpenAI, same thing. They have a plethora of gadgets coming up on AI devices, and glasses may be one of them.

This is an interesting area. That is why we talk about it, discuss it, and evaluate it in terms of what is around the corner in our future.

All-knowing AI taking over PhD jobs and doctors is decades in the future. Part of the reason is the infrastructure, in the trillions of dollars, and that capital does not come overnight.

### AI Smart Glasses: Tech Seeking Product

Meta is trying glasses with no camera at all, Snap has landed at 2,195 dollars, and Apple is coming next year with sensors rather than cameras.

MP Take: still a technology looking for a product and an application. No real product market fit for AI smart glasses yet. I use them for Bluetooth headphone functionality and occasionally for talking to Meta AI, but not the cameras, again because of the creepiness.

### AI Smart Glasses: Not Yet Mainstream

The biggest hurdle is not the AI. It is the price, and the price is memory.

MP Take: RAMageddon. A year ago these were three hundred and fifty dollars. Today it is five or six hundred, and two or three styles puts you at seven or eight hundred.

Like most people with multiple pairs of sunglasses, you are easily into a two, three, four thousand dollar collection. Early adopter nerd products, and the companies are spending billions to explore it.
