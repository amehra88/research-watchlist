# [‘Setting the Stage’ Again — The US-China AI Theater ahead of 2nd Xi/Trump meeting. ARD #123](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=207954487&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIwNzk1NDQ4NywiaWF0IjoxNzg0NjYwMjgzLCJleHAiOjE3ODcyNTIyODMsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.BgyW6uFCmjbMYu8c-TRyPDCPXsXnlqwAYLithXtnA48)

### ...ahead of the next Xi–Trump meeting at the White House on Sept 24, the open-vs-closed AI fight and the ‘distillation’ charges heat up, US tech giants lean into China’s open models, and more.

Today’s theme: they’re ‘setting the stage’ again — more theater ahead in US-China AI, in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/457d845b-c9e5-48a6-a958-17071d84a225?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) at its most geopolitical. The two countries are prepping for their second presidential meeting at the White House on September 24, and the big issues remain wide open: open vs closed AI models and their ‘distillation,’ and how US AI data centers and their power ramp up here versus there — a lot of cross-winds for the AI industry across the two largest AI markets in the world. Three events, each with my Take, then my Overall Take. Plus a Gadget AI on IMAX’s biggest moment in decades, and two questions. Let’s get started.

## (1) Prep Underway — Preliminary Negotiating Positions Being Set by Each Party

MP TAKE: As the daily posturing around open vs closed AI models from China accelerates, it’s worth remembering these are the preliminary chess moves ahead of the second top-level US-China meeting in September. Reuters confirms the two sides are set to [hold AI talks in September](https://substack.com/redirect/c999b989-f14b-47e1-a412-4ee6cbc9a388?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA); Axios reports a ‘secret’ administration battle to fight Chinese AI — centered on China’s open models like Kimi K3 (Moonshot AI) & Qwen 3.7 Max (Alibaba) . The first meeting had its own dramas, culminating in Nvidia’s Jensen Huang being picked up in Alaska on Air Force One — almost a Home Alone scenario — to join the President’s trip to China. Expect a similar set of machinations this time.

On China’s side, President Xi just wrapped a big AI conference in Shanghai, where the country’s big and small companies put out their best work. Moonshot’s Kimi K3 — at 2.8 trillion parameters — goes toe-to-toe with the best US frontier models, and Alibaba released its 2.4-trillion-parameter Qwen 3.7 Max around the same keynote. The geeky details matter less than the posturing: China is telling the world to build on its open models rather than the frontier models the US is guarding in a protective manner. It’s a compelling pitch for a global audience — and that’s exactly what’s different now.

What’s also different: more of the bigger US tech companies — Microsoft, Meta, Amazon — are leaning into open-source models from China than they were just a few months ago. Even mainstream opinion is shifting: the Washington Post now argues that [powerful AI models being given away free](https://substack.com/redirect/d9fecd77-c1bd-4225-a47b-f8bb7e9b88f4?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) is good competition — and was inevitable. These US players, along with Nvidia, Apple and Elon’s SpaceX-AI/Tesla interests in China, are the ones to watch ahead of the DC meetings.

## (2) The Key AI Companies Posture and Position Themselves — Both Here and There

MP TAKE: Why are US tech giants leaning in? Because today, in this AI tech wave, the market for US tech is global. Unlike the internet wave — when yours truly was head of internet research at Goldman Sachs, and 70-80% of the overall market was in the US, with a little in Europe and almost nothing in China — the world has changed. Microsoft, Amazon and Meta are now going to the White House saying they’d support working with more open Chinese AI models, because it gives customers — most of them global companies across every industry — a wider set of choices: large models and small, open and closed, plus all the harnesses and applications being built around them. Playing the game as if it’s a US-only context “sets us up more for failure than success.”

Expect strong words from the administration in the months ahead, pro and con on open source. On the hawkish side, CNBC reports Treasury’s Bessent floating that the US could sanction China over AI-model ‘theft’ — the ‘distillation’ charge in another guise. On the other side, the domestic open-weights ecosystem keeps building anyway — Thinking Machines just entered as the next US open-weights model maker, with Nvidia’s backing. Ironically, Thinking Machines is said to have ‘distilled’ its Inkling model with Chinese AI models. With pro-open-source voices recently departing the administration, US policy will carry a thematic inconsistency — hawkish sanctions talk pulling one way, a homegrown open-weights ecosystem pulling the other — right through the September meetings. Watching which voices win each week are next global AI football games to watch.

## (3) The Regulatory Fight — Open vs Closed AI, and Data-Center Build-Outs

MP TAKE: The regulatory fight over open vs closed AI, and over AI data-center build-outs, remain the two biggest issues being fought by both parties — at the Federal, State and local levels — with some big unintended consequences for the US if taken too far.

For now, it’s OpenAI and Anthropic against open source — under the guise of cybersecurity, geopolitically-driven ‘national interests,’ and US leadership in the [US-China](https://substack.com/redirect/af709e18-7e66-432d-9d15-8fe3ceb3dcc1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)[‘AI Space Race.’](https://substack.com/redirect/af709e18-7e66-432d-9d15-8fe3ceb3dcc1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)[](https://substack.com/redirect/af709e18-7e66-432d-9d15-8fe3ceb3dcc1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)But the ground under that argument is contested: Axios reports a top Pentagon official publicly blasting OpenAI’s Dean Ball — a fault line inside the government over the closed-lab position — while Senator Mark Warner is putting forward [his own AI plan](https://substack.com/redirect/2b0bba50-f6e3-4532-920b-1f13857d3c83?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), a bipartisan approach to regulating the build-out of AI data centers. ## MP OVERALL TAKE

For now, open vs closed AI — along with the fights over building US AI data centers and their power at scale — remain the two biggest political footballs in US tech this year, full stop. The September 24 White House meeting is the near-term forcing function that concentrates all of it. And China’s real structural advantage in the data-center race, worth underlining, isn’t chips or chip-making machines — it’s power, and the ability to use eminent domain more aggressively there than here.

A word on ‘distillation,’ since it’s become central to the geopolitical AI kerfuffles. There’s a position being pushed — by the Anthropics and OpenAIs — that distillation is somehow Chinese ‘stealing,’ unfair and terrible. Recognize that distillation is just a technique as old as the large language models themselves. The very first models were trained by distilling all the data we’ve put on the internet over decades — that’s how GPT models were built. Those same US companies then went on to distill other US models and data stores; OpenAI, you can look it up, distilled YouTube videos from Google to train its multimodal capabilities. US labs have used these techniques against each other from the very beginning. So to wield the distillation charge as a stealing charge against China’s models is a theatrical technique to gain political advantage — not a charge with substance. There’s plenty of technical detail people can debate until the sun goes down, but that’s the technically factual take.

Much depends on the long-term opportunity for the US to build and keep its global tech leadership — and, to my mind, the path to that larger prosperity does not run through a handful of closed frontier models. It runs through a diverse, competitive ecosystem of open and closed models, US and global, with American champions like Nvidia and Apple helping anchor the open-source side. Even the Washington Post and Stratechery are now making versions of that argument in the mainstream. The theater in the weeks ahead will be loud — but the strategic question underneath it is the one that matters.

## Going Big and Not Going Home to watch a cool movie— IMAX’s Biggest Moment in Decades

### MP TAKE — Gadget AI (take-first, Ep 103+)

The Medium as Marshall McLuhan famously said, ‘is the message’. But often it’s the message that can re-make the medium. In this case, it was Christopher Nolan’s ‘The Odyssey’ — filmed on custom-built IMAX cameras — that turned a multi-decade-old format, IMAX, into the movie theater place to experience the visionary director’s message. IMAX was introduced to the world in 1971 but didn’t go mainstream in the US until around 2008; now, after years of people staying home to stream, the Odyssey has had a blockbuster opening — over a quarter of a billion dollars — and theaters are sold out for days and weeks as everyone wants the full IMAX experience. The Verge’s reviewer went in a skeptic and came out [an IMAX believer](https://substack.com/redirect/fd996b71-a0ac-4ea5-9468-5380d58f7101?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

For a movie-and-technology nerd, how Nolan made it is the fascinating part. IMAX effectively reinvented its cameras for him — he needed specialized, more soundproof rigs the actors had to work around — with very little CGI and casts of hundreds and thousands in the major scenes. It’s a story as old as time — literally, Homer’s Odyssey — introducing a new generation to the oldest of stories, and a vivid case that technology adoption makes a real difference when it’s paired with the right message. Marshall McLuhan famously said the medium is the message; here, a new message that remade the medium. A format that’s been around for decades ([IMAX’s long history](https://substack.com/redirect/dab155de-0eea-486c-a86d-60348bdb87c0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)) is suddenly the must-have experience again.

# QUESTIONS

## Q1 — What’s Michael’s last similar ‘Wow’ moment watching a movie with new technology?

Answer: The Apple Vision Pro still delights me. Introduced two or three years ago at $3,400, it’s been all but written off as a commercial disappointment — but I said back then, and I’ll say again, that the IMAX experience at home is available through it, in a manner of speaking. I watch almost every movie, show and podcast on it. Just recently I watched an old favorite — Jaws — and it was a visceral experience, in both the video and the spatial audio. Whether it’s a real IMAX theater or a computer-and-display strapped to your head, the huge screen in front of you remains the wow.

## Q2 — What’s Michael’s biggest issue with the Vision Pro?

Answer: Long-term comfort. You’ve got a computer with big displays on your head, so it runs a little heavy — but that’s manageable, and all the more excuse for a couple of ‘mini intermissions’ to refill a beverage in a two-hour-plus movie. The real fix comes later, when the lighter, more capable smart glasses I’ve written a lot about finally arrive as a more comfortable alternative. Until then, it still feels like an IMAX at home.
