# [AI: China’s CXMT’s #4 HBM rank, & Apple iPhone bid. AI-RTZ #1203](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=214674169&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNDY3NDE2OSwiaWF0IjoxNzg4ODQzOTY1LCJleHAiOjE3OTE0MzU5NjUsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.n-P2mNaEd3XanLPxERYK_uxMpMvdIfpURwBG5_RHuBc)

### ...10% of global DRAM already, Xiaomi’s flagship in, Apple in the wings, & closing in on the HBM oligopoly of three.

China’s leading memory and high bandwidth memory (HBM) chip company, CXMT, just landed a prime slot in the next flagship phone from Xiaomi, China’s leading smartphone maker. It is worth walking through the broader implications for this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cc922ef7-9d03-46f6-91b3-61ec04c823b9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The occasion was ChangXin Memory Technologies, CXMT, holding its first investor event as a public company on Monday. The WSJ has the readout in [“CXMT Highlights Smartphone, AI Advances as Market Share Climbs”](https://substack.com/redirect/5a97e7e7-1d42-4493-b564-983c15a1aa43?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Three things stand out, all threads I have been pulling here since June: the share numbers, the Xiaomi flagship win, and the unsaid implications for CXMT landing broader memory and HBM deals with US companies like Apple.

It is the first time the company itself has laid out the case in public, and worth reading alongside what the three global HBM incumbents, SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron, have been telling Washington.

The numbers, a year ahead of the forecasts. Counterpoint Research, per the WSJ, puts CXMT at 10% of the global DRAM market in the second quarter, up from 4% a year earlier. That is the double-digit threshold, reached years before some industry forecasts had it.

The company reported a nearly ten-fold rise in first-half revenue and a swing to net profit, which is what rising memory prices do to a producer with the fabs already running. And general manager Zhao Lun made the point incumbents least want to hear: some CXMT products, he said, now compete with the leading global memory makers on performance, quality and supply stability.

He also stressed the flexibility of an integrated manufacturing model, one that can “dynamically adjust product mix and production pace” as the market moves. Translation for this cycle: the same fabs can be pointed at whichever DRAM the buyers are short of this quarter.

The context for those share gains is the one I have been calling [‘RAMageddon’](https://substack.com/redirect/b301317c-bbdf-4ddb-9c68-4740d53c0e79?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) since the spring: memory prices have roughly quadrupled, [global shortages of 20% plus are projected through the decade](https://substack.com/redirect/3757949a-5e5c-403b-acea-4bdf8ef330ce?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), and every AI data center and every AI-enabled phone is bidding for the same DRAM wafers. In that market, a supplier growing from 4% to 10% of the world in twelve months is not a rounding error. It is the marginal supplier.

I flagged CXMT here in [‘US hamstrung on Memory Chip Supply via China’](https://substack.com/redirect/adffb60c-fc7a-4ae1-9827-bde0876574d9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) (AI-RTZ #1128) in June, then in [‘‘RAMageddon’ Lobbies DC, & Apple vies for China CXMT’](https://substack.com/redirect/2d722247-2d4e-46eb-991e-ab5b7467d1f0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) (AI-RTZ #1175) last month, and on the video side in [ARD #140](https://substack.com/redirect/98243254-3c0c-4d3a-bf7c-c4f461a086f8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) when its stock went through the roof past Tencent, and [ARD #146](https://substack.com/redirect/fa524e5a-a5ac-4c5a-8941-06223d8c5e3a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) when sister company YMTC filed its own IPO.

Xiaomi, China’s leading smartphone vendor, is now a CXMT customer. Xiaomi has confirmed its coming flagship will use CXMT’s latest generation of mobile memory, one of the first commercial deployments of the part. Next-generation phone memory is a market long led by Samsung and SK Hynix, and increasingly the battleground for on-device AI, where the model’s working memory is the phone’s DRAM.

A Chinese flagship shipping on Chinese mobile memory is the proof point CXMT needed, and it comes from one of its priority customers. As I noted in [#1175](https://substack.com/redirect/2d722247-2d4e-46eb-991e-ab5b7467d1f0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), CXMT’s capacity is maxed out this year and its priority list runs ByteDance, Tencent and Xiaomi. Xiaomi just moved from the list to the spec sheet.

Apple, as a possible customer, remains the open question. The Xiaomi news fueled the obvious speculation, and the WSJ asked. Zhao said CXMT remains open to working with customers worldwide. Not surprisingly, he declined to discuss specific commercial relationships. That likely means discussions are under way, with all sides watching for government signals from Washington and Beijing.

Recall from [#1175](https://substack.com/redirect/2d722247-2d4e-46eb-991e-ab5b7467d1f0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA): the WSJ reported in August that Apple has been testing CXMT memory across product lines including iPhones and MacBooks, has held early talks about using it in devices sold in China, and has been seeking the White House’s blessing to avoid political blowback.

HP and Acer already ship CXMT memory in devices sold outside the US. Apple tried the China memory route once before, with YMTC in 2022, and had it quashed by lawmakers.

This time the economics are pushing harder: the iPhone 18 Pro’s bill of materials was set to rise roughly 38% on memory alone, memory has gone from around a tenth of a flagship phone’s parts cost to a third and heading toward 40%, and Apple has already raised Mac, iPad and iPhone prices 15% to 25%.

Tim Cook called it a 100-year flood in memory pricing on the July earnings call. And as another recent WSJ headline put it, Apple’s war chest cannot win the memory war. Cash does not make wafers.

Apple’s supply chain, as I wrote in [‘Apple’s supply chain locks out most tech memory pressures’](https://substack.com/redirect/fd56e793-2624-4c93-9cc0-679c556a7411?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) (RTZ #1010) back in February, is the most resilient in the business, and it still needs a fourth source.

Two of the three incumbents are Korean and sold out. The third, Micron, is the one lobbying the US Government to keep CXMT out.

A memory supplier that is now 10% of the world, growing fastest, and already qualified by Xiaomi for a flagship is not a curiosity for Apple’s procurement team. It is a valve. And this is the [Cook to Ternus](https://substack.com/redirect/3dc48c53-6e07-42fe-9b3f-9ad043403587?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) handoff year, with Tim Cook still the political operator of record for exactly this kind of blessing.

CXMT now ranks as the fourth global HBM memory company, after the trillion-dollar three.

When Zhao was asked about high-bandwidth memory, the stacked DRAM that sits beside every AI accelerator, he said only that CXMT would keep advancing products and process technologies in line with its road map and commercial plans.

The WSJ carries a Goldman Sachs estimate that frames the ambition: CXMT could supply roughly half of China’s DRAM demand and as much as 40% of the country’s HBM demand by 2028. Analysts add that its investments in packaging and high-performance computing are what could carry it from advanced DRAM into AI memory.

The HBM business today is those three, SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron, each now a trillion-dollar company enjoying software-like 75% plus margins, as I laid out in [#1128](https://substack.com/redirect/adffb60c-fc7a-4ae1-9827-bde0876574d9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

HBM and commodity DRAM come off the same fabs, which is why the AI data center boom starves the phone and PC makers, and why a fourth fab base anywhere in the world changes the arithmetic for everyone. I have tracked that squeeze here since [SK Hynix ready for its AI close-up](https://substack.com/redirect/28ef0ea3-bb02-4c37-b7a9-3f0ca27e8d9b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) (RTZ #557) in late 2024, through [memory as the next big AI bottleneck](https://substack.com/redirect/dc58f514-b5f8-4eaf-bba6-c441c57cf1d1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) (RTZ #918) and the [price hikes going into this year](https://substack.com/redirect/1a1ad252-eef9-4a16-bb59-e213330a1a29?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) (RTZ #951).

CXMT holds that fourth chair, and is closing the gap on the three above it. Scale is the work ahead: HBM needs the advanced packaging and the yields, and Zhao’s road-map language is what a company says while it is still building them out.

The 2028 estimate is the viable one, and for now the demand is Chinese. Every domestic Chinese AI accelerator, and every China-specific part Nvidia is allowed to ship there, needs HBM from somewhere, and the three incumbents are constrained by both capacity and export policy in what they can sell.

A home HBM supplier at 40% of domestic demand would be the memory half of the China AI stack I walked through in [‘No Nvidia (or Apple) without China’](https://substack.com/redirect/f3107e2a-409e-4849-ae40-fa4c732d268a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), only built out from the other side.

DC stays focused on keeping the Chinese option at bay.

The bipartisan wall I described in [#1175](https://substack.com/redirect/2d722247-2d4e-46eb-991e-ab5b7467d1f0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) is intact: a bipartisan group of senators urged Apple in July to commit to never using Chinese memory, Representatives John Moolenaar and George Whitesides jointly urged the entity list for CXMT with a ban on American purchases from listed firms, and Micron, fresh off its $250 billion US investment pledge and its own [$50 billion Boise expansion](https://substack.com/redirect/fa524e5a-a5ac-4c5a-8941-06223d8c5e3a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), is the self-motivated incumbent working the referee. Every CXMT chip kept out of Apple’s supply chain is pricing power for Boise.

China’s President Xi comes to the White House around September 24, the second Xi/Trump meeting of the year, whose [stage-setting](https://substack.com/redirect/d88b92dd-41c2-40d6-b778-264d62e559cb?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) I covered on ARD #123 and whose [cross-purposes](https://substack.com/redirect/74f8f9d7-b564-4481-8488-ae554f742a09?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) on ARD #115. CXMT’s capacity is one of the cards on the table. Apple’s request for a blessing is a potential deliverable. Monday’s investor event, with its careful non-answers on Apple and HBM, hints at the negotiations under way.

The valuation action warrants a word. CXMT went public in Shanghai in July, ran up close to 500% and, as I covered on [ARD #140](https://substack.com/redirect/98243254-3c0c-4d3a-bf7c-c4f461a086f8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), passed Tencent to become the most valuable listed company in China at more than half a trillion dollars. A memory maker from the basement of the tech stack trading above the super-app company at the top of the mountain.

Monday’s numbers, 10% share and a swing to profit on ten-fold revenue, are the fundamentals Mr. Market was pricing in ahead of time. Whether the stock has run ahead of a 2028 HBM story is a separate question from whether the story is real. On the evidence of Xiaomi and Counterpoint, it is.

My take. Three points.

First, CXMT is the global #4 in memory and closing the gap on the top three, regardless of the politics. Memory is the most concentrated critical layer in the whole [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cc922ef7-9d03-46f6-91b3-61ec04c823b9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), three suppliers and 20% plus shortages through the decade. A market that lopsided needs its fourth supplier, and the one with captive domestic demand, state backing and a half-trillion-dollar currency to fund capex is CXMT. The Goldman 2028 numbers are directionally right even if the year slips.

Second, Apple is likely to get some governmental blessing, eventually, and in stages. China-sold devices first, as the August reporting already sketched, with the September 24 theater the likeliest venue for the first card to be played. Global iPhones on CXMT memory are a longer road through the entity-list politics, but the Xiaomi flagship removes the technical excuse. The remaining objection is purely political, and it is Micron’s.

Third, my standing view from [ARD #146](https://substack.com/redirect/fa524e5a-a5ac-4c5a-8941-06223d8c5e3a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) holds: the US hamstrings itself by fencing off CXMT and YMTC from American buyers while the three incumbents run 75% margins and pass the bill to every American phone, PC and data center. Monday’s share numbers say the fence is not working anyway. Better to let Apple, HP and the rest buy where the wafers are, and keep the pressure on the incumbents to build here, which Micron is doing regardless.

None of this changes what is in your phone this fall.

It changes what your next phone costs, and who gets paid for the part inside it that this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/cc922ef7-9d03-46f6-91b3-61ec04c823b9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) cannot get enough of. Stay tuned.
