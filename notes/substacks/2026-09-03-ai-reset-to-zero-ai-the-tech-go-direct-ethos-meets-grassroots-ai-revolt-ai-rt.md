# [AI: The Tech ‘Go-Direct’ Ethos Meets Grassroots AI Revolt. AI-RTZ #1198](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=213630667&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxMzYzMDY2NywiaWF0IjoxNzg4NDExOTQ0LCJleHAiOjE3OTEwMDM5NDQsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.rms1MAAfbofQynnG1rmABI1txUlOLw9zjnLpguXYDfo)

### ...big tech talking to itself: mega podcasts, supervoting shares, & a decade-old ‘accel/decel’ sentiment gap.

One unique aspect of this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/1c82e1ab-ff1d-4073-9106-6b7022163cd7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) vs prior ones is how the early mainstream reaction has been more anti the [tech/AI management and founders rhetoric](https://substack.com/redirect/c75ea14d-5624-40ac-872e-80e5c80ffa6b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) vs prior ones. A topic I’ve [discussed](https://substack.com/redirect/ba878197-89d2-4c81-a929-ee77f025fc1b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) at l[ength](https://substack.com/redirect/c032b353-6fae-4a90-8a8e-018b199a2031?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The WSJ’s Tim Higgins recently published a piece that pulls together threads we have been writing about here for a long while: “[How Big Tech Blinded Itself to the Grassroots AI Revolt](https://substack.com/redirect/03133e61-c1b6-453d-b75f-b065be3da8b8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)”.

His core observation is blunt.

Even if the Anthropic and OpenAI mega-IPOs live up to Wall Street’s expectations, these companies will be, in his words,

> “Birthed as the bad guys.”

A remarkable swing from the early 2010s, when founders were venerated as cultural heroes. Longtime tech comms veteran Rachael Horwitz calls the industry’s AI communications “the next great comms case study,” of what not to do. Even Sam Altman conceded the field has not done a good job explaining AI’s benefits, in a recent podcast mea culpa.

The piece is worth reading in full. But the more interesting question is the one underneath it: HOW did an industry this smart blind itself?

My partial and preliminary answer, from watching tech waves since the 1980s: the blindness was built, piece by piece, on purpose. Let me walk through the building of the backlash mountain from the molehills.

- The ‘go-direct’ turn. Higgins dates it to Elon Musk’s 2022 acquisition of Twitter/X. Uber founder Travis Kalanick, back this year with an AI startup, credits exactly that:

> “Elon buying Twitter is the beginning of us being able to speak our minds.”

Founders and VCs redirected from talking just to institutions, media, regulators.

And started talking directly to their stakeholders. Both on their own and friendly podcasts by mega-tech ‘influencers’. The reach was infinite and cheap. As learned more broadly from social media by the success of YouTube, TikTok, Meta Reels and nore.

The audience, though, was Sand Hill Road, Wall Street and Pennsylvania Avenue. Not Main Street. An echo chamber of their own creation, as Higgins puts it.

- The direct-media buildout. This is no longer just founders posting. It is infrastructure. Andreessen Horowitz has rebuilt itself around a [full media playbook](https://substack.com/redirect/66b92cd6-81c7-4774-bf92-07cfd0387351?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), podcasts, shows and channels, a year into calling it ‘New Media’. And OpenAI went further: it [acquired TBPN](https://substack.com/redirect/07354e56-3b9b-4510-8fef-b7d8bc0c5f1f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), the buzzy founder-led tech talk show, in April, for a price reported in the hundreds of millions. The AI industry now owns pieces of its own press.

The mega-podcast flywheel, and its poster case. Big tech CEOs are more comfortable than ever thinking out loud for hours with friendly mega-podcasters, Dwarkesh Patel, Lex Fridman and the like. It moves real money now. The poster case: a five-hour Dwarkesh podcast helped take Leopold Aschenbrenner from a 165-page ‘Situational Awareness’ manifesto to a hedge fund that reached [$45 billion](https://substack.com/redirect/94de8cd9-6aa1-4069-a86c-c1e8b471c7f2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Institutional money, not meme-stock traders. Dwarkesh reportedly got a chance to invest.

That is the new part versus prior tech waves: the institutions are drinking the direct-communication Koolaid too, positive and negative. And the coda writes the caution: the fund [lost most of it in days](https://substack.com/redirect/94de8cd9-6aa1-4069-a86c-c1e8b471c7f2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) this summer, a thread we covered via the [Jane Street hit](https://substack.com/redirect/9667d823-a109-4a7a-8b5f-9d654012b662?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

- The insulation layer: supervoting shares. Here in my view, is a contributory factor not mentioned in the WSJ piece. The the comms critiques miss. Many of the loudest founder-CEOs are structurally insulated from the shareholder feedback loop itself. Zuckerberg controls Meta through Class B shares. Alphabet’s founders keep 10x votes. Palantir has founder shares, Snap’s public stock carries no votes at all. Musk holds supervoting control at SpaceX and xAI, and at single-class Tesla, his control runs through pay packages and board fights instead. Anthropic plans supervoting shares for Dario Amodei and its co-founders at its IPO. OpenAI took a different route to the same place: a nonprofit-controlled structure. When your shareholders cannot vote you out, the incentive to listen, to anyone, weakens.

Now note the other column on the right above. Apple. Microsoft. Amazon. Nvidia. Netflix. One share, one vote, through every era. The stalwarts became what I have called ‘Internet Treasures’ without voting shields.

Decades of delivering mainstream utility, and learning from stakeholder feedback directly, did the insulating for them.

That is the standard the AI era’s [trillion-dollar M&A prices](https://substack.com/redirect/3dd7207f-1589-441c-9994-0b343b007b5d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) implicitly claim, and have not yet earned.

What makes a company an ‘AI Treasure’ over a decade or two is the same thing that made the Internet Treasures: billions of people quietly better off, not billions of impressions.

- ‘Founder Mode’, formalized. Paul Graham’s 2024 [‘Founder Mode’ essay](https://substack.com/redirect/959c7ec9-dced-4fa2-8b0f-f298cd27f0f4?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) gave the culture its scripture: founders should run things directly, managers and process be damned. We [assessed founder mode across the Mag 7](https://substack.com/redirect/bc297428-b1ec-4113-9eda-3bde1a058352?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) when the essay landed, and watched the [blind ambitions version](https://substack.com/redirect/75a685f5-cc03-48b9-a938-b391e4142911?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) play out across the incumbents since. Founder mode built these companies. Unchecked by boards, shareholders, or a listening habit, it is also exactly how you build an echo chamber with excellent production values.

The founders have been tripping on their own narrative from the start. This is the piece of history I keep returning to.

The [accel-versus-decel schism](https://substack.com/redirect/3d4e5e88-1bb8-4e0c-8568-3d657ba74c28?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) is not a Twitter-era invention. It goes back to the [2015 origin story of OpenAI itself](https://substack.com/redirect/1abf9219-1384-492f-889a-2228fa3029c1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA):

Elon Musk and Larry Page falling out over whether AI would save or doom humanity, and OpenAI founded in the aftermath.

Ever since, the industry’s leaders have alternated between curing-cancer utopias and extinction warnings, [essays and counter-essays](https://substack.com/redirect/2772b93b-d1cf-487d-a003-1c9b62d617e1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), Altman’s abundance, Amodei’s loving grace and cautions, Gates’ [turbulence](https://substack.com/redirect/c833dd3b-e114-4003-bd3a-f2da2a8f35b8?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), each [striking a pose](https://substack.com/redirect/b5ac3ce4-218b-4265-a570-d2d02d6c0ab6?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) for their own audiences. Higgins nails the result: the public heard both the godlike promises AND the doom, believed the costs, and discounted the benefits. The narrative machine worked. On the wrong audience.

And the revolt is here, running the DC flywheel.

The sentiment gap we have tracked all year, US mainstream sentiment on AI at roughly 30-40% positive against 70-80% across Asia, is now electoral fuel. Data centers became the [midterms’ political football](https://substack.com/redirect/fe191ca4-1653-488d-9178-543268946a2d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Pennsylvania just moved to limit data-center construction, [per the WSJ.](https://substack.com/redirect/f89fade8-f8d5-4a74-a4d7-36216bc5bb9f?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) A former NFL star is going viral asking fans to bottle their urine to cool data centers.

Zach Bryan is trying out a stadium singalong with the line

> “The robot killed the working man.”

Meanwhile the regulator flywheel spins in both directions, the pauses, tariffs, blacklists and bans we cataloged in [‘Fumbling the Ball’](https://substack.com/redirect/22b78eb1-d492-4c9d-b8d8-39e2e7d0fd5e?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), each government action feeding the next industry overreaction, and back.

My take: Higgins’ closing contrast is the one to let sink in.

Uber, a decade ago, could summon its own users to lobby city halls on its behalf, because riders felt the utility every day.

The AI labs have no such ‘daily habit’ joyous muscle yet, because for most Americans the felt experience of AI so far is slop, job anxiety and bigger utility bills.

Tech guys ranting on X, as he puts it, do not have the same influence. The fix is not more podcasts, more owned media, or thicker voting shields. Or even millions of contributions to their favorite partisan lobbies and PACs. Despite the ever-rising [mainstream protests](https://substack.com/redirect/f7adf88e-e189-4637-aa4c-f2fdf7780f22?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

They’re even considering [hedging themselves](https://substack.com/redirect/fa286683-e2d7-473a-b2a8-5cef84815397?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)contributing to the other party ahead of the midterms.

Those are the step by step sequences that built the current tech echo chamber around AI.

The fix is the tedious one this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/1c82e1ab-ff1d-4073-9106-6b7022163cd7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) has barely started: mainstream utility, delivered visibly, at Main Street prices, until the technology defends itself the way the Internet Treasures learned how to do and earn.

A decade or two of that earns ‘AI Treasure’ status. No Short Cuts.

A deviant trend of this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/1c82e1ab-ff1d-4073-9106-6b7022163cd7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) worth tracking. Stay tuned.
