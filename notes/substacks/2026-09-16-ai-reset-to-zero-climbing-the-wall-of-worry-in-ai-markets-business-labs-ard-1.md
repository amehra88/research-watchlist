# [‘Climbing the Wall of Worry’ in AI. Markets, Business & Labs. ARD #164](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=215939715&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNTkzOTcxNSwiaWF0IjoxNzg5NTg3MzU0LCJleHAiOjE3OTIxNzkzNTQsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.xuAAw4OOc4QraUOopycBZHx8DEknuWL1XXZhPamyLxo)

### ...Mr. Market took the warnings in stride. Four rival fixes for who grades the labs. OpenAI weighs $1.2T. Meta puts a price on AI.

It is Wednesday, and the AI ‘pace pause’ story has moved from the microphones to the money.

Today’s theme is “Climbing the Wall of Worry.” It is an old Wall Street phrase. It describes a market that keeps going up while everyone has a reason to be afraid.

This week gave everyone reasons. Dario Amodei asked the industry to pace the frontier. Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Demis Hassabis agreed. Researchers resigned. The president called the whole thing a hoax.

I covered that on [Monday’s show](https://substack.com/redirect/8e1551c4-ffb0-45f7-9617-42d0b88c91b3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and [yesterday’s](https://substack.com/redirect/d0627b00-735f-4988-a0f8-db1ffc804d9a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

And then Wall Street bought more. Businesses kept deploying. And the AI companies kept maneuvering, in four different directions, in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/9730e3f7-250b-487c-8b70-f4c98f571cad?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Three things today, my takes on each, and an overall take. Then the Gadget AI. Meta puts a price on social media and AI.

## (1) Wall Street Carries On. The Warnings Got Louder and Mr. Market Took It in Stride.

The Wall Street Journal’s headline said it plainly. AI leaders are calling for a slowdown, but Wall Street keeps on buying.

The detail underneath is more interesting than the headline.

Investors did not sell the AI trade. They rotated inside it. Monday the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell about five to six percent, while Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta each rose a couple of percent.

Money moved out of the chip makers who supply the hardware, and into the hyperscalers whose data centers buy it.

The logic is not complicated. Microsoft and its peers stay among the most profitable companies in the world even if AI investment slows. Micron and Intel need new orders to keep the growth story going.

MP Take: watch what the market actually priced.

It is anticipating robust growth over the next two or three years. Everyone recognises that demand for this stuff, from millions of businesses and billions of consumers, is going to be relatively strong.

And that demand is going to face a market that is supply constrained. That’s the trade.

MP Take: my anticipation is that the market is not going to be overly concerned just yet, until we see additional issues around this. If we do, we will see where that takes us.

I wrote about this exact dynamic in [RTZ #544, back in November 2024](https://substack.com/redirect/c25d5a79-2c47-4255-9380-533f46fb5ef5?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), Wall Street leaning into AI compute capex despite rising fundamental and financial concerns.

Two years later. Same trade, louder warnings, bigger numbers.

## (2) Businesses Carry On. Half the Room Wants a Slowdown. None of Them Wants It at Home.

Second. The Journal’s CIO Journal reports that companies show little sign of slowing their AI deployments, despite the warnings.

The evidence comes from the WSJ Technology Council Summit, which opened in New York on Monday. They polled the room by show of hands.

Only a few people said they feared AI could kill us all in the next decade. About half said they supported slowing development at the frontier and prioritising safety guardrails.

And then the important part. That slowdown did not apply to their own organisations.

The reason is simple. Most businesses are not at the bleeding edge that Anthropic and OpenAI are knocking on regulators’ doors about.

They are taking the more prosaic, pragmatic bits of AI and doing useful things with them, in customer service, in marketing, in running their businesses.

FedEx’s Vishal Talwar put it this way. It is in our hands, and it is up to us to apply AI for good.

And KKR’s chief investment officer Ruchir Swarup described the real work. How they allocate tokens and budgets across strategies, and what he called agent economics.

MP Take: businesses are focused on the practical problem of making AI work in their business. The AGI and existential risk conversation, whether we are in the foothills or not, is not material to that.

The liability question very much is. If something goes terribly wrong, who is held liable?

For now it looks like the AI companies. That remains to be seen.

MP Take: and it is likely the leaders of those companies are doing some of this to make sure they have a we-told-you-so on the record. A CYA, in case something does go wrong.

MP Take: notice what the CIO job just became. Allocating tokens. Modelling agent economics. That is a budgeting function, not a philosophy seminar.

This is the measurement problem I wrote about in [AI-RTZ #1205, coding has a compiler and coworking has none](https://substack.com/redirect/7104d333-7800-480c-9368-e1b9e8e68b84?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

## (3) The Companies Carry On Maneuvering. Four Fixes, Four Different Graders.

Third. Four of the biggest names each proposed a fix this week. All four are different, and the difference is the story.

OpenAI went to Congress. Per [Politico](https://substack.com/redirect/51b68b4e-68c9-4856-bb09-b73321c3e69c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), OpenAI backed a bipartisan House plan that would put third-party safety assessors inside the big labs by law.

Remember that is one of the things Dario asked for. Outside governance infrastructure, from Congress.

At the All-In Summit in Los Angeles, per [CNBC](https://substack.com/redirect/788a488b-6d95-4157-8caf-d1b9f34abced?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), he said xAI, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and three or four of the leading Chinese companies should let rivals run a test harness on each other’s models before release.

His line. Instead of grading your own homework, you would at least have competitors grading your homework, and raising the alarm if they see concerns.

Jensen went to the enterprise software market. At Marc Benioff’s Dreamforce, per [Bloomberg](https://substack.com/redirect/d75daa04-8947-4350-9fd2-088f18d37b6d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), Huang said the industry does not need any new laws or regulations.

He called safe-or-fast a false choice. You can have both. You pace yourself until you are confident. The market forces are already there.

And Mark Zuckerberg went to each lab individually, [on X](https://substack.com/redirect/b8e1260a-df06-4186-99f3-07bf69fb0df9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

His answer is the fourth one. The labs themselves should do the safety work, without being asked.

> “Every lab has the responsibility and incentive to move at the pace required to train its models safely, and the ability to take its own actions to ensure that happens.”

He points at Meta’s own record. They delayed shipping Muse Spark for months to make sure it was safe from their perspective. No law told them to.

> “We didn’t call for everyone else to do this before we would. We just did it as part of our day-to-day work because it was clearly the right thing for people and for us.”

He also backs independent evaluators. Meta’s superintelligence lab already uses them, and his point is that other labs can just do the same.

And then a fifth move, which is not about grading at all. It is about money.

The [Financial Times](https://substack.com/redirect/931a041c-12bb-4eaf-b11b-228433b989d0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) reports OpenAI has held early talks with investors about another private round, at a valuation of about 1.2 trillion dollars, before its planned IPO.

For scale. In March it raised 122 billion dollars at an 852 billion dollar valuation. Anthropic sits near 965 billion on the private side, with an S-1 expected any day at around 2 trillion.

MP Take: four proposals, four different graders. Congress, your competitors, the market, or yourself.

They are all more or less going along with the notion that everyone will be more careful releasing their latest models. They do think there is some sort of group effort to watch each other’s homework.

But the exact mechanics are not clear, and that is what is going to need to be figured out.

MP Take: Elon’s version runs straight into a problem the industry already has.

The business of asking rivals to run test harnesses on each other’s models is an enormous ask. There is a lot of trade secret in there.

Remember the industry is already up in arms about distillation charges, where probing a rival’s model with queries is treated as distillation and actually theft. It is not clear how some of this ends up getting done.

MP Take: there is one genuinely different idea in the pile, and it is Zuckerberg’s second one.

He is not treating alignment as a brake on capability. He is calling it a capability. Trust and alignment are becoming what differentiates agents, and any lab that ignores that falls behind.

That is the only argument this week that safety and speed are the same investment rather than a trade between them.

MP Take: he also made the only checkable commitment anyone offered, and it is not a number.

Meta says the significant majority of its compute goes to serving people rather than racing toward recursive self-improvement (RSI).

MP Take: meanwhile both OpenAI and Anthropic are pedal to the metal on the actual business.

They are competing vigorously on AI coding and on the enterprise market, which is the white hot part. Anthropic is ahead of OpenAI by at least fifteen to twenty billion dollars on revenue run rate. That is the current race.

So both companies have two feet on the brake and the accelerator at the same time. Not a great way to drive a car, as most of us know. Expect a very jerky ride for the rest of the year.

## My Overall Take

Put the week together so far, and it is only midweek. Fear of missing out is beating fear, for now.

Wall Street is basically shaking its head and carrying on. Businesses are saying they understand there is a risk, and continuing to do what they are doing.

MP Take: for those reasons I think the markets pull through these concerns, and through the wall of worry being raised by the founders and their own AI researchers.

We should obviously continue to watch this closely, which we will do. But so far, so good. No new major issue.

Everyone is agreeing to slow down a bit on the Autobahn. But the race goes on.

And that is the thing to hold onto in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/9730e3f7-250b-487c-8b70-f4c98f571cad?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Words are valuable in front of the media, and on podcasts and social media.

Capital and deployment are slow, they lag the latest tech, and they are expensive. For now they seem to flow on despite the sentiment ebbs.

## Gadget AI: Meta Puts a Price on Social Media and AI

Meta is now starting to put a price on social media and on its agent AI models, per [The Verge](https://substack.com/redirect/617f5da7-97e3-46eb-9ab7-9e27ca5832d1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

I talked about Muse Spark 1.3 launching a few weeks ago. Meta is now very focused on deploying AI agents, particularly in WhatsApp.

That service has a huge number of users. Over a billion across Asia, in India, Indonesia, Malaysia and elsewhere.

And Meta has aspirations to make it something like WeChat. A super app, where small businesses in these markets, with hundreds of millions of people, use it to run their businesses.

So Meta One is rolling out with various tiers. A small business can subscribe at anywhere from six or seven dollars a month up to fifty dollars and higher, to use AI agents on these services.

Those services will also be offered on Instagram, on Facebook, and on AI smart glasses.

MP Take: Meta has a steeper hill to climb on trust and privacy than its peers. Mainstream users have their own wall of worry with Meta on exactly those issues.

Ask 3.5 billion daily users, trained for two decades that these services are free and ad supported, to start paying.

Google has billions of customers around the world and a long history of subscriptions for prosumer users. Apple already has a services business north of a hundred billion dollars.

So the three major consumer companies in the world are now all in the subscription business, on top of advertising.

MP Take: Meta is starting much further back, and starting globally. But I think they know that, and they are going to give it the royal go. Zuckerberg is going in with eyes wide open.

MP Take: the AI glasses are where this gets hardest, and they are next in the bundle.

Meta has roughly seven million of them out in the market and expects to sell tens of millions, with several new models coming this fall. Privacy and trust on those is the open question.

### Q&A

Q1: Would I pay for Meta One?

ANSWER: I will probably try them. But I do not have as much need for the Meta One small business services.

I am more a user of OpenAI Codex, ChatGPT Max, Gemini’s Ultra subscription, and of course Claude Cowork and Claude Code. Those are higher tier subscriptions catered to business project work.

Meta’s services are focused on the small business running type of work. I suspect this finds traction among small businesses around the world where WhatsApp especially is popular. India, Brazil, Indonesia.

Meta just did an acqui-hire of an Indian entrepreneur, for several hundred million dollars, to run all of its WhatsApp businesses around the world. This falls into that bucket too.

Q2: Would I get the AI smart glasses?

ANSWER: As I have said before, I use these things mostly for the Bluetooth headset capability, not the camera side of the equation.

Privacy continues to be a big issue, and we will see how Meta addresses it.

Apple is expected to release its smart glasses next year, potentially with sensors that do not have cameras but do have visual sensors. We will see how that works.

This is going to be a very competitive market, and yours truly will be a gadget nerd till the end and try them all.

## Clips from today

### AI Warnings Ignored As Markets Stay Calm

AI leaders spent the weekend calling for a slowdown. Wall Street bought more. Investors did not sell the AI trade, they rotated inside it: the semiconductor index down about five to six percent on Monday while Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta each rose a couple of percent.

MP Take: watch what the market actually priced. It is anticipating robust growth over the next two or three years, with demand from millions of businesses and billions of consumers meeting a supply-constrained market. That’s the trade.

### OpenAI And Anthropic Race In AI Coding

While the labs argue about who should grade their safety work, the actual business has not slowed down. OpenAI and Anthropic are competing hard on AI coding and the enterprise market, where Anthropic is ahead by at least fifteen to twenty billion dollars on revenue run rate.

MP Take: both companies have two feet on the brake and the accelerator at the same time. Not a great way to drive a car, as most of us know. Expect a very jerky ride for the rest of the year.

### Meta’s WhatsApp Expansion In India

Meta is putting a price on AI agents, and WhatsApp is where it gets decided. Over a billion users across India, Indonesia and Malaysia, WeChat-style super-app ambitions, and Meta One tiers from about six or seven dollars a month up to fifty and higher for small businesses.

MP Take: this is where it finds traction, and not with me. An agent answering customers around the clock is a real product for a small business in India, Brazil or Indonesia. Meta already has huge pools of mainstream prospects in exactly those markets.

### Meta’s AI Agents: Pricing And Privacy

Meta One extends beyond Instagram and Facebook to AI glasses, which is where the trust question gets sharpest. Google has billions of users and a long subscription history. Apple has a services business north of a hundred billion dollars.

MP Take: Meta has a steeper hill on trust and privacy, and mainstream users have their own wall of worry with Meta on exactly that. I mostly use the glasses for the Bluetooth headset, not the camera. Apple is expected next year with sensors rather than cameras.
