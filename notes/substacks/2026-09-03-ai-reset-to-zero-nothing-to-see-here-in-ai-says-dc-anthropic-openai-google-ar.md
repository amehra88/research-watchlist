ARD #155](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=214048569&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNDA0ODU2OSwiaWF0IjoxNzg4NDY1OTI1LCJleHAiOjE3OTEwNTc5MjUsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.78vMeXw9mvkpSU57rWqelXniv5DFGYWEN1jiQ8-2kDE)

### ...Commerce clears Anthropic. DOJ backs OpenAI on fair use. Google keeps its ad stack. Ternus up next at Apple Sep 9.

‘Nothing to see here, move along.’ Three events today, from three different corners of Washington. The Commerce Department. The Justice Department. And a federal courtroom in Virginia. Three different actors, one message to US big tech and AI.

Commerce waves Anthropic back through the gate. The Justice Department backs OpenAI on ‘fair use’. And a federal judge spares Google’s ad business from a breakup. All on the same day, and all blowing the same way on this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/705cbd62-fd2b-4b65-ba54-5321384dcbf5?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Plus a Gadget AI on Apple’s September 9 launch, the first under new CEO John Ternus. Lots to see there, most likely.

Plus my Takes below, as discussed on the show.

## (1) Anthropic Gets a Green Pass from Commerce

First up. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick says the Trump Administration now trusts Anthropic. His words to Axios, asked if he trusts CEO Dario Amodei:

> ‘We trust Anthropic. They’ve done what we asked. They’re back on the right side.’

Remember where this started. Back in June, in [#1117](https://substack.com/redirect/db50268d-cbbc-4ba8-b611-898c53605c81?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), I covered Anthropic being flagged to a screeching halt by the US, after a bitter fight over national security and AI safeguards. Sweeping export controls on its most advanced models. A Pentagon blacklisting as a supply-chain risk, which a federal judge struck down in August. A separate designation the company is still fighting in the DC Circuit.

Anthropic was the most in the crosshairs of the frontier labs. The companies have since carved their latest models into safer and general versions, with far more restrictions on distribution. That is the status quo this green pass lands on.

How did the thaw happen? Mostly through Tom Brown, one of Anthropic’s seven co-founders, who has become the company’s point person with the White House. Per Axios, Brown did the patching up over the past few months, in multiple conversations with Lutnick and the National Cyber Director.

The venue for Lutnick’s comments was the G20 Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, North Carolina this week, a gathering of G20 technology ministers the US is hosting. Lutnick put Brown on stage as the headliner and introduced him personally. Brown returned the favor, praising the President’s pro data center post and highlighting Anthropic’s support for the Administration’s ratepayer pledge. That is what ‘back on the right side’ looks like in practice.

My take: for now, the Administration has decided to bury their differences with Anthropic. They’ve decided that geopolitical negotiations with China are more important for now than individual differences with Anthropic.

The tell is the calendar. Lutnick was at a G20 table this week, selling other countries on US AI. The next US/China White House meeting is weeks away. You do not walk into that room with one of your two frontier AI champions, one about to do a mega IPO, sitting on a blacklist.

I saw the same reflex in [#1085](https://substack.com/redirect/d7642f25-10ef-496d-a797-014b38a07b24?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in May, when Nvidia’s Jensen got a last-minute seat on Air Force One to China after weeks of being on the outs. When the geopolitics turn, the Administration’s posture toward its AI champions turns with it. Both directions. Nothing to see here. Move along.

## (2) OpenAI Gets an Assist from DC on ‘Fair Use’

Story two. The US government has filed a brief backing OpenAI in the New York Times copyright case. Filed Tuesday in Manhattan federal court, per Reuters and the WSJ. The position: training large language models on copyrighted text is ‘fair use’ under US copyright law.

This appears to be the first time the US government has weighed in on the whole wave of AI training cases. Authors, publishers, music labels, news outlets. Dozens of them, against OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and others.

The argument, per the Justice Department’s filing: training is ‘exceedingly transformative’. Constraining it would ‘thwart creative and scientific progress’. And the national security framing is explicit. Rules that make it harder to build a robust US AI industry ‘give a competitive advantage to foreign adversaries who are not so encumbered’.

Two more points from the brief worth noting. Narrowing fair use would mean only the largest tech companies could afford licensing fees. And it would disproportionately benefit legacy media outlets over smaller independents. The same haves and have-nots divide we see across the economy, arriving on the content side.

The NY Times’ response, via its spokesman:

> “The Administration is ‘siding with a handful of trillion-dollar AI companies at the expense of the countless American creators whose work they stole.’”

Some context. A brief like this has advisory weight, not legal weight. The first two judges to rule on AI training and fair use went different ways last year. And the timing is pointed: it lands days before both sides file new motions asking the judge to rule on their core arguments, now that discovery is done. Lutnick, separately, told the G20 this week that their countries should embrace fair use for AI training too.

My take: a pivotal set of issues for the AI industry writ large. And OpenAI was front and center in the ‘Fair Use’ cross-hairs. Unclear how much impact this assist will end up being, but a welcome one for the industry nevertheless.

This is a global issue. It will be sorted out in courts in the US, Europe and Asia, and that will take years. If you look at how the technology works, it is transformative, in my take. At the same time, creators have to be compensated in some mechanism. Most likely a threading of the needle, category of content by category of content. That will take ages.

I’ve been on this critical topic since the NY Times filed in December 2023.

That week I wrote [‘On the Shoulders of Giants’](https://substack.com/redirect/c032e48d-99d2-4971-af07-18519de4f157?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), on how these AI models are built on all of our data, and then the [‘Fair Use’ Tussles of 2024](https://substack.com/redirect/e65ab5b0-046c-4093-b218-92c13301b0e2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), on why a lawsuit like this one is a negotiating tactic as much as a legal claim. Then a year ago, in [#839](https://substack.com/redirect/2a9dbf0b-fa39-4030-a765-022ca0b6d38b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), Anthropic paid $1.5 billion to settle its book copyright case.

So the pattern holds: licensing deals where the money makes sense, litigation where it doesn’t, and now Washington leaning on the scale. Nothing to see here. Move along.

## (3) Google Gets Relief on the Breakup Front

Story three. A federal judge rejected the Justice Department’s bid to break up Google’s ad tech business. Judge Leonie Brinkema in Alexandria, Virginia, per the WSJ and Bloomberg. No forced sale of Google’s ad exchange.

Instead, a two-page order adopting ‘most’ of the behavioral remedies: Google’s ad tools have to work with rivals’ tools. The full opinion is sealed for now, for redactions. The two sides have 30 days to file a joint final judgment.

This is the second time a court has found Google liable as a monopolist and then declined to break it up. Last year Judge Mehta in the search case ruled the same way: no Chrome sale, share some search data instead. I covered that liability ruling in [#440](https://substack.com/redirect/53f1faa3-edc1-4dc1-8d55-ac7e7e0c20d0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), ‘a legal dent on the power of defaults’, and the [remedies phase](https://substack.com/redirect/c1f1d8ea-a889-4270-9090-dd63974cab90?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in #547.

And Meta beat the FTC’s breakup case last year, on the rise of TikTok. The WSJ calls this ruling a marker that effectively closes the book on nearly two decades of US antitrust risk for Google.

Keep the numbers in perspective. Google made $7.3 billion from network ads last quarter, out of $119.8 billion in total Alphabet revenue. The display business at the center of this case is a small piece of a very large machine. The stock moved less than 1% on the news.

My take: a pragmatic turn by the judge in question on this complicated case. There was a lot to see here and needed solomonic judgments indeed. For now, it’s business as usual for the most part, for Google at least.

The judge telegraphed this at trial. She asked out loud who would buy the ad exchange, and who would run it. Stopping the illegal conduct was the more practical remedy in her view, and that is where she landed. In that context, Google and others can keep focusing on the opportunities and challenges around AI, and the ad businesses getting rebuilt around AI-created content. A thread I’ve written about a lot.

Two caveats for the record. The Justice Department can still appeal, and says it is weighing next steps. And the states, private plaintiffs and European regulators are all still in the queue. But on the breakup question, the one that mattered most to Google: nothing to see here. Move along.

My overall take: three companies, three corners of Washington, and one message. Anthropic gets waved back through the gate by Commerce. OpenAI gets a friend-of-the-court assist from Justice on fair use. Google gets spared a breakup by a federal judge.

All three narratives today highlight the US government deciding, in an uncoordinated way, to side with US Big Tech on a variety of fronts. This is likely as much a coincidence, given the wild swings of uncertainty in every direction for US companies, as they invest unprecedented sums in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/705cbd62-fd2b-4b65-ba54-5321384dcbf5?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). We are just in year four of all this.

Think about the swings. This same government flagged Anthropic to a halt in June. Inherited the Google breakup case and argued it all the way to a ruling. And this President has sued the New York Times and the Wall Street Journal over their coverage, while his Justice Department sides with OpenAI against the Times on copyright. It makes one’s head turn every which way.

Uncoordinated is the right word. None of these three actors was checking with the other two.

For now, the winds are all blowing in one direction, offering tail winds for the US companies in question. Enjoy it while it lasts. Because the winds do shift. ‘Nothing to see here, move along’ is what an official says on a good day. The same official was waving a red flag three months ago.

## Gadget AI: Apple’s New CEO Teases the September 9 Launch

Today’s Gadget AI is a launch that hasn’t happened yet, and a memo. Apple CEO John Ternus, on his first day in the job, told employees the company has a ‘phenomenal’ iPhone launch coming next week, per Bloomberg.

The memo is short. Ternus thanks Tim Cook, now executive chairman after 15 years. He calls next week’s launch ‘huge’ and ‘phenomenal’. And he points past it, to ‘the incredible products already in the works and the ones we haven’t even imagined yet’. He also [posted on X for the first time](https://substack.com/redirect/c7298469-b037-4eaf-9e3b-6d09edfa249c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) that day. One word: ‘hello’.

He already has almost a million followers vs Tim Cook’s almost 20 million.

Per Bloomberg, the September 9 event is expected to bring the first foldable iPhone and a push into smart home devices. Further out on Apple’s road map, per the same reporting: AirPods with cameras, smart glasses, a tabletop home robot and a touch-screen MacBook. Cook will not be on that stage as CEO for the first time in 15 years.

My take: lots to see here apparently, on the first Apple product launch under new CEO John Ternus. The focus likely will be on Siri AI powered by Apple Personal Intelligence, and the new iPhone Fold. Possibly named ‘Ultra’, as key showcase products. All done the ‘Apple Way’.

Both of Ternus’ predecessors had iconic debuts of their own, in product or in the job. Steve Jobs [introduced the iPhone](https://substack.com/redirect/f3a2b4a9-26d8-4eec-9854-7ae796ca2c3b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) at Macworld in January 2007 (the [full keynote](https://substack.com/redirect/a56c1dcf-b43c-4592-b2c5-168081d77a0a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) is still worth the 80 minutes). Tim Cook’s first product launch as CEO came on [October 4, 2011](https://substack.com/redirect/77fe9833-cc58-44fd-9dfc-171ea0071e24?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), six weeks into the job: the iPhone 4S, iCloud, iOS 5, and a virtual assistant called Siri. That was the day before Steve Jobs passed.

Siri is the symbolic thread. Cook introduced the original fifteen years ago. On September 9, Ternus gets to truly introduce Siri AI, rebuilt from the ground up, the thread I laid out in [#1196](https://substack.com/redirect/90c6b4ec-b291-4f49-ad8b-1b1c67a18d73?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) on Tuesday: AI as job one, Apple Silicon on up, the new Siri AI rolling out this month. September 9 is the first public test of that framing.

It will also be impacted by ‘[RAMageddon’](https://substack.com/redirect/145249c7-1897-4750-b411-1bdbf8b7532c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), the memory-cost squeeze I’ve covered in [#1145](https://substack.com/redirect/4df7aa16-2d97-41a2-ba00-a0a9e752015a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Expect the fold to price north of two thousand dollars, and [higher memory costs](https://substack.com/redirect/9d579a90-19df-41f8-a518-01fb474b9fb6?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)of 400%+ across the new iPhones. With Apple likely spreading some of that across other products and services. The one day this week where the message is the opposite of our theme. Plenty to see here. Do not move along.

### Q&A

Q1: What’s MP most looking forward to on the September 9 Apple Event?

ANSWER: the way Ternus navigates his first official Apple product launch. An iconic rite of passage for this company’s CEOs. I’ve watched every Apple leader from Steve Jobs on do these big launches over the years, some in person, some not. Ternus is the third CEO we all recognize, after Jobs and Cook. A new CEO continuing the legacy of the prior two, especially the global supply chain that serves over two and a half billion users of Apple hardware and software. And an engineer stepping into an iconic role. Watching that alone will be quite a show.

Q2: Where will it likely disappoint industry watchers?

ANSWER: as usual, Apple will be measured in its enthusiasm and direction on product visions ahead. They will roll it all out at the cadence and timing of their choosing. September 9, 2026 will likely be no different. There will be plenty of hints in the hardware and software they do show, and we will all do our usual figuring out of Apple’s direction. But unlike Google, Meta and a lot of the other big tech companies, which point out their product directions years ahead, Apple does not. That gap will continue, even with a new CEO on his first launch.
