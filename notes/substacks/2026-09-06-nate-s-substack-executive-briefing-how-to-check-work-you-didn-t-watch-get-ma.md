### What changed this week, with GPT-6 Astra and Fable 5.1, is how little you have to say to start one, how long it runs without you, and what your job is when it comes back.

The machine used to wait for you. You decided what it would do and when, it did that, and it stopped. That was true for as long as there have been computers.

Agents started to change that in 2025, when they could take a goal and work toward it, and for the past year I’ve been setting goals for Sol, Opus 5, GLM 5.3, and others and judging what came back. Setting the goal and judging the result has been my job for a while. What changed this week, when OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra, is everything in between. Computing is now self-directed: you can state a goal briefly and imprecisely, the machine runs for days without you and without derailing, and what you get back is finished work that embeds hundreds of decisions you never saw it make.

Which leaves you answerable for decisions you never made. Your job is catching the ones that went the wrong way before anyone else does.

The written companion below carries the seven takeaways I didn’t get to in the video.

This briefing covers:

- The middle of your job disappears. What’s left is a short specification at the front and a judgment call at the back.

- Big companies lose their middle too. The layer that existed to hold loose ends stops being necessary, and small firms get far more capable without it.

- You are liable for what your agent agrees to. There is already a body of law covering people who act on your behalf, and it applies now to machines that do.

- The last two percent of trust is worth trillions. Reliability decides what people actually hand over, and the distance between 98 and 100 percent is where the enterprise value sits.
