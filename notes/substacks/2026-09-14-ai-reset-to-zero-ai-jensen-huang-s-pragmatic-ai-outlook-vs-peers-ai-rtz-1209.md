# [AI: Jensen Huang’s Pragmatic AI Outlook vs Peers. AI-RTZ #1209](https://substack.com/app-link/post?publication_id=684161&post_id=215482771&utm_source=post-email-title&utm_campaign=email-post-title&isFreemail=true&r=8ne7oq&token=eyJ1c2VyX2lkIjo1MjMwMjM3MjIsInBvc3RfaWQiOjIxNTQ4Mjc3MSwiaWF0IjoxNzg5MzYyNDUyLCJleHAiOjE3OTE5NTQ0NTIsImlzcyI6InB1Yi02ODQxNjEiLCJzdWIiOiJwb3N0LXJlYWN0aW9uIn0.FYXOJXRBqSusruLeVB6qhPn3QstUvdICBBlMnaD7kZg)

### ...no AI security drama at Nvidia, and a high-growth AI roadmap driven by methodical, annual execution.

Nvidia founder/CEO [Jensen Huang](https://substack.com/redirect/486c35e3-bbaa-407a-a62b-b97230614a94?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) continues to differentiate himself vs US peers and customers in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/05687e1f-df3f-45b7-9bd3-5e289af8bf3c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Up and down the tech stack.

And the one at the top of the heap, with a $5 trillion plus market cap thus far.

While founder/CEOs Dario Amodei, Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Demis Hassabis, Bill Gates, and others [fret via essays](https://substack.com/redirect/b7475420-d6ba-4163-ad64-0401cb3c082d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and posts over AI risks to come, he executes his roadmap with pragmatic precision. As he has done for over three decades.

And his growth plans are just starting, as could be seen by his comments at an industry conference last week.

I recounted the essays in yesterday’s [Sunday ‘Bigger Picture](https://substack.com/redirect/b7475420-d6ba-4163-ad64-0401cb3c082d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)’, after Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceX’s xAI and Google DeepMind all agreed on Saturday to pace frontier AI progress, and called for government gating on frontier model development.

Today’s AI Ramblings Daily podcast, ARD #162, goes into all of that in detail. Watch for its Substack writeup later today as well.

Jensen took the other side of that debate at the same Goldman conference on Thursday. [The cybersecurity alarm](https://substack.com/redirect/354862ef-dba6-4b73-ad32-cb945d1a8b6b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), he said, is a way to create demand by creating a problem, and the doom talk is nonsense. I sided with him in [Sunday’s piece](https://substack.com/redirect/b7475420-d6ba-4163-ad64-0401cb3c082d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Today is about the other half of his week: the growth.

TechCrunch lays out the growth plans in [“Jensen Huang explains why Nvidia will grow an astounding 70% next year”:](https://substack.com/redirect/fd660748-904f-4b24-8855-b85b6ce41673?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)

> “Jensen Huang told attendees at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology conference on Thursday why his company’s AI domination — and revenues — will continue its record-breaking growth streak through the end of next year.”

He addressed investor reservations head-on, as TechCrunch outlined:

> “There’s been endless hand-wringing over whether Nvidia’s party will end as it faces increasing competition for GPUs and AI chips from all directions: the hyperscalers (Amazon, Microsoft, and Google, each building their own) and the AI labs (Anthropic and OpenAI, which are building their own), as well as from [newly public competitor Cerebras](https://substack.com/redirect/a9d49a34-45bb-48bf-bdf6-9bca8bee93e0?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and startups such as [Etched](https://substack.com/redirect/d097899f-4aca-4b47-af07-6a1a4870e2f9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).”

And he proceeded to reframe his company’s mission, and its historical transition over the last four years or so in particular:

> “Most people think Nvidia builds a chip. I mean, you need airplanes to ship what we build,” Huang said, adding that the company continues to battle a perception from its early days. Nvidia invented the GPU, which back then were largely sold to consumers to improve PC gaming.”“One GPU now is not $399. It’s $8.5 million dollars. That’s one GPU, all connected with NVLink, 2 million parts, right? … That’s a GPU, and we ship thousands of them.”“He added that orders for just one product, [a computer system](https://substack.com/redirect/25ad760c-ad32-479c-8f32-a43b96212758?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) that combines 36 Grace CPUs with 72 Blackwell GPUs, is currently experiencing 27% month-to-month sales growth.”

The [whole piece](https://substack.com/redirect/fd660748-904f-4b24-8855-b85b6ce41673?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) is worth a full read for the methodically stated details.

That is the physical reality behind every gigawatt AI Data Center number I write about. I walked through the [gigawatt as the unit](https://substack.com/redirect/61e2310b-c1a2-4c84-af6e-7c5b91e35b9b?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) two weeks ago, at $50 billion plus each, going up the ever-growing layers of AI Inference loops.

I discussed it the day after the August quarter on [ARD 150, ‘Bloom, Gloom & Doom’ Reign On](https://substack.com/redirect/c05af7f1-ef02-4a66-8459-3b9fbc321731?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Nvidia printed $96 billion, up 106%, with data center at $89 billion, up 117%, gross margins holding at 75%, and a guide to $108 billion with zero China compute assumed.

Then the CFO told the call to expect roughly 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028, against the 45% analysts had modeled.

That was the latest ‘Reign On’ quarter in a long series of quarterly ‘meet and beats’, running for 14 quarters and counting.

From [‘Nvidia Reigns’](https://substack.com/redirect/56bc0fe9-3088-4c83-9ae1-43e0156b99a7?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in February 2024, through [‘still Reigns’](https://substack.com/redirect/acdfc89d-bb72-4653-84a8-383ec3dea171?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), [‘reigns on’](https://substack.com/redirect/6bc180bd-c16e-4cc0-875b-ccf4c724b4cd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), [‘despite China hits’](https://substack.com/redirect/ac72602e-910f-4554-9dda-aef813a14555?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), [‘despite AI bubble concerns’](https://substack.com/redirect/126181a2-19b4-4016-a274-6eeedba0c79c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), [‘accelerates in 2026’](https://substack.com/redirect/549b8e75-0b15-4f22-8a63-0c142c43a2da?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and [‘sans China’](https://substack.com/redirect/b90a6836-cc41-49f2-842c-05fcfcb6a2dd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in May. The headline has not needed changing in two and a half years.

At the Goldman Sachs Conference, he repeated it, and said the company is confident. Analysts have this fiscal year ending near $400 billion. Seventy percent on that is about $680 billion next year.

For scale, that is more than Microsoft, Google and Meta’s combined capital budgets a year ago, arriving as one supplier’s revenue. The [roadmap](https://substack.com/redirect/ae0fe1f2-6c2c-4020-b092-c42ad8369796?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) I covered at GTC in March pointed to a trillion dollars of visibility across Blackwell and Vera Rubin through 2027, on the annual cadence Nvidia now ships to. Feynman is the GPU generation after that.

And the ‘Vera’ CPU half is now a business of its own, near [$20 billion a year](https://substack.com/redirect/b90a6836-cc41-49f2-842c-05fcfcb6a2dd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) standalone, riding AI Agents that need CPUs for the massive inference loops between model calls.

Jensen cites the reasons for his confidence in plain terms.

Nvidia runs every model on its growing racks of AI infrastructure.

Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and the open-weight models too. It calls itself the foundational platform of the AI industry, and there’s a strong base for that.

Part of the Nvidia advantage is the immense information loop with its customers globally.

Nvidia sees and tracks almost every gigawatt of land, power and shell around the world, literally everything on the planet. The neoclouds report back. The OEMs report back. The clouds report back. The AI-native companies report back.

So Nvidia knows where everything is, collectively from around the world. That is a powerful information advantage.

This is [the moat](https://substack.com/redirect/0091454f-5ba8-49bf-a16c-9d31b3d376c1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) investors should really focus on.

More than any chip family. A company that sees every data center shell before it has racks in it is not forecasting demand. It is reading it off the order book of the whole industry.

I called Nvidia the [‘Kingmaker’](https://substack.com/redirect/1bc4b9ca-6d46-4d5a-b53a-b2397dfe6ddf?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in August for its financing and [‘uber-banking’](https://substack.com/redirect/1bc4b9ca-6d46-4d5a-b53a-b2397dfe6ddf?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and backing capabilities. The visibility is what makes the kingmaking safe in its certainty, in Jensen’s telling.

It also explains the [Hugging Face purchase](https://substack.com/redirect/92a53499-fc21-4216-84d0-f3155f1032fd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and the [open, secure AI alliance](https://substack.com/redirect/5c04cbc3-14df-43ee-bd1f-9b03c352ae10?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) before it. The open half of the market is how Nvidia sees the other half. Every open model, from every country, runs on its silicon and reports back the same way.

He then went on to address head-on the investor concerns over ‘[circular financing](https://substack.com/redirect/f4fc8644-3af5-47ea-9f43-3bc76399c5e6?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)’ and what I’ve called [‘boomerang deals’](https://substack.com/redirect/30ec2fd2-fd21-46e6-9be3-b71c484663a1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/05687e1f-df3f-45b7-9bd3-5e289af8bf3c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Jensen’s demand certainty claims at the conference led, as TechCrunch notes, to the inevitable question about Nvidia’s so-called circular deals.

Nvidia invests in companies that turn around and buy its wares.

Jensen’s answer was cheeky, and direct.

> “Well, it’s not circular because we put a little bit of money in, and a lot of money comes back.”“Look at the spreadsheet, we put in $1 and $100 comes back in. Is that circular? If that is, let’s do more of that.”

The serious part came after the laugh.

Before any of these companies gets an investment, he said, Nvidia makes sure it has real customer contracts generating revenue. He has seen a hundred billion dollars of such contracts. He is not taking risks.

> “I’m not taking any risks. … I need a sure thing.”

I have tracked this issue for a while now on AI-RTZ and ARD.

The [‘Boomerang’ deals](https://substack.com/redirect/f4fc8644-3af5-47ea-9f43-3bc76399c5e6?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) when Nvidia pledged $100 billion to OpenAI.

The [tail wagging the dog](https://substack.com/redirect/41175a7a-2c38-4b83-9e57-cefad0f12996?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) as the vendor became the financier. The [$750 billion round](https://substack.com/redirect/1bc4b9ca-6d46-4d5a-b53a-b2397dfe6ddf?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) of deals in August, the $500 billion of financing guarantees, the $250 billion Ohio backstop for OpenAI. Startup [musical chairs](https://substack.com/redirect/b65792d3-5cca-4ccd-b39c-c4e9c1372663?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) on the venture side.

Three points worth focusing on.

One, contracts are the key piece. Nvidia’s money follows signed customers. I wrote about tech history rhyming from Intel to Cisco to Nvidia in [‘Peak Paranoid’](https://substack.com/redirect/486c35e3-bbaa-407a-a62b-b97230614a94?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in 2024. The rhyme holds only if the contracts are soft.

Two, the edges of the deals.

TechCrunch’s own caveat: much of AI’s growth is coming from AI-native startups raising vast sums and spending most of it on their own AI use. Those companies are Nvidia’s customers’ customers. Their contracts are the ones that make Jensen’s hundred billion ‘sure’, and they are the least sure part.

Three, the IPO prospectuses are starting to detail it all.

SpaceXAI back in June, Anthropic’s shortly, OpenAI’s in 2027 now. The [$517 billion and $750 billion](https://substack.com/redirect/99f74924-43f8-4a43-87ed-ff10b2e3b369?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) of compute commitments I laid out last week are somebody’s take-or-pay, or they are ceilings. The audited pages will say which. That is where ‘$1 in, $100 back’ gets tested.

There is one more thing the bears are looking at, and The Information laid it out Sunday in [‘Nvidia’s Growing Dependence On a Few Big Customers’](https://substack.com/redirect/18dfd94b-674b-4c7f-93f7-67e553a27969?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Three customers were 44% of Nvidia’s sales in the first half of this fiscal year, through July. Two customers were 36% last year. Three years ago, none was over 10%.

The CFO said in February that the five biggest clouds and hyperscalers are more than half of data center revenue. That is the arithmetic of a $680 billion year sold to a handful of buyers, who are also building chips of their own.

The number I would highlight is accounts receivable (A/R).

It rose 64% in the first half of the fiscal year, to $63 billion, which the company attributes to extended payment terms on large, multi-quarter agreements with certain investment-grade customers. Five customers now owe 70% of it. In January it was 56%, owed by three. At the end of the last fiscal year, 33%, owed by two.

Standard terms have moved to 60 days from 45. The filing allows for terms of 90 days up to a year on the biggest deals. Down the supply chain, Dell’s payables to its suppliers rose 48% to nearly $50 billion. The credit is stretching in both directions.

I flagged the rising receivables after the August quarter on [ARD 150](https://substack.com/redirect/c05af7f1-ef02-4a66-8459-3b9fbc321731?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The customers are investment grade, and the cash follows the shipments. But it is the circle’s outer ring showing up on the balance sheet: the vendor extends terms, the customer commits, and the receivable records the bet in between. One more number that bears continual watching.

Now hold Thursday against Saturday.

On Saturday the frontier labs asked to slow the pace of capability.

On Thursday their biggest supplier guided to 70% growth. Both can be true, and Dario Amodei’s own essay says why: pacing capability is not pacing compute. Progress, he wrote, will still seem fast.

The inference loops, the agents, the CPUs, the shells and the gigawatts do not pause because model releases do.

If anything, the labs’ pause makes Jensen’s visibility more valuable.

A slower frontier is a longer runway for everyone else on the platform, the AI-natives, the enterprises, the open-weight labs in China that are not pausing at all.

That is the same point [I made Sunday](https://substack.com/redirect/b7475420-d6ba-4163-ad64-0401cb3c082d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

The global AI toothpaste is out of the tube. Nvidia sells the tube.

What I would watch, three things, in order.

The month-over-month order growth on the rack, because 27% compounding is the number behind the 70%.

The contract disclosures in the two mega-IPO filings, because that is one place where the circle either closes or does not.

And whether the [neoclouds](https://substack.com/redirect/e903a7af-15e7-4dc8-a78e-022fc97d104a?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) that report back to Nvidia keep finding tenants at the prices SpaceX and Oracle got [last week](https://substack.com/redirect/6845851a-427e-4a10-a707-5646de2106bd?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Jensen says he can see the future.

For now, the future keeps reporting back to him pragmatically, in this [AI Tech Wave](https://substack.com/redirect/05687e1f-df3f-45b7-9bd3-5e289af8bf3c?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Stay tuned.
