### Google’s Waymo leads an all-out expansion of Robotaxis that will shift gears starting in 2027.

Good Morning,

I hope you have enjoyed your summer. I like to think about large macro trends in the AI space to give you a better idea about how fast things are moving and in what direction. Autonomous vehicles at scale on our roads came late, about a decade late but there are some promising signs that the robotaxi dream is about to accelerate in a meaningful way. It’s being led by Alphabet spin-out [Waymo](https://substack.com/redirect/c846630c-0259-4025-a690-3dc585f314ce?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

It’s getting Tesla and Uber really nervous. The Uber [layoffs of over 3,000 employees](https://substack.com/redirect/18904b3c-cf57-46c4-9570-4de610fe4435?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)this week means it needs to pivot back harder into its Robotaxi to face the coming storm. The key signal occurred earlier this year in February when [Waymo raised an astounding $16 Billion](https://substack.com/redirect/4aa0fd76-91ec-49a1-b221-ba3e3b56bec2?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA).

Now in September things are moving fast, Waymo plans to accelerate robotaxi [expansion with launches in Denver, San Diego, and Tampa](https://substack.com/redirect/ba271749-eab6-4d4f-bffc-d8dcce9b19e3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). I have enough data to predict that 2027 and the late 2020s broadly speaking will be an “inflection point” for Robotaxi scaling. This isn’t just occurring with Waymo but the entire ecosystem with several U.S. and Chinese players. It’s happening globally and at scale.

This week according to Bloomberg we have learned that Alphabet's Waymo is in the final stages of talks [to raise debt for the first ​time](https://substack.com/redirect/766cebee-d905-4459-86f5-6cf8d2ae70e9?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA), seeking more than $3 billion from lenders ‌including Pacific Investment Management and Blackstone. This is the kind of capital, maturity and execution that cannot be beaten by the likes of Uber, Tesla or other Robotaxi competitors. This means Waymo raised nearly $20 Billion in 2026 alone, and it’s not even done yet.

At a time when robotics is being hyped but lacks real world demand, the space economy is being built by a NASA with big promises, and U.S. datacenters and manufacturing is being pushed (even on other parts of the world and with trade tariff diplomacy) - the advent of the American robotaxi is being a little, overshadowed. But it’s actually a big deal.

Now as Autumn approaches Waymo has started to [offer its robotaxi service to the public](https://substack.com/redirect/a11611e7-4165-49b2-94c9-a5f48e9b56ea?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)in Denver, San Diego, and Tampa, extending the Alphabet-owned company’s commercial operations to 14 U.S. cities. I’ve been looking into the bull case for Waymo and I personally think it could totally disrupt Uber. The bull case for Waymo has elements of an R&D advantage, commercial leadership, superior unit economics, capital advantages and that will be accompanied by a scalable expansion beyond simple ride-hailing.

While I question Tesla’s place in the robotaxi ecosystem in 2026, Waymo appears poised to provide Uber with a real long-term structural threat to its core business model in the years and decades ahead. As dominant as Uber’s monopoly and business model looks today in 2026, it’s not safe. Instead of Tesla, it appears like Amazon’s Zoox could be a legit future competitor.

As Tesla is set to[hold a Cybercab Robotaxi event](https://substack.com/redirect/76d7004a-1a2e-4078-a295-4a1da760b5aa?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) in Austin this week, I cannot get over Waymo’s momentum.

Forget speculative humanoid robots, laser defenses for drones and all powerful agents - the robotaxi revolution is nearly here. After 15 years of setbacks I don’t think society is fully acknowledging the shift. Waymo, which has a commanding market lead in the U.S. is now also [expanding into Germany](https://substack.com/redirect/58341ac0-640f-463e-aae6-1ef5927c1012?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)where Europe and the Middle East are a kind of battleground of Chinese and American players. Waymo’s ramp up from [Phoenix to San Francisco to L.A](https://substack.com/redirect/ba271749-eab6-4d4f-bffc-d8dcce9b19e3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). is now ready for the prime time in 2027.

With many American GenZ and many young people globally using ride-sharing apps as a primary replacement for buying a car or learning to drive in urban centers, Waymo could on a cost-basis outcompete Uber. Obviously on a medium term basis and on a pure cost-per-mile basis, Waymo can become cheaper than Uber primarily by eliminating driver labor pretty much the largest cost in the traditional rideshare model. Uber didn’t sufficiently invest in AVs and scaling autonomy. Now in the late 2020s, they are too behind. Uber could try to acquire Microsoft backed Wayve, Intel’s Mobileye, Aurora or Avride but it won’t really matter.

According to my research and analysis, Waymo is going to take marketshare fast. Tesla, Zoox and even many Chinese players are functionally too far behind as well. The [Waymo One app](https://substack.com/redirect/689581da-8869-44ab-8975-50a48fe050e1?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)only has around 500k paid rider trips a week so far, but I expect this number to surge in 2027 and way more and faster than most are expecting. Early data indicates people [prefer Waymo to taking](https://substack.com/redirect/7ff8f5a6-0ea6-4f9f-9a92-355504c0e266?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) an Uber.

Uber’s [massive 2026 layoffs](https://substack.com/redirect/ba598513-e6a1-4872-a8f5-8c828eace9e3?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA)are really about the $10 Billion they were trying to raise for their Robotaxi plan. Uber has been strategically over-confident in its monopoly on ride-hailing in the U.S. pretty much dwarfing Lyft in recent years. As of March, 2026 Waymo has tracked nearly 221 million fully autonomous miles. Capital alone can’t buy all of that accumulated R&D and experience. In its lifespan so far Waymo has raised somewhere between $27 and $30 Billion but the majority of that has been in 2026, this signals that 2027 will be the start of a significant scaling plan. We are still in the early days of the robotaxi roll-out, which could easily take another decade still.

The Machine Economy proper will not move as fast as people think and watching the autonomous vehicle race the last 15 years really displays that pragmatically. Elon Musk’s claims about Tesla’s robotaxi developments (more than deceptive marketing) have really been by far the biggest disappointment for many U.S. consumers and tech watchers. The driverless fleet expansion is going to be expensive for Waymo, and nobody else can afford to do at this kind of scale all that it requires, least of all Tesla. Tesla expect its own Capex to surpass an unprecedented $25 Billion in 2026 alone for the first time. It’s clear Tesla’s robotaxis roll-out has likely failed and I think 2027 will show and display that in greater detail. Waymo will be pretty much uncontested in its robotaxi roll-out of the next few years in America. It’s such a hard problem to solve and it’s not perfect, but autonomous vehicles are finally coming.

### Waymo One will be the dominant Robotaxi Solution

Their active commercial service areas and newly launched cities include as of September, 2026 to the best of my knowledge:

- Phoenix, Arizona (Metro Phoenix area)

- San Francisco Bay Area, California

- Los Angeles, California

- Austin, Texas (available via Uber)

- Atlanta, Georgia (available via Uber)

- Dallas, Texas

- Houston, Texas

- San Antonio, Texas

- Miami, Florida

- Orlando, Florida

- Nashville, Tennessee

- Denver, Colorado (recently launched)

- Tampa, Florida (recently launched)

- San Diego, California (recently launched)

- Las Vegas, Nevada (scaling fully autonomous operations)

All other competitors are effectively too late with too little capital to execute properly. I don’t expect Tesla, Uber, Zoox, Wayve, Mobileye, Baidu, Pony.AI, or whoever else to be serious competitors. I expect rapid consolidation and M&A. But who else are involved in funding Waymo the last few years when the going was way slower, tougher and longer than expected for AVs? Waymo has raised multiple external funding rounds from investors including Silver Lake, Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), Fidelity, Perry Creek Capital, and T. Rowe Price. Spinning out Waymo in late 2016 was one of the best things Alphabet has ever done.

This is one of the reasons [Warren Buffet invested so heavily in Alphabet](https://substack.com/redirect/990f3ba4-e46d-442e-8b1e-952d6423f701?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA). Not only is Alphabet doing[great work in AI chips](https://substack.com/redirect/ced88543-73e3-49af-a8e2-962567763a9d?j=eyJ1IjoiOG5lN29xIn0.bqmJ9TjykdtTXCviJ3jD2X5vxhprRGd5tpCaC89FQIA) and Energy partnerships, Alphabet maintains majority control of Waymo, retaining an estimated 75% to around 80% ownership stake along with majority voting control. Do you understand how valuable Waymo is going to be? Waymo is going to be another U.S. monopoly and is going to make a lot of money one day. I project Alphabet experiencing full-on Robotaxi Supremacy by 2035. American urban transportation will never be the same again. In a future article I will analyze the other players in the ecosystem, but the key signal is how far Waymo is ahead.
