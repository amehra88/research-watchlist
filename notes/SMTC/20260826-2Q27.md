## 3. Forward guidance

Q3 FY27 outlook, all figures verbatim from the 8-K Ex-99.1 outlook table:

- Net sales **$410.0M ± $5.0M** — **+20% sequential** off the $341.9M just reported (computed from the press release)
- Adjusted gross margin **58.3% ± 100bps**; adjusted gross margin excluding business held for sale **63.9% ± 100bps**
- Adjusted operating expenses $112.0M ± $3.0M; adjusted operating income $127.0M ± $4.0M; adjusted operating margin 31.0% ± 60bps
- Adjusted diluted EPS **$1.05 ± $0.03**; adjusted EBITDA $134.3M ± $4.0M (32.8% margin)
- Adjusted normalized tax rate 18%; non-GAAP diluted share count 99.0M

**Magnitude and direction.** A +20% sequential revenue guide stacked on a quarter that was itself +17% sequential is an unusually steep two-quarter ramp. The EPS guide is the more striking line: $1.05 against $0.71 delivered is **+48% sequential**, and it is arithmetically coherent rather than aspirational — $127.0M adjusted operating income less $0.5M net interest, taxed at 18%, over 99.0M shares gives $1.048. The leverage comes from gross margin (54.5% → 58.3%) against adjusted opex rising only $102.8M → $112.0M, so roughly 90% of the incremental revenue dollar falls through.

**Tone and philosophy.** Management guides a point estimate with an explicit symmetric range on every line, which is unchanged in form. The change worth noting is a **presentation change**: this quarter Semtech **added a second gross-margin line** ("adjusted gross margin, excluding business held for sale") to both the reported table and the outlook table, driven by the pending cellular-module divestiture. Read the two lines carefully — **the $410.0M Q3 guide is module-inclusive**, which is exactly why guided GM is 58.3% consolidated but 63.9% ex-modules. The structural >500bps lift lands in Q4, not Q3.

**Divestiture.** Semtech entered a definitive agreement on August 13, 2026 to sell the cellular module business to **Compal Electronics (TWSE: 2324) for $62 million in cash**, subject to customary adjustments (8-K, 2026-08-13, Item 8.01). That 8-K gives **no closing date**. The call summary describes the close as expected in "the fourth quarter of 2026" in one passage and "Q4" in another — **ambiguous between fiscal Q4 FY27 (roughly Oct 26, 2026 – Jan 24, 2027) and calendar Q4 2026**, which overlap but are not identical. Recorded here as fiscal Q4 FY27 per the summary's framing, flagged as unverified: it could not be checked against a verbatim transcript (see Sourcing). The held-for-sale business contributed $48.1M of net sales and $10.9M of adjusted gross profit in Q2'27 — a ~22.7% gross margin, which is the arithmetic behind the >500bps consolidated lift on exit.

CEO Hong Hou, verbatim from the press release: _"Accelerating bookings and record backlog point to a strong inflection in growth with new revenue drivers and visibility expected to extend well into next fiscal year. Growing our focus areas and expanding margins all serve the same goal: building a predictable, high-margin and high-return business."_

CFO Mark Lin, verbatim from the press release: _"We enter the third quarter with conviction, expecting broad-based bookings strength to translate into sustained revenue, margin and earnings growth."_

## 4. Thesis read by theme

> **Evidence labelling.** Every item below is tagged either **[verbatim — press release]** (quoted from 8-K Ex-99.1, a primary document) or **[summary-derived paraphrase]** (from InsiderScore's AI-generated call summary). ### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** **[verbatim — press release]** Signal Integrity segment net sales of $126.2M at a 65.3% segment gross margin, versus $76.8M at 62.4% in Q2'26 — revenue up 64% year-over-year with margin expanding simultaneously. Total Semiconductor Products gross margin 62.8%. **[summary-derived paraphrase]** Management claimed share above 50% in both 800G and 1.6T, with 1.6T products expected to surpass 50% of data center revenue in Q3; described "minimal pricing pressure due to supply constraints and high customer urgency"; and projected content per transceiver rising from high single digits to $80–$90. Roadmap extension into CW lasers, semiconductor optical amplifiers, photodiode arrays, drivers and TIAs, with CW laser revenue for transceivers starting in 1H fiscal 2028, broadened by the HieFo photonics acquisition.
- **Implication:** Volume growth with rising margin is the signature of winning the design-in slot rather than buying share, and it is the strongest single datapoint in the print. The content-per-transceiver expansion is the more durable claim — it says Semtech's dollar take per port grows with each speed transition independent of unit growth. The caveat is that this position is re-won node by node, and the 3.2T node is exactly where management took its only real pushback (see §8).

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** **[verbatim — press release]** Consolidated net sales +33% year-over-year to a record $341.9M, with the Q3 guide of $410.0M representing +20% sequential; Signal Integrity, the AI-levered segment, grew 64% year-over-year. **[summary-derived paraphrase]** Data center revenue of $100M, up 39% sequentially and 91% year-over-year, guided to +45% sequential in Q3; record backlog extending into fiscal 2028 with over 70% of next year's target already booked; management securing front-end wafer and back-end tester capacity for fiscal 2028 and beyond, including prepayments and clean-room space.
- **Implication:** Confirms the thesis in the strongest available form — this is not a demand forecast, it is booked backlog a year out. The forward-looking risk inverts from demand to Semtech's own ability to supply, and to whether >70%-booked backlog is firm or reschedulable. Note that Semtech itself now names AI concentration as a risk factor: **[verbatim — press release]** the forward-looking statements cite "the cyclical nature of our industry, including due to a growing concentration of demand in artificial intelligence-related semiconductors."

### semiconductor_cycle

- **Status:** **Drift**
- **Evidence:** **[verbatim — press release]** The portfolio is bifurcating within a single quarter: Signal Integrity +64% year-over-year while IoT Systems and Connectivity was $98.3M versus $88.8M a year ago (+11%). The IoT segment gross margin optically fell from 39.5% to 34.0%, but **treat that as mix, not deterioration**: the held-for-sale module business ($48.1M of net sales at $10.9M adjusted gross profit, ~22.7% GM) sits inside this segment, and backing it out leaves a residual ~$50.2M running near 45%. That compression is a business being divested and reverses on close — the Drift call below does **not** rest on it. **[summary-derived paraphrase]** High-end consumer net sales of $39M were up only 2% sequentially and **down 5% year-over-year**, with the TV business constrained by industry-wide memory pricing; industrial was $179M (+25% year-over-year) but driven substantially by LoRa rather than by broad industrial recovery.
- **Implication:** Drift, not Confirm, and the distinction matters for how the name is sized. Semtech's results are decoupling from the classic broad semiconductor cycle: the AI-adjacent legs are inflecting violently while consumer is outright declining year-over-year and the residual IoT segment is growing slowly at compressing margin. The mechanism has shifted from "own the cyclical recovery" to "own an AI-concentrated ramp with an unrelated, softer remainder." That is a better business today and a more fragile one if AI-optical order patterns turn, because there is no longer a diversified cyclical base underneath to cushion it.

### data_center_deployment_constraints

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** **[summary-derived paraphrase]** The customer-behavior evidence is the load-bearing part: record backlog extending into fiscal 2028, **over 70% of next year's target already booked**, and management characterizing pricing as facing "minimal pricing pressure due to supply constraints and high customer urgency." Analyst Cody Acree (Benchmark) probed backlog visibility specifically and management responded by pointing to direct hyperscaler engagement.
- **Implication:** Customers committing volume a full year forward, at prices that are not eroding in a component category where erosion is the historical norm, is a deployment-pace signal — it says buildout demand is running ahead of the supply chain's ability to serve it, and that the scarcity rent is currently accruing to Semtech. Secondary and distinct observation: Semtech's own capacity actions (front-end wafer prepurchases, back-end tester and OSAT capacity, clean-room space) are an **upstream-supply** constraint on Semtech, not a constraint at the deployment site, so they support the theme only indirectly. The reversal condition to watch is explicit: the favorable pricing is a function of tightness, so it unwinds when industry capacity catches up.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: **not scored — T3 minimal entry**)
  - Evidence: **[summary-derived paraphrase]** Vertical extension into photonics via the HieFo acquisition, adding high-power CW lasers, semiconductor optical amplifiers and photodiode arrays alongside the existing driver/TIA franchise; explicit engagement on 3.2T, CPO, NPO and XPO; CW laser volume shipments guided to 1H fiscal 2028; early customer feedback described as very positive. **[verbatim — press release]** GAAP product development and engineering spend rose to $60.6M from $48.2M a year ago (+26%), funding that roadmap.
  - Recommendation: **Initial proposal: 4**. Real, fundable cadence with vertical extension up the optical stack, not just incremental node shrinks. Below 5 because the 3.2T timing question was the single exchange where management did not get a clean pass, and CW laser revenue is still four-plus quarters out.
- **Distribution** (current: **not scored — T3 minimal entry**)
  - Evidence: **[summary-derived paraphrase]** Data center product reaches hyperscalers **through transceiver module makers rather than direct** — an intermediated and structurally concentrated channel — though management cited direct hyperscaler engagement on backlog and capacity planning. The offsetting leg is LoRa: three distinct pillars (LoRaWAN industrial/commercial, LoRaPLUS smart home and security, Amazon Sidewalk mass-market consumer), with Sidewalk expanding into Canada and Mexico and Europe, Australia and Japan to follow, and China now under 50% of the LoRa mix.
  - Recommendation: **Initial proposal: 3**. Genuinely broad and geographically diversifying in LoRa, but narrow and intermediated in the segment that is driving the story, with the customer base for optical components being a short list of module makers.
- **Overall** (current: **not scored — T3 minimal entry**)
  - Recommendation: **Initial proposal: 4** — deliberately above the 3.5 mechanical average of 4 and 3.
  - Reasoning: For a component supplier into a supply-constrained ecosystem ramp, **innovation rate is the distribution mechanism**. Semtech does not need a broad channel; it needs to be first with the part that gets designed into the next-generation module, and winning that slot converts directly into share at the module maker regardless of how few module makers there are. The >50% share at 800G/1.6T combined with the absence of price erosion is the evidence that the conversion actually happened, not just that the products exist. Layered on top, the two legs — AI optical and LoRa IoT — sit in unrelated end markets reached through unrelated channels, so they diversify revenue without diluting focus. That interaction argues above the average. It is capped below 5 because the channel is intermediated (Semtech does not own the customer relationship at the hyperscaler) and the moat must be re-won at each speed node rather than compounding. **Anchor for the operator:** a 4 here puts SMTC level with ANET (4/4/4) and AMD (4/4/4) in the same watchlist file. That is a defensible but aggressive placement for an ~$11.9B (market cap per InsiderScore `earnings_transcript_info`, 2026-08-26) intermediated component supplier on one quarter of evidence, and it is flagged as the single judgment in this note most worth the PM's disagreement.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

> All items summary-derived paraphrase — no verbatim analyst or management language was retrievable (see Sourcing).

1. **The questions nobody asked — the most notable feature of this Q&A.** Across ten analyst exchanges there were **zero** questions on the $101.4M income-tax benefit that produced GAAP EPS of $1.59, **zero** on the $62M sale price for the cellular module business, and **zero** on the $143.4M that moved into the current portion of long-term debt against $204.1M of cash. Ten analysts spent the entire call on data center and LoRa. The balance sheet and the tax line went entirely unexamined, which is where an unpleasant surprise would be least likely to be priced.
2. **Harsh Kumar (Bank of Montreal) — the only exchange flagged as challenging.** Kumar probed 3.2T timing and growth rates for ACC and LPO, and pushed back specifically on the content-growth claim. Management "clarified the timeline," but the summary does not record what date was given. This is the emphasis gap worth tracking: next-node timing is the one area where management did not get a clean pass, and it is precisely the node-by-node re-win risk that caps the competitive-advantage score below 5.
3. **N. Quinn Bolton (Needham) — wafer pre-purchases.** Management confirmed prepayments to secure front-end capacity. That is cash committed ahead of revenue to lock supply. It is a reasonable move in a shortage, but it converts a demand risk into a working-capital and obsolescence risk, and the amounts were not quantified in the summary. Watch cash flow and prepaid/other-asset lines in the 10-Q — other assets already rose from $113.7M to $136.5M since January.
4. **Joseph Moore (Morgan Stanley) — optical pricing.** Management described favorable pricing conditions and minimal erosion. Note carefully what was and was not claimed: the benefit was attributed to supply constraints, not to product differentiation or contractual structure. That is a **cyclical** pricing benefit, and it reverses when industry capacity catches up. Do not capitalize it into a terminal margin assumption.
5. **Scott Searle (Roth Capital) — IoT platform strategy post-divestiture.** Management outlined direction but the summary is notably thin here relative to every other exchange. The residual IoT Systems and Connectivity segment is $98.3M of revenue at a 34.0% gross margin that **compressed from 39.5% a year ago**, and it is the part of the portfolio with the least articulated plan after the module sale closes. The obvious follow-on question — whether more of this segment is for sale — was not pressed.
