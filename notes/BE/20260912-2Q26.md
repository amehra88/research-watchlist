## 3. Forward guidance

FY26 guidance raised (Ex-99.1, "Guidance" section): revenue $3.9B–$4.2B (100% YoY growth at the midpoint, implying FY25 revenue of roughly $2.03B — derived); non-GAAP gross margin ~34%; non-GAAP operating income $800M–$900M; non-GAAP EPS $2.55–$2.85. Per the InsiderScore summary of prepared remarks, the operating-income guide was originally $425–450M (14% margin) at the start of the year and now implies ~21% margin. No quarterly guidance is given; BE guides on the full year only, as ranges.

Derived: H1 revenue is $1,816.4M ($751.1M + $1,065.4M, Ex-99.1), so the guide implies H2 revenue of $2.08–2.38B, i.e. an average of ~$1.04–1.19B per quarter — flat to roughly +12% versus the 2Q run-rate. Against a quarter that grew 42% sequentially, the implied H2 cadence reads conservative, but there is no quantitative guide history to calibrate that against (see Section 8).

Guidance philosophy changes: (a) free cash flow guidance was withdrawn this quarter (Q&A, Amicucci/Evercore — paraphrase from InsiderScore summary); the CFO framed it as aligning guidance metrics and pointed to strong operating-income-to-cash conversion; the summary attributes an "implied baseline of at least $375 million" to management, which I cannot verify against a verbatim transcript and would treat as low-fidelity. (b) Management states the revenue guide is built from backlog conversion plus in-year bookings, with capacity reserved for customers needing rapid deployment, and that it is not dependent on any single project (Q&A, Arcaro/Morgan Stanley — paraphrase).

Balance-sheet signals that bear on the guide (Ex-99.1 balance sheet and cash flow): deferred revenue and customer deposits rose to $445.0M (current $327.1M + long-term $117.9M) from $143.8M at year-end, with +$211.7M in 2Q alone — customers are prepaying; contract assets rose to $365.5M from $178.9M; accrued warranty rose to $77.8M from $20.0M (volume ramp, but a 4x move worth watching).

Next earnings date: 2026-10-27 (InsiderScore `future_earnings_dates`).

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### data_center_deployment_constraints

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** CEO in the release: "customers who traditionally defaulted to combustion technologies are now proactively choosing Bloom as a superior power solution... Bloom is now a standard for AI onsite power" (Ex-99.1). Prepared remarks (InsiderScore summary paraphrase): management cites 30–40 GW of new AI data-center capacity expected to be turned on in 2027 and says BE has reduced the "friction points" of capital, community, permitting and speed. The 10-Q adds a new MD&A section, "Increasing Opposition to Data Center Development," disclosing that in July 2026 the New York governor signed an executive order creating a moratorium on new hyperscale data centers (pausing state environmental permits for up to one year); management argues its islanded systems insulate communities from utility-rate effects and that a resulting regulatory framework could further limit traditional alternatives (10-Q Q2'26, MD&A, section flagged as new).
- **Implication:** The constraint set (interconnection queues, permitting, community opposition) is the demand driver for on-site generation, and this quarter shows it converting to revenue at scale. The NY moratorium is two-sided — it validates the constraint thesis while introducing a new category of risk (state-level pauses on the data centers themselves) that the thesis did not previously have to price.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Revenue +165.5% YoY, product revenue +215.4% (Ex-99.1). Brookfield expanded its financing partnership from $5B to $25B within nine months; IDF with Oaktree, MUFG and Morgan Stanley took cumulative commitments to $2.6B (prepared remarks, InsiderScore summary paraphrase). Backlog described as growing faster than revenue, with several customers not in the prior year-end backlog now placing multi-project orders (prepared remarks, paraphrase). 10-Q MD&A attributes R&D and S&M increases to "the scaling of our solid oxide platforms and AI data center power programs." Related-party revenue was $2.8M in 2Q vs $373.3M in 1Q (Ex-99.1 income statement footnote 1) — the record quarter was direct sales, not JV-channel sales.
- **Implication:** BE is capturing AI capex flow-through to power, and third-party financing capacity ($27.6B of announced shelf across Brookfield and IDF) means customer capex constraints are not the gating factor on conversion. The dependency runs the other way: BE's growth is now partly a function of continued third-party capital appetite for AI-power projects, which is itself a function of the AI-capex cycle — a magnitude and mechanism note, not a break.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

Verbatim transcript body was not reachable (InsiderScore returned the AI summary only); all exchanges below are paraphrased from that summary, with analyst names and firms as reported.

1. **FCF guidance withdrawn** — Amicucci (Evercore) asked why free cash flow guidance was removed. Edwards: aligning guidance metrics; expect strong conversion from operating income to cash. Flag: withdrawing a cash metric in a quarter where working capital moved heavily in both directions (contract assets +$123.0M, AR +$101.4M, prepaids +$78.2M, other LT assets +$70.6M, offset by deferred revenue/deposits +$211.7M and accrued liabilities +$90.8M; Ex-99.1 cash flow) leaves cash conversion opaque exactly when the Hunterbrook report is questioning the accounting. The "at least $375M" baseline attributed to management is unverifiable from the summary.
2. **Scandium and project delays** — Arcaro (Morgan Stanley) asked about exposure to project delays and scandium supply. Edwards cited contractual protections; Sridhar said the 2026 guide is not dependent on any single project and that scandium supply is not China-dependent with visibility for 25 GW. The summary notes details were "limited due to proprietary considerations." Flag: the rebuttal is assertion rather than disclosure (no supplier, country, or contracted-volume detail), and the 10-Q language ("we believe we have sufficient supply...") is softer than the 8-K's "categorically rejects."
3. **Competition** — Kallo (Baird), the call's one exchange InsiderScore tags as challenging, asked about competition and commodity models. Sridhar acknowledged the landscape but said BE focuses on customer value rather than competitors. Flag: no quantified cost or footprint comparison against turbines, engines or other fuel cell types; the "very high-90s percent" share claim (Ocalan/Bernstein exchange) is unsupported by disclosed data.
4. **Capacity** — Mandloi (Mizuho) asked about capacity expansion; management asserted capacity will not be a bottleneck but declined to give capacity numbers. Flag: unusual emphasis on "not a bottleneck" without a GW figure, in a quarter where the FY guide implies H2 revenue only modestly above the 2Q run-rate.
5. **Guidance track record (Store B):** BE `guides_quantitatively: false` for SALES, INC_GROSS and EPS — no guide midpoints are captured for any period, so there is no guide hit-rate or sandbag index to test this quarter's guide against, and none is inferred from tone. The only recorded pattern is the consensus-beat fallback: SALES beat rate 0.64 (n=25, avg beat vs consensus +4.4%), EPS beat rate 0.48 (n=23). This quarter's +29% SALES and +92% EPS surprises are far outside that historical distribution — the signature of consensus lagging a ramp, not of a sandbagged guide. INC_GROSS is not used for any credibility judgment because Store B's actuals are GAAP gross profit while consensus is non-GAAP. Operator note: BE does issue quantitative FY guidance (Ex-99.1 ranges), so the Store B "no quantitative guidance" flag is a capture gap (annual-only guidance not being recorded), not a fact about the issuer.
