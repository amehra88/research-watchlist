## 3. Forward guidance

- **2Q27 (Sep-2026 quarter):** revenue $1.38B ± $50M, +22% y/y (transcript summary, Guidance and Outlook). License and other revenue expected +~30% y/y; royalty revenue expected "low teens" (+13% y/y per the Royalty Revenue sentiment block). Non-GAAP opex guided to $780M, with mid-single-digit % q/q increases through the rest of the year. Non-GAAP EPS guide midpoint $0.47 per Store B (state/thesis/context/ARM.md, 2Q27 row) — the EPS guide is not surfaced in the InsiderScore summary, so it is sourced to the context file only.
- **Versus consensus at guide:** Store B records cons@guide of SALES $1,346.5M and EPS $0.444; the guide midpoints of $1,380M and $0.47 sit ~2.5% and ~5.8% above consensus. This is a guide-above-the-Street posture, not a conservative one.
- **Full year FY27:** royalty growth revised down from ~20% to "high teens" (Smartphones and Royalty Revenue blocks) on smartphone unit weakness driven by higher memory costs and OEM price increases; management says cloud AI overperformance offsets and that it retains "confidence in full-year and next year's numbers" (Child, Q&A with Naji). Overall spending plan for the year unchanged.
- **AGI CPU:** demand pipeline now exceeds $2B (up from the >$1B outlined last quarter for FY27–FY28); "confidence in achieving more than $1 billion has increased" over the past 90 days due to improved supply chain conditions; manufacturing capacity secured for current targets. First-generation gross margin guidance unchanged at high-30s to low-40s %, with a more detailed update at the end of Q3 (Child, Q&A with Quatrochi).
- **Long term:** FY29–FY31 royalty growth framed as returning to 20%+; the projected $15B AGI revenue for FY31 "contemplates current supply chain-related headwinds." Silicon revenue will be broken out separately once it reaches 10% of total revenue, expected by FY28 (Child, Q&A with Rakesh).
- **Guidance philosophy:** unchanged — quantitative point-with-range on revenue, point on EPS and opex; no shift to wider ranges despite the supply-chain uncertainty on AGI CPU.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### chip_design_competition

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Arm's own AGI CPU (introduced March 2026) has shipped initial product to multiple customers in the US and China with a demand pipeline exceeding $2B; Haas said it fits traditional server, head-node, and agentic workload categories with a strong customer base in each (Q&A, O'Malley/Barclays). Arya (BofA) pressed on proprietary accelerators; Haas pointed to potential paths for Arm to connect to custom accelerators (Q&A, challenging exchange). Silicon to be reported separately at 10% of revenue by FY28 (Q&A, Rakesh/Mizuho). (Source: InsiderScore AI summary, AGI CPU and Q&A blocks.)
- **Implication:** Arm is now a merchant chip vendor competing in the data-center CPU socket alongside its licensees, not just an IP supplier. The thesis that chip-design competition is intensifying at the hyperscaler CPU layer is supported; the open question is channel conflict with Neoverse licensees, which the summary does not show any analyst raising.

### silicon_architecture_competition

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Neoverse shipments surpassed 1.5B cores, with the latest 500M in the last nine months; NVIDIA, Google, AWS, Microsoft, and Qualcomm cited as deploying Arm-based CPUs for AI infrastructure; management cited IDC data that Arm-based server platform spending nearly doubled in two quarters and surpassed x86 (prepared remarks per AI Data Center sentiment block). No competing ISA (RISC-V or otherwise) was referenced anywhere in the summary.
- **Implication:** The architecture share-gain narrative is intact and accelerating in the data center; the x86 crossover claim (IDC-sourced, not audited here) is the strongest single datapoint in the call for this theme.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Data center royalty revenue was a record and more than doubled y/y; cloud AI is described as the largest driver of royalty growth, offsetting smartphone softness (AI Data Center and Royalty Revenue blocks). AGI CPU demand doubled to >$2B within a quarter but is capped by tight supply — Arya asked why Arm couldn't secure an additional $1B of supply; Haas acknowledged the tight supply market (Q&A, challenging exchange).
- **Implication:** Hyperscaler capex is flowing to Arm at both the royalty layer and the new silicon layer. The constraint on monetization is supply, not demand — a familiar pattern across AI-infrastructure names this cycle, and one that pushes AGI CPU revenue recognition to the right rather than eliminating it.

### hyperscaler_revenue_concentration

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Revenue mix is shifting toward a small set of named hyperscalers (NVIDIA, Google, AWS, Microsoft) as smartphone royalties weaken; license revenue included $193M from SoftBank (the controlling shareholder) in the quarter, ~34% of license revenue and ~15% of total revenue, with a ~$200M quarterly run rate expected for the rest of the year (Licensing block). AGI CPU customers described as "multiple" in the US and China but not named.
- **Implication:** Concentration is rising along two axes — hyperscaler royalty exposure and SoftBank related-party licensing — while the historically diversified smartphone base shrinks in relative terms. Not a break (the customer list is broad and growing), but the quality-of-revenue debate should now include the SoftBank run rate explicitly.

### inference_compute_economics

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Mobley (StoneX) asked about FY29–FY31 demand; Haas and Child pointed to increased CPU demand driven by inference workloads, and Child said supply constraints are expected to ease, potentially allowing Arm to exceed internal targets (Q&A). Haas separately positioned AGI CPU across head nodes and agentic applications (Q&A, O'Malley). Management framed the broader opportunity as "efficient compute across cloud, edge, and physical AI" (Market Opportunities block).
- **Implication:** Management's argument is that inference and agentic workloads are more CPU-intensive per dollar of accelerator spend than training, which pushes value toward Arm's layer. This supports the theme's premise that the inference shift changes the compute bill of materials; it remains a management assertion rather than a disclosed metric.

### automotive_semiconductor_demand

- **Status:** Confirm (thin evidence)
- **Evidence:** The only automotive reference is that licensing growth was driven in part by "renewed long-term agreements with large hyperscalers, automotive, robotics, and handset OEMs" (Licensing block), plus the "physical AI" framing in the opportunity section. No automotive royalty or unit data was disclosed in the summary.
- **Implication:** Licensing renewals with automotive OEMs are consistent with the theme but carry little information on end-market demand; treat this quarter as non-diagnostic for automotive. The classification is Confirm only because the sole datapoint is positive.

- **Overall** (current: not populated — schema gap)

## 8. Management Q&A flags

All items are paraphrased from the InsiderScore AI summary; verbatim quotes are not available from the vendor body.

1. **Guidance pattern fit (Store B).** This quarter fits the recorded pattern exactly: SALES beat guide mid by +2.3% (record avg_beat_vs_guidance 3.5%, guide_hit_rate 1.0 over 9 periods, sandbag_index -0.019) and EPS beat guide mid by +12.5% (record avg 12.8%, sandbag_index -0.040). ARM guides at or above consensus and then beats modestly on revenue and materially on EPS — the EPS beat is structural (opex timing, $27M under guide this quarter), not sandbagging. The 2Q27 guide (mid $1,380M / $0.47, both above consensus) continues the same posture. The one departure from pattern is qualitative: the FY27 royalty growth framing was cut from ~20% to high teens, which is the first downward revision to a stated growth target visible in the context file's window.
2. **Arya (BofA) — supply and proprietary accelerators (challenging exchange).** Pressed why Arm could not secure an additional $1B of AGI CPU supply and what proprietary accelerators mean for Arm. Haas acknowledged the tight supply market and cited potential paths for Arm to connect to custom accelerators. This is the one place management was on the back foot; the accelerator-attach answer was directional, not specific.
3. **Quatrochi (Wells Fargo) — AGI CPU gross margin.** Child said margin expectations are unchanged (high-30s to low-40s % for first generation) and deferred a more detailed update to the end of Q3. Reasonable, but it means the mix-dilution question stays open for two more quarters as silicon ramps.
4. **Shi (Needham) — royalty growth revision.** Asked what offsets the lower royalty growth; Child said license growth compensates and cloud AI deployments may add upside, and reiterated confidence in 20% royalty growth in future years. Note the offset relies on license revenue, which management itself describes as lumpy and which includes the SoftBank run rate.
5. **Rakesh (Mizuho) — silicon revenue breakout.** Child said silicon will be reported separately only once it constitutes 10% of revenue, expected by FY28. Until then, AGI CPU revenue is embedded in "license and other," which will blur the licensing-versus-product growth signal for several quarters.
