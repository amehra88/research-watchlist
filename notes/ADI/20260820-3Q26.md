_Prepared by earnings-reviewer, 2026-08-20. Tier: T2._

## 1. Headline read

Confirm, and the strongest single datapoint is a reversal of last quarter's central modeling instruction: in 2Q26 CFO Puccio told Rasgon that 73% gross margin was "a likely ceiling," and the prior note (`notes/ADI/20260521-2Q26.md` §8) accordingly told modelers not to underwrite expansion beyond ~72.5% GM / 49% OM. ADI printed exactly that in Q3 (72.5% GM, 50% OM) and then guided Q4 to **74% GM and 52% OM** — through the stated ceiling one quarter later. The print also beat ADI's own guide, not just consensus: revenue $4.022B vs. $3.9B guided, adjusted EPS $3.45 at the exact top of the $3.30 ± $0.15 range.

## 3. Forward guidance

Q4'26 (Oct-quarter) guide:
- Revenue: **$4.3B ± $100M** — ~7% sequential on top of Q3's +11%.
- Gross margin: **74%** — explicitly framed as a return to 2022 peak levels, and above the 73% "likely ceiling" management set one quarter ago. Attributed to favorable mix, higher fixed-cost absorption, and price adjustments.
- Operating margin: **52% ± 100bps** (from 50% in Q3).
- Adjusted EPS: **$3.86 ± $0.15**.
- Non-operating expense ~$80M; tax rate 12–14%.

Guidance philosophy is unchanged in form (point-estimate revenue with a ± band, same as prior quarters) but materially more ambitious in substance. Two forward markers beyond the quarter: Roche framed 2027 as a "brisk growth year" driven by AI infrastructure, defense and industrial; and management put a **2030** horizon on data-center growth ("strong double-digit growth through at least 2030"). Price actions are still flowing through and will be **fully realized in Q1 2027** (Sur exchange) — so the pricing tailwind extends past the guided quarter. Maxim synergies $700M in 2026 rising to >$1B in 2027. CapEx held within the long-term 4–6%-of-revenue model.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### automotive_semiconductor_demand

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Auto was 25% of Q3 revenue, +14% sequentially and +16% YoY (prepared remarks) — well ahead of the mid-/high-single-digit sequential growth ADI guided for Q3 on the May call. Management attributes above-market growth to content and share gains in **both** combustion and electric vehicles, plus next-gen infotainment and electric powertrains. Q4 sequential growth is guided to moderate to low single digits.
- **Implication:** The auto inflection called in the prior note has more than delivered — ADI guided mid-/high-single-digit and printed +14%. The content-and-share framing (explicitly spanning ICE as well as EV) makes this less dependent on EV unit growth than the BMS-centric story of two quarters ago. The Q4 deceleration to low single digits is the one thing to watch, though against a +14% base it reads as normalization rather than a turn.

### semiconductor_cycle

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Record revenue +40% YoY. Industrial — the most cyclically-levered segment — was 49% of revenue, +10% QoQ / +53% YoY, broad-based across ATE, electronic test & measurement, aerospace & defense and automation. Management flagged this as the **ninth consecutive quarter of above-seasonal growth with a tenth guided**, and characterized end-market consumption as "well below historical levels," implying the restock is not yet the demand. On the Arya exchange, Puccio said customer inventory levels remain lean.
- **Implication:** The cycle-confirm from the prior note holds and extends. The "consumption below historical levels" claim is the key bull argument — it says the upcycle is being driven by content and end-demand rather than channel refill, which would make it longer than a typical analog cycle. Counterweight: ADI's *own* inventory is at record levels on both the balance sheet and in the channel (see next theme), so "lean customer inventory" and "record ADI inventory" need to be held together, not read selectively.

### data_center_deployment_constraints

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Genuinely two-sided this quarter. Constraint side: inventory at **record levels both on the balance sheet and in channels**; lead times **extending**; management cited "industry-wide soft spots"; Prisco (Cantor) asked directly about supply capability amid rising demand, and Puccio/Roche answered with hybrid-manufacturing scenario planning and capacity partnerships rather than a flat "no constraint." Demand/content side: 800V DC distribution driving significant design uptake, OCS revenue set to roughly double in 2026 with a similar 2027 target.
- **Implication:** The prior note flagged this theme as possibly mis-assigned to ADI, on the grounds that ADI is a beneficiary rather than a constrained supplier. This call partly rehabilitates the tag — extending lead times and industry-wide soft spots are real constraint signals — but the constraint sits **upstream of ADI in its own supply chain**, not at the end-customer deployment layer the theme name describes. Recommendation to the PM: rather than dropping the tag as the prior note contemplated, re-scope it. The live question for ADI is whether it can *supply* into AI deployment, not whether deployment constraints throttle its demand.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm (strengthening materially)
- **Evidence:** The densest theme on the call. Communications +18% QoQ / +84% YoY, with data center now **80% of communications revenue** and **>100% YoY growth in both optical and power**. Management stated the addressable market for data center and energy is **"more than double what was envisioned a year ago."** The "grid-to-chip" strategy spans energy generation, storage and delivery into the rack. The Empower Semiconductor acquisition **closed in July for $1.5B** and enables power delivery into the processor package, quantified as a **10–15% reduction in compute power consumption in 1GW data centers**. OCS revenue doubling in 2026, similar target 2027. Roche extended the frame to "AI's second generation" beyond data centers — robotics, digital health, autonomous mobility.
- **Implication:** ADI has moved from "credible AI beneficiary by adjacency" (prior note's framing) to a named content supplier with a quantified customer value proposition and a 2030 growth horizon. The 10–15% compute-power-reduction figure is the single most commercially concrete AI claim ADI has made — it converts Empower from a portfolio gap-fill into a specifiable customer ROI. This theme now carries most of the incremental thesis weight for the name.

### china_us_tensions

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** **Evidence gap — this is classified on the absence of commentary, not on findings.** The transcript summary contains no China-specific and no tariff-specific commentary this quarter: no export-control discussion, no China demand call-out (unlike 2Q26, where China EV BMS strength was named), no tariff impact. The only adjacent language is generic — growth described as "broad-based across markets and regions," and the hybrid manufacturing network framed as supporting supply-chain agility, which is the same standard hedge as last quarter.
- **Implication:** Two consecutive quarters with no China-US tension operating as a binding constraint, and this quarter with no China commentary at all. The theme is drifting toward dormancy for ADI — it remains a legitimate tail-risk overlay but has produced no signal for two quarters running. Caveat on method: this read is from the AI-generated summary; a China question in the full transcript could have been dropped in summarization, so treat the absence as weak evidence rather than confirmation.

## 5. AI positioning signal

- **Current score:** none — not yet scored. ADI has no `ai_positioning` attribute in either watchlist entry. The prior note (2026-05-21) *proposed* an initial of "3+"; that proposal was never written to the watchlist, so what follows is an initial score, not a revision.

(3) Empower closed July, $1.5B, delivering in-package power with a quantified 10–15% compute-power reduction at 1GW scale. (4) OCS revenue doubling 2026, similar 2027 target. ## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: none — not yet scored; prior note proposed 3)
  - Evidence: Empower closed and reframed — last quarter it read as a tell that organic R&D could not deliver IVR/silicon-capacitor capability on the AI timeline; this quarter it is a delivered capability inside the grid-to-chip stack with a quantified 10–15% compute-power saving. OCS revenue doubling two years running. 800V DC with "industry-leading power densities" and customers demanding >98% efficiency plus advanced protection and telemetry. Maxim synergies $700M 2026 → >$1B 2027. Targeted R&D plus hybrid-manufacturing expansion called out as strategic priorities.
  - Recommendation: propose initial **4** (prior note proposed 3).
- **Distribution** (current: none — not yet scored; prior note proposed 5)
  - Evidence: Growth "broad-based across markets and regions." Industrial breadth across ATE, ETM, A&D and automation simultaneously. Auto content gains in both combustion and electric platforms plus next-gen infotainment. Hybrid manufacturing network providing supply-chain agility and capacity optionality. Channel depth sufficient to hold record inventory without disruption.
  - Recommendation: propose initial **5** (matches prior note's proposal).
- **Overall** (current: none — not yet scored; prior note proposed 4)
  - Recommendation: propose initial **4**.
  - Reasoning — explicitly not a mechanical average (4 and 5 would average 4.5): for ADI the two dimensions are becoming **less correlated, not more**. The legacy franchise is distribution-anchored — a long tail of tens of thousands of customers, multi-decade lifecycles, design-in stickiness, and ASPs several times the industry average, where breadth is what monetizes incremental innovation. But the fastest-growing part of the business, AI data center, is the opposite shape: a concentrated, design-win-driven franchise contested against MPS, TI, Infineon and Vicor, where the long-tail distribution moat confers essentially no advantage and the win is purely on power density and time-to-market. So the moat is excellent where growth is slow and *unproven* where growth is fast. That argues for 4 rather than rounding up on the strength of the distribution score — the distribution 5 is real but is doing less work on the marginal dollar of revenue than it was two years ago.

## 7. Potential investor interest signal

- **Current score:** none — not yet scored. The prior note proposed an initial of 4.
- **Evidence from this call:** (1) Beat both consensus and ADI's own guide; EPS at the top of the guided range. (2) Q4 guide well above the Q3 print on every line — revenue $4.3B, GM 74%, OM 52%, EPS $3.86. (3) **The gross-margin ceiling broke.** Management guided through the 73% level it called a likely ceiling one quarter earlier, back to 2022 peak, and said it expects to sustain it through the upcycle with potential further upside on mix. (4) Price actions not fully realized until Q1 2027 — a known, dated tailwind. (5) FCF $4.9B TTM, 36% of revenue; **over 100% of FCF returned** via dividends and buybacks, with a standing 100% long-term target. (6) Maxim synergies >$1B in 2027. (7) 2027 pre-framed as a "brisk growth year"; data center double-digit through 2030. (8) Roche's industry-level claim that analog's structural growth rate may re-rate from high-single to double-digit.
- **Recommendation:** Propose initial **5** (prior note proposed 4).
- **Reasoning:** The prior note gave one specific reason for holding at 4 rather than 5 — management had called gross margin a near-term ceiling, capping upward EPS revision skew. That exact constraint was removed this quarter, and removed by management itself. Combined with a dated pricing tailwind into Q1 2027, >100% FCF return, and a 2030 growth frame on the AI franchise, the earnings-revision setup is about as clean as this schema is designed to capture. What would argue for 4 instead: the stock has already run hard into and through this print, and record inventory on both balance sheet and channel is the one datapoint that could reverse the revision direction if end demand cools. Flagging both for the PM, but the schema attribute measures investor interest, and on the call's evidence that should be high.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

- **Buchalter (Cowen) — 74% gross margin sustainability.** The most important exchange on the call, and the direct counterpart to last quarter's Rasgon ceiling exchange. Buchalter questioned whether 74% is sustainable and how ADI balances revenue growth against margin. Puccio and Roche defended it via innovation and strategic investment despite acknowledged inflationary pressure. **Modeling instruction from the prior note is now superseded** — do not carry forward the ~72.5% GM / 49% OM cap.
- **Sur (JPMorgan) — gross margin bridge.** Puccio decomposed the Q4 step-up into favorable mix, higher fixed-cost absorption, and price adjustments, and specified that the **full impact of price actions lands in Q1 2027**. This is the cleanest modeling detail on the call: the pricing tailwind has a known duration extending a quarter past the guide.
- **Arya (BofA) — 2027 secular vs. cyclical.** Roche pointed to defense, AI and consumer; Puccio's supporting argument was that **customer inventory levels remain lean**. Worth flagging as an internal tension rather than a clean positive: ADI simultaneously reports its own inventory at record levels on balance sheet and in channel. Both can be true, but the bull case leans on the customer-side number while the risk sits on ADI's side.
- **Prisco (Cantor) — supply capability and capacity.** Asked whether ADI can supply rising demand. Puccio and Roche answered with capacity expansion planning, the hybrid manufacturing model, and partnerships — constructive, but scenario-planning language rather than a flat denial of constraint. Read alongside extending lead times and "industry-wide soft spots," this is the main watch item into Q4.
- **Rasgon (Bernstein) — data center / communications growth.** Answered by Jeff Ambrosi alongside Roche, on rising analog content and share-gain investment. **Note on the source payload:** this row in the InsiderScore summary carries a "Q2'26 Challenging Exchange" tag listing gross margins, revenue upside and outsourcing. That is a back-reference to *last* quarter's exchange, not this call — this call had zero challenging exchanges. Do not read it as a Q3 flag.
