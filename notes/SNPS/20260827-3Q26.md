### silicon_architecture_competition

- **Status:** **Confirm**
- **Evidence:** Q&A (Vruwink, Baird): Ghazi described an **acceleration in multi-die EDA demand** driven by advanced packaging and 3D-IC architecture. Q&A (Simpson, Morgan Stanley): the shift to **"Factory II"** — customized IP with a transition toward a **license-plus-royalty model**, versus "Factory I" standard IP. Prepared remarks: die-to-die IP business **on track to double YoY, now 100+ cumulative wins**; IP offerings expanding to more customized solutions for AI workloads.
- **Implication:** Architectural proliferation continues to pull SNPS content, and the Factory I/Factory II framing is a concrete commercial structure around what was only a narrative last quarter ("new business models with hyperscalers"). Die-to-die doubling is the hardest datapoint in the print for this theme. The license-plus-royalty shift, if it lands, converts IP from a one-time-license business toward a participation in customer volume — margin- and durability-positive, but it back-end-loads revenue recognition.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

Korea revenue growth tied explicitly to Samsung/SK Hynix leadership in AI infrastructure. Customers named as adopters: AMD, NVIDIA, Samsung, MediaTek.
- **Implication:** The clearest confirm on the page, and better-quality than last quarter because the non-AI base has stopped deteriorating — the AI acceleration is now additive rather than merely offsetting. Record emulation/prototyping revenue is the most capex-correlated line SNPS has and it is at a high. SNPS remains a high-margin software toll on the AI silicon buildout.

### chip_design_competition

- **Status:** **Drift**
- **Evidence:** **Challenging exchange (Shi, Needham):** Shi questioned the potential threat of **AI disrupting the EDA industry** and the opportunity from agentic AI agents. Ghazi acknowledged AI advancements but argued SNPS's own position in autonomous workflows mitigates disruption risk, adding that **foundational tools remain essential for accuracy and determinism** and that customers' own AI models are not a threat. Separately, management confirmed **new business models — subscription and consumption-based licensing — are being piloted** to monetize increased software usage from AI-driven workflows, and is exploring **hybrid models combining SNPS agents with customers' proprietary agents**.
- **Implication:** Drift on **mechanism**, not direction. The competitive question for EDA is shifting from "who has the better tool" to "does the AI layer commoditize or re-entrench the tool vendor, and how does the vendor get paid when an agent does the work of an engineer." Management's answer — determinism and accuracy as the moat — is credible but is a defensive framing, and the pricing model that captures agentic usage is still **pilot-stage**, not contracted. The hybrid-agent concession (customers bringing their own agents) is the most consequential single line in the call: it concedes that the agent layer will be contested even where the underlying engine is not. Thesis intact; the monetization mechanism is now the thing to watch.
