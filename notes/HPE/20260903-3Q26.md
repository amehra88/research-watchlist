# HPE — 3Q26 earnings read

_Prepared by earnings-reviewer, 2026-09-03. Tier: T2._

## 1. Headline read

A second consecutive **Confirm**, and a materially stronger one than June: revenue $12,213M (+34% YoY), record non-GAAP gross margin 40.4%, first-ever non-GAAP EPS above $1.00 at $1.11, and a double raise to both the FY26 and FY27 frames (FY27 non-GAAP EPS now $4.40–$4.60 vs. the 12–16% growth frame introduced last quarter). Two themes drift rather than confirm — **data_center_deployment_constraints** (supply moved from an explicitly *unmodeled* upside option in June into the FY27 guidance base case, via multi-year supply agreements) and **hyperscaler_revenue_concentration** (June's favorable drift is under pressure; three analysts pressed hyperscale this call and the Oracle AI-cloud win is a genuine large-scale deployment).

**Tier trajectory — flag.** The prior note (2026-06-02) is headed **Tier: T1**; HPE is now in `tier_2_active_candidates`. Under this operator's convention T1 means an actual BCTK holding, so the name appears to have been exited between June and now. I have no evidence on the file as to why, and I do not assert a reason — but nothing below should be read as commentary on a current position. Surfaced as an observation for the PM.

## 3. Forward guidance

Raised on both horizons, and the FY27 raise is the more informative one.

- **Q4 FY26:** revenue $13.9B–$14.8B; GAAP diluted EPS $1.12–$1.22; **non-GAAP diluted EPS $1.20–$1.30** (excludes net after-tax adjustments of ~$0.08/diluted share). (8-K Ex-99.1, "Fiscal 2026 Fourth Quarter Outlook")
- **FY26 full year:** revenue growth raised to **34%–37%**; Networking segment revenue growth raised to **73%–74%**; non-GAAP EPS raised to **$3.75–$3.85** (from $3.35–$3.45 in June); GAAP EPS $2.93–$3.03; FCF raised to **at least $3.75B**. (8-K Ex-99.1 + transcript summary)
- **FY27 framework:** revenue growth raised to **13%–17%** (from 8–12% in June); networking 14–17%; cloud & AI 14–18%; operating profit growth 14–18%; company operating margin 14–15%; networking operating margin mid-to-high 20s; cloud/AI margin ~13%; non-GAAP EPS **$4.40–$4.60** (+16–20%); FCF **at least $5B** (from ≥$4.5B in June). (8-K Ex-99.1, "Fiscal 2027 Outlook Framework"; transcript summary)

**Read the 73–74% FY26 networking guide correctly:** that is Juniper-acquisition-driven, not organic. Organic/normalized networking revenue grew **10%** in Q3. Do not model 73% as underlying demand.

**Tone and philosophy.** Still ranges, not point estimates, but the framing shifted from June's defensive posture to a confident one, and the *reason* is specific rather than rhetorical: management attributed the FY27 raise to **secured multi-year supply agreements** (Vogt/UBS exchange), not to a demand upgrade alone. Two deliberately conservative elements remain: the **AMD Helios opportunity is excluded entirely** from the FY27 outlook pending deployment-schedule certainty (Cardoso/JPMorgan), and Myers said a comprehensive FY27 guide comes at the end of Q4 — so this is a framework, not a final guide. Management also committed to returning **at least 75% of free cash flow** to shareholders in Q4 FY26.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm (strengthened)
- **Evidence:** Cloud & AI revenue $9.0B, +25% YoY, with segment operating profit over $1.5B and a 17% operating margin; AI systems orders **+30% sequentially** and traditional server orders up strong double digits; AI systems backlog at a new high; a **multi-billion-dollar inferencing deal signed post-quarter**; FY27 cloud & AI revenue growth guided 14–18%. Niknam (Truist) probed whether pricing actions were denting demand — Neri reported no hesitation, citing increased customer budgets for AI and the cost advantage of on-prem deployment. (Transcript summary, Cloud & AI and Q&A sections)
- **Implication:** The capex thesis is not just intact, it is broadening — the raise is carried by both AI systems and traditional servers, and pipeline is described as a multiple of backlog. Note the composition caveat from the Newman (Bernstein) exchange: server revenue growth is currently **ASP-driven** because supply caps units, with stronger unit growth expected in Q4. Growth is real but partly priced, not shipped.

### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm (strongest single item in the print)
- **Evidence:** Networking revenue $2.9B (+10% normalized) with **orders up 36%** — a record order book far outpacing revenue; AI networking orders hit a quarterly record **$700M**, and the FY26 cumulative AI-networking order target was raised to **$2.5–3B** (from ≥$2B in June). Expanded **Oracle collaboration** to deploy Juniper routers and switches across Oracle's AI cloud infrastructure. Juniper integration ahead of schedule; DOJ settlement approved by the US federal court, removing regulatory overhang; $600M annual run-rate synergy on track by end-FY28. Management cited Juniper's **Express 5 custom silicon** and rapid 1.6Tb switch time-to-market as the differentiators. Sub-lines: campus/branch revenue +8%, routing +23%, security +12%, data center networking revenue **−6%** on shipment timing (orders stronger). Networking operating margin 22%, guided to mid-to-high 20s in FY27. (Transcript summary, Networking and Juniper sections; Q&A Long/Barclays)
- **Implication:** The 36%-orders vs. 10%-revenue gap is the number to watch, and Daryanani pressed exactly there (see §8). Management's answer — supply, not competitive loss — is consistent with the rest of the call, but this is now the key falsifiable claim: if the gap does not close as supply eases in Q4/FY27, the competitive read changes. The Oracle win is the first evidence that HPE can win large-scale AI-cloud infrastructure on technical merit rather than installed-base incumbency.

### enterprise_ai_adoption

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** GreenLake customers reached **52,000, +18% YoY** (from 44,000), with customers expanding into new software and cloud services and net retention rising. Management described a shift toward AI-driven enterprise initiatives receiving **higher investment levels than traditional IT projects**. PCAI orders up triple digits; Alletra MP storage up strong double digits. HPE Financial Services posted record Q3 financing volumes, residual value and ROE (>20%), positioned as a differentiator as customers seek to finance AI capex. (Transcript summary, Cloud & AI, GreenLake & Services)
- **Implication:** Adoption is showing up as consumption breadth (more customers, more services per customer) rather than one-off cluster sales. The financing angle is underrated: when AI capex strains customer budgets, a captive finance arm converts a balance-sheet objection into a deal — that is an adoption accelerant peers without one lack.

### data_center_deployment_constraints

- **Status:** **Drift** (mechanism change — was Confirm in June)
- **Evidence:** June's read was that management **explicitly declined to assume any FY27 supply improvement**, framing relief as upside outside the base case. This call reverses that stance: HPE has signed **multi-year supply agreements**, purchase commitments **more than doubled quarter-over-quarter**, improvement is expected to begin in Q4 FY26 and continue into 2027, and the raised outlook is attributed to "strong demand, a substantial backlog, and **improved supply chain conditions**." In the Vogt (UBS) exchange Neri attributed the FY27 raise specifically to secured supply agreements. Constraints themselves persist — DDR5/DDR4, NAND, wafer capacity (Merchant/Citi) — and inventory rose to **$11.8B** on higher commodity costs and targeted pre-purchases. (Transcript summary, Supply Chain and Inventory; Q&A Vogt, Merchant)
- **Implication:** The thesis is not broken — supply still gates revenue conversion — but the *mechanism* changed, which is the Drift trigger. Supply has moved from an unmodeled option into the guidance base case, which means the FY27 raise is now **underwritten by supply assumptions rather than insulated from them**. That cuts both ways: it removes June's built-in sandbag, and it transfers risk onto the $11.8B inventory position and the long-term purchase commitments. The flip side of de-risking supply is carrying it — HPE now owns commodity-price and obsolescence exposure it did not have a quarter ago. Watch inventory turns and the cash conversion cycle.

### hyperscaler_revenue_concentration

- **Status:** **Drift** (June's favorable drift is partially reversing)
- **Evidence:** June's read was that HPE was positioning *away* from hyperscaler concentration toward enterprise/sovereign mix. This call, hyperscale was the single most-pressed topic — Murphy (Goldman), Rakers (Wells Fargo) and Mohan (BofA) each raised it, alongside the **Oracle AI-cloud collaboration** and a **multi-billion-dollar inferencing deal**. Neri's response was a framing move: the hyperscale customer is "treated as an **enterprise client**" buying traditional servers for internal AI inferencing, and the strategic direction is unchanged. (Q&A Murphy, Rakers, Mohan)
- **Implication:** Record that as *management framing, not established fact*. Whether a hyperscaler buying at scale for internal inferencing carries enterprise-like margin and durability, or hyperscaler-like pricing power and lumpiness, is unresolved by anything disclosed on this call. The June favorable drift should not be silently re-asserted: HPE is now visibly winning large hyperscale-class AI infrastructure business, and both the Oracle deal and the multi-billion inferencing deal are concentration events. The thesis is not broken, but the direction of drift has changed sign versus June and needs segment-level disclosure to adjudicate. **Request:** customer-concentration disclosure in the 10-Q when filed.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: **none on file**; recommended 3+ / 4 on 2026-06-02, never written to watchlist)
  - Evidence: Juniper **Express 5 custom silicon**; 1.6Tb switches with rapid time-to-market; Alletra MP storage up strong double digits; PCAI orders up triple digits; networking + security convergence continuing. Product cadence is now backed by proprietary silicon, not only portfolio integration.
  - Recommendation: **revise upward to 4** (from the June 3+/4 recommendation). The custom-silicon evidence is what moves it.
- **Distribution** (current: **none on file**; recommended 4 on 2026-06-02, never written to watchlist)
  - Evidence: GreenLake 52,000 customers, +18% YoY, with rising net retention and expanding services attach; unified HPE+Juniper go-to-market producing record networking orders (+36%); Oracle as a new large-scale channel; HPE Financial Services at record volumes and >20% ROE, financing customer AI capex.
  - Recommendation: **reaffirm 4**, with a credible case for 4+ if GreenLake net retention and the finance-arm attach are disclosed quantitatively next quarter.
- **Overall** (current: **none on file**; recommended 4 on 2026-06-02, never written to watchlist)
  - Recommendation: **revise to 4+**.
  - Reasoning: June's judgment was that distribution is the dominant axis and that adequate innovation compounds *through* the channel — installed base, GreenLake consumption, combined enterprise networking footprint. That remains the base case, but this quarter adds an interaction the June note could not observe: HPE now has a proprietary silicon asset (Express 5) that is being cited as the reason it competes for AI-networking business, and the demand response is visible in hard numbers — record $700M AI-networking orders, networking orders +36%, and FY27 networking operating margin guided to the mid-to-high 20s. Margin at that level in a competitive networking market is evidence of differentiation, not just reach. The **expanded** Oracle collaboration extends an existing relationship into large-scale AI cloud infrastructure — a genuine step up in deal class, though it is a relationship extension rather than a cold technical win, so I do not lean on it as proof that innovation opened a door distribution could not. Net: innovation is beginning to raise the *class* of deal distribution can carry, rather than merely riding the channel — more durable than a 4 implies, and above a mechanical average of a 4 innovation and a 4 distribution. Held below 5 because the differentiation is one product generation old and largely unproven outside networking. All three marks remain analyst-draft; operator owns the watchlist edit.

## 9. Vocabulary candidates

- **"AI networking orders"** — HPE's distinct order-tracking bucket (record $700M in Q3; FY26 cumulative target $2.5–3B). Distinct from generic networking demand; a cleaner leading indicator than revenue.
- **"Express 5" / custom networking silicon** — Juniper's proprietary switching silicon, now cited as the competitive differentiator in AI networking. No existing vocabulary captures networking-silicon differentiation as separate from accelerator silicon.
- **"Helios opportunity"** — the AMD Helios rack-scale program, explicitly excluded from FY27 guidance. Relevant beyond HPE as an AMD-ecosystem theme.
- **"multi-year supply agreements" / "purchase commitments"** — the mechanism by which a supply-constrained vendor converts scarcity into guidance certainty while absorbing inventory risk. A cross-ticker pattern worth naming.
- **"hyperscaler-as-enterprise inferencing"** — management's reclassification of a hyperscale customer as an enterprise buyer on workload grounds. Directly relevant to `hyperscaler_revenue_concentration`; surfaced because it is a framing device the PM should be able to search for across names.
