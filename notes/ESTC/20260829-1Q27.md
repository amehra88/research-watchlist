## 3. Forward guidance

FY2027 guidance was **raised** following the Q1 beat:

- Total revenue **$1.998B–$2.010B**, ~15.3% growth at the midpoint on a constant-currency basis (the sentiment section of the same report renders this as ~15.2% — a rounding difference in the source, not two different guides)
- Sales-led subscription revenue **$1.682B–$1.694B**, +17.5% at the midpoint in constant currency
- Non-GAAP operating margin **~19.4%** for the year (vs 16.2% delivered in Q1 — implies meaningful back-half margin expansion)
- Non-GAAP diluted EPS **$3.29–$3.37**
- Adjusted free cash flow margin **21.5%**

**Direction, magnitude, tone.** Guidance philosophy is unchanged — ranges, not point estimates, and constant-currency framing throughout. Management explicitly expects *acceleration in the second half*, which is a more committal posture than a pure raise-by-the-beat. Welihinda attributed confidence to three named drivers when pressed by Lenschow (Barclays): pipeline build, consumption performance, and sales productivity increases. Notably, when Ma (Guggenheim) asked whether guidance embeds cross-sell acceleration, Welihinda said guidance **does not assume a change in customer behavior beyond current trends** — conservative framing that leaves room above the guide if multi-product attach does accelerate. Management also guided NER to improve within four quarters as constant-currency revenue acceleration builds.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

ESTC carries exactly one theme, so this section carries the whole thesis read.

### ai_infrastructure_software

- **Status:** **Drift** — Confirm on direction and attach; Drift on the revenue mechanism the thesis actually asserts.

- **Evidence:**

  *Supporting the thesis (attach is real and accelerating):*
  - AI-feature penetration among $100K+ ACV customers reached **37%, up from ~21% a year ago (~670+ customers)** — prepared remarks, and re-confirmed by Kulkarni to Hedberg (RBC) in Q&A. Kulkarni further stated that the AI-using cohort shows **higher growth propensity** than non-AI customers, and Welihinda repeated to Calitri (Needham) that AI users show higher expansion rates.
  - Concrete retrieval-layer product cadence: **VectorDB index mode and auto-calibration** released for vector search across data types; **Jina multimodal and multilingual semantic search extended to on-premises and air-gapped environments**; Agent Builder enhancements — prepared remarks / Product Innovation section.
  - Kulkarni to Singh (Morgan Stanley), asked directly why Elastic is better positioned for AI search than other data platforms: pointed to **optimization for unstructured data** and advanced search capabilities, framing early AI investment as the source of forward advantage. To Essex (JPMorgan), he framed Elastic's role as handling unstructured data and **"providing context for AI models"** — i.e. explicitly the retrieval layer under an application.
  - Competitive wins attributed to AI features, including a global semiconductor company.

  *Qualifying the thesis (the mechanism is unproven from this call):*
  - **Welihinda declined to disclose segment revenue contribution** when Essex (JPMorgan) asked what Security contributes — "specific revenue percentages are not disclosed." There is consequently **no disclosed AI/vector/retrieval workload revenue anywhere in this call.** 37% is an *adoption/attach* statistic, not an ARR-attribution statistic.
  - The growth story management themselves narrate is **security-first**: "Security was cited as the highest growth solution area in Q1" — driven by Attack Discovery, FedRAMP High, CISA SIEM-as-a-Service, IDC MarketScape SIEM leader, Forrester XDR strong performer. Observability is the second narrated driver (Columnar Mode in Elasticsearch 9.5, native Prometheus ingestion, Kubernetes support, ~20% lower storage cost, Deductive AI acquisition for agentic SRE).
  - Cloud +27% vs total +15% tells you **deployment mix, not workload type** — none of that 27% is attributed to retrieval/RAG workloads specifically.

- **Implication:** The thesis survives and its leading indicator is moving the right way fast — a near-doubling of AI attach inside the cohort that produces 90% of sales-led subscription revenue is not noise, and the product cadence at the retrieval layer (VectorDB index mode, on-prem/air-gapped Jina) is real engineering against the exact thesis mechanism. But the P&L this quarter is being carried by security and observability, and Elastic has chosen not to disclose the split. **What would upgrade this to a clean Confirm:** disclosed AI/vector workload revenue or an AI-attributed ARR figure, *or* the arithmetic tell — security/observability growth decelerating while total growth holds up, which would prove the AI-attached cohorts are carrying the number. Until then, treat ESTC as a company with a credible and improving claim on the retrieval layer whose retrieval revenue is unmeasured, and size any position accordingly.

- **Competitive frame — what this call does and does not cover.** The call gives usable material against **Splunk/Datadog** on the observability and SIEM side (SIEM migration speed for large agencies per Kulkarni to Sultan/UBS; Gartner Magic Quadrant observability leader for a third consecutive year; metrics relaunch with ~20% storage savings; native Prometheus ingestion) and an implicit angle against **AWS/OpenSearch** via the deployment-flexibility moat — cloud through to air-gapped data centers, which Kulkarni framed to Jump (Truist) as "operational sovereignty" demand in regulated industries. It gives **essentially nothing direct on pgvector/Postgres, Pinecone, or Databricks** — those competitors were not raised by any analyst and not addressed by management. I am not generating commentary on them that the transcript does not support; that gap is itself worth noting, since pgvector commoditization is the primary bear case on the retrieval-layer thesis and it went entirely unexamined on this call.

- **Overall** (current: **not scored — T3 minimal entry**)

  - Reasoning: **explicitly not the mechanical average of 4 and 3.** For this specific business model the two dimensions are multiplicative in one direction and capped in another. That interaction argues for an overall above the distribution component. But the cap is real: 15% total growth, a net expansion rate still drifting down, and 220bps of GAAP gross margin erosion say the innovation is not yet converting into either faster expansion or better unit economics. ## 8. Management Q&A flags

The InsiderScore payload flagged zero "challenging" exchanges and rated overall sentiment Positive, so there is no adversarial exchange to report. The following are emphasis-and-omission flags drawn from the Q&A review summaries. **These are paraphrases of analyst questions and management answers as summarized by InsiderScore's AI, not verbatim quotes** — see §10 on why the verbatim body was unreachable.

1. **Gross margin non-answer — the most notable flag on the call.** MacWilliams (Wells Fargo) asked directly about gross margins and cloud mix impact. Welihinda replied that subscription gross margins remain above 80% and that long-term improvement is expected from serverless scaling, with **"no specific trends emerging this quarter."** FactSet's GAAP total-company gross margin shows **74.50% vs 76.70% a year ago, -220bps**. The two are different bases and not strictly contradictory, but an analyst asking about margin trend received "no trend" in a quarter where the measurable trend was down. Worth pressing at Q2.

2. **Fed vertical asymmetry.** Jump (Truist) asked about the federal vertical; Welihinda said the public sector is important but there are **no specific headwinds or tailwinds to call out.** This sits oddly against prepared remarks that promoted FedRAMP High authorization, CISA SIEM-as-a-Service, and a large U.S. public sector agency win as evidence of momentum. Promoted in the script, flattened under questioning.

3. **Security revenue contribution withheld.** Essex (JPMorgan) asked what Security contributes to revenue. Welihinda declined — specific percentages not disclosed — while simultaneously characterizing Security as a strong growth vector, and prepared remarks called it the highest-growth solution area in Q1. Non-disclosure of the segment being promoted as fastest-growing is the same disclosure gap that prevents a clean Confirm in §4.

4. **NER decline given a forward promise.** Calitri (Needham) probed cohort expansion trends and the NER impact. Welihinda attributed the 112% → 111% slip to trailing-twelve-month and constant-currency mechanics and said it will take time to catch up, with management elsewhere committing to improvement **within four quarters.** A metric decline explained by measurement lag plus a dated forward commitment is a checkable claim — hold management to the four-quarter window.

5. **Guidance sandbag framing.** Ma (Guggenheim) asked whether the raised guidance factors in cross-sell acceleration. Welihinda: guidance **does not assume a change in customer behavior beyond current trends.** Read as deliberate conservatism — it sets up beats if the multi-product motion Kulkarni described actually accelerates, and it is the tell that management is protecting the second-half acceleration they have now publicly guided to.
