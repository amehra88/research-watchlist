First observation, so there is no prior-quarter read to trajectory against. ## 3. Forward guidance

3Q26 (8-K Ex. 99, verbatim): "management expects core sales to grow approximately 16% year over year to a range of $4.9 billion to $5 billion and core EPS to grow approximately 28% year over year to a range of $0.85 to $0.89." Against the Store B 3Q25 actuals (core sales $4,272M, core EPS $0.67), the midpoints imply +15.9% sales and roughly +30% EPS, so the stated percentages are consistent with the ranges. Guidance philosophy is unchanged: one quarter at a time, narrow range, core basis only (the 10-Q states management cannot provide a GAAP outlook because it does not forecast FX).

Longer-dated framing (call summary and 8-K, paraphrase-fidelity except where quoted): the upgraded Springboard Plan targets an annualized sales run rate of $20B by end-2026, $30B by end-2028, $40B by end-2030, and "a sales CAGR of 19% from Q4 2026 to Q4 2030" (Weeks, 8-K Ex. 99) with earnings growing faster than sales. Internal plan sits at a 70% confidence interval; the "high confidence" plan is $27B by end-2028 and $35B by end-2030. Management indicated the $20B run rate could arrive a quarter early. Capex: $754M in 1H26, full-year 2026 "approximately $2.0 billion" (10-Q, Capital Expenditures), with the investment rate stepping up in 3Q/4Q to support optical capacity. Solar: sales and profitability expected to improve in 3Q as production normalizes after the extended maintenance shutdown and equipment upgrade at the solar wafer facility (8-K Ex. 99). Photonics: reiterated $10B revenue stream by 2030; management declined to give a near-term (2027) figure and acknowledged timing uncertainty on optical scale-up adoption (call summary).

Tone: confident, with the notable choice not to raise the 4Q run-rate framing despite tracking ahead (Merchant exchange, Section 8).

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** NVIDIA–Corning long-term partnership under which Corning "will expand its U.S.-based optical connectivity manufacturing capacity by 10x and expand its U.S. fiber production capacity by more than 50% to meet the accelerating demand driven by AI factory buildouts" (8-K Ex. 99, news summary). Amazon "multiyear, multibillion-dollar agreement" for fiber, cable and connectivity across U.S. data centers (8-K Ex. 99). Optical segment NPAT margin at a record 21% (call summary, prepared remarks). Weeks, on Notter's pricing question, said profitability is driven by innovation rather than price increases, and that customers are prioritizing Corning for new projects and high-density product sets with no sell-through constraints (Q&A, paraphrase).
- **Implication:** Within the AI-datacenter passive-optics layer, GLW is consolidating a supplier-of-record position with the largest buyers rather than competing on price; the competitive question shifts from share to capacity delivery and execution on the 10x connectivity build.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Enterprise sales $1.27B, +65% y/y; AI-data-center-related sales nearly doubled; orders described as "accelerating" (call summary, prepared remarks). 10-Q MD&A: Optical Communications growth "primarily due to continued growth in our Enterprise business driven by strong demand for our Generative AI products." GLW's own capex rising to ~$2.0B for 2026 (10-Q) and the Springboard Plan upgraded at the May 2026 investor day (8-K Ex. 99).
- **Implication:** GLW is a direct beneficiary of the hyperscaler capex wave and is now spending into it; the thesis holds, with the incremental risk moving from demand to GLW's own capital intensity (capex up $238M y/y in 1H26 per 10-Q, funded by a $1.0B customer deposit and $500M pre-funded warrant proceeds).

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Management cited larger cluster sizes and bandwidth increases as drivers that could let enterprise sales outpace GPU growth, with fiber content per GPU rising "1.3x–1.5x by 2028" (call summary, prepared remarks, paraphrase). On photonics/optical scale-up, management maintained the $10B-by-2030 target but declined near-term revenue guidance and acknowledged timing uncertainty on adoption, saying industry chatter about delays does not reflect its internal visibility (Spector and Hosseini exchanges, paraphrase).
- **Implication:** The scale-out fiber-content thesis is intact and quantified; the scale-up (co-packaged/photonics) leg is the part with timing drift — management is protecting the 2030 number while refusing to put a 2027 marker on it, so the topology payoff is real but later and lumpier than the fiber-content payoff.

### hyperscaler_revenue_concentration

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Named long-term commercial agreements with Meta, NVIDIA and Amazon (call summary; Amazon and NVIDIA also in 8-K Ex. 99). "Receipt of an upfront customer deposit of $1.0 billion, related to a long-term supply agreement" in 1H26 operating cash flow (10-Q, Cash Flows). Weeks: long-term agreements will become the "lion's share" of the optics business and are the basis for capacity expansion and risk-sharing (Notter exchange, paraphrase). Counterparty to the $1.0B deposit and the $500M pre-funded warrant is not named in the MD&A sections read.
- **Implication:** Concentration is increasing, not decreasing, but its mechanism is changing — from spot exposure to contracted, prepaid, risk-shared exposure. That lowers cycle risk while raising counterparty and renegotiation risk; the theme needs to be read as "contracted concentration" going forward.

### solar_supply_chain

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Solar sales $438M, +90% y/y, driven by polysilicon, wafer and module sales (10-Q); segment net loss of $7M "primarily driven by temporarily higher costs to ramp up capacity" (10-Q, Segment Analysis) and an "extended maintenance shutdown and equipment upgrade at Corning's solar wafer facility," with profitability expected to improve in 3Q (8-K Ex. 99). Management reiterated the $3B solar revenue target with above-corporate-average profitability, cited supportive U.S. trade and tax policy, and — in the Hosseini exchange — acknowledged increasing allocation of polysilicon to higher-margin electronic-grade (semiconductor) use (call summary, paraphrase).
- **Implication:** Volume thesis is ahead of plan; margin thesis is unproven and now carries a management-set 3Q26 test. The electronic-grade allocation comment is a mix-shift lever that was not in the original solar framing.
