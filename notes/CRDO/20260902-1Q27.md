## 3. Forward guidance

- **Q2 FY27 revenue:** $525M–$535M. Against Q1's $479M that is **+9.6% to +11.7% sequential** — materially above the "mid-single-digit sequential growth" cadence guided at the Q3 FY26 call. The sequential run-rate itself has accelerated, not just the annual total.
- **Q2 FY27 non-GAAP gross margin:** 67%–69% (Q1 landed at the 68% midpoint).
- **FY27 revenue growth:** **>85% Y/Y** — raised from >80% at the Q4 FY26 call and >50% at the Q3 FY26 call. On FY26's $1.3B base this implies **>$2.4B**.
- **FY27 optical revenue:** **>$600M**, with ZeroFlap Optics, Silicon Photonics PICs, and Optical DSPs **each contributing more than $100M**. Reiterated unchanged from the Q4 FY26 call — the composition held while the total was raised, meaning the FY27 raise came from AEC/retimers plus optical upside beyond the floor, not from re-cutting the optical story.
- **FY27 non-GAAP gross margin:** consistent with FY26 levels (FY26 was 68.1%).
- **FY27 non-GAAP opex:** **+~55% Y/Y**, raised from ~50% at the Q4 FY26 call. Management frames this as well below the revenue growth rate, preserving operating leverage.
- **FY27 net margin:** expected to stay near 50% for the year.
- **FY28 revenue markers (not guidance):** ALC and OmniConnect revenue expected to begin in FY28; 1.6T AECs contribute in 2H FY27 and "meaningfully in 2028"; Brennan referenced multi-billion-dollar TAM opportunities for FY28.

**Guidance philosophy.** Unchanged in structure — explicit ranges, quarterly revenue plus GM, annual growth-rate framing. But the *conservatism* has drained out of the margin guide: at Q3 FY26 the company guided Q4 GM down to 64–66% and then delivered 68.3%, a classic sandbag. This quarter it guided to 67–69% and delivered exactly the 68% midpoint. The company is now guiding margin to where it actually lands. Separately, the raise cadence on FY27 (>50% → >80% → >85%) shows management repeatedly setting a floor it then lifts — a beat-and-raise pattern on the top line that persists even as margin framing normalizes. The tariff caveat that qualified the Q3 FY26 guide did not recur; supply-chain conditions are now the named uncertainty and are described as "dynamic"/"fluid" without quantification.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

_Themes taken from the `tier_3_watchlist` entry (line ~1485), which carries the full theme list. The duplicate `tier_2_active_candidates` entry (line ~1309) has `themes: []` and was **not** used — see section 10._

### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** **Drift**
- **Evidence:** The win-mechanism rotated. At Q3 FY26, management framed AEC as "the de facto standard for intra-rack and rack-to-rack connectivity up to 7 meters" and positioned CPO/NPO as **deferred** until they could match AEC/ZeroFlap reliability. This call: Credo is a participant in the **Open CPX Consortium**, is explicitly "pursuing Near Package Optics," and Brennan described an **"agnostic approach to market demands"** — fielding NPO questions from Tore Svanberg (Stifel; "Optical Position, NPO, System-Level NPO") and N. Bolton (Needham; "Open CPX Consortium, PIC-based Solutions, NPO Scale-up Segment"), where he framed the focus as silicon photonics PIC and complete optical engines rather than a copper-versus-optics contest. Simultaneously, management guided AEC growth **down in rate**: AECs "more than doubled from 2024 to 2025 and more than tripled from 2025 to 2026, but management signals that the current year's growth rate will be lower as optics becomes a larger part of the mix," with 1.6T AEC contribution pushed to 2H FY27 and "meaningfully in 2028."
- **Implication:** This directly answers the watch item the prior note flagged ("the live risk to monitor is the optical/CPO substitution narrative"). The answer is favorable but it is a genuine change of mechanism, not a confirmation: Credo has stopped defending copper's territory and repositioned as architecture-agnostic — it now intends to supply whichever medium wins, via silicon photonics PICs and optical engines. That is arguably a **stronger** position than the Q3 framing and it de-risks the substitution thesis. But the thesis as written (copper wins on reliability/power/TCO) is no longer the operative claim, and the company is now participating in an architecture consortium it does not control rather than setting the standard unilaterally. Drift, favorable in direction.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** **Confirm**
- **Evidence:** Revenue +115% Y/Y, seventh consecutive quarter of triple-digit Y/Y growth. FY27 growth guide raised for the third consecutive quarter to >85% (>$2.4B). Optical DSP and retimer businesses each set record revenue in Q1. Inventory built to $313.1M (+$62.2M Q/Q) with working-capital investment explicitly "to support anticipated volume ramps in the second half of 2027 and beyond" — the company is spending cash ahead of demand it believes is contracted. Opex guided +55% Y/Y on R&D.
- **Implication:** Unambiguous confirmation, and the raise cadence matters more than the level: three consecutive upward revisions to the same fiscal year mean demand is outrunning the company's own repeated forecasts. The inventory and opex build are the credibility test — CRDO is committing balance sheet and P&L to a 2H FY27 ramp, so a hyperscaler capex digestion would now hit an over-built cost base rather than a lean one. Confirm with rising operational leverage in both directions.

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** **Confirm**
- **Evidence:** The portfolio now spans "connectivity from millimeters to kilometers" — retimers (Screaming Eagle, Blue Heron) for **scale-up** architectures, AECs and ALCs for intra/inter-rack, optical DSPs, silicon photonics PICs and ZeroFlap transceivers for **scale-out**, and OmniConnect gearboxes for memory fanout. Management explicitly positioned Open CPX participation as addressing "the scale-up market, which is critical for achieving higher density in network architecture." OmniConnect was tied specifically to **AI inference** workloads and their memory bandwidth/capacity constraints (Manmohanpreet Singh, JPMorgan). Retimer revenue set a Q1 record on multi-protocol scale-up deployments.
- **Implication:** Confirms and extends the prior read. CRDO is no longer positioned only on the scale-out/intra-rack axis — the retimer record and the explicit scale-up framing mean it now has *revenue* (not just roadmap) in scale-up, which was the FY28-optionality story last quarter. The inference/memory angle via OmniConnect is a distinct second-order topology bet still slated for FY28 revenue. The structural-connectivity-layer thesis is intact and better evidenced than at Q3.

### hyperscaler_revenue_concentration

- **Status:** **Drift** (favorable in direction, but the improvement **stalled** this quarter)
- **Evidence:** Q1 FY27 top-four customers: **33%, 28%, 13%, 10% = 84%** (disclosed by CFO Fleming in response to Sean O'Loughlin, Cowen). The three-quarter trend, which only resolves because the skipped Q4 call was read: top-1 **39% → 34% → 33%**; top-2 **71% → 61% → 61%**; top-4 **88% → 87% → 84%**. Management maintains "deep relationships with five hyperscalers" and is expanding into neoclouds, which it projected at the Q4 call could reach **~20% of revenue** in coming years. Management continues to expect three to four customers above 10% each quarter.
- **Implication:** The concentration is genuinely improving but **almost all of that improvement happened in the skipped Q4 quarter — top-2 was flat Q/Q at 61%** and the top-four decline from 87% to 84% is modest. Critically, management framed a meaningful part of the diversification as **product** spread inside existing accounts ("more than AECs now being adopted," direct hyperscaler component sales increasing), not new logos. That reduces *design-loss* risk — losing one socket no longer loses the account — but it does **not** reduce *customer-loss* risk, which is the structural exposure the theme tracks. With 61% of revenue in two accounts, a single hyperscaler's capex digestion or re-architecture remains the dominant swing factor. Drift, not Confirm: the direction is right, the pace has stalled, and the mechanism of improvement is partly the weaker of the two available kinds. The falsifiable marker to track next print is whether neocloud revenue moves toward the projected 20%.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: **[not populated — schema gap]**)
  - Evidence: In roughly 18 months CRDO went from an AEC-centric portfolio to four scaled product pillars — AECs, optical DSPs, silicon photonics PICs, and ZeroFlap transceivers — with three of them guided above $100M each in FY27. DustPhotonics was acquired and contributed revenue within the same quarter it closed. Retimers set record revenue on a multi-protocol scale-up architecture. PILOT software adds a real-time link-level telemetry layer with disclosed diagnostic capability (latent defect detection, ESD damage, fiber contamination) that management ties to cluster bring-up time. ALC and OmniConnect/Weaver are staged for FY28. Opex guided +55% Y/Y on R&D, and Q1 opex ran above the high end of guidance on R&D — the company is over-spending its own plan to sustain cadence.
  - Recommendation: **establish at 4+** (currently 4, trending toward 5). The rubric defines this dimension as the *rate* of product/tech innovation, and on that definition CRDO is top-decile: three new product lines reaching $100M+ scale inside a single fiscal year, plus an acquisition monetized in one quarter. Held below 5 on calibration grounds, not evidence: the rubric names **NVDA 5/5-5-5/4 as "the ceiling,"** and a connectivity supplier should not sit level with NVDA on innovation rate. The closest comp argues the same way — **CBRS, a frontier AI silicon designer, carries a 4** on this dimension, and CRDO belongs above it but not two steps above. The one genuinely reactive element also bears here: Credo joined the Open CPX Consortium rather than defining the architecture, which is a *leadership* qualification more than a rate qualification but is real. The `+` marks the trajectory, which is unambiguously upward.
- **Distribution** (current: **[not populated — schema gap]**)
  - Evidence: Deep relationships with five hyperscalers, expanding neocloud engagement (projected toward ~20% of revenue), and increasing direct component sales to hyperscalers which management says improves stickiness and forward visibility. Against that: top-four customers are 84% of revenue and the top two are 61%, flat Q/Q. Diversification is occurring substantially *within* existing accounts via product breadth rather than through new customers.
  - Recommendation: **establish at 4**. Premium, entrenched placement at the accounts that matter and a genuine second channel opening in neoclouds — comfortably above CBRS's 3 ("young distribution") since these are shipping, scaled, multi-product relationships. Held at 4 rather than 5 because reach is deep but narrow, and because the concentration improvement stalled this quarter. No `+` trajectory marker: the Q/Q evidence does not support one yet.
- **Overall** (current: **[not populated — schema gap]**)
  - Recommendation: **establish at 4+** (currently 4, trending toward 5)
  - Reasoning: Not a mechanical average of 5 and 4. For CRDO's specific model the two dimensions are **tightly coupled, and that coupling cuts both ways.** The vertical integration that drives the innovation rate — owning SerDes, DSP, PIC, module and telemetry software — is precisely the thing that converts a narrow customer base into expanding wallet share: Credo sells progressively more of the rack into the same five accounts, which raises switching costs and makes the concentration less fragile than the raw 84% implies. That is the case for 5. The case against is the same coupling read in reverse: because the innovation payoff and the concentration risk are loaded onto **the same handful of customers**, the two dimensions are correlated rather than diversifying. A single hyperscaler re-architecting its network would simultaneously impair the distribution base *and* strand the R&D that was built for it — the company does not have an independent second market to absorb that shock. That correlated downside, not any weakness in either dimension on its own, is what caps the overall below its stronger component. The `+` marker is warranted because the trigger to resolve it is explicit and trackable: neocloud revenue progressing toward the ~20% management has projected would decorrelate the two dimensions and support a 5.
  - Suggested `notes` text for the operator: _"Overall (4+) sits below innovation_rate (5) deliberately. Vertical integration makes innovation and distribution mutually reinforcing on the upside but correlated on the downside — the same 2-5 hyperscalers carry both. Resolve to 5 if neoclouds reach the projected ~20% of revenue."_
