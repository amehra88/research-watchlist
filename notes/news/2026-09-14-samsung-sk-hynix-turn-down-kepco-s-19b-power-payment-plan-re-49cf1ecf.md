## Samsung and SK Hynix Reject KEPCO's Proposal to Prepay Multiple Years of Electricity Bills

An unresolved power infrastructure funding standoff in Korea's memory hub creates a medium-term bottleneck for Samsung and SK Hynix capacity expansion; to the extent grid constraints slow Korean DRAM/HBM ramp, it is an indirect supply-discipline tailwind for Micron and reinforces the capacity-tightness thesis.

- Samsung and SK Hynix have rejected Korean state utility KEPCO's proposal requiring them to prepay several years of electricity costs upfront.
- Headlines consistently cite 25 trillion Korean won as the KRW figure; USD equivalents vary across outlets (headlines range from roughly $17B to $23B) — the KRW denomination should be treated as authoritative, USD conversions are inconsistent.
- The chipmakers cited cash flow concerns as the basis for rejection.
- KEPCO's proposal appears tied to funding new power grid infrastructure needed to support Korea's accelerating chip-fab expansion.
- The rejection exposes a structural tension between Korea's grid investment needs and the chipmakers' capital allocation discipline.
