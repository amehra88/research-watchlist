## Oracle renews four-year-old GPUs at 20% premium as HBM demand broadens to major tech buyers

A 20% premium on four-year-old hardware is hard evidence of compute scarcity, reinforcing NVDA's pricing power and the bull case for elevated HBM ASPs at SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron; this strengthens the thesis that the AI infrastructure supply cycle has not peaked.

- Oracle is renewing contracts on four-year-old GPUs at a 20% price premium, indicating willingness to pay up rather than wait for newer supply.
- HBM demand is characterized as expanding to 'big tech,' broadening the addressable customer set for memory suppliers beyond early adopters.
- Paying a premium on older-vintage hardware signals that scarcity is acute enough to sustain value in aging equipment.
- The combination of premium pricing and demand breadth supports a durable supply-demand imbalance narrative for NVDA and HBM producers.
