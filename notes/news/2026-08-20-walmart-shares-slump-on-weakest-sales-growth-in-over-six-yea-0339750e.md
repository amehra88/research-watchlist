## Walmart Reports Slowest US Comparable Sales Growth Since 2020, Shares Fall Sharply Despite Earnings Beat

Walmart is the highest-frequency real-time read on US consumer health — the slowest comp growth since the pandemic, with gas prices named as a headwind, points to softening among lower-to-middle income consumers. For a tech PM, the key second-order question is whether this softness spills into discretionary tech spending or remains isolated to WMT's customer demographic.

- Walmart's US comparable sales growth was the slowest in over six years — since approximately 2020 — missing sales expectations despite delivering an earnings beat.
- Shares fell materially on the print; outlets cite figures ranging from approximately 8% to 9%, with direction unanimous across 12 outlets.
- The company raised guidance, but the market prioritized the top-line deceleration over the beat-and-raise dynamic.
- Gas prices are cited in at least one headline as a direct drag on consumer spending, pointing to an external macro headwind.
- Sector read-across appears differentiated: Target is described as 'holding steady' and Costco as 'easing' rather than declining sharply.
