### 2a. Fresh-start accounting — why there is no YoY column

Wolfspeed emerged from a prepackaged Chapter 11 and adopted **fresh-start accounting on September 29, 2025** (the "Effective Date"). Per the 8-K: assets and liabilities "were recorded at their estimated fair values which, in some cases, are significantly different than amounts included in our financial statements prior to the Effective Date. Accordingly, our condensed consolidated financial statements after the Effective Date are not comparable with our condensed consolidated financial statements on or before that date."

Q4 FY26 is **Successor**; Q4 FY25 is **Predecessor**. Two specific distortions make a printed YoY delta actively misleading:

- **Depreciation collapsed by construction.** PP&E was written down from **$3,916.5M to $691.4M**, and quarterly D&A fell from **$60.4M to $31.7M**. Fresh-start therefore *flatters* every margin and EBITDA line below the revenue line.
- **The share count is a different instrument.** Diluted weighted-average shares went from **155,631k to 51,799k**. Non-GAAP net loss was nearly flat in dollars — **($116.9M) vs ($119.8M)** — while non-GAAP diluted LPS moved from **($0.77) to ($2.26)**. That ~3x deterioration in per-share loss is **entirely the recapitalized share count, not operating deterioration.** Any model or screen reading that LPS line as a blowup is wrong.

The analytically sharp point cuts the other way, though: **GAAP gross margin still deteriorated from (13)% to (25)% despite the lower depreciation base.** A ~$29M/quarter reduction in D&A should have been a tailwind to cost of revenue and margin went materially more negative anyway. The confound to concede: revenue also fell **24%** ($197.0M → $149.6M) over the same span, so lost fixed-cost absorption does much of the work here. This is not a clean like-for-like — but it does establish that fresh-start relief has not offset the volume problem.

**Revenue is the one line that survives the boundary.** Top-line is unaffected by fair-value remeasurement, so FY26 can be summed across periods: Predecessor $196.8M + Successor $468.3M = **~$665.1M for FY26 vs $757.6M in FY25, about -12%**. Derived quarterly path for FY26: **$196.8M → ~$168.5M (derived) → $150.2M → $149.6M** — decline, then flat at ~$150M for two quarters, with a third flat quarter guided. (The ~$168.5M Q2 figure is derived by subtracting reported Q3 and Q4 from the Successor nine-month total; the Successor period boundary is offset by a day from the fiscal quarter, so treat it as approximate.)

Segment detail, same non-comparability caveat: **Power Products $106.3M** (vs $118.6M PY, +6% sequentially per the call) and **Materials Products $43.3M** (vs $78.4M PY, **-45%**). The materials business is where the damage is concentrated.

## 3. Forward guidance

Q1 FY27 (September quarter) guide, per the 8-K Business Outlook:

- **Revenue: $140M–$160M** — a $150M midpoint, i.e. **flat again** and a third consecutive ~$150M quarter.
- **Non-GAAP gross margin: "expected to remain negative."** No number given, no timeframe for crossing zero.
- **Non-GAAP operating expenses: $62M–$66M.**

Two observations the guide itself implies. First, **Q4 non-GAAP opex was $62.3M** (derived: non-GAAP operating loss of $92.1M less non-GAAP gross loss of $29.8M), so opex is guided **flat to up ~6%** on flat revenue — the implied non-GAAP operating loss for Q1 FY27 is roughly $90–95M, essentially unchanged from Q4's $92.1M. **No sequential improvement is being guided.** Second, the distance to the stated goal is not closing: management put breakeven gross margin at an **~$800M annual run rate** (AI summary of the call), versus a current ~$600M run rate. Flat revenue guidance means the ~34% volume gap to breakeven is unchanged quarter over quarter.

**Guidance philosophy:** a revenue range plus an opex range, with gross margin given only as a direction ("remain negative") rather than a number, and — notably — **no full-year FY27 frame of any kind**, no gross-margin-neutrality date, and no revenue trajectory beyond the single quarter. For a company whose entire equity story rests on reaching a volume threshold, declining to frame the year is itself the signal. The company also explicitly declines to reconcile forward non-GAAP opex to GAAP, citing unpredictable share-based comp, transformation and restructuring charges.

Tagging it `semiconductor_cycle` risks attributing idiosyncratic distress to an industry cycle that most peers are not experiencing. Recommend omitting unless the PM wants WOLF in cycle-wide screens.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: none — no scoring block)

  - **Proposed initial value: `4-`** (currently 4, trending toward 3). The trajectory marker is doing deliberate work: current output is genuinely 4-caliber, but a 26% R&D cut is not consistent with sustaining it.
- **Distribution** (current: none — no scoring block)
  - Evidence: revenue declining in **both** product lines — Materials -45% YoY, Power down YoY — with the Materials decline reflecting customers migrating away from 150mm faster than WOLF is capturing them at 200mm. Automotive demand "difficult to predict" with "limited near-term visibility." Against that: new onboard-charging design wins including a European Tier 1, the LITEON collaboration, a newly created Silicon Valley data center sales team, and stated engagement with merchant optical/RF players. Customer concentration and the post-bankruptcy counterparty question (the 8-K's own risk factors flag the ability "to retain customers, and third parties willing to do business with Wolfspeed on acceptable terms or at all") remain live.
  - **Proposed initial value: `2+`** — weak in current fact, with a specific and newly funded improvement program attached.
- **Overall** (current: none — no scoring block)
  - **Proposed initial value: `2+`**
  - Reasoning: **deliberately below the arithmetic midpoint of `4-` and `2+`, and this is the whole point of the field.** For a vertically integrated, capital-intensive manufacturer with this cost structure, innovation and distribution do not average — they multiply, and distribution is the binding constraint. Technology leadership only becomes competitive advantage when it is absorbed across a fixed asset base; at ~$150M of quarterly revenue against an ~$800M annual breakeven, every additional Gen5 or 10kV product is a fixed cost carried at a **(20)% gross margin** rather than a moat. Wolfspeed is currently the technology leader in a market where it cannot convert leadership into economics. The CFO's defense of vertical integration against a breakup question (see §8) is the coherent counter-argument — that materials-to-device integration is what produces the Gen5 performance advantage — and it is why the `+` is there rather than a flat `2`: if volume arrives, the same integration that is a fixed-cost anchor today becomes the advantage. The score should move on **utilization**, not on product announcements.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

Note: InsiderScore scored this call `challenging_analyze_exchanges: 0` and overall sentiment Neutral. I disagree that nothing was flag-worthy — the exchanges below were not adversarial in tone, but they were substantively pointed.

1. **The breakup question, dismissed on principle rather than on economics.** Jed Dorsheimer (William Blair) asked directly whether the business could be **separated into Materials and Power segments**. CFO van Issum **dismissed the idea**, emphasizing the benefits of the vertically integrated model and citing Gen5 product performance as evidence. Worth flagging plainly: a company running (20)% non-GAAP gross margins, with one of its two segments down 45% YoY, declined a structural-separation question by appealing to engineering synergy rather than by addressing the capital-allocation case. This is the single most important exchange on the call for anyone sizing the equity.
2. **Breakeven quantified — and the quantification is unflattering.** Joshua Buchalter (Cowen) pressed on the gross margin trajectory. Van Issum answered that gross margin neutrality depends on volume and asset utilization, and put breakeven at an **~$800M annual run rate**. Credit for giving a hard number; the number implies roughly **+34% revenue** from a base the company just guided flat. Management did not pair it with a date or a path.
3. **"Is June the low point?" — not answered.** Christopher Rolland (Susquehanna) asked whether the June quarter marked the **trough** for automotive and industrial. CEO Feurle responded with diversification progress and design-win traction but explicitly noted demand unpredictability from customer product-mix changes. A trough question met with a diversification answer is a dodge, and the flat Q1 guide is consistent with management not being willing to call it.
4. **The "meaningful" question, asked twice, never dimensioned.** Both Rolland and Buchalter pushed for a **timeframe or magnitude** on when data center revenue becomes material. Management responded with momentum language, ongoing product qualifications and ecosystem engagement — no dollar figure, no percentage of revenue, no date. Given that this is the entire growth narrative, the persistent refusal to size it is the most telling omission on the call.
5. **Unusual emphasis: debt cost over operations.** The CFO's prepared quote in the press release leads with "aggressively targeting initiatives to further reduce our debt and cost of capital," and the $46M conversion was volunteered. Management's own framing of the story is currently financial, not operational — which is informative about where they believe the near-term value inflection sits.
