# AMZN — 2Q26 earnings read

_Prepared by earnings-reviewer, 2026-09-13. Tier: T1._

## 1. Headline read

First observation for AMZN (no prior earnings notes in `notes/AMZN/`). The print confirms the thesis across all nine themes and all six open assumptions: AWS grew 37% to $42.2B (fifth straight quarter of acceleration, fastest in 18 quarters) on a $496B backlog, advertising grew 26%, and the two leading AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI) committed to Trainium at multi-gigawatt scale. The counterweight is cost: 2026 capex guide raised to ~$220B (from ~$200B) on memory inflation, TTM free cash flow now negative (-$7.6B), and long-term debt roughly doubled since year-end — the thesis holds, but the funding profile has shifted.

## 3. Forward guidance

Q3 2026 (8-K Ex-99.1 "Financial Guidance", verbatim ranges): net sales $197.0B–$202.0B, +9% to +12% y/y, including ~80 bps FX headwind; "Excluding the impact of Prime Day in both 2025 and 2026, third quarter 2026 year-over-year growth would be nearly 400 basis points higher" (Prime Day fell in Q2 this year versus Q3 last year). Operating income $22.5B–$26.5B vs $17.4B in 3Q25 (midpoint +41% y/y). Guidance assumes no energy-derivative remeasurement impact.

Full-year capex: raised to approximately $220B from the prior ~$200B, "primarily due to the higher cost of memory" (transcript summary, prepared remarks). Management said even this will not meet all 2026 demand, with similar constraints expected in 2027 and significant demand already visible for 2028; AWS power capacity is on track to double by end-2027 versus 2025 (transcript summary).

Philosophy unchanged: range guidance for sales and OI, no EPS or capex range — capex given as a point approximation. Tone is more ambitious than prior quarters on AWS (Jassy floated a "$1 trillion annual revenue business" long-term framing, transcript summary) while the sales guide itself remains conservative in the usual pattern (see Section 8).

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### hyperscaler_capex_buildout

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** 2026 capex guide raised to ~$220B from ~$200B; Q2 cash capex $53.1B (Ex-99.1 cash flow: purchases $54,208M less $1,132M proceeds), TTM purchases up $66.1B y/y "primarily reflects investments in artificial intelligence" (Ex-99.1). Management: capacity-constrained through 2027, power capacity doubling by end-2027 (transcript summary, prepared remarks and Post/BofA Q&A).
- **Implication:** Buildout is demand-led and still supply-constrained; the incremental $20B is cost inflation (memory), not incremental volume, which matters for supplier-side reads — dollars up, units not necessarily up.

### silicon_architecture_competition

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Chips business (Trainium + Graviton) exceeded a $25B annual run rate growing triple digits; "the two leading AI labs in the world, Anthropic and OpenAI, making multi-year, multi-gigawatt commitments" to Trainium; Uber and Pinterest cited; Graviton5 GA, Graviton used by 98% of top-1,000 EC2 customers, revenue commitments up ~3x q/q (all Ex-99.1 highlights). Jassy said Amazon is in discussions about selling Trainium separately to third-party data centers while maintaining the NVIDIA partnership (transcript summary, Nowak/MS Q&A).
- **Implication:** OpenAI adopting Trainium is the strongest external validation yet of custom silicon as a credible alternative to merchant GPUs; a merchant-Trainium channel would make AMZN a direct silicon competitor rather than only a captive one.

### ai_agent_monetization

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Bedrock: "hundreds of thousands of customers," more added in the last six months than the first two years post-launch, "customers spent more in Q2 than all prior quarters combined"; AgentCore added Payments, Web Search, Harness; Kiro usage tripled q/q; Quick added autonomous background agents and 16 integrations; $1B AWS Forward Deployed Engineering team (all Ex-99.1). AWS AI run rate >$25B, triple-digit growth.
- **Implication:** Monetization mechanism is consumption (inference, agent runtime) plus first-party agent apps, not seat licensing; the Sebastian/Baird exchange confirms AMZN is deliberately moving up-stack into the application layer.

### ad_market_strength

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Advertising services $19,809M, +26% y/y (accelerating from 22% in each of the prior four quarters, Ex-99.1 supplemental table); Prime Video ads and live sports sold out (transcript summary); Ads Agent expanded to 11 new countries with 8% lower CPM and 6% lower CPA for users (Ex-99.1).
- **Implication:** Ads now ~9.9% of net sales and growing faster than the company; the acceleration despite a tough comp argues for pricing and inventory strength rather than a one-off.

### space_economy

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Amazon Leo completed four additional launches, "bringing the total constellation to nearly 400 satellites in orbit — enough to begin initial satellite internet service this year" (Ex-99.1). No financial disclosure.
- **Implication:** Program is moving from capex to initial service; still immaterial financially and not a driver of the print — low-weight confirmation of timeline, not economics.

### vertical_ai_applications

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** AWS Secret Cloud for Industry GA (Northrop Grumman first classified workloads, up to $1B in credits for U.S. Intelligence Community migration); Forward Deployed Engineering early customers Allen Institute, Cox Automotive, NBA, NFL, Ricoh, Southwest; Amazon Pharmacy new customers >2x and same-day prescriptions ~5x (Ex-99.1). Connect and Continuum expanding (transcript summary).
- **Implication:** AMZN's vertical play is embedded-engineering plus sovereign/regulated cloud rather than packaged vertical apps; that is a distribution-led version of the theme, consistent with the competitive-advantage framing.

### agentic_commerce

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Rufus and Alexa+ merged into "Alexa for Shopping" with Price Alerts and Auto-Buy; active users close to doubling, interactions up >5x y/y; Alexa-for-Shopping users spend >40% more per order; customers engaging sponsored prompts are 48% more likely to convert with 21% higher spend (Ex-99.1 and transcript summary). AgentCore Payments lets third-party agents execute transactions on AWS.
- **Implication:** AMZN is building both sides — the consumer shopping agent and the payments rail for other agents — which protects the marketplace from disintermediation by external agents.

### local_commerce_delivery

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Amazon Now (30-minute delivery) added 80 U.S. cities and towns plus Egyptian cities, now 9 countries and 250+ cities, gross sales and units up >80% q/q; over 40% more items delivered same-day or overnight in H1; monthly active perishables customers up 50% since January (Ex-99.1, transcript summary); Amazon Supply Chain Services launched with P&G, 3M, Lands' End, American Eagle as first customers.
- **Implication:** Speed and grocery are pulling frequency (paid units +17%); opening the network to third parties (Supply Chain Services) is a new monetization of the logistics moat.

### agent_framework_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Bedrock added 10+ models including OpenAI GPT-5.6, Anthropic Claude Opus 5, Google DeepMind Gemma 4, SpaceXAI Grok 4.3; AgentCore new primitives; Lambda MicroVMs with 8-hour stateful sessions for long-running agent loops; next-gen OpenSearch Serverless; AWS DevOps Agent Release Management (Ex-99.1).
- **Implication:** AWS is positioning as the model-agnostic agent runtime (hosting OpenAI alongside Anthropic); the framework battle is being fought on infrastructure primitives (state, payments, search), where AWS's incumbency is an advantage.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

Q&A is paraphrased from the InsiderScore AI summary (verbatim transcript body not exposed this run).

1. **Guide vs track record (Store B):** Q3 sales guide midpoint $199.5B sits 2.2% below consensus-at-guide of $203.9B. Recorded pattern: 27 guided periods, guide hit rate 0.963, sandbag index 0.0075, average beat vs guide midpoint 3.3%; 2Q26 landed 2.1% above its $196.5B midpoint. Applying the historical 3.3% beat to $199.5B implies ~$206B, above consensus. Conclusion: this guide fits the recorded pattern — a below-consensus midpoint is the norm, not a signal — and the ~400 bps Prime Day timing shift plus ~80 bps FX are mechanical, disclosed in the release. Not judged as sandbagging beyond the established (small) index. INC_GROSS rows in the context file are not used for credibility (GAAP actual vs non-GAAP consensus basis mismatch).
2. **Anmuth (JPMorgan) — frontier model necessity:** Asked whether Amazon needs its own frontier model. Jassy: one is in development, but success "doesn't hinge on having a single dominant model." Partial dodge — no timeline, and the Bedrock shelf argument does double duty as the answer.
3. **Nowak (Morgan Stanley) — merchant Trainium:** Asked about selling Trainium chips outside AWS. Jassy expressed optimism, "ongoing discussions." Unusual emphasis — first time management has floated a merchant-silicon channel on a call; watch for this to become a disclosed business.
4. **Sebastian (Baird) — funding the buildout:** Olsavsky named debt issuance as a funding option; the balance sheet already shows LT debt roughly doubled since December. Combined with negative TTM FCF, the funding mix is a new variable the release does not foreground.
5. **Gawrelski (Wells Fargo) — capex raise is inflation:** Jassy confirmed the $20B raise is memory cost, and that AWS contract pricing "accounts for cost inflation, ensuring stable returns." Watch item: if memory stays elevated, returns depend on repricing power at renewal, not on volume.
