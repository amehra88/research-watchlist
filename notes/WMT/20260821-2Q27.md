# WMT — 2Q27 earnings read

_Prepared by earnings-reviewer, 2026-08-21. Tier: T2._

## 1. Headline read

Q2 FY27 (quarter ended 2026-07-31, call 2026-08-20) beat on every line and the FY27 guide went up on sales, operating income and EPS — but **the beat is tariff-refund-inflated and the Q3 guide is the tell**. Adjusted operating income grew 17.4% cc, of which roughly 750 bps was tariff refunds; ex-refunds, underlying growth was at the high end of the 7-10% guided range (InsiderScore AI summary, Operating Income section). Against that, Q3 FY27 is guided to operating income cc **+2.0% to 4.0%** and adjusted EPS $0.62-0.64 versus a $0.62 base — call it 0-3% EPS growth — as the price investments ("11,000 rollbacks in the quarter, up from 7,200 in Q1", vendor summary) hit fully. The FY raise and the Q3 guide-down are the same fact viewed twice: refund timing pulled earnings into Q2, and the second half absorbs the price investment those refunds funded.

Versus the 1Q27 note's read (Confirm at headline, with a fuel-cost drag caveat), this is **Confirm on the monetisation theses, softer on the demand line, Drift on earnings quality**. The distinction matters and the Q/Q bridge in §2 makes it: the mix engine genuinely accelerated (advertising +37%→+38%, Marketplace ~+50%→+52%, membership +17%), but the demand line decelerated — Walmart U.S. comp fell from 4.1% to 2.6%, and **even ex-health-&-wellness at +3.4% it is still below last quarter's 4.1%**, so the MFP/pharmacy drag does not fully explain the slowdown. Global eCommerce decelerated from +26% to +23%. Consolidated gross margin expansion (+96 bps) is explicitly refund-driven, not operating leverage, and the FY27 capex guide stepped up from ~3.5% to ~4.0% of net sales. Net: refund-funded price investment is buying transactions into a decelerating comp — which is the coherent version of "beat, raise, and guide Q3 to zero EPS growth."

Theme-level: one strengthens (`ad_market_strength`), one confirms strongly (`local_commerce_delivery`), one drifts favourably on operational evidence (`ai_re_architected_incumbent`), and one — **`agentic_commerce` — is downgraded from the prior quarter's Confirm-with-caveat to Drift** (see §4).

**Read on tone and philosophy.** The 1Q27 note characterised WMT's posture as "beat-and-maintain" sandbagging — a full-year reiteration despite a Q1 beat. That posture broke this quarter: management raised all three metrics plus capex. But the raise is less confident than it looks. Sequenced, cc operating income runs +5.1% (Q1) → +17.4% (Q2) → +2.0-4.0% guided (Q3) against a FY guide of +7.0-8.5% — which means Q4 has to carry a meaningful load, and the Q2 spike is refund timing rather than run-rate. Per the vendor summary, management framed the average of Q2/Q3 at about 10% growth and expects to "largely contain" the refund-and-reinvestment cycle within the fiscal year. Management explicitly tempered the raise by citing **>$2B in incremental fuel costs** for the year, a **20 bps OI headwind from the Vibe acquisition**, and a softer consumer than when February guidance was set.

**The capex step-up deserves standalone attention.** Moving from ~3.5% to ~4.0% of net sales on a ~$735B revenue base is roughly $3.5-4B of incremental spend. It supports the automation and fulfillment build-out (see §6), but it sits in tension with management's continued claim of double-digit free cash flow growth (vendor summary, Operating Income section). That tension is unresolved on this call and is the single most trackable item into Q3.

**Forward-looking FY28 flags raised on this call** (vendor summary): a ~125 bps headwind to Walmart U.S. comp from health & wellness brand-to-generic transitions and deflation, and the GLP-1 benefit expected to roughly halve in 2027. Management also noted the challenge of lapping 19% EPS growth.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

Themes taken from the `tier_3_watchlist` WMT entry (line ~1620), which carries the authoritative theme list. The `tier_2_active_candidates` entry (line ~1345, the current tier) has `themes: []`, which is a data-entry gap rather than a statement that WMT has no themes — see §10.

### agentic_commerce

- **Status:** **Drift — a downgrade from 1Q27's formal "Confirm (with caveat)"**
- **Evidence:** The only product-level candidate is Sparky: "Walmart continues to integrate AI to enhance customer experience and operational efficiency, with tools like Sparky increasing customer engagement and order values" (InsiderScore AI summary, Technological Advancements — vendor paraphrase, not verbatim). The word "agentic" appears in the 8-K **only** in forward-looking risk boilerplate: "consumer acceptance of and response to our stores, clubs, eCommerce, digital, and agentic platforms" (Ex. 99.1, forward-looking statements). No OpenAI, Perplexity, or third-party agent-commerce integration was announced or discussed. The genuine progress is infrastructural: Marketplace +52%, Marketplace platform expansion into Mexico and Canada, Walmart+ launched in Canada.
- **Why this is a downgrade, not continuity:** 1Q27 carried a formal Status of "Confirm (with caveat)" — the caveat being that no agentic narrative was present but the underlying digital commerce infrastructure was strengthening, which was judged sufficient to hold Confirm. That caveat no longer carries it. A second consecutive quarter with zero product-level agent evidence, while the infrastructure argument stays exactly where it was, is not a Confirm; the infrastructure was always going to keep compounding and it cannot indefinitely substitute for the thesis's actual mechanism. Sparky's arrival is the reason for a downgrade rather than an upgrade: it reveals WMT's chosen answer to agentic commerce, and that answer is an owned-funnel assistant.
- **Implication:** Sparky is assistant-led shopping **inside Walmart's own surface** — it deepens WMT's owned funnel but is not evidence that WMT is building or winning third-party agent rails, which is what the aggressive form of the thesis requires. Marketplace at +52% and international platform replication continue to build the prerequisites (assortment breadth, structured product data, fulfillment speed). The thesis is intact but its mechanism is now unproven two quarters running: WMT is defending the discovery layer with its own assistant rather than positioning to be the fulfillment layer beneath someone else's agent. These are different businesses with different margin structures, and the market will price them differently. Track whether any third-party agent integration surfaces by Q3; a further quarter of silence would put this closer to Break than Drift.

### local_commerce_delivery

- **Status:** **Confirm (strong)** — the cleanest operational Confirm in the print
- **Evidence:** Expedited deliveries under 3 hours reached ~**37%** of store-fulfilled orders, up from ~36% in Q1 FY27; store-fulfilled delivery grew ~**43%**; "eCommerce sales increased 24%, with strength in store-fulfilled delivery, advertising, and marketplace" (8-K Ex. 99.1, Walmart U.S. segment). Per the vendor summary: fast delivery in the U.S. increased 48%, sub-30-minute delivery is now available in **38 U.S. markets**, over **50%** of U.S. eCommerce fulfillment volume is processed through automated facilities, and **3,100** U.S. stores use automated freight. Walmart U.S. eCommerce is now >23% of segment sales, roughly double five years ago; international eCommerce mix is ~30%.
- **Implication:** The store-as-fulfillment-node model is compounding, not plateauing. The 1Q27 note called ~36% expedited share "approaching DASH/UBER speed on a fundamentally different cost structure"; a quarter later that share ticked up while sub-30-minute coverage extended to 38 markets and automation penetration crossed 50% of eCommerce fulfillment volume. Critically, the vendor summary reports U.S. eCommerce achieved **double-digit incremental margins in the first half** — this is the first evidence in our coverage that the speed advantage is being delivered profitably rather than bought. Read-through to DASH (T2) remains mixed-to-negative: WMT is closing the speed gap on infrastructure it already owns. Read-through to AMZN: the non-coastal store footprint advantage is unchanged and now automation-levered.

### ai_re_architected_incumbent

- **Status:** **Drift — favourable**, upgraded in confidence from the 1Q27 "Drift (slightly weak)", but see the coverage caveat
- **Evidence:** Operational, not narrative. Over 50% of U.S. eCommerce fulfillment volume through automated facilities; 3,100 U.S. stores on automated freight; Sparky cited as lifting customer engagement and order values; "Investments in automation and fulfillment are improving customer experience and operational efficiency" (InsiderScore AI summary, Strategic Investments — vendor paraphrase). The hard corroborating datum from the filing is the **FY27 capex raise from ~3.5% to ~4.0% of net sales** (8-K Ex. 99.1 guidance table), and Rainey attributing OpEx growth partly to depreciation and supply-chain automation investment (vendor summary, Lejuez exchange).
- **Implication and coverage caveat:** The 1Q27 note graded this Drift-weak largely because press-release language was generic. **That specific input has not changed** — "artificial intelligence technologies" and "agentic platforms" appear in this 8-K only in forward-looking risk boilerplate, identical in placement to 1Q27. Every new AI/automation datum above comes from the InsiderScore AI summary and the financial presentation, neither of which was available for 1Q27. **The narrative improvement is therefore substantially a coverage artifact, and I am not treating it as a change in management disclosure.** What *is* genuinely new and filing-sourced is the capex step-up: a 50 bps-of-sales increase is management putting ~$3.5-4B behind the re-architecture, which is a harder signal than any language change. The thesis firms on capital commitment and automation penetration, not on rhetoric. It remains Drift rather than Confirm because the spend is being deployed into logistics and fulfillment efficiency — a cost-structure re-architecture — rather than into a re-architected customer interface, which is what the theme's stronger form implies.

### ad_market_strength

- **Status:** **Confirm (strong)**
- **Evidence:** "Global advertising business up 38%, with strength across segments. Walmart U.S. advertising up 38%" and "Strong advertising growth continued, up 38% overall, including 43% increase in Walmart Connect (ex-VIZIO)" (8-K Ex. 99.1). Consolidated gross profit rate improvement "reflect[s] favorable business mix led by global advertising"; Walmart U.S. gross profit "also reflects improved business mix primarily from growth of digital advertising." Per the vendor summary, advertising growth outpaces eCommerce growth, and the platform broadened via the **Vibe** acquisition (which carries a 20 bps consolidated OI headwind this year) alongside VIZIO.
- **Implication:** Second consecutive quarter of high-30s global ad growth (+37% in 1Q27, +38% in 2Q27), with the core retail-media product holding roughly flat ex-acquisition — Walmart Connect ex-VIZIO at +43%, marginally below 1Q27's +44%. That is a plateau at a very high rate, not an acceleration, and it should be described as such: the growth is coming from a broadening base (VIZIO, now Vibe, International/Flipkart) rather than from the core product re-accelerating. The important development this quarter is that advertising is now visibly showing up in the margin bridge: the company attributes gross-margin mix improvement to advertising in both the consolidated and Walmart U.S. commentary. That converts advertising from a growth talking point into a demonstrable margin mechanism, and it is the main reason the digital-profitability claim (double-digit incremental eCommerce margins) is credible. Read-throughs unchanged from 1Q27: constructive for TTD (T3) on inventory and CTV; validating for AMZN's retail-media playbook; structurally cautionary for META/GOOGL on ad-budget migration to retail media. The Vibe acquisition is worth tracking as a signal that WMT intends to buy rather than build further up the ad stack.

Tier-2 candidate; themes/scoring pending next earnings cycle." This is that cycle, so the proposals below are **initial**, not revisions.

It is held at 3 rather than 4 because the existential question — whether an AI-native discovery layer reroutes commerce above WMT — got no new evidence in either direction this quarter, and because Sparky defends the owned funnel without contesting third-party agent rails. Note the coverage caveat from §4: part of why this looks better than 1Q27 is that we have summary and presentation content we did not have then.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

No `competitive_advantage` object exists for WMT in either watchlist entry. All three fields below are **initial proposals**. The 1Q27 note's non-binding proposals are cited for continuity only.

- **Innovation rate** (current: _no value — no scoring block_; 1Q27 note proposed 3+)

  - Recommendation: **initialize at 3+** (consistent with the 1Q27 non-binding proposal; this quarter reinforces rather than moves it).
- **Distribution** (current: _no value — no scoring block_; 1Q27 note proposed 5)
  - Evidence: Membership fee revenue at an all-time high, +17% globally, with Walmart+ posting its best first half in history and Sam's Club U.S. membership +~6%; members spend roughly 4x non-members (vendor summary). Walmart U.S. eCommerce now >23% of segment sales; international eCommerce mix ~30%; International net sales $35.2B (+7.9% cc) with 181 new stores opened LTM. Global advertising +38% on an ad surface only WMT's traffic can supply. Store base doubling as fulfillment infrastructure with 3,100 stores on automated freight.
  - Recommendation: **initialize at 5** (consistent with 1Q27). This is the anchor-level attribute for WMT — comparable to the distribution component of the NVDA/AMZN tier.
- **Overall** (current: _no value — no scoring block_; 1Q27 note proposed 4)
  - Recommendation: **initialize at 4**.
  - Reasoning (explicitly not a mechanical average of 3+ and 5): for a mass-retail incumbent, distribution is not merely one input — it is the mechanism that converts ordinary innovation into extraordinary economics. The evidence this quarter is precise on that point: advertising at +38% and membership at +17% are *monetisations of distribution*, not products WMT invented, and they are what produced the favourable gross-margin mix the company cites. So innovation of 3+ does not drag the overall to the midpoint; distribution carries it to 4. It does not reach 4+ or 5 because the innovation tax is real and asymmetric — a competitor that wins the AI-native discovery layer does not need WMT's distribution to take share above it, and Sparky is a defensive answer to that risk rather than a winning one. A confirmed third-party agent-commerce position would be the evidence that justifies 4+; a demonstrated loss of discovery share would take this to 3+.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

**Framing caveat — read before using.** The InsiderScore transcript body was not retrievable this run (see §10); what follows comes from the AI summary's structured Q&A review, which supplies analyst names and affiliations but **paraphrases** the exchanges. Names and topics are reliable; the characterisations below are vendor paraphrase, **not verbatim quotation**, and no words are attributed as quotes to any executive. Separately, InsiderScore's `challenging_analyze_exchanges` metric returned **0** for this call while the same summary enumerates 13 analyst exchanges — treat that zero as a vendor metric artifact, not as evidence that management faced no pushback.

1. **Christopher Nardone (BofA) → Rainey — the earnings-quality question, and the most useful answer on the call.** Nardone asked about digital business margins and rollback plans for FY28. Per the vendor paraphrase, Rainey detailed digital margin growth driven by advertising and eCommerce and expressed confidence in continued improvement — then **acknowledged the challenge of lapping 19% EPS growth next year**. That volunteered concession is the clearest management signal that Q2's optics are not a run-rate, and it aligns with the Q3 OI guide of +2-4%.
2. **Gregory Melich (Evercore) → Rainey — FY28 operating income, answered without numbers.** Melich pressed on tariff rates and FY28 operating income growth. Rainey reportedly expressed confidence in growing the bottom line faster than the top line and stressed the durability of share gains. Note what is absent: no quantification and no framework for how the refund-funded price investments annualise. Given that this is the central modelling question coming out of the print, the qualitative-only answer reads as a deliberate deferral to the February guide.
3. **Bradley Thomas (KeyBanc) → Furner / Guggina — health & wellness, pivoted rather than sized.** Thomas asked for the multiyear outlook on H&W. The response, per the summary, emphasised that H&W customers spend more and that pharmacy is a relationship-deepening convenience — i.e. it reframed a sizing question as a strategic-value question. The ~125 bps FY28 headwind and halving GLP-1 benefit surface in the vendor summary's Health and Wellness section rather than as a direct answer here. Mild dodge on the number that matters.
4. **Simeon Gutman (Morgan Stanley) → Rainey / Furner — consumer health and the price-investment lag.** Gutman asked about lower-income consumer pressure and price elasticity. Rainey acknowledged the higher-fuel-price impact and, importantly, **a lag before price reductions show up as traffic benefit**; Furner noted rollbacks first lift units and transactions, driving share gains especially in food. This lag is the stated mechanism behind the weak Q3 guide and is the assumption most worth stress-testing.
5. **Paul Lejuez (Citi) → Furner / Rainey — OpEx, and the capex tell.** Lejuez asked about comp assumptions and OpEx growth. Rainey attributed OpEx pressure to depreciation and group health, citing investments in supply-chain automation and associate retention. The depreciation point connects directly to the capex raise to ~4.0% of net sales: the automation build carries a P&L cost that is now visible in the expense line and will grow.
