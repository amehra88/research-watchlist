**First observation for this ticker — no prior earnings notes exist in `notes/CBRS/`**, so all classifications below are anchored against the watchlist thesis (scored 2026-06-01) rather than against a prior quarter's read. ## 3. Forward guidance

**Q2'26:** core revenue ~**$194M** (+88% YoY); core gross margin **36–38%**.

**FY26:** core revenue **$855–865M** (+69% YoY at midpoint); core gross margin **38–41%**; core operating margin **-28% to -32%**.

**Shape and philosophy.** Guidance is given as ranges with an explicit back-end-loaded ramp — management expects YoY growth rates to *increase* each quarter as cloud capacity comes online, with OpenAI ramping in Cerebras Cloud. Komin attributed the back-half weighting to OpenAI deployments (Buchalter Q&A). Hardware revenue is expected to *decline* near-term as production shifts to cloud deployments, though management notes mix could shift quickly on customer deployment choices — a deliberate hedge that makes the hardware/cloud split low-confidence for modeling.

**The margin arc is the story.** Management frames the gross-margin compression as temporary, quantifying a **10–15 point** drag from renting systems (notably from G42) to serve near-term demand, and reaffirms a medium-to-long-term target of **60% gross / 40% operating** margin as capacity becomes self-owned.

**Derived (my arithmetic, not management-stated):** Q1 core opex ran ~48.5% of revenue (46.5% GM less -2% OM). The FY26 guide implies ~69.5% (≈39.5% GM less -30% OM) — roughly **21 points of opex deleverage** against only ~7 points of gross-margin compression. The rental drag management quantifies sits in COGS and explains the gross-margin move; the substantially larger degradation is *below* the gross line. That points to a step-function ramp in operating and depreciation cost from the owned-data-center build, not merely rented capacity. Flagged as the single most important unresolved question for the Q2 review.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### silicon_architecture_competition

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Paraphrase, InsiderScore AI summary (Technology / Market Dynamics sections) — management claims wafer-scale inference more than 10x faster than leading GPUs, citing a demo of "21 seconds vs 4m37s" for a trillion-parameter model, and asserts the architecture suits small, medium and large models across on-prem and cloud. Q&A (Buchalter, Cowen): Feldman affirmed architectural suitability for large models such as Kimi.
- **Implication:** The technical-differentiation leg of the thesis is intact and now has a paying frontier-lab customer validating it in production rather than benchmark-only support. Speed-as-differentiator remains an assertion sourced to management demo, not independent benchmark.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Paraphrase, AI summary (Data Center Expansion / Capital Position) — data center capacity, *not* chip supply and not demand, is the primary constraint (reiterated to Moore, Morgan Stanley). Aggressive global buildout across North America and Europe, with discussions in Israel, UAE, Australia, Singapore, India, Indonesia. $3.3B cash; $850M credit facility (Apr 2026).
- **Implication:** The underlying capex environment is confirmed — demand is not the constraint — but the *mechanism* by which CBRS participates in it shifts: the company converts from a capex *beneficiary* into a capex *spender*, having to fund and operate data centers to monetize demand. That changes the capital intensity and risk profile versus a pure merchant-silicon framing, and is the driver behind the FY26 operating-margin guide.

### inference_compute_economics

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Paraphrase, AI summary (Gross Margin) and Q&A (Bolton, Needham) — Feldman confirmed fast inference services command premium pricing "due to their value"; cloud/services margin reached 52.9% on higher pricing and utilization. But FY26 core gross margin guides to 38–41% and core operating margin to -28% to -32%, with a stated 10–15pt rental drag and a 60%/40% target deferred to the medium-to-long term. Bryson (Wedbush) asked directly about rental impact on gross margins; Komin confirmed it is inside the guidance.
- **Implication:** Pricing power for fast inference is confirmed — the unit economics *at the service level* work. What drifts is the timeline and the capital path to the steady-state margin: the economics are gated by who owns the data center, pushing the profitability inflection out and making FY26 a heavy-investment year. Thesis magnitude and timeline shift; mechanism holds.

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Paraphrase, AI summary (Strategic Partnerships) and Q&A (Arcuri/UBS, Bryson/Wedbush) — definitive agreement with AWS (March 2026) to deploy CS-3 inside AWS data centers **alongside Trainium 3 in a disaggregated solution**, described as an industry first; impact expected in 2027. Feldman highlighted the opportunity to provide *decode* solutions to GPU users and discussed value capture from disaggregation for hyperscalers.
- **Implication:** Strongly supportive of the compute-topology thesis: the disaggregated prefill/decode split lets Cerebras attach to incumbent accelerator fleets rather than displace them wholesale, which materially widens the addressable footprint and lowers the switching barrier. This is the most strategically important item on the call and the least reflected in the current watchlist notes.

## 5. AI positioning signal

- **Current score:** 5
- **Evidence from this call:** OpenAI definitive agreement (signed 2025-12-24) restated at **>$20B of compute over several years**, with production reached within **35 days** of signature; OpenAI running GPT 5.4 on Cerebras with GPT 5.5 planned. AWS definitive agreement for disaggregated CS-3 + Trainium 3 deployment. Bell Canada 120MW facility referenced. Supply-chain positioning is a genuine differentiator: SRAM rather than HBM, 5nm node, sidestepping both HBM scarcity and TSMC CoWoS bottlenecks; CS-3 manufactured exclusively in the US via Flextronics and Sanmina.
- **Recommendation:** hold at 5
- **Reasoning:** Nothing on this call weakens the top score, and the AWS disaggregation deal plus the 35-day OpenAI production ramp arguably strengthen it. 5 is already the ceiling, so the incremental good news has nowhere to go — note that the watchlist's supporting text should be refreshed: it cites an "OpenAI 750MW commitment," whereas the call frames the commitment in dollars (>$20B). The operator may want the note text reconciled to the contracted basis.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: 4)
  - Evidence: Claimed >10x inference speed advantage vs leading GPUs with a specific demo cited; architecture validated across small/medium/large models; the AWS disaggregated inference approach presented as an industry first; CS-3 in volume production with US manufacturing partners. Q1 opex +51% against +92% revenue indicates R&D is scaling sub-linearly to revenue — efficient, though it also means cadence evidence on the *next* WSE generation was absent from this call.
  - Recommendation: hold at 4 (the case for 5 needs next-generation silicon cadence evidence, which this call did not provide)
- **Distribution** (current: 3)
  - Evidence: Named at-scale relationships now include OpenAI (>$20B), AWS (in-datacenter deployment), Bell Canada (120MW), and G42/Middle East. Feldman expressed satisfaction with new-customer acquisition and existing-customer expansion (Rakesh, Mizuho) and flagged discussions with multiple hyperscalers. Geographic expansion in progress across seven-plus named countries. Counterweight: Feldman **declined to name top-10% customers**, deferring to future filings (O'Malley, Barclays), and withheld the scale of the AWS agreement (Bolton, Needham).
  - Recommendation: revise to **3+** — the AWS in-datacenter channel is a structural distribution change (access to the largest cloud install base without owning the customer relationship), but the concentration disclosure gap and 2027 revenue timing argue against a clean 4.
- **Overall** (current: 4)
  - Recommendation: hold at 4
  - Reasoning: **Not a mechanical average, and the two dimensions now interact in a way that argues against upgrading despite better inputs on both.** Innovation and distribution both improved this quarter, yet the binding constraint on the business moved to a *third* axis entirely — data center capacity — which neither dimension captures. Cerebras' architectural edge only converts to revenue where it has powered, cooled floor space; distribution wins (AWS, OpenAI) currently *outrun* the capacity to serve them, which is why the company is renting from a customer (G42) at a 10–15pt margin cost. For this business model, distribution breadth and revenue concentration also moved in *opposite* directions: more named logos, but a single contract (OpenAI) driving the back-half ramp. Until owned capacity catches up, incremental distribution wins convert into margin drag rather than compounding advantage. Overall 4 is the right holding place; revisit on Q2 with capacity-delivery evidence.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

1. **Refused to name concentration (O'Malley, Barclays).** Asked directly about top-10% customers, Feldman deferred to future filings. For a name whose back-half guide is explicitly driven by one customer's ramp, this is the most consequential non-answer on the call. The Q2 10-Q (filed 2026-08-12) should now contain the customer-concentration disclosure — worth pulling independently.
2. **Withheld AWS deal scale (Bolton, Needham).** Feldman declined to size the AWS agreement while simultaneously promoting it as an industry first. Strategically significant, financially unquantified, and pushed to 2027 — the combination invites the market to credit it early at an unknown value.
3. **Unusual emphasis on supply-chain positioning.** Management volunteered the SRAM-not-HBM and 5nm-not-CoWoS framing prominently. This is a real differentiator, but the emphasis also functions to redirect from the constraint that actually binds — data center capacity — which management does concede when asked (Moore, Morgan Stanley).
4. **Hardware margin quality flagged by management itself.** The 42% hardware margin (vs 30.6% PY) was attributed to performance-based incentive pricing and timing, with hardware revenue guided *down* near-term. Creditable disclosure rather than a dodge, but it means Q1's headline gross margin overstates the run rate — the sequential Q2 guide to 36–38% is the cleaner read.
5. **"Dog fight" characterization.** Management described the market for data center capacity as highly competitive (quoted in the InsiderScore summary), an unusually candid word choice that supports taking the capacity constraint at face value rather than as conservatism.
