## 1. Headline read

First observation, so there is no prior-quarter read to anchor to. The print confirms the upcycle exposure the five theme tags imply: record revenue of $3.66B (+15% y/y, +7% q/q) at the upper end of guidance, a September-quarter guide of $4.0B ± $200M that sat ~1.9% above pre-print consensus, and a third consecutive raise of the 2026 WFE outlook to "the low $150 billion range" with management explicitly planning for growth into 2027 (transcript summary, prepared remarks; 8-K Ex-99.1 2026-07-28). The one soft spot is gross margin: non-GAAP 62.4% is down ~90 bp y/y on memory input costs and tariffs, and the pricing-power exchange with Cowen was the most challenging of the call.

## 3. Forward guidance

September quarter (fiscal Q1 2027) guidance, 8-K Ex-99.1 2026-07-28:
- Revenue $4.0B ± $200M (midpoint +9.4% q/q vs $3,657.6M actual; +1.9% above the pre-print FactSet consensus mean of $3,925.3M as of 2026-07-28, fiscalEndDate 2026-09-30).
- Non-GAAP gross margin 62.5% ± 1.0 pt (GAAP 61.6% ± 1.0 pt) — flat to the 62.4% just reported despite ~9% more volume, i.e. memory input costs and tariffs are still absorbing operating leverage.
- Non-GAAP diluted EPS $1.16 ± $0.10 (GAAP $1.14 ± $0.10) — +1.6% above the pre-print consensus mean of $1.1416.

Framing beyond the quarter (transcript summary, paraphrase — verbatim not available, see Sourcing):
- 2026 WFE raised to "approximately the low $150 billion range" from "$140 billion-plus", a mid-20% increase on ~$120B in 2025. Management said it anticipates continued strong growth in 2027 across leading-edge logic/foundry, DRAM, NAND and advanced packaging; the summary attributes a ~$190B 2027 WFE figure to management's outlook.
- Second half of calendar 2026 expected to be ~20% above the first half, with sequential growth continuing into 2027.
- Advanced packaging process control revenue now expected at ~$1.1B in 2026, up >70% y/y (prior expectation was high-50% growth); the advanced packaging market growth view moved from ~20% to mid-to-high 30%.
- Services: long-term model 13–15% growth; management expects to be at the high end in 2027; semiconductor spares on track for ~20% growth; ~80% of service revenue is contract-based.
- Gross margin: 62–63% for calendar 2026, within the 60–65% long-term range, with memory pricing expected to normalize and potentially become a tailwind through 2027.
- Supply chain: most product lead times ~12 months, some 18–24 months; capacity agreements being planned for 2029 and beyond. 10-K FY26 shows purchase commitments of $5.97B, a majority due within 12 months.

Guidance philosophy: unchanged — point-midpoint with symmetric ranges on revenue, GM and EPS. Tone was more assertive than a typical KLA quarter (explicit 2027 growth call, third WFE raise), but the numeric guide itself was only modestly above consensus.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### foundry_capacity

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Management cited "the increasing number and sophistication of leading-edge designs across foundry/logic" as driving process control demand (CEO quote, 8-K Ex-99.1, verbatim). Q&A with JPMorgan (Sur) and Morgan Stanley (Brett): broadening of leading-edge logic investment and greenfield fab opportunities (transcript summary, paraphrase). 10-K FY26 MD&A: TSMC is the only >10% customer; Taiwan revenue +13.7% y/y to 26.8% of FY26 revenue; North America +29.0%.
- **Implication:** Foundry capacity additions at the leading edge are flowing directly into KLA's core Semiconductor Process Control segment (+13% y/y in the quarter). The thesis that leading-edge foundry build-out is a multi-year tailwind is intact and, per management, extends into 2027.

### semiconductor_cycle

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** 2026 WFE raised for a third time to the low-$150B range (+mid-20% y/y); management planning for 2027 growth; backlog ~$12.5B; deferred system revenue up to $932.9M from $816.8M (transcript summary; 8-K Ex-99.1). Inventory up to $3.65B and purchase commitments of $5.97B (10-K FY26) show the company positioning for further volume.
- **Implication:** The name is squarely in an equipment upcycle, and the call extended its expected duration by at least a year. The cycle risk is now one of magnitude of eventual reversion, not timing of the peak; the working-capital build (OCF down 22% y/y while revenue rose 15%) is the metric to watch if the cycle turns.

### china_export_controls

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** 10-K FY26 MD&A (filed 2026-08-06): China revenue $4.05B, flat y/y, falling to 29.8% of revenue from 33.3% (FY25) and 42.8% (FY24); "continued investments in legacy-node technologies by domestic semiconductor companies were largely offset by export control restrictions affecting certain advanced technology transactions." Specialty Semiconductor Process segment −1% on lower China product sales. On the call, BofA (Arya) asked about domestic Chinese competition; Wallace said KLA sees no material share loss and "generally wins when competition is fair" (transcript summary, paraphrase). Press-release risk language references "evolving Bureau of Industry and Security ... rules" (8-K Ex-99.1).
- **Implication:** The export-control headwind is real but is being diluted rather than growing: China's share of revenue has fallen 13 points in two years while total revenue grew, so the sensitivity of the equity to further restrictions is lower than the theme tag implies. The drift is in mechanism — the risk is shifting from revenue loss to domestic-competitor share gain in legacy China, which management downplays but did not quantify.

### lithography_roadmap

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** 10-K FY26 MD&A: "continued adoption of EUV in HVM for logic and DRAM (including high-bandwidth memory) ... increasing process control requirements and expanding our served market"; leading-edge nodes "such as 2-nanometer" increasing process control intensity. Patterning revenue +23% y/y in FY26 to $2.71B (20% of revenue), the fastest-growing major product category after PCB. Transcript summary: Wallace on rising process control intensity from more sophisticated device architectures.
- **Implication:** The lithography roadmap (EUV in HVM, 2nm ramps, EUV DRAM) is translating into process control intensity gains and a patterning business growing faster than the company. Thesis intact; the patterning line is the cleanest quantitative read of it.

### nand_demand_cycle

- **Status:** Confirm (thin evidence)
- **Evidence:** NAND was named alongside leading-edge logic, foundry, DRAM and advanced packaging as a driver of "broad-based investment" in the 2026/2027 WFE outlook (transcript summary, prepared remarks, paraphrase). No NAND-specific quantification in the summary, press release or 10-K MD&A; the memory commentary that did get airtime was DRAM/HBM (Korea revenue +26.2% in FY26 on HBM and advanced DRAM, 10-K MD&A). Note that the "memory pricing" headwind discussed on the call is KLA's own DRAM component input cost, not memory-customer capex.
- **Implication:** NAND is participating in the upcycle but is the least-evidenced of the five themes on this call; the theme tag is not contradicted, but the PM should not read this print as a NAND-specific data point. Look to memory-maker calls for the NAND signal.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

No Store B guidance track record exists for KLAC (state/thesis/context/KLAC.md: no rows, no credibility scores), so the sandbag judgment below rests on this quarter's guide-vs-consensus only, not on a hit-rate pattern. All Q&A citations are paraphrased from the InsiderScore AI summary; verbatim exchanges were not reachable (see Sourcing).

1. **Guide vs consensus (sandbag read):** September-quarter guide midpoint $4.0B / $1.16 vs pre-print consensus $3,925.3M / $1.1416 (FactSet, 2026-07-28, fiscalEndDate 2026-09-30). Consensus reset to $4,037.9M / $1.183 by 2026-07-29 (15 of 20 revenue estimates up), i.e. the street immediately priced the guide as conservative and set itself above the midpoint. Provisional read: modest-sandbag pattern typical of large-cap semicap; cannot be confirmed without a track record.
2. **Krish Sankar (Cowen) — equipment pricing vs gross margin; revenue vs WFE.** Flagged by InsiderScore as a challenging exchange. Wallace and Higgins acknowledged input-price increases and defended the pricing model as resilient, but the numbers show non-GAAP GM at 62.4% vs 63.2% a year ago with revenue up 15%. The pricing-power question is live and management did not commit to a margin expansion timeline beyond "normalization" of memory costs through 2027.
3. **Stacy Rasgon (Bernstein) — supply for bullish scenarios; relevance of the 2030 target model.** Flagged as challenging. Management described flexible planning across demand scenarios and reiterated the 2030 model's assumptions without updating them, despite a WFE outlook that has moved well beyond the model's base case. Implicit dodge: the model is likely stale to the upside and management chose not to reset it on the call.
4. **Vivek Arya (BofA) — China domestic competition and WFE share.** Wallace said KLA sees no material share loss and "generally wins when competition is fair" — the qualifier is an unusual emphasis, implying management sees non-commercial factors in some China awards. Not quantified.
5. **Atif Malik (Citi) — visibility into 2027 and specific customer engagements.** Wallace cited strong demand signals and backlog but declined to discuss specific customer projects. Standard practice, noted because the 2027 growth call rests on unnamed engagements.
