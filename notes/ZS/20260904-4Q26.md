## 1. Headline read

**Confirm on the print, Drift on the forward model.** Zscaler beat its own Q4 FY26 guide on every guided line (revenue $898.2M vs $875–878M guided; non-GAAP EPS $1.19 vs $1.08–1.09; non-GAAP operating income $218.4M vs $206–208M) and closed FY26 above its full-year guide, with organic net new ARR *accelerating* to +17% — the strongest evidence in this print. But the FY27 guide steps growth down to 16.6–17.5% (from 25% reported / 20% organic in FY26), holds FCF margin flat at 23.0–23.5% despite ~80bps of operating-margin expansion, and absorbs a 3% workforce restructuring whose drag management placed in H1 FY27. The thesis magnitude and mechanism move; the thesis does not break.

## 3. Forward guidance

**Q1 FY27** [8-K Ex-99.1, verbatim]: revenue $935–939M (~19% YoY growth); non-GAAP gross margin ~80%; non-GAAP income from operations $215–217M (~25–26% growth, 23% operating margin); non-GAAP EPS ~$1.15–1.16 on ~170M diluted shares at a 21% tax rate.

**FY27** [8-K Ex-99.1, verbatim]: ARR $4.396–4.426B (+16.6–17.4%); revenue $3.908–3.938B (+16.6–17.5%); non-GAAP gross margin ~80%; non-GAAP income from operations $924–932M (+~21%); non-GAAP EPS $4.86–4.90 on ~173M diluted shares; free cash flow margin ~23.0–23.5%.

**Magnitude and direction.** The revenue step-down is the headline: 16.6–17.5% guided against 25% reported / 20% organic in FY26. Some of that gap is arithmetic — FY26's 25% carried Red Canary, and management said it expects no net new ARR contribution from Red Canary standalone in FY27 — so the honest organic comparison is roughly 20% → ~17%, a real but far less dramatic deceleration than the headline implies.

**The margin/cash divergence is the most decision-relevant line in the guide.** FY27 non-GAAP operating margin guides to ~23.7% (midpoint $928M on $3.923B) against FY26 actual 22.9% ($767.109M on $3,352.523M) — roughly +80bps of expansion. Yet FY27 free cash flow margin guides *flat* at 23.0–23.5% versus FY26's 23%. Operating leverage is being earned and then consumed before it reaches cash. Q4 FY26 already showed the mechanism in extreme form: free cash flow of $60.764M, a **7% margin against 24% in Q4 FY25**, on purchases of property and equipment of $199.837M in the quarter versus $60.046M a year earlier. Every figure in this paragraph is from the 8-K. Management attributed the capex pressure to higher component prices and tighter availability, particularly memory, and guided capex to "low teens" percent of revenue in FY27 [summary-derived paraphrase — the press release contains no capex guide].

**Guidance philosophy.** Ranges throughout, unchanged in form from Q3. Two things are notable about the framing: management explicitly built the H1 FY27 sales-transition drag *into* the guide rather than carving it out, and — per the Q&A — declined to include any AI-security contribution in the guide at all (§8). The combination reads as deliberate de-risking of the first half.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### cybersecurity_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Chaudhry, press release [verbatim]: "By connecting users, workloads, branches, and now agents directly to the applications they need without placing them on the network, we are uniquely equipped to help companies both combat the threats created by agentic AI and securely deploy AI agents and models." Rubin [verbatim]: "record large-deal activity." Corroborating [summary-derived paraphrase]: Zero Trust enterprises exceeded 950, up from ~700 in Q3 and over 350 at the end of FY25; 7-figure wins in manufacturing, financial services and healthcare, often displacing legacy vendors; customers with >$1M ARR reached 785 (+18%) and >$100K ARR 4,182 (+20%); in the Boyd/UBS exchange Rubin argued customers prefer one integrated solution over multiple point AI-security products.
- **Implication:** The watchlist's stated distribution risk — "platform encroachment from CRWD/PANW/Microsoft" — did not show up as damage to the SSE core this quarter; the architectural differentiation is still winning bake-offs and the Zero Trust enterprise count nearly tripled year over year. But the thesis acquires a **new front that runs the other way**: with Agentic SecOps and the Red Canary integration, ZS is now advancing into managed detection and response, where it is the challenger and CrowdStrike the incumbent. Confirm on the core; monitor the SecOps expansion as a distinct competitive exposure rather than as more of the same.

### enterprise_ai_adoption

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Chaudhry, press release [verbatim]: "AI represents one of the most significant opportunities in Zscaler's history... As AI becomes foundational to how organizations operate, we are well positioned to extend our leadership as the cybersecurity platform for the AI era." Corroborating [summary-derived paraphrase]: AI described on the call as the largest demand driver the company has ever seen; Security-for-AI bookings up over 50% sequentially in Q4 with strong inbound interest and no reported budget constraints; non-seat-based metered solutions ~30% of new and upsell ACV with associated ARR up over 100% YoY.
- **Implication:** Enterprise AI adoption is showing up in ZS's bookings, not merely in its marketing — and the non-seat, consumption-metered packaging means adoption converts to revenue without a seat-count negotiation, which is the right structure for this demand. The material qualification: management confirmed AI-security contribution is **not called out in the FY27 guide** (§8). Adoption is real in bookings and absent from the underwritten numbers, which makes it optionality rather than a committed driver.

### ai_infrastructure_software

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Entirely from the 8-K [verbatim]: FY27 non-GAAP operating margin guides to ~23.7% (midpoint $928M on $3.923B revenue) versus FY26 actual 22.9% ($767.109M on $3,352.523M) — ~80bps of expansion — while FY27 free cash flow margin guides *flat* at 23.0–23.5% against FY26's 23%. Q4 FY26 purchases of property and equipment were $199.837M versus $60.046M in Q4 FY25, driving quarterly FCF margin to 7% from 24%. Corroborating [summary-derived paraphrase]: capex $277M in FY26 (8% of revenue), guided to "low teens" percent of revenue in FY27 on higher memory, storage and processor prices and tight supply.
- **Implication:** The thesis has treated ZS as a beneficiary of AI infrastructure buildout on the demand side. This print reveals the relationship is **two-sided**: ZS runs its own global data-center estate, so AI-driven component inflation lands in its capex line and converts operating leverage into flat cash conversion. That is a mechanism change, not a contradiction — the demand tailwind is intact and strengthening, but the AI-infrastructure exposure now cuts both ways, and a software name whose FCF margin is capped by memory pricing deserves a different valuation frame than one whose costs are fixed. Drift, and the single most important thing to re-underwrite before this name is sized.

## 5. AI positioning signal

- **Current score:** 3
- **Evidence from this call:** Chaudhry's press-release quote names the product architecture explicitly [verbatim]: "Our continued innovation across Zero Trust SASE, **Agentic SecOps**, **Data Security**, and **Security for AI** is driving increased platform adoption and creating new avenues for growth, as reflected in our strong Q4 results." Rubin [verbatim]: "Our growth engine continues to broaden beyond users, with a strong contribution from **non-seat-based solutions**, continued **Z-Flex** momentum, record large-deal activity, and improving sales productivity." The 8-K's Item 2.05 restructuring is justified in the company's own words as "strategically reallocating resources to provide additional capacity to support our **AI and growth initiatives**" [verbatim]. Corroborating [summary-derived paraphrase]: Security-for-AI bookings +50% sequentially; non-seat ARR +100% YoY including consumption-based AI security modules; Agentic SecOps launching early September 2026, with Zero Trust Exchange for agents and endpoint AI security scaling in H2 FY27. Prior-quarter context from the Q3 8-K: Symmetry Systems acquisition (access-graph technology to govern AI agent communication), Anthropic Project Glasswing, and the OpenAI DayBreak partnership.
- **Recommendation:** **drift to 4**
- **Reasoning:** The current score's own language set the bar — "Security-first, not AI-first; 3+ on AI-security feature expansion" — and that expansion has now demonstrably happened with bookings attached and named product lines in the CEO's own results quote, not merely a roadmap. ZS is monetizing AI security as a distinct consumption-metered line and is restructuring its workforce explicitly to fund it. What holds this at 4 rather than 5 is that management declined to underwrite any AI-security contribution in the FY27 guide, and the agent-native products do not scale until H2 FY27; the revenue is prospective. Revise to 4 now, and revisit for 5 once Agentic SecOps contribution appears in a guide rather than in a bookings anecdote.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: 4)
  - Evidence: Chaudhry names four concurrent innovation vectors in the results quote — Zero Trust SASE, Agentic SecOps, Data Security, Security for AI [verbatim]. FY26 research and development expense of $903.399M versus $672.485M in FY25, +34% [verbatim, 8-K statements of operations]. Corroborating [summary-derived paraphrase]: Agentic SecOps launching early September 2026 offering agent-native detection and closed-loop remediation, built on platform telemetry management sized at ~750 billion transactions per day; Zero Trust Exchange for agents and endpoint AI security to scale in H2 FY27; Red Canary and Symmetry technology folded into the integrated SecOps offering; ZFlex as a commercial-model innovation with over $1.7B of FY26 bookings.
  - Recommendation: **hold at 4**
  - _The cadence is high and the products are landing — Zero Trust enterprises nearly tripled year over year. What blocks a 5 is that the flagship new products contribute nothing until H2 FY27 and the telemetry-derived SecOps advantage is asserted rather than yet demonstrated in revenue. A successful Agentic SecOps ramp in H2 FY27 is the trigger for 5._
- **Distribution** (current: 4)
  - Evidence: The load-bearing evidence is verbatim from the 8-K. **Item 2.05**: on 2026-09-01 the company committed to reduce worldwide headcount by ~3% and incur charges of $30.0–33.0M, "consisting primarily of employee severance and benefit costs," with the majority recognized in H1 FY27. FY27 revenue guides to 16.6–17.5% against FY26 actual 25%. Offsetting, from Rubin's results quote [verbatim]: "improving sales productivity" and "record large-deal activity." Corroborating context [summary-derived paraphrase]: two sales leaders departed last quarter and have been backfilled internally and externally, with the transition expected to affect H1 FY27 and explicitly incorporated into guidance; Q4 delivered the highest quarterly sales productivity in company history and the best annual productivity since 2022; the company is now building dedicated new-logo enterprise sales capacity and leaning on channel to reach commercial and SMB; the federal vertical contributed high-single-digits of new ACV, flat versus FY25.
  - Recommendation: **revise to 3**
  - _The impairment is real and self-inflicted rather than competitive: a go-to-market organization mid-transition, a 3% restructuring, and a company that is only now building dedicated new-logo capacity — an acknowledged coverage gap — while guiding the first half down for it. Record productivity per rep is a genuine offset, but it is productivity on the existing account-focused expansion motion, not evidence of reach. Note this is a **different** risk from the one the watchlist priced: the note anticipated encroachment from CRWD/PANW/Microsoft, and that is not what showed up. This is reversible — restore to 4 if H1 FY27 shows the transition contained and new-logo adds inflecting._
- **Overall** (current: 4)
  - Recommendation: **hold at 4**
  - Reasoning: Not the average of 4 and 3. ZS is a land-and-expand platform in which architecture wins the beachhead and distribution monetizes module expansion across the installed base — so the two dimensions are ordinarily multiplicative, and a distribution downgrade would normally drag the overall down with it. What breaks that coupling here is **ZFlex specifically**: because customers can activate and swap modules without opening a new procurement cycle, expansion revenue is structurally decoupled from salesforce capacity in a way it is not at most security platforms. The distribution impairment therefore lands almost entirely on *new-logo acquisition*, while the expansion engine that generates the majority of ZS's ARR growth — NRR, upsell, non-seat module attach, evidenced by organic net new ARR accelerating to +17% in the very quarter the GTM disruption occurred — keeps running. Overall holds at 4, but its **composition has shifted**: materially more of it now rests on innovation and the self-serve expansion model, and materially less on salesforce reach. If ZFlex momentum stalls, the 4 loses its support and should go to 3 immediately.

## 7. Potential investor interest signal

- **Current score:** 3
- **Evidence from this call:** Against — FY27 revenue guides to 16.6–17.5% from 25% reported / 20% organic, which is precisely the growth-deceleration scrutiny the watchlist flagged, now quantified by the company itself; Q1 FY27 EPS guides to $1.15–1.16, a *sequential step down* from the $1.19 just reported; FY27 operating margin expands ~80bps yet FCF margin stays flat at 23.0–23.5%, so cash conversion does not improve; Q4 FCF margin printed 7% against 24% a year ago; a 3% restructuring lands in H1 FY27; no buyback and no dividend were announced, and none appears in the 8-K. For — every guided Q4 line beat, FY26 finished above the full-year guide, organic net new ARR accelerated to +17% from a much weaker FY25 base, non-GAAP operating margin set a record at 24%, and AI security is an unusually hot sector with the CEO articulating a specific and credible architectural claim rather than generic optimism. Capital allocation news this quarter was acquisitive rather than shareholder-returning: FY26 payments for business acquisitions net of cash were $918.074M [verbatim, 8-K cash flow statement].
- **Recommendation:** **hold at 3 — but promoted from analyst-draft to evidence-backed**
- _This is a decision, not a default. The watchlist's 3 was explicitly provisional ("analyst-draft pending earnings cycle... Refine after next earnings cycle"), and this run is that cycle. The deceleration and competition concerns that set the 3 are now confirmed and quantified rather than merely suspected; the offsetting organic net-new-ARR acceleration is genuinely better than the prior note assumed; and one new negative the prior scoring did not contemplate — capex-driven flat FCF conversion — has been captured. Those roughly cancel, which is why the number does not move, but the score should no longer be carried as provisional. **Downgrade to 2** if H1 FY27 shows a net new ARR miss or capex running above low-teens percent of revenue. **Upgrade to 4** on an Agentic SecOps ramp that appears in guidance plus a visible new-logo inflection. Note also the `tier_change_note` context — ZS was held in the May BCTK position and exited in the June rotation; nothing in this print argues for re-entry ahead of the H1 FY27 transition checkpoint._
