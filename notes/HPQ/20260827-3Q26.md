# HPQ — Q3'26 earnings read

_Prepared by earnings-reviewer, 2026-08-27. Tier: T3 (watchlist)._

## 1. Headline read

Q3'26 was a **clean operating beat wrapped in a misleading headline**: revenue $15.68B (+12.5% YoY, ninth straight quarter of growth) and non-GAAP EPS $0.83 vs. a $0.61–$0.71 guide, but **$0.11 of that EPS is a one-time tariff refund**. Strip it and the beat is +8.4% on EPS, +8.6% on revenue, +8.5% on EBITDA — three independent lines converging on ~+8.5%, which is the real number. Against the Q2'26 note's five forward calls, HP delivered or beat on all five, but the mechanism has changed: **units fell 16% while revenue rose 18%**, so growth is now entirely cost-pass-through pricing and premium mix, not the Windows 11 refresh volume story the thesis was built on. Personal Systems earnings from operations were **$537M vs. $541M a year ago — flat-to-down in dollars on +18% revenue**. I score `pc_demand` as **Drift**, not Confirm: the profit-pool leg holds, the unit-refresh leg does not.

## 3. Forward guidance

- **Q4'26 non-GAAP diluted EPS: $0.69–$0.79**, including **$0.08** of estimated tariff refunds → **ex-refund $0.61–$0.71**. That is *below* Q3's ex-refund $0.72. The Q2'26 note's call that **Q4'26 is the Personal Systems margin trough is confirmed by the guidance arithmetic itself**, not merely by management's verbal reaffirmation.
- **Q4'26 GAAP diluted EPS: $0.74–$0.84** — note this sits **above** the non-GAAP range, i.e. HP is excluding a net ~$0.05 *benefit* from its non-GAAP Q4 figure, against $0.67 of full-year add-backs (restructuring, acquisition/divestiture costs and related). Unusual directionally and worth a glance at the Q4 reconciliation when it lands.
- **FY26 non-GAAP diluted EPS raised to $3.19–$3.29** from $2.90–$3.10 — **but the new range includes $0.19 of estimated tariff refunds, and the Q2'26 call explicitly stated refunds were "not factored into the current financial outlook."** Decomposition:

  | | Low | Mid | High |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Prior FY26 guide (Q2 call) | 2.90 | 3.00 | 3.10 |
  | New FY26 guide | 3.19 | 3.24 | 3.29 |
  | New guide **ex-$0.19 refund** | 3.00 | 3.05 | 3.10 |
  | **Core operating raise** | **+0.10** | **+0.05** | **0.00** |

  So the apparent ~+8% guidance raise is **~80% tariff refunds**. The genuine operating raise is +$0.05 at the midpoint and **zero at the high end**. Any read of this print as "FY26 EPS raised 8%, thesis confirmed" is wrong.
- **FY26 free cash flow raised to $3.0–$3.2B** from $2.8–$3.0B. This one is clean — no refund adjustment disclosed against it, and it is corroborated by $2.5B+ YTD FCF running ahead of normal seasonality.
- **FY27 framing:** memory and storage costs keep rising into FY27 but at a slower rate; management wants to bring the PS operating profit rate back into its long-term range "as quickly as possible in FY27" (transcript). Print margins guided to a stable 16–19% excluding one-time items. **No FY27 volume guidance** — declined on planning-cycle grounds.
- **AI PC mix:** on track for ~50% of shipments in FY26, targeting 60–70% in FY27.
- **Guidance philosophy:** unchanged point-ranges, but the disclosure quality improved — HP is now explicitly quantifying the tariff-refund contribution at quarter, Q4 and full-year level. That is helpful and makes the underlying number recoverable. Tone is confident on FY27 recovery while conspicuously declining to quantify it.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### pc_demand

- **Status:** **Drift**
- **Evidence:**
  - Personal Systems revenue $11.8B, **+18% YoY** (Commercial +22%, Consumer +10%) — accelerating from +13% in Q2'26.
  - **Units -16% YoY.** Management reaffirmed PC unit TAM down high-teens in 2H'26, exactly as guided on the Q2 call. Implied revenue per unit +~40%.
  - **Personal Systems earnings from operations $537M vs. $541M in Q3'25** — down 0.7% in absolute dollars on 18% more revenue. PS operating margin 4.6%, down from 5.2% in Q2'26 and ~5.4% in Q3'25.
  - Consolidated GAAP gross margin 18.8%, down ~210bps sequentially from 20.9% in Q2'26 — the memory/storage cost pass-through showing up on the P&L.
  - AI PCs 46% of shipments, vs. 44% (Q2) and 35% (Q1).
  - Channel inventory described as higher than normal but well controlled (transcript).
  - Windows 11 refresh still cited as a catalyst, but management explicitly reframed AI PCs and AI workloads as becoming the more significant demand driver (Sankar/Cowen exchange, per AI summary).
- **Implication:** Every Q2 forward call was met — the Q3 EPS guide was beaten, the TAM-down-high-teens call was accurate, pricing/premium-mix offsets worked better than framed, and FY26 FCF went up. But the **mechanism has shifted**, which is the definition of Drift under the rubric rather than Confirm. The Q2 note split this theme into a demand-strength leg and a margin-leverage leg; both now need restating. **If the thesis is "the Windows 11 refresh drives PC units," -16% breaks that leg outright.** If the thesis is "the PC revenue and profit pool expands," +18% revenue confirms the revenue half — but $537M vs. $541M of PS operating dollars says the *profit* pool has not expanded at all; HP is running 18% more revenue through the machine for the same absolute profit. What HP is actually doing is passing memory costs through at a ~40% revenue-per-unit uplift and defending dollars, not compounding them. That is a materially lower-quality version of the thesis than the Q2 note underwrote, and it is entirely dependent on memory-cost normalization arriving in FY27 on the schedule management describes. The margin-leverage leg is now pushed a further quarter right (Q4 trough confirmed by the ex-refund guide) and remains unproven.

- **Overall** (watchlist: unpopulated; prior note's unratified proposal: **6**)

### 11.1 The §6 "secured supply" claim is not corroborated by the commitment disclosure — qualify it

§6 rated **distribution 7** substantially on the argument that HP "secured supply in a constrained memory market and had product to sell while the market tightened." The 10-Q's contractual-obligations disclosure does not support a supply-lock-in reading:

| Unconditional purchase commitments | As of |
|---|---|
| $2.2B | 2026-04-30 (Q2 10-Q) |
| **$2.1B** | **2026-07-31 (Q3 10-Q)** |

Commitments **declined ~$0.1B sequentially** into the quarter management describes as memory-constrained, and the disclosure language is unchanged boilerplate ("primarily related to inventory and service support," majority due within five years). If HP had contractually locked incremental memory capacity, this is the line where it would appear, and it does not. Corroborating: **DIO flat at 73 days** QoQ (up 5 YoY, and HP attributes that increase to *higher commodity costs*, i.e. price not quantity — "partially offset by inventory optimization"). HP is not building physical or contractual memory inventory.

What HP actually did is better described as **demand shaping** (§9's own term) — steering the order book toward configurations it could supply and repricing — than as securing supply. The distribution score is still defensible on the other evidence in §6 (channel breadth, APJ/EMEA/Americas, big-tank share, ability to push a ~40% ASP increase through the channel at all). **The specific "secured scarce memory that smaller OEMs could not" clause should be struck or downgraded to unsourced** — it was inferred from management framing on the call and the filing does not back it. Flagged for the Q4 re-test.

### 11.2 ASP confirmed as ASP — but part of it is currency, not pass-through

§2 deliberately hedged: "revenue per unit +~40% ... note 'revenue per unit,' not ASP." HP now discloses ASP directly, and the hedge was unnecessary:

| Personal Systems, Q3'26 YoY | ASP | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Total PS | **+40.8%** | **−15.8%** |
| Commercial PS | +41.1% | −13.6% |
| Consumer PS | +37.1% | −19.4% |
| _Memo: Q2'26 total PS_ | _+22.3%_ | _−7.0%_ |

The note's +~40% inference was right to within rounding, and the ASP/unit split is now company-disclosed rather than derived. Two refinements:

- **HP attributes the ASP rise to "pricing actions to mitigate higher commodity costs, favorable currency impacts and mix shifts"** — currency is an explicit named driver. PS revenue grew 18.5% reported vs **16.7% constant-currency**, so ~1.8pts of segment growth is FX. The §7 conclusion ("do not credit pricing power — this is cost pass-through") is unchanged and if anything reinforced: part of the optical ASP gain is not even pass-through, it is translation.
- **The Q2→Q3 acceleration is the real story**: ASP +22.3% → +40.8% while units went −7.0% → −15.8%. The mechanism didn't just shift, it doubled in intensity in one quarter. Supports the §4 **Drift** call.

### 11.3 The profit-pool point is a Q3 phenomenon, not a year-to-date one — state it precisely

§1, §4 and §6 all lean on "$537M vs. $541M — flat-to-down on +18% revenue." Correct for the quarter. The nine-month view is different and the note should say so:

| Personal Systems, earnings from operations | Q3'26 | 9M'26 |
|---|---|---|
| Dollars | $537M (−0.7% YoY) | $1,578M (**+8.3%** YoY) |
| % of net revenue | 4.6% (vs 5.4%) | 4.9% (vs 5.2%) |

YTD the PS profit pool **has** expanded 8.3%; it is specifically Q3 where dollar growth went to zero as the commodity shock outran the repricing lag. Same for units: **−15.8% in Q3 but only −4.2% for the nine months**, and HP explicitly credits "the Windows-based PC operating system refresh" as a partial offset in the 9M discussion while dropping that attribution entirely from the Q3 discussion. **This is the cleanest available evidence for the §4 Drift call**: the refresh was still carrying units through 1H'26 and stopped carrying them in Q3. The thesis leg didn't erode gradually — it broke in one quarter. Worth saying that way rather than "flat-to-down," which understates how recent the inflection is.

### 11.4 Tariff refunds quantified — the Q4 $0.08 is largely already in hand, and the pool is nearly exhausted

New disclosure not available from the 8-K:

- HP has **applied for recovery of ~$0.3B** of previously paid IEEPA tariffs.
- **$127M received during Q3'26** (this is the $0.11/sh in §2).
- **$91M received subsequent to 2026-07-31** — i.e. already collected in Q4 as of the filing date.
- Refunds are recognised as a **reduction of Products cost of net revenue when received** — cash-basis, so they land unpredictably rather than accruing.

Two consequences for §3 and §7:

1. **The Q4 guide's $0.08 of refunds is already banked, not estimated.** The arithmetic closes exactly. Diluted share count implied by the 10-Q is ~931M (net earnings $661M / GAAP diluted EPS $0.71). Q3: $127M / 931M = $0.136 pre-tax, ×(1 − 17.2% effective rate) = **$0.113 ≈ the disclosed $0.11** — so refunds are quoted after-tax and the share count checks out. Q4: $91M / 931M = $0.098 pre-tax, after-tax **$0.081 ≈ the guided $0.08**. In other words the entire Q4 refund contribution was already in hand at the filing date; it is a receipt, not a forecast. That de-risks the Q4 range's composition — but note it de-risks the *low-quality* component, not the operating one. Ex-refund Q4 of $0.61–$0.71 still sits below Q3's ex-refund $0.72, so **§3's "Q4 is the trough" call stands unchanged.**
2. **The refund pool is close to exhausted, which strengthens §7.** $127M + $91M = $218M received against ~$0.3B applied for, leaving roughly $80M of applied-for refunds outstanding (HP is "continuing to assess the recoverability of additional IEEPA tariffs previously paid," so the ceiling could move). **FY27 therefore carries little or no refund tailwind against an FY26 base that contains $0.19 of it.** Any FY27 model built off reported FY26 EPS of $3.19–$3.29 inherits a ~$0.19 headwind that is invisible unless the refund is stripped. This is the §7 revision-quality argument with a number attached — hold the **5**, and treat this as the specific thing that makes sell-side FY27 numbers likely too high.
