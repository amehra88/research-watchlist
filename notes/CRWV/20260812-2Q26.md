## 1. Headline read

**First observation for CRWV — no prior notes exist in `notes/CRWV/`, and the watchlist entry carries themes only (no written thesis, no schema scores), so Confirm/Drift/Break below is classified against theme membership rather than against a prior stated thesis.** Directionally the print confirms the AI-infrastructure demand and power-deployment themes and drifts the economics themes: revenue was essentially **in line** (+0.80% vs consensus, versus +5.45% in 1Q26 — FactSet EstimatesConsensus surprise, 2026-08-12), while the beat came from **EBITDA (+5.46%) and EPS (+14.5%)** — i.e. from margin and cost, not from demand upside. The one line that came in light was **street-basis gross profit (-0.70%)**, so the EBITDA beat sits below the gross line, in opex leverage rather than in unit cost of delivery. The raise (FY26 revenue to $12.4–13.2B, YE power to 1.85GW) is real but back-half loaded, and GAAP net loss widened to $626M from $290M a year ago on interest expense — the financing stack, not demand, is the binding constraint to watch.

## 3. Forward guidance

- **FY26 revenue raised to $12.4–13.2B**; FY26 adjusted operating income raised to **$960M–$1.15B**; exit-2026 annualized run-rate revenue **$18.5–19.5B**. (InsiderScore AI summary, Guidance and Outlook.)
- **The raise embeds a steep back-half ramp.** 1H26 actual revenue = $2,078M (1Q26, FactSet FF_SALES) + $2,575M (2Q26) = **$4,653M**. Against the FY26 midpoint of $12.8B, that implies **~$8.15B in 2H26** — roughly +75% versus 1H, or an average of ~$4.07B/quarter versus $2.58B in 2Q. The adjusted operating income guide is more demanding still: 1H26 adj. op income of ~$149M ($21M + $128M) against a $960M–$1.15B full-year range implies **$811M–$1.0B in 2H**.
- Management ties the ramp to power: **1.85GW active by year end (raised from 1.7GW)** off 1.5GW at quarter end, with additions explicitly described as **back-end loaded** (Q&A Review — Turrin, Wells Fargo). Contribution margins on recent deals are said to run **5–10 points higher** than prior quarters, and management expects sequential operating-margin expansion to **low teens by 4Q26**.
- **Guidance philosophy:** ranges rather than point estimates, and framing is confident-to-promotional ("near-term demand exceeds supply," capacity "effectively sold out"). Management asserts **no current material supply-chain or regulatory impact on guidance** — an unusually unqualified statement given the acknowledged rise in local opposition to data centers.
- Next earnings date: **2026-11-11** (InsiderScore `future_earnings_dates`).

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Capex of $9.4B in 2Q26 (slightly above guidance), FY26 capex guided to $35–39B, construction in progress at $11.9B; $18B raised in the quarter including a first Eurobond and a delayed draw term loan, bringing total capital raised above $32B, with weighted average cost of debt down ~300bps YoY. (Paraphrase — InsiderScore AI summary of the 2Q26 call, iacc 43623244: Key Financials and Capital & Liquidity sections.)
- **Implication:** CRWV remains one of the purest listed expressions of AI infrastructure capex, and the capital markets are still funding it at improving cost. The theme is intact; what has changed is that the spend is now increasingly debt-financed at scale, which converts a demand thesis into a demand-plus-financing-access thesis.

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Management claims industry-first bring-up and validation of NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 and new MLPerf records, with strong demand and margin expansion attached to Vera Rubin; separately, 2020-vintage A100s are described as re-contracting at attractive prices. (Paraphrase — InsiderScore AI summary: AI Platform, and Q&A Review — Turrin (Wells Fargo) and Knoblauch (Cantor Fitzgerald).)
- **Implication:** Two distinct topology signals point the same way — first-mover position on the newest NVIDIA rack-scale generation, and evidence that prior-generation fleet retains pricing power rather than stranding. If the A100 re-contracting datapoint generalises, useful-life and residual-value assumptions across the neocloud group are too conservative.

### inference_compute_economics

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Managed inference booked ARR grew from ~$1M to over $100M within a quarter, targeted at $250M+ by year end; new-deal contribution margins are 5–10 points above prior quarters; adjusted operating income rose to $128M from $21M. Against that, GAAP net loss widened to $626M from $290M a year ago, explicitly attributed to interest expense on expanded debt. (Paraphrase — InsiderScore AI summary: AI Platform, Margins, Key Financials; Q&A Review — Zelnick (Deutsche Bank).)
- **Implication:** Pre-financing unit economics are improving on both price and mix, but the economics of the *entity* are moving the other way as D&A and interest scale with the buildout. The theme is not broken — it is shifting mechanism: the swing factor for CRWV's inference economics is now cost of capital and asset-life realisation, not compute pricing. Watch the low-teens 4Q operating-margin target as the falsifiable checkpoint.

### data_center_deployment_constraints

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** +500MW of active power added in 2Q (a record, with 300MW in June alone) to 1.5GW active; contracted power 4.2GW as of August 2026 with a further ~1.5GW in powered land/options/LOIs; YE target raised to 1.85GW; 8GW by 2030; >1GW contracted outside the US including 360MW in Indonesia roughly 18 months out. Management acknowledges increased regulatory scrutiny and local opposition to data centers but states no current impact on guidance or deliverable capacity. (Paraphrase — InsiderScore AI summary: Power & Capacity, Regulatory; Q&A Review — Liu (Evercore) and Turrin (Wells Fargo).)
- **Implication:** Execution against the constraint is currently excellent, which argues the constraint is being solved with capital and land options rather than being absent. The drift is in risk shape, not in level: additions are back-end loaded into a period in which management has also conceded rising local opposition, so the "no current impact" framing is asymmetric — it can only be confirmed quarter by quarter and can only surprise negatively.

- **Overall** (current: **not populated**)
  - Recommendation: **under review** — proposed initial **4, flagged fragile** (analyst-draft).
  - Reasoning: deliberately **not** the mechanical average of 4 and 3. For this business model the two dimensions are not additive — they are substitutes under scarcity. In a supply-constrained market, being first to stand up each new NVIDIA generation *is* the distribution: allocation priority and time-to-deploy do the work a channel would otherwise do, which is why a company with a narrow named-customer base can nonetheless be sold out at premium prices. The fragility is the same mechanism read in reverse — the advantage is contract-and-capital-access based, not owned-demand based, so if GPU supply loosens or financing spreads widen, innovation rate stops compensating for thin distribution and the overall score should fall toward distribution, not stay near innovation. The customer-concentration gap (see §4) is the specific unknown that would resolve this.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

1. **2027 ARR target not given (dodge).** Liu (Evercore) asked about the 2027 ARR target in the context of pricing pressure and regulatory challenges; Agrawal answered by restating the **2026** ARR guidance, and Intrator redirected to community collaboration on data-center siting. The 2027 number was not provided. On a name whose valuation rests on a multi-year run-rate, declining to frame 2027 is the most informative omission on the call.
2. **Renewal economics asserted but unquantified (sandbag).** Chatterjee (JPMorgan) asked about renewal opportunities on older contracts; Agrawal noted only a limited part of the fleet is up for renewal while pointing to significant ARR growth by end-2026. Re-contracting upside is repeatedly implied and never sized — consistent with sandbagging a real option.
3. **Financing structure is driving commercial terms (unusual emphasis).** Knoblauch (Cantor Fitzgerald) asked how DDTL 5.5 affects contract duration; Intrator explained it opens new markets by supporting shorter-term contracts and diversified terms for enterprise. Management volunteering that a debt facility determines what contract durations it can sell is a candid and important disclosure: the financing stack is shaping the product, not just funding it.
4. **Regulatory framing is asymmetric.** Management acknowledged rising scrutiny and local opposition to data centers while asserting no current impact on guidance or on the ability to deliver planned capacity. The claim is unfalsifiable in the affirmative and can only surprise negatively; pair it with the back-end-loaded power schedule (Turrin, Wells Fargo).
5. **Tone datapoint:** InsiderScore recorded **0 challenging exchanges** on this call, versus **1** in 1Q26 and 2 on the FY25 call (`earnings_transcript_info`). Analyst posture softened alongside the raise.

## 9. Vocabulary candidates

Surfaced for operator review only — **not** added to the watchlist.

- **"managed inference"** — CRWV's own product-category label for the fastest-growing line ($1M → $100M+ booked ARR in a quarter). Sits between `inference_compute_economics` and `ai_infrastructure_software` and is not cleanly captured by either.
- **"contracted power" vs "active power" vs "powered land"** — three distinct disclosure states (4.2GW / 1.5GW / ~1.5GW of options and LOIs). The distinction is the core forward indicator for `data_center_deployment_constraints` and deserves a vocabulary anchor.
- **"re-contracting" / GPU fleet asset life** — 2020-vintage A100s re-contracting at attractive prices. Candidate theme: `gpu_asset_life_recontracting` — a residual-value question that cuts across CRWV, NBIS, DOCN and the hyperscalers.
- **"delayed draw term loan" / DDTL** — financing structure explicitly shaping sellable contract duration. Candidate theme: `debt_financed_compute_buildout`, distinct from `ai_infrastructure_capex` because the risk is on the liability side.
- **"annualized run-rate revenue"** as a guided metric distinct from revenue — CRWV guides both; a model refresh should not conflate them.
