## 3. Forward guidance

**Q3 FY27:** revenue $101–105M, non-GAAP gross margin 56–58% (8-K Ex-99.1).

**FY27 (year ending 2027-01-31):** revenue $430–441M — the **low end was raised** — representing 40–43% YoY growth; non-GAAP gross margin 55–57%; adjusted EBITDA $3–10M; capex $100–115M. Management reiterated intent to hold Rule of 40 for the year and to reach annual free-cash-flow positivity.

Three things the guide says that the headline does not:

1. **Q3 guides to a sequential decline** — $101–105M against $116.052M just delivered. Management attributed this to one-time Satellite Services revenue in Q2 that does not repeat, with Q4 expected to bounce back as backlog converts. This is the revenue-quality tell: the beat was partly lumpy delivery revenue, not run-rate.
2. **The implied H2 growth rate decelerates sharply.** H1 FY27 revenue was $210.202M vs $139.651M (+50.5% YoY). The FY27 guide implies H2 of $219.8–230.8M against H2 FY26 of $168.076M, i.e. **+30.8% to +37.3%** — roughly 15–20 points of deceleration off the H1 pace. The "40–43%" full-year headline is carried by a first half that has already happened.
3. **Guidance philosophy is unchanged** — ranges, not point estimates, on all four metrics, with the low-end raise rather than a full-range raise. That is a conservative framing consistent with a business whose quarterly shape depends on delivery timing it does not fully control.

**Backlog moved the wrong way and this was not in the guidance narrative.** Per the 8-K Ex-99.1 backlog reconciliation: backlog **$814.863M at 2026-07-31 vs $900.427M at 2026-01-31** (−9.5%), and remaining performance obligations **$753.117M vs $852.435M** (−11.7%). Approximately 50% of backlog is expected to convert within 12 months (46% of RPO within 12 months, 68% within 24). Revenue is currently being drawn out of backlog faster than new bookings replace it. This is not necessarily alarming for a company converting large sovereign contracts on schedule, but it does sit awkwardly against the "strong visibility from backlog" framing and it is the single number in this print most worth watching next quarter.

**Next earnings date:** InsiderScore `future_earnings_dates` returns no scheduled future date for PL as of this run; the most recent row is the 2026-09-03 Q2 FY27 call just processed. On PL's historical cadence (2025-12-10, 2026-03-19, 2026-06-04, 2026-09-03) the Q3 FY27 call should fall in early-to-mid December 2026.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

PL carries exactly one theme in `config/watchlist.yaml` (`ai_native_vertical`), so there is one subsection. For a T3 name the operator's inline comment is the whole thesis, and it is what section 4 is graded against:

> "Planet Labs - temporal Earth corpus cannot be back-filled, but much of revenue is imagery ACCESS not inference; ~35x sales" (added 2026-08-20)

### ai_native_vertical

- **Status:** **Drift** — and specifically, drift toward the theme fitting PL *less* well than it did at the time the comment was written.

- **Evidence:** The three clauses of the operator's comment resolve differently against this call.

  *On the corpus moat (holds, and was defended explicitly).* Asked by Filatov (Berenberg) about data consistency across hardware generations, Marshall described calibrating new-generation data back to prior iterations specifically to keep the archive compatible — i.e. the back-fill-proof asset is being actively protected against being fragmented by Planet's own hardware refresh, which is the main endogenous way that moat could have been damaged. Asked by Yu (Deutsche Bank) about AI-model proliferation, Marshall argued that commoditization of models *raises* the value of proprietary data. Marshall also repeatedly grounded competitive differentiation in the daily-scan capability (vs Walsh, Citizens JMP; and vs Koontz, Needham, on Maritime Domain Awareness, where he conceded others supply analytics but claimed Planet is uniquely positioned on the underlying daily collection).

  *On "access not inference" (confirmed, and the mix got worse this quarter).* Growth was overwhelmingly Satellite Services and D&I: **D&I +90% YoY, Commercial +15%, Civil Government +5%**. Satellite Services is dedicated sovereign capacity — the furthest thing from inference revenue in Planet's catalogue. Point-in-time revenue recognition was **12% of revenue vs 1% a year ago**, i.e. more delivery/hardware-shaped revenue. Meanwhile the actual inference product, the Queryable Earth AI app, reached only **open beta**; asked directly by Latimore (Northland) about general-availability timing and monetization strategy, Marshall said the focus is on value creation before marketing and gave no monetization mechanism or date. Capex guidance of $100–115M for the year reinforces the direction: the incremental dollar is going into satellites and factories (San Francisco, Berlin), not into an inference layer.

  *On the multiple (needs re-striking by the operator, not by me).* At the $7.124B market cap InsiderScore carries on this record, PL trades at roughly **18.8x TTM sales** (TTM revenue $378.278M) and **~16.4x** the FY27 guide midpoint. That is materially below the "~35x sales" in the 2026-08-20 comment. I cannot reconcile the gap from this call: the as-of date and basis of both the operator's 35x and the vendor market-cap field are unestablished here, and either could explain it. **Flagging rather than asserting** — the operator should re-strike the multiple on their own basis before treating valuation as changed.

- **Implication:** The moat leg of the thesis is intact and was actively reinforced. The concern leg was confirmed and deepened — Planet is executing very well, but it is executing as a **sovereign space-systems supplier**, not as an AI-native vertical company, and this quarter's outperformance moved the mix further in that direction. That matters because the two legs price differently: capacity-and-access revenue is capital-intensive (rising capex), lumpy (Q3 guides down sequentially), procurement-gated, and does not earn a software multiple. The `ai_native_vertical` tag is currently a claim about the archive's *option value*, not about how PL earns money today. If the operator is holding PL as an AI-native name, the thing to underwrite is Queryable Earth monetization — and this call gave no date, no pricing model, and no revenue disclosure for it. The nearest thing to AI monetization evidence was Marshall's and Johnson's argument (to Pendy, Clear Street) that Owl's onboard AI and faster delivery justify **higher pricing on the data** — which is better data monetized as access, i.e. the concern restated as a positive.

## 5. AI positioning signal

- **Current score:** **none — attribute absent.** PL is a T3 (`tier_3_watchlist`) entry, and T3 entries are minimal by design (`ticker`, `themes`, `added_date` only). There is no prior `ai_positioning` score to hold or revise.

- **Recommendation:** **INITIAL PROPOSED SCORE — 3 / 5.** First-time scoring, submitted for operator approval; this is not a revision of anything.
- **Reasoning:** Planet owns a genuinely non-replicable input to Earth-observation AI and is putting real compute at the edge (on-orbit inference, Owl), which is more than a data vendor bolting on a chatbot — that floors the score above 2. But every AI artifact is pre-revenue: beta app, pilots, and a *prospective* price increase. A 4 would require monetized AI revenue or at least a disclosed pricing mechanism, and the call declined to supply one when asked directly. 3 reflects a strong asset with an unproven monetization layer.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: **none — attribute absent**, T3 minimal entry)
  - Evidence: Unusually dense hardware cadence for a company this size — Pelican next-gen tech demo launched and reported successful (payload, on-orbit compute, sat-to-sat comms; enables 30cm-class), additional Tanager and SuperDove satellites shipped, Owl program (3m → 1m class, 10x data and speed) **accelerated in response to customer demand**, optical cross-links brought in-house explicitly for supply-chain independence (Quilty, Quilty Analytics). Satellites launched within 2–4 months of contract award. Archive-calibration discipline across generations is a subtle but real engineering capability. No slipped programs or delays disclosed.
  - Recommendation: **INITIAL PROPOSED SCORE — 4 / 5** (first-time scoring, for operator approval).
- **Distribution** (current: **none — attribute absent**, T3 minimal entry)
  - Evidence: Sovereign channel is genuinely widening — Germany (plus a new Berlin manufacturing facility expected to double capacity, and an Isar Aerospace launch partnership framed as German sovereign capability), Swedish Armed Forces commissioning, a seven-figure European D&I contract and an **$8M NGA** deal (both signed in August, so **not** in Q2 figures), Rwanda Space Agency as the first African national program, New Mexico renewal, a new London office. Pipeline stated at **>$4B identified** with >25% (~$1B) near-term, which Marshall clarified to Latimore means quarters not years, spread across EMEA, APAC and North America. Against that: revenue is concentrating rather than diversifying — D&I is taking a rising share while Commercial (+15%) and Civil Government (+5%) grow slowly, so the non-government motion is not yet a distribution engine. Sales cycles are procurement-gated, and there is no platform or channel leverage (no hyperscaler/marketplace distribution of consequence disclosed).
  - Recommendation: **INITIAL PROPOSED SCORE — 3 / 5** (first-time scoring, for operator approval).
- **Overall** (current: **none — attribute absent**, T3 minimal entry)
  - Recommendation: **INITIAL PROPOSED SCORE — 3 / 5** (first-time scoring, for operator approval).
  - Reasoning — **deliberately not the average of 4 and 3, and lower than innovation alone would imply.** For this specific business model the two dimensions interact *badly* in one respect and *well* in another, and the negative dominates. Negative: Planet's innovation is **capital-embodied** — a capability improvement is not shipped as a software release, it must be manufactured, launched, and then re-sold into a sovereign procurement cycle. So each increment of innovation consumes capex (guided $100–115M) and then waits on a slow, tender-driven distribution channel to monetize; innovation does not compound through distribution the way it does in software, and margin gets absorbed on the way (non-GAAP GM guided down to 55–57% from 59% delivered). Positive, and the reason this is not a 2: the archive creates a **time-asymmetric** advantage that distribution cannot erode — every quarter of collection widens a gap a competitor cannot close by outspending, and the daily-scan claim converts that into **sole-source** wins, which is the rare case where a weak-ish commercial distribution motion still yields uncontested pricing. Net: a durable, defensible, but capital-hungry and procurement-paced franchise. 3 is the honest read, with the upgrade path running through Commercial/Civil re-acceleration or AI monetization rather than through more satellites.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

The InsiderScore transcript **body is unreachable in this environment** — `earnings_transcript_report` returns only the vendor's AI-generated summary even with `text_only`. The items below are therefore **paraphrase-derived**, cross-checked against the 8-K Ex-99.1 where a number is involved; analyst names and firms survive the summary, verbatim quotes do not. The vendor also scored this call at zero challenging exchanges, and I have not manufactured flags to fill the section — these four are grounded in either a filed figure or a directly-dodged question.

1. **RPO/backlog characterization vs the filed number — verify against the full transcript.** Koontz (Needham) asked about RPO dynamics; the summary records Johnson attributing an RPO *increase* to progress on large contracts. The 8-K Ex-99.1 reconciliation shows RPO **down** to $753.117M from $852.435M and backlog **down** to $814.863M from $900.427M since 2026-01-31. I am flagging this as a **discrepancy to resolve, not as a misstatement** — the summary is lossy, and Johnson may have been describing a different measure, a different comparison period, or gross bookings. But if the filed direction is what was verbally framed as an increase, that is the most important thing on this call. Resolve against the full transcript at `https://www.infilings.com/tscript.php?iacc=43717165`.
2. **AI monetization deflected — and it is the question the thesis turns on.** Latimore (Northland) asked specifically when Queryable Earth reaches general availability and how it will be monetized. The answer — beta, focused on value creation before marketing — supplied neither a date nor a pricing model. For a name tagged `ai_native_vertical`, a non-answer here is the single most thesis-relevant moment of the call.
3. **The Q3 sequential decline was pre-explained as "one-time."** Hibshman (Craig-Hallum) drew out that Q3 declines on one-time Satellite Services revenue with Q4 recovering on backlog delivery. Management got ahead of it, which is good practice — but it confirms the Q2 beat contained non-repeating delivery revenue, so the beat's quality is lower than the +11.0% headline suggests. Watch whether Q4 actually bounces.
4. **Capital raise rationale is non-specific.** Benvenuto (Morgan Stanley) asked why raise at all; Johnson answered with "strategic balance-sheet flexibility" and sensitivity to dilution. With ~$865M already on hand and FCF turning positive, "flexibility" without a stated use of proceeds usually means either M&A under consideration or a capacity commitment not yet announced. Worth watching for a subsequent deal.
