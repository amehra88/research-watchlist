## 1. Headline read

Confirm on three themes (`ai_compute_topology`, `inference_compute_economics`, `data_center_deployment_constraints`), Confirm-on-direction-with-Drift-on-mechanism on `ai_infrastructure_capex`, and Drift on `hyperscaler_revenue_concentration` — no Breaks. The single most important fact on the call is a guidance-credibility event rather than a growth datapoint: management guided Q2 group margin "temporarily lower" at Q1, then printed **41% vs. 32% in Q1** — they sandbagged their own guide and beat it. Revenue $582M (+454% YoY, +46% QoQ) beat pre-print consensus by 2.1%, EBITDA $236M beat by 39.8%, ARR hit $3B (+58% QoQ), and contracted power target was raised again to 5 GW. Two structural changes deserve more weight than the beat: (1) the **asset-light partnership model** decouples capacity growth from NBIS's own capex line — a mechanism change inside the still-intact `ai_infrastructure_capex` thesis; (2) the **first capacity auction cleared 15% above any previous price** and short-duration deals are signing at roughly double the per-MW rate of long-term contracts, which effectively breaks the bear case on inference-compute margin compression that the Q1 note had already downgraded to "Drift-positive." The asterisk: FY26 guidance (ARR $7–9B, revenue $3.0–3.4B, 40% margin, $20–25B capex) was **reaffirmed, not raised**, despite H1 tracking ahead and margin already above the full-year target — the first quarter in three with no upward revision to the headline guide.

## 3. Forward guidance

- **FY26 (all reaffirmed, none raised):** ARR exit $7–9B; group revenue $3.0–3.4B; group adj. EBITDA margin ~40%; capex $20–25B. This is the first quarter since Q4'25 without an upward revision to the headline guide — notable because Q2 margin (41%) already exceeds the full-year target and H1 revenue is tracking to the upper half.
- **Contracted power:** year-end target **raised to 5 GW** (from 4+ GW at Q1'26, 3.5 GW contracted then).
- **Connected power:** 800 MW–1 GW for 2026 reaffirmed, contributing to active capacity in **H1 2027**. Note the gap between 5 GW contracted and ~1 GW connected — connected power, not contracted, is the near-term revenue constraint.
- **2027 capacity:** more than **1 GW of new capacity per year starting 2027**, supported by >$40B contracted backlog. Management stated it could sell its entire 2027 capacity today at current terms but is **deliberately withholding capacity** to capture higher-priced short-duration and later-priced contracts.
- **Financing:** $775M asset-backed facility secured July 2026 at SOFR+250bps against contracted cash flow; >$9B of customer prepayments expected in 2026; almost no corporate-level debt, leaving headroom for additional debt or equity-linked instruments. Management floated GPU assets becoming a financeable asset class.
- **Tone / philosophy:** sentiment Positive, unchanged from Q1'26 (InsiderScore AI scoring; note their model was recalibrated to be *less* generous, so flat Positive is mildly favorable on a like-for-like basis). Guidance philosophy has shifted again — from Q4'25's "ARR is the meaningful metric" to a Q2'26 framing built on **priced capacity**: $/MW, auction clearing levels, backlog, and contracted-vs-connected power. That is a more falsifiable disclosure set than ARR alone and should be treated as an improvement in disclosure quality.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm (direction) / Drift (mechanism)
- **Evidence:** Q2 capex $5.7B, FY26 $20–25B reaffirmed, contracted power target raised to 5 GW, >1 GW of new capacity annually from 2027 (prepared remarks, Guidance and Outlook; Infrastructure and Expansion). Against that, the newly introduced **asset-light partnership model**: partners "finance, build, and operate facilities, while Nebius provides the platform and demand," yielding high-margin revenue with minimal balance-sheet impact; management reports "dozens of partner inquiries" and explicitly flags the model as early-stage (Asset-Light Model section).
- **Implication:** The thesis that NBIS is a top-tier independent source of AI-infra capex is confirmed on direction — the spend is real, funded, and the power target went up again. But the *mechanism* is changing: if asset-light scales, NBIS's own capex line stops being a clean proxy for the capacity it commands. For read-throughs this cuts two ways — the physical build still happens (unchanged positive for VRT, BE, GEV, PWR, ANET, COHR, LITE, and NVDA silicon pull-through), but sizing that build off NBIS's reported capex will progressively understate it. Treat this as Drift on how to model the thesis, not on whether it holds. Discipline check: no signed asset-light partner was disclosed, only inquiries — do not yet put revenue on this line.

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Rapid deployment of new GPU generations (Vera Rubin and Blackwell); Token Factory providing day-0 support for leading open-source models with performance validated by independent benchmarks; further integration of the Tavily, Eigen AI and Clarifai acquisitions; developer community scaled to **2.5M** (vs. ~700K cited at Q4'25); explicit positioning as a vertically integrated platform spanning training, inference and agentic workflows (Technology & Product section).
- **Implication:** The topology thesis — AI compute consolidating into vertically integrated platform layers rather than commoditizing into bare IaaS — continues to hold, and the developer-community number is the cleanest new proxy for it (3.5x growth in roughly two quarters). Where Q1 showed the stack being *assembled* via M&A, Q2 shows it being *used*: day-0 model support and benchmark validation are adoption evidence, not roadmap evidence. The capacity auction (see below) is itself a topology signal — a company selling undifferentiated compute cannot run a price-discovery auction and clear above prior highs.

### inference_compute_economics

- **Status:** Confirm — and the bear case, downgraded to "Drift-positive" at Q4'25 and "approaching Break" at Q1'26, should now be treated as broken
- **Evidence:** First capacity auction for Blackwell-generation chips cleared **15% above any previous level and 20% above the pipeline**; short-duration deals (up to 6 months) signing at **$40–50M per MW** against **$20–25M per MW** on the four long-duration landmark deals; group adj. EBITDA margin 41% (from 32% in Q1), Nebius AI segment margin 50% (from 45%); management deliberately shifting to selling capacity closer to deployment to optimize price (Pricing & Auctions; Key Financials).
- **Implication:** The bear thesis was that supply growth compresses per-GPU economics. Two quarters of margin expansion plus an auction that cleared above every prior print is direct contrary evidence, and it is now price evidence rather than margin inference — a company with deteriorating unit economics does not voluntarily expose its pricing to an auction. The Q1 note's caveat ("if Q2 margin compresses as guided and Q3/Q4 don't recover, revisit") resolved in the bull direction and then some. **Caveat carried forward:** the $40–50M/MW vs. $20–25M/MW comparison is not stated on a like-for-like basis in the summary — a 6-month contract at $45M/MW and a multi-year contract at $22M/MW may differ in annualization, not in price. The auction premium (15%/20%) is the cleaner datapoint because it is explicitly like-for-like against prior Blackwell pricing. Do not carry the 2x per-MW figure into a model without resolving the basis.

### data_center_deployment_constraints

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Construction at Vineland, NJ reported complete with engineering fit-out underway, but progressing through "procedural delays" and ongoing public hearings; switch to Bloom Energy fuel cells for on-site power framed as an enhancement with no significant timeline impact; contracted power target raised to 5 GW while connected power for 2026 stays at 800 MW–1 GW, contributing to active capacity only in H1 2027; management emphasizes community engagement and regional flexibility in cloud contracts to minimize location risk (Data Center Development; Infrastructure and Expansion).
- **Implication:** The constraint thesis is confirmed with unusual precision this quarter. The 5 GW contracted vs. ~1 GW connected gap is the constraint quantified: NBIS can contract power far faster than it can energize it, and the binding delay is regulatory/procedural (public hearings) plus grid interconnect — which is why on-site generation (Bloom fuel cells) is being adopted. Read-through positive for BE specifically (on-site fuel cells as an interconnect workaround is a demand mechanism, not just a green-power story), and for GEV/PWR/VRT on the build. The Q1 note flagged Pennsylvania's 1.2 GW campus and asked whether US sites would hit permitting friction; the answer arrived at a **different site** — Vineland NJ — and Pennsylvania was not mentioned this call. Treat the Q1 question about Pennsylvania as still open.

### hyperscaler_revenue_concentration

- **Status:** Drift (direction reverses — concentration easing, but unverified)
- **Evidence:** Four landmark AI cloud deals closed in Q2, each averaging over $1B; >$40B contracted backlog described as being with "investment-grade customers"; neither Meta nor Microsoft named in the Q2 summary; management emphasizes flexibility in deal durations, pricing and customer mix, and is holding back 2027 capacity rather than preselling it to a small number of counterparties (Capacity & Contracts).
- **Implication:** This is the first quarter in three where the concentration read moves toward diversification rather than away from it. Four separate >$1B counterparties plus a deliberate strategy of withholding capacity from large presales is structurally de-concentrating, and the auction mechanism is de-concentrating by construction — it sells to whoever bids highest, not to whoever is largest. **But this is a direction call, not a measurement.** Backlog composition is undisclosed; "investment-grade" is a credit-quality descriptor, not evidence of a diversified counterparty list, and the $27B Meta contract from Q1 presumably still sits inside that $40B backlog — which would mean the backlog is *more* Meta-weighted than the four new deals suggest. The metric flagged at Q1 (% of contracted revenue from top 2 customers) remains unextracted; the 20-F/A filed 2026-05-22 covers FY25 and predates all of this. Keep the cautionary Drift label but note the arrow has turned.

## 5. AI positioning signal

- **Current score:** unpopulated in both watchlist entries (schema gap — see §10). Prior recommendations: initialize at 5 (Q4'25, reaffirmed Q1'26).
- **Evidence from this call:** Day-0 support in Token Factory for leading open-source models with independently benchmarked performance; simultaneous deployment of Blackwell and Vera Rubin generations; developer community at 2.5M; four >$1B AI cloud contracts closed in a single quarter; a capacity auction clearing 15% above prior highs; $40B contracted backlog. The asset-light model is a positioning signal in its own right — it asserts that NBIS's platform and demand aggregation, not its balance sheet, is the scarce asset.
- **Recommendation:** hold at the prior recommendation of **5**.
- **Reasoning:** Nothing on this call challenges a 5, and the auction result strengthens it — NBIS is demonstrating pricing power in the scarcest input in the AI stack. The score is already at ceiling, so the marginal information should go into competitive advantage and PII rather than here.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: unpopulated; prior recommendation: 4+ at Q1'26, raised from 4 at Q4'25)
  - Evidence: Vera Rubin and Blackwell both deploying; Token Factory day-0 model support with independent benchmark validation; three acquisitions (Tavily, Eigen AI, Clarifai) moving from closed to integrated; developer community 700K → 2.5M; two genuinely novel commercial mechanisms shipped in one quarter (capacity auctions, asset-light partnerships).
  - Recommendation: **hold at 4+**. Q2 is strong but it is execution on the Q1 agenda plus commercial-model innovation, not a step-change in technical capability. A 5 should require evidence that the platform layer is defensible against hyperscaler equivalents, which this call does not provide.
- **Distribution** (current: unpopulated; prior recommendation: 4 at Q1'26, raised from 3+ at Q4'25)
  - Evidence: four >$1B deals with distinct counterparties; >$40B backlog; capacity auction generating competitive bidding with winning bidders signalling intent to return; short-duration premium segment opened as a new demand channel; "dozens of partner inquiries" on asset-light; management claims it could sell all 2027 capacity today.
  - Recommendation: **revise to 4+** from 4. The auction is the reason. The Q4'25 rationale for withholding credit was that NBIS had no native enterprise channel and distribution was supply-constrained rather than demand-driven. An auction that clears above prior highs, with repeat-bidder intent, is a distribution mechanism NBIS built itself — it converts scarce supply into a price-discovery channel rather than a bilateral negotiation, and it scales without headcount.
- **Overall** (current: unpopulated; prior recommendation: 4+ at Q1'26)
  - Recommendation: **hold at 4+**.
  - Reasoning: Innovation 4+ and distribution 4+ do not mechanically imply a raise to 5, and the interaction for this specific business model argues against one. NBIS's advantage is a flywheel between capacity execution (5 GW contracted, owned build, on-site power) and commercial mechanism design (auctions, short-duration premium, asset-light) — the two dimensions reinforce because scarce capacity is what makes the auction clear high, and the auction's price signal is what justifies the next capex tranche. That flywheel is real and is why 4+ is right rather than 4. But it remains conditional on scarcity: every mechanism NBIS deployed this quarter monetizes a shortage, and none of them would survive a supply-normalized market intact. A 5 should be reserved for advantages that are structural rather than cyclical (NVDA/CUDA), and NBIS's is still cyclical — extremely well-played, but cyclical. The asset-light model is the first genuine candidate for a structural moat here, because it monetizes demand aggregation rather than owned capacity; if it converts inquiries into signed partners at scale, revisit the overall score.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

The InsiderScore report is an AI-generated summary organized by topic, not a verbatim Q&A transcript. Flags below are therefore attributed to summary sections and to how management framed topics — no flag is attributed to a named analyst or a specific question.

- **Margin sandbag, confirmed by outcome (highest-signal item on the call):** at Q1'26 management explicitly guided Q2 group margin "temporarily lower" on investment timing before an H2 acceleration. Q2 delivered 41% against Q1's 32%. Guided down, printed up. Read charitably this is conservatism; read strictly it means Q1's forward color was materially wrong in a self-serving direction. Either way, discount future "temporarily lower" framings from this management team and treat the reaffirmed 40% FY26 margin target as a likely floor rather than a target.
- **Guidance reaffirmed, not raised — the one place the print and the guide disagree:** margin is already above the full-year target and H1 revenue is tracking to the upper half of $3.0–3.4B, yet nothing moved. The benign reading is conservatism consistent with the sandbag above. The non-benign reading is the ARR arithmetic: $3.0B at end-June against a $7–9B December exit requires a $4–6B step in two quarters, gated by connected power that management says contributes to active capacity in **H1 2027**, not H2 2026. These two disclosures sit in tension and the summary does not reconcile them. This is the single highest-leverage diligence item for Q3.
- **"Could sell out 2027 capacity today" is unfalsifiable as stated:** management claims it could sell all of 2027 at current terms but is deliberately withholding for higher-priced short-duration deals. This is a strong statement of pricing confidence and it is consistent with the auction result — but no figure was given for how much of 2027 is already contracted, which is the number that would make the claim checkable. Ask for 2027 contracted-vs-available capacity at Q3.
- **Asset-light is narrative ahead of substance:** "dozens of partner inquiries" and "early positive signs," with the model explicitly described as early-stage. Zero signed partners, zero revenue, no economics disclosed. Management is getting credit in the framing for a model that has not yet transacted. Track signed partners as the conversion metric; until then this belongs in the thesis discussion (§4) and not in the model.
- **Per-MW pricing disclosed but basis unstated:** $20–25M/MW on long-duration deals vs. $40–50M/MW on ≤6-month deals is presented as evidence of short-duration premium. If those are contract values per MW rather than annualized rates, the comparison overstates the premium substantially. New and welcome disclosure, but do not annualize it without confirmation.
- **Zero challenging exchanges:** `earnings_transcript_info` reports `challenging_analyze_exchanges: 0` for this call, against a Q4'25 call that featured explicit analyst pushback on the ARR-vs-revenue framing. The Street has stopped pressing. That is a sentiment tailwind and simultaneously a diligence warning — the tension flagged in the second bullet went unchallenged on the call.

## 9. Vocabulary candidates

- **Capacity auction / compute price discovery** — the most consequential new concept on the call. Auctioning compute capacity converts an opaque bilateral market into a public price signal. If CRWV or others adopt it, per-MW clearing prices become a trackable industry indicator. Strong candidate under `inference_compute_economics`.
- **Asset-light infrastructure partnership** — partner finances/builds/operates, platform owner supplies demand and software. A neocloud analogue to the hotel-management or franchise model. Candidate under `ai_infrastructure_capex`; materially changes how capex is read as a capacity proxy.
- **$/MW as the unit of AI cloud pricing** — NBIS disclosed $20–25M/MW (long duration) and $40–50M/MW (short duration). If this becomes standard disclosure, it is the cleanest cross-company comparability metric in the neocloud group.
- **Contracted vs. connected power** — 5 GW contracted against 800 MW–1 GW connected. The ratio is a deployment-constraint metric and is more informative than either figure alone. Candidate under `data_center_deployment_constraints`.
- **Contracted backlog with investment-grade counterparties** — $40B, and explicitly framed as the collateral base for asset-backed financing. Credit quality of backlog is emerging as a financing input, not just a demand signal.
- **GPU financing as an asset class** — management floated GPUs becoming financeable collateral. If this develops, it changes the cost of capital for the entire neocloud group and is a thesis-relevant cross-ticker item.
- **Short-duration premium contracts** — ≤6-month deals at a large per-MW premium; a new contract-tenor axis alongside the take-or-pay/option structures flagged at Q1.
