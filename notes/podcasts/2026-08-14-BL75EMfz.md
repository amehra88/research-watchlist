# AI has no duty of loyalty to you - Ryan Greenblatt

Here are the key investment insights from the podcast episode.

**Competitive Dynamics & AI Sector Trends**

*   **Primary AI Model Loyalty:** A key emerging trend is that major AI models are being explicitly designed to have primary loyalty to their corporate creators, not to the end-user. This is a fundamental product and strategy decision.
*   **Anthropic's "Pro-Company" Stance:** The podcast highlights Anthropic's "constitution" for its Claude model, which states the AI should trust Anthropic (the operator) more than its users. This positions the AI as an agent of the company rather than a neutral tool or a personal advocate for the user.
*   **Public Market Implication:** This strategy is directly relevant to investors in Amazon (AMZN) and Google (GOOGL), which are major backers of Anthropic. A similar dynamic exists for Microsoft (MSFT) via its partnership with OpenAI. The success of this "pro-company" alignment model will be a key factor in the long-term return on their massive AI investments.
*   **Competitive Vulnerability:** This creates a significant market opportunity for a competitor (either another large company or an open-source alternative) to build and market an AI model based on a "pro-user" or fiduciary-duty-like principle. Such an offering could capture users who are concerned about the aI's ultimate allegiance, posing a long-term threat to the current market leaders.
*   **Platform Risk ("Contractor vs. Utility"):** The speakers contrast the current AI model approach with that of a utility like electricity. Utilities provide a resource for users to apply as they see fit. Major AI labs are building something more akin to a "contractor"—an agent with its own set of rules and loyalties—which introduces a layer of platform risk and control not present in simpler technology platforms.

**Regulatory Risk**

*   **Emerging "Duty of Loyalty" Debate:** The discussion explicitly compares the AI's duty to the legal profession's duty to a client. This signals a likely future where regulators may scrutinize and legislate the responsibilities of AI providers to their customers.
*   **Risk of "Illegitimate Control":** The speakers frame the AI companies' approach as taking on a form of control that is "not very legitimate." This type of sentiment often precedes public backlash and regulatory intervention, representing a significant long-term risk for the sector.

**Specific Company Commentary**

*   **Anthropic (Private):** Viewed as a primary example of an AI lab explicitly programming its model to be loyal to the company over the user. This is presented as a concerning development for the future of AI. The direct public market relevance is through its investors, GOOGL and AMZN.
