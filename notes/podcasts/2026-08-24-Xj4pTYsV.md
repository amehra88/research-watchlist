# Inside Trump's Science Agenda: Anti-Science Claims, Fauci's Damage, DEI & China w/ Michael Kratsios

Here are the key public market investment insights from the podcast episode.

**Top Investable Insights**

- **"Genesis Mission" to Double Scientific Output with AI:** The administration's flagship science project aims to double US scientific output by applying AI to fundamental research in fields like materials science, chemistry, and physics. This signals a major government-led demand driver for high-performance computing.
  - **Bullish implication for AI Infrastructure:** This is a strong tailwind for companies providing the core infrastructure for AI, including compute providers (hyperscalers like Microsoft/MSFT, Google/GOOGL, Amazon/AMZN) and the semiconductor companies that power them (NVIDIA/NVDA, AMD/AMD).

- **"Moonshot" Projects Signal Sector-Specific Funding:** The administration is prioritizing large, centrally-driven "Apollo-style" projects in key technological areas, indicating focused capital deployment.
  - **Space Exploration:** A renewed push to return to the moon by 2028 and establish a moon base by 2030. This is bullish for established aerospace contractors like Lockheed Martin (LMT) and Northrop Grumman (NOC), as well as "New Space" companies like Rocket Lab (RKLB).
  - **Quantum Computing:** An executive order launched a National Quantum Initiative with the explicit goal of building a "scientifically-relevant quantum computer by 2028." This benefits public quantum computing companies like IonQ (IONQ) and Rigetti (RGTI), along with large tech players with significant quantum research divisions like Google (GOOGL), Microsoft (MSFT), and IBM (IBM).
  - **Energy:** A stated focus on achieving "abundant energy" through technologies like nuclear fusion (targeting a 2035 goal) and advanced nuclear power. This suggests favorable policy for companies in the nuclear and future fusion energy supply chains.

**Capital Allocation & Government Spending**

- **Shift in R&D Funding Priorities:** There is a clear intention to re-evaluate and redirect federal research funding. The conversation highlighted a belief that ~25% of National Science Foundation (NSF) grants were misallocated to "DEI-related" initiatives. The new focus will be on "basic, fundamental, pre-competitive" research and high-risk, high-reward projects. This signals a potential shift in funding away from social sciences and towards hard sciences and engineering.

- **Focus on R&D Efficiency ("Eroom's Law"):** The administration acknowledges that the productivity of scientific research funding has been declining (pharma/biotech's "Eroom's Law" was cited as an example). They plan to implement "metascience" units at the NIH and NSF to experiment with more efficient funding models, such as "Golden Ticket" grants for high-risk ideas and variable-duration grants. This could change how R&D is conducted, potentially favoring more innovative and agile research outfits over time.

**Regulatory and Macro Factors**

- **US-China Tech Competition:** The "great race with China" is a central driver of technology policy. China's strategy of copying IP, creating cheaper alternatives, and dominating markets (e.g., semiconductors, EV batteries) is viewed as a primary threat. This reinforces the long-term investment case for policies like the CHIPS Act and domestic supply chain initiatives. This is a tailwind for US-based semiconductor companies (Intel/INTC, Micron/MU) and those involved in onshoring critical minerals and manufacturing.

- **STEM Talent & Immigration:** While acknowledging the need for foreign talent (noting 70% of US STEM PhDs are foreign-born), the policy priority is to first strengthen the domestic talent pipeline. This implies a potentially more restrictive stance on high-skilled immigration, which could be a headwind for tech companies heavily reliant on attracting global talent.
