# The Problem With How LLMs Generate Text - Grant Sanderson

Based on the podcast episode, here are the key insights for public technology and AI investors.

**AI Sector Trends & Architectural Risk**

The core investment insight is a potential limitation in the dominant Large Language Model (LLM) architecture (autoregressive transformers) used by major AI players like Google (GOOGL), Microsoft (MSFT)/OpenAI, and others.

- **Autoregression's Weakness:** The current method of generating text by serially predicting the most probable next word ("autoregression") is inherently biased against creativity and true novelty. The model is optimized to find the *most likely* next step, whereas groundbreaking insights often come from making *unlikely* connections between different domains.

- **Potential Capability Ceiling:** This suggests a potential ceiling on the capabilities of the current generation of AI. While models will continue to improve, the architecture itself may be a barrier to achieving human-like creativity or generating truly disruptive ideas. This challenges the narrative that simply scaling up existing models with more data and compute will lead to AGI.

**Competitive Dynamics & Valuation Implications**

- **Homogenized Risk:** This architectural limitation is a systemic risk that applies to all current leaders in the LLM space. No single player currently has a clear architectural advantage to overcome this. The long-term winner may not be the one who scales the current paradigm the fastest, but the one who develops a fundamentally new architecture.

- **Questioning Valuations:** This technical limitation calls into question the long-term, super-exponential growth assumptions embedded in the valuations of AI leaders. If the models are fundamentally better at summarizing and predicting the probable rather than innovating the improbable, their ultimate economic value may be lower than current market expectations.

- **Capex ROI Risk:** The massive capital expenditures by hyperscalers (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META) on GPUs for training these models could face diminishing returns. If the underlying architecture has inherent flaws in producing high-value, novel output, the long-term ROI on this massive compute build-out could be lower than anticipated.
