# Why AI labs are shelving their best models - Dylan Patel

**AI Investment Insights: Dylan Patel on Dwarkesh Patel**

**Key Thesis: Slowdown at the AI Frontier Poses Risk to Incumbents**

Frontier AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic are intentionally shelving their most advanced models due to safety concerns and regulatory advocacy. This self-imposed slowdown erodes their competitive advantage and creates an opportunity for competitors to catch up, posing a risk to the valuation of the AI leaders and their public backers (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN).

**Valuation & Capital Allocation Signals**

*   **"Revenue per Megawatt" is the Key Metric:** The ability to generate revenue from energy/compute consumption is the critical ROI measure for AI.
*   **Stalling ROI Threatens Capex Cycle:** When frontier labs withhold their best models, their "revenue per megawatt" stalls or declines as the rest of the market catches up. This makes it harder to justify buying more incremental, expensive compute.
*   **Bearish Signal for Hyperscalers:** A prolonged pause in releasing superior models challenges the massive capex narrative for hyperscalers backing the leading labs. Their ability to monetize a performance edge diminishes if that edge is not deployed. This is a potential headwind for Microsoft, Google, and Amazon.
*   **Potential Risk for Semiconductors:** While not immediate, a deceleration in model capability improvement could eventually temper the frantic pace of GPU orders, posing a long-term risk to the growth trajectory of companies like NVIDIA (NVDA).

**Competitive Dynamics**

*   **Closing the Gap:** The slowdown is not due to a lack of progress, but a deliberate choice to not release new models. This allows competitors, including open-source and Chinese models, to close the performance gap with currently available models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet).
*   **Erosion of Moat:** The competitive moat of OpenAI and Anthropic is directly tied to their model superiority. By holding back their next-generation models ("Astra," "Model 2"), they are compressing their lead over the field.

**Regulatory Factors**

*   **Self-Inflicted Headwinds:** Regulations advocated by the major labs are slowing their own progress more than that of open-source or international competitors. This "regulatory capture" effort appears to be backfiring by levelling the playing field for others rather than cementing the leaders' position.
*   **Regulatory Uncertainty as a Drag:** The risk of future regulation is a primary cause for the labs' cautious deployment strategy. Any actual implementation of stringent regulation could permanently cap the monetization potential of the most powerful models.
