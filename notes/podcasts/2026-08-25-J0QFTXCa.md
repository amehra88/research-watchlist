# David Sacks: If Dario Got His Way on AI Regulation, It Would Destroy Anthropic

### AI Investment Thesis & Risk Analysis

The core investment thesis for frontier AI model companies (like Anthropic, backed by GOOG/AMZN, and OpenAI, backed by MSFT) is based on a fragile competitive advantage.

- **Valuation Driver:** These companies justify their high valuations and premium pricing ("massive premium for tokens") by maintaining a slim, ~6-month technological lead over open-source models. A significant portion of the market is willing to pay this premium for access to the most advanced "frontier intelligence."

- **Primary Risk (Commoditization):** This business model is a "hamster wheel." The companies must continuously innovate to maintain their lead. If they ever slip up or the pace of open-source development closes the gap, their pricing power will evaporate, and their revenue will collapse as their models become commoditized.

### Regulatory Factors Affecting AI Valuations

The most significant external threat to the frontier AI model business is regulation.

- **"FAA for AI" Threat:** A proposed stringent regulatory framework, likened to an "FAA for AI," would require lengthy pre-approval for new model releases.

- **Market Impact:** Such a framework, with potential approval times of years (the FAA takes a minimum of 5 years to certify new airframes), would instantly erase the ~6-month lead that underpins the entire business model of companies like Anthropic and OpenAI.

- **Competitive Dynamics Shift:** This would halt US innovation, allowing open-source and Chinese competitors to catch up and surpass the current leaders. Any legislative movement in this direction is a major bearish signal for the ecosystem built around these frontier models, including their public market backers.
