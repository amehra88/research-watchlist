# David Friedberg: AI Models are Training on YOUR DATA

Here are the key public market and AI investment insights from the podcast clip.

### Enterprise AI Adoption & Data Security

The central thesis is that major AI models (LLMs) are using proprietary data from user conversations to train future versions of the model. This creates a significant risk of intellectual property (IP) leakage for enterprise customers.

- **Anecdotal Evidence of IP Leakage:** David Friedberg provides a specific example where a novel scientific insight shared in a chat with an LLM was later incorporated into a newer version of that same model, accessible from a different account.
- **"Unidentifiable Data" is a Misnomer:** While the model strips personal identifiers, it still absorbs and utilizes the core IP and novel insights from the conversation. The "insight" itself becomes training data.
- **Barrier to Enterprise Adoption:** This represents a major trust issue and a potential headwind for the adoption of closed-source AI models by enterprises that handle sensitive R&D, legal, or strategic data. They risk inadvertently training the model for their competitors.

### Competitive Dynamics

The issue of data security creates a new competitive vector in the AI market, primarily between closed-source and open-source models.

- **Risk for Closed-Source Providers:** Companies like Google (GOOGL), Microsoft (MSFT) via its partnership with OpenAI, and Amazon (AMZN) via its partnership with Anthropic, face a significant trust hurdle. Their terms of service, which allow for using anonymized data for model improvement, are a liability when selling to IP-sensitive enterprises.
- **Tailwind for Open-Source Models:** This dynamic creates a strong argument for enterprises to use open-source models (like Meta's (META) Llama or others). Open-source allows a company to run the model on its own private infrastructure, ensuring that chat logs and proprietary data are never sent to a third party and cannot be used for training a public-facing model.
- **Shift in Value Proposition:** The competitive advantage may shift from having the single best-performing model to having the most secure and trustworthy model for enterprise use cases.

### Key Investment Theses

- **Bearish/Risk Factor for Closed-Source AI:** The long-term enterprise monetization strategy for public hyperscalers building proprietary models is at risk if the market perceives them as insecure "IP vacuums." Scrutiny of enterprise AI contracts and data-handling provisions will intensify.
- **Bullish for Open-Source & Private AI Infrastructure:** The need for data control will likely drive investment towards companies that enable private AI deployments. This includes the hyperscalers themselves as infrastructure providers (renting out GPUs for private training/inference on open-source models) and companies in the data infrastructure space like Snowflake (SNOW) or Databricks (private) that facilitate secure data management for AI.
