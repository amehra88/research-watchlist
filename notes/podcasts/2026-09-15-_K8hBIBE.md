# Elon Proposes Adversarial Peer Reviews for AI Safety

Here are the public technology and AI investing insights from the podcast clip.

**Regulatory & Product Liability Risk**

The central thesis is that a framework for AI product liability is emerging, which presents a significant and unquantified financial risk for AI companies.

- **Adversarial Peer Reviews:** Elon Musk proposes that major AI competitors (e.g., Google/GOOGL, Microsoft/MSFT, Meta/META, and their associated labs) should be required to test each other's models for safety before public release.
- **Enormous Liability Risk:** If a company is warned by a competitor that its model is unsafe but releases it anyway, and that AI subsequently causes harm, the legal liability would be "enormous." This is compared to a "big tobacco level settlement," implying a catastrophic financial risk.
- **Investment in Safety:** This liability framework creates a strong financial incentive for companies to invest heavily in safety R&D and internal testing. This could increase operating expenses but is necessary to mitigate massive legal and reputational downside. Existing product liability laws are seen as applicable to AI.

**Competitive Dynamics**

Musk's proposal would introduce a new, formal layer of competition focused on safety and security.

- **Offense and Defense:** Companies would be incentivized to find flaws in competitors' models (offense) while hardening their own (defense).
- **Public Scrutiny:** This system would allow competitors to publicly raise alarms about each other's products, potentially influencing public perception, enterprise adoption, and stock prices based on perceived safety.

**Macro & Geopolitical Factors**

- **The China Factor:** Any Western AI regulation or safety framework must be something China would also accept. Otherwise, it risks "handicapping" Western companies in the global AI race, ceding an advantage to Chinese competitors who may operate with fewer constraints. This is a key long-term risk for US-based AI leaders.
