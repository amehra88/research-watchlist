# Where Does the Money Actually Go in AI? - Dylan Patel

Here are the key investment insights from the podcast episode.

**Key Market Call: Shift in Semiconductor Value Capture**

*   Value capture is shifting within the semiconductor supply chain, specifically from foundries to memory producers.
*   The speaker makes a direct call: "TSMC (TSM) makes way less value than the memory guys" currently. This is a significant reversal from 2023 when memory companies were making "no money" despite being critical to AI systems.
*   This implies a bullish view on memory-focused semiconductor companies (e.g., Micron - MU, SK Hynix) relative to foundries like TSMC, as they are now capturing a larger portion of the hardware profit pool.

**AI Sector Value Chain Dynamics & Valuations**

*   **Model Layer Inflection:** The "Model Layer" (companies developing foundational models) has undergone a dramatic profitability shift. A year ago, they had negative gross margins (selling tokens for less than the infrastructure cost). Now, they are generating "massive positive gross margins." This signals a fundamental improvement in the business models of companies like OpenAI (impacting Microsoft - MSFT) and Anthropic (impacting Amazon - AMZN, and Alphabet - GOOGL).
*   **Hardware Layer (Historical):** A year ago, the hardware supply chain (chips, fabs) was the only profitable part of the AI stack, capturing all the gross margin while other layers lost money. This dynamic is now changing.
*   **Application Layer Lag:** The "App Layer" has so far generated "very little value." This is a bearish signal for pure-play AI application companies and suggests that established enterprise software players have not yet demonstrated significant profit capture from integrating AI features.

**Overarching Investment Thesis**

The primary thesis is that the locus of value capture in the AI stack is highly dynamic and is shifting in real-time. Investors should not assume that the winners of the last 12-18 months (primarily core GPU makers and foundries) will continue to capture the lion's share of profits. The analysis suggests that value is moving "up the stack" from core compute to memory and now to the model layer itself, while the application layer remains unproven.
