# Why Jane Street Makes More From Claude Than Anthropic Does - Dylan Patel

Here are the public market investing insights from the podcast episode.

**Key Takeaway**
The primary investment thesis is that the majority of economic value from AI will be captured by the companies *applying* the models (the application layer), not the foundational model providers themselves. The value users derive far exceeds the cost of the models, indicating that the best investment opportunities are in the beneficiaries of AI, not necessarily the creators.

**Public Company-Specific Insights**

*   **Meta (META) - Bullish View:** Meta is highlighted as a prime example of a company capturing immense value by applying AI. As a large customer of foundational models (rumored to be up to 10% of Anthropic's business at one point), Meta generates far more value through efficiencies in its core business—such as optimizing ad algorithms and increasing user engagement—than it pays for the AI services. This makes Meta a key beneficiary of the AI trend, as the technology directly enhances its existing profit centers.

**AI Sector & Value Chain Dynamics**

*   **Value Capture at the Application Layer:** The core dynamic discussed is the significant disconnect between the value created by AI models and the profit captured by the model providers (like private companies Anthropic and OpenAI).
*   **Customer ROI is Extremely High:** Enterprise customers are extracting massive ROI from using these models. The example given is Jane Street (a private trading firm) generating "way, way more value" from Anthropic's Claude than Anthropic generates in profit. This suggests the pricing power of model providers may be limited as the value accrues to the end-user's application.
*   **Implication for Model Provider Valuations:** This trend implies a cautious stance on the long-term profitability and valuation multiples for pure-play foundational model companies. They are an enabling technology, but like other infrastructure, may not capture a large percentage of the total economic value they unlock.

**Macro/Valuation Commentary**

*   **Framing the Total Addressable Market (TAM):** The potential economic impact of AI is framed in terms of replacing labor. A "gigawatt" of AI compute could potentially sustain a population of one million "AI white-collar workers." At an average salary of $100,000, this single gigawatt of AI could unlock $100 billion in value annually, indicating a massive TAM for AI-driven productivity.
*   **Deflationary Impact on Labor:** A major long-term consequence of AGI will be the collapse in the "value of labor." As AI becomes capable of performing tasks like software engineering at scale, it will exert significant downward pressure on the wages of human white-collar workers, fundamentally altering the cost structure for technology companies.
