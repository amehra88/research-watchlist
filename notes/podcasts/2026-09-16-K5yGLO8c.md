# Why the Next Generation of Enterprise Software Looks Nothing Like Salesforce

Here are the key investment insights from the podcast episode.

**High-Level Thesis: The CRM Moat is Under Attack**

The core thesis is that the traditional "System of Record" model, pioneered by companies like Salesforce, is being replaced by an AI-native "System of Reality."

-   **Old World (Salesforce):** Value is in the rigid, structured database (the schema). Humans are responsible for manually entering data into predefined fields. This model is vulnerable because the actual "source of truth" (emails, call logs, documents) lives outside the CRM.
-   **New World (AI-Native):** Value is in the intelligence layer that automatically synthesizes unstructured data from multiple sources (email, call recorders, data warehouses) into a coherent "world model." This model automates data entry and allows users to query business reality using natural language, fundamentally changing the user interface from forms to chat. This shift suggests the primary moat of legacy CRMs—their structured database—is becoming less defensible.

**Public Company Commentary**

-   **Salesforce (CRM) & Hubspot (HUBS):** The discussion frames these incumbents as having a significant "brownfield" advantage. Their deep entrenchment in large enterprises creates high switching costs due to user training and existing workflows. A new VP of Sales, for example, is likely trained on Salesforce and will resist adopting a new system. However, their core architectural reliance on manual data entry and rigid schemas is a long-term vulnerability. The new paradigm is "intelligence is greater than schema," which directly challenges the value proposition of these legacy systems.

-   **Snowflake (SNOW) & Databricks (private):** The speaker notes that the true "context box" for a business is increasingly the data warehouse, not the CRM. This implies that as AI models need access to comprehensive, clean data, the value layer is shifting toward data platforms that aggregate this information. This is a structural tailwind for companies that serve as the central data repository for an enterprise.

**Key Enterprise AI/Tech Trends**

-   **Shift from "Greenfield" to "Brownfield":** The easiest path for AI-native startups is to sell to "greenfield" customers (new companies with no existing system). However, the major market opportunity is displacing incumbents in "brownfield" accounts. The successful strategy to penetrate these accounts is not just offering a better workflow, but providing a fundamentally better understanding of the customer's own business reality through superior data synthesis and intelligence.

-   **The Death of the "Software is Dead" Narrative:** The idea that companies can easily build their own internal AI tools using foundation models is misguided. The hardest problem isn't the AI model itself, but accurately modeling the business, its data, and customer relationships. This is a complex systems-building and data integration problem, reinforcing the value of specialized application software companies that solve this.

-   **Evolving SaaS Pricing Models:** Pure per-seat pricing is becoming outdated. The market is moving toward hybrid models:
    1.  **Platform Fee:** A fixed fee for the core system of record.
    2.  **Per-Seat Fee:** For the core workflows and daily use.
    3.  **Consumption/Outcome Fee:** For high-value, automated work and intelligence (e.g., pipeline generation, forecasting, AI-driven workflows).
    This hybrid model is seen as a way to align value with cost, especially as AI agents begin to perform more work.

-   **Expansion Over Land:** In highly competitive "red ocean" markets like CRM, the key to success is being an "expansion company." The initial sale is less important than the ability to grow revenue within an account over time by solving more and more problems. This makes Net Revenue Retention (NRR) a critical metric for evaluating companies in this space.
