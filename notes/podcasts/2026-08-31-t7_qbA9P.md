# The Export Controls Are Working - Dylan Patel

### Key Investment Insights: AI Compute & US-China Tech Policy

**Regulatory & Macro Factors**

*   **US Export Controls Are Working:** The primary thesis is that US regulations restricting AI chip sales to China, enacted since 2022, have been highly effective. This has caused a dramatic shift in the global deployment of AI compute infrastructure.
*   **Geographic Shift in Capex:** This policy has successfully redirected the global AI infrastructure buildout towards the United States. This is a major tailwind for US-based companies in the AI supply chain.

**AI/Tech Sector Trends & Forecasts**

*   **Massive Compute Deployment Shift:**
    *   **2022 (Pre-Controls):** The US represented 45-50% of new global compute deployment, while China was 30-35%.
    *   **Today (Post-Controls):** The US now accounts for approximately 70% of new compute (measured in watts), while China's share has plummeted to less than 10%.
*   **Long-Term Compute Deficit for China:**
    *   The forecast suggests this trend will continue. By 2028, China is projected to have 30 gigawatts or less of AI compute, significantly lagging the US.
    *   This growing "compute stock" deficit means China will be severely disadvantaged in developing next-generation AI applications (e.g., automated researchers, advanced models), solidifying the US lead.

**Specific Company Implications**

*   **NVIDIA (NVDA):** While the loss of the Chinese market has been a stated concern for CEO Jensen Huang, this analysis implies the impact is more than offset by the supercharged, policy-driven AI buildout in the US. The export controls have effectively concentrated global demand for high-end GPUs within markets NVIDIA can freely serve, creating a massive, protected growth engine.
