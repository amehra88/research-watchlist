# How Lassie Is Automating Healthcare Administration

**Key Investment Insights**

The fundamental investment thesis is that AI transforms software from a "digital filing cabinet" (a system of record) into a "system that does the work." This is not an incremental change; it represents an orders-of-magnitude expansion of the Total Addressable Market (TAM) for software companies. Instead of pricing per seat, AI-native software can capture a percentage of the labor costs it automates. The opportunity is particularly large in industries with significant administrative overhead and labor shortages, such as healthcare.

**AI/Tech Sector Trends & Valuation**

*   **Software's TAM is Massively Expanding:** The prior software era, exemplified by companies like Workday (WDAY), NetSuite (ORCL), and QuickBooks (INTU), was about digitizing paper records. The new AI era is about automating the labor associated with those records. This shift in value proposition from data storage to task automation dramatically increases the potential revenue per customer.
*   **Market Mispricing of "AI" vs. "Software":** Public markets are currently penalizing traditional software companies while rewarding "AI" companies. The view is that these categories are converging: "AI is software, software is AI." This suggests a potential re-rating for software incumbents that successfully integrate AI to automate labor, and a risk for those that don't.
*   **AI Adoption is "Underhyped":** The impact of AI is "overhyped in Silicon Valley but underhyped in Iowa." This indicates that real-world adoption in mainstream small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs) is still in its infancy, representing a large, untapped greenfield market for companies providing tangible automation and ROI.

**Competitive Dynamics**

*   **Startup vs. Incumbent:** The classic battle is whether the startup gets distribution before the incumbent gets innovation. In the AI era, this dynamic is changing.
    *   **Incumbent Advantage:** Incumbents like Workday (WDAY) have distribution. AI tools can make their existing "bad engineers into pretty good engineers," potentially allowing them to innovate and add AI features faster than in previous tech cycles.
    *   **Startup Advantage:** Many new AI opportunities are in "non-categories" where the true incumbent is not another software company but a manual, human-driven process (e.g., an office manager named "Betty"). In these verticals, startups can build AI-native solutions from the ground up without legacy constraints, giving them an advantage.

**Regulatory & Macro Factors**

*   **Labor Shortage as a Tailwind:** A significant macro driver for AI automation is the inability of businesses, particularly in healthcare, to find qualified administrative staff. AI is not just about replacing jobs that exist, but about filling roles that companies cannot hire for.
*   **Legacy Inefficiency:** Large, regulated industries remain highly inefficient. For example, major health insurance carriers like Cigna (CI) still often remit payments via paper checks, creating massive administrative burdens that are ripe for automation. This "intentional delay" to manage float creates an opening for more efficient, AI-driven platforms.
