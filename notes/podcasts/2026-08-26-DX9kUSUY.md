# Vercel CTO + RSI Neolab Inherent's First Paper

### Analyst Briefing: Key Investment Insights

**Podcast:** The Cognitive Revolution
**Episode:** Vercel CTO + RSI Neolab Inherent's First Paper

---

### Key Investment Theses & Company-Specific Views

*   **Bullish on Compute Providers (NVDA, AMZN, MSFT, GOOGL):** The discovery that smaller AI models have "inherent" (not just "emergent") abilities does NOT signal a decrease in compute demand. The opposite is argued: it unlocks a massive number of new, economically viable use cases that were previously too expensive. This will drive a broader base of adoption, leading to an explosion in total inference demand. This is a significant tailwind for NVIDIA (NVDA) as the primary provider of inference chips and the hyperscalers (AWS/AMZN, Azure/MSFT, GCP/GOOGL) who sell the compute instances.
*   **Bullish on Edge & On-Device AI (AAPL, QCOM, NET):** The increased capability of smaller models makes edge and on-device AI much more powerful and practical. This trend directly benefits:
    *   **Apple (AAPL) & Qualcomm (QCOM):** Validates their strategy of integrating powerful neural processing units (NPUs) into devices. More capable on-device models create a stronger product moat and upgrade cycle incentive.
    *   **Cloudflare (NET):** The trend of running AI inference at the edge plays directly into Cloudflare's strategy and infrastructure. As developers push models closer to users for lower latency, NET's platform becomes more critical.
*   **Hyperscalers Benefit from Abstraction Layers:** The success of platforms like Vercel (private) is a positive indicator for hyperscalers (AMZN, MSFT, GOOGL). Vercel builds *on top of* core cloud infrastructure, making it easier for developers to deploy applications. This abstraction drives more underlying compute, storage, and network consumption, reinforcing the hyperscalers' core business. They are the "picks and shovels" for this developer-led movement.

---

### AI Sector Trends & Valuation Implications

*   **Commoditization of "Good Enough" AI Models:** The "inherent abilities" thesis implies that highly capable small, open-source models (e.g., from Meta, Mistral) will commoditize the low-to-mid tier of the AI market. This will put significant pricing pressure on proprietary models from OpenAI/Microsoft and Anthropic/Google/Amazon for tasks that do not require frontier-level intelligence. The value may shift from the model itself to the application, data, and distribution.
*   **Inference as the Long-Term Driver:** While training runs create massive, spiky demand for compute, the long-term, sustained driver of compute consumption will be inference. The trend towards more capable and efficient small models dramatically lowers the cost per inference, which is expected to catalyze a far greater volume of AI-powered applications, driving total market growth.
*   **Shift in Developer Tooling:** The rise of frameworks like Next.js (Vercel) and edge-first development platforms indicates a architectural shift in web development. This benefits companies providing the infrastructure for this new stack (e.g., NET, FSLY) and the underlying hyperscalers.

---

### Competitive Dynamics

*   **Meta's Open-Source Strategy as a Competitive Weapon:** Meta's (META) strategy of releasing powerful open-source models (Llama series) appears increasingly effective. By demonstrating the immense capability of smaller models, they directly challenge the moats of closed-model competitors (OpenAI, Anthropic) and accelerate the commoditization of the model layer. This fosters an ecosystem around their technology and puts pressure on Google's model leadership.
*   **Vercel vs. Hyperscalers:** Vercel is not a direct competitor to the core IaaS/PaaS offerings of AWS, Azure, or GCP. It is a higher-level "platform-as-a-service" that leverages their infrastructure. The competitive dynamic is one of "coopetition," where Vercel's growth is beneficial to the underlying cloud provider.
