# David Friedberg: America’s $40 Trillion Debt Problem Is About To Get Much Worse

**Investor Summary: All-In Podcast on US Debt**

This analysis focuses exclusively on the macro framework presented and its direct implications for public technology and AI company valuations.

### Macro Framework for Tech Valuations

The core argument is that structurally higher long-term interest rates will act as a significant headwind for technology and AI stock valuations.

- **The Problem is Fiscal, Not Just Monetary:** The primary driver of rising long-term interest rates is the US government's "fundamental fiscal spending problem," leading to persistent inflation and massive debt issuance. The Federal Reserve has limited power to influence the long end of the curve.
- **Supply & Demand Imbalance:** The US must refinance $10 trillion in debt over the next 12 months. This massive supply of new bonds, combined with a ~$2 trillion annual deficit, requires the market to demand a higher yield to absorb it.
- **Valuation Impact:** Higher long-term Treasury yields (the "risk-free rate") directly increase the discount rate used to value future cash flows. This disproportionately harms high-growth tech and AI stocks, as their value is heavily weighted towards earnings in the distant future. As the risk-free rate rises, the present value of those future earnings falls, compressing valuation multiples.

### Notable Market Call

- **Long-Term Rates to Remain Elevated:** The speaker argues that the bond market is now pricing in a long-term fiscal solvency risk for the US, not just short-term inflation. The 30-year Treasury yield rising to 5.2% is cited as a key signal. This implies that even if the Fed pivots, long-term rates may not fall significantly, capping potential upside for tech multiples.

### Key Supporting Data

- **Debt Rollover:** $10 trillion of US debt is coming due for refinancing in the next 12 months at much higher rates.
- **Current Average Cost of Debt:** The government's current average interest rate on its $40 trillion debt is 3.4%. Refinancing will push this average cost up significantly.
- **Interest Cost Sensitivity:** For every 1% increase in the average interest rate, the government's annual interest expense increases by an amount equal to 1.25% of GDP.
