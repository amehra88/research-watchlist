# Flock CEO on Catching Bad Cops: “It’s a Ton. It’s More Than I Ever Would’ve Hoped.”

Here are the investment insights from the podcast episode.

**AI / Tech Sector Trends**

*   **AI for Audit & Compliance is a Key Differentiator:** The primary insight is the emergence of AI not just for core functionality but for auditing the use of a platform. The private company Flock Safety is mandating an AI-driven "misuse detection" feature to flag abnormal user behavior (e.g., an officer repeatedly searching a license plate without an active case, suggesting stalking). This demonstrates a trend where providing automated governance, compliance, and auditing is becoming a required feature for technology sold into government and other regulated industries.

*   **"Trust & Safety" as a Product Mandate:** Flock is turning a liability (potential for misuse) into a core product feature. This suggests that for companies in sensitive data-centric fields (GovTech, healthcare, finance), a proactive and technologically-enforced ethics and compliance stance is shifting from a PR talking point to a mandatory, non-negotiable part of the product offering. This can create a moat by increasing customer trust and reducing headline risk.

**Regulatory Factors for Tech/AI**

*   **Proactive Self-Regulation as a Strategy:** By making misuse detection mandatory, the company is getting ahead of potential privacy and surveillance regulation. This is a signal for investors to look for companies in high-scrutiny sectors (like AI surveillance) that build in robust, automated guardrails. Such companies may face lower regulatory risk and a smoother path to adoption compared to competitors who wait for government-mandated rules.

**Public Company Implications (Inferred)**

*   **Setting New Standards for GovTech:** While Flock Safety is a private company, its actions set a new standard for the public safety technology market. Public competitors like **Axon Enterprise (AXON)** and **Motorola Solutions (MSI)** will likely face pressure from customers and civic groups to implement and mandate similar transparent, automated audit capabilities for their own surveillance and data platforms (body cameras, evidence management, etc.). Investors should monitor if these public companies are addressing this emerging requirement. Failure to do so could become a competitive disadvantage in winning government contracts.
