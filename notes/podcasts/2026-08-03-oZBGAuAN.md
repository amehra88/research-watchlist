# Why smarter AI models could drive up compute prices 10x

Here are the investment insights from the podcast episode.

**Key Investment Thesis: Rising Compute Prices**

The core thesis is that a fundamental supply/demand imbalance will drive AI compute prices significantly higher. AI lab revenues are growing at 10x year-over-year, while the underlying supply of compute is only growing at ~3x year-over-year.

This gap must be resolved in one of two ways:
1.  **AI labs capture the value** via massive margin expansion (e.g., to >90%). This would require a single lab to have a durable, massive lead over competitors, which seems unlikely.
2.  **The compute stack captures the value** through rising compute prices. This is presented as the more likely scenario, benefiting semiconductor companies and cloud providers.

Evidence of this is already happening: H100 spot prices are up over 40% from their February 2024 lows.

**AI/Tech Sector Trends**

*   **Compute Scarcity is the Bottleneck:** The supply of compute is inelastic. It is constrained by the manufacturing capacity for new fabs and specialized equipment from companies like ASML. AI is projected to absorb 90% of TSMC's N3 wafer demand by 2027, crowding out other sectors like smartphones and PCs.
*   **Monetization of Intelligence:** As AI models get smarter and more capable (e.g., reaching human-level software engineering), their ability to generate value from the same unit of compute increases exponentially. A hypothetical human-level AI on an H100 could justify a rental price 15x higher than today's spot market.
*   **Inference vs. Training Spend:** AI labs are shifting capital from training/R&D to inference. OpenAI's compute spend was ~25% inference in early 2024 but is now likely closer to 50% or more. This shows models are moving from a cost center to a revenue generator.
*   **High-Value Use Cases Dominate:** As compute costs rise, lower-value AI applications will be priced out. The market will be dominated by high-value tasks like AI research automation, where labs will be willing to pay a significant premium for compute access.

**Capital Allocation & Company Strategy**

*   **Long-Term Contracts Command Premiums:** Google's (GOOGL) deal to pay SpaceX nearly $1B/month for 110,000 Nvidia GPUs is priced at roughly 2x the current spot market rate per hour. This indicates that securing large, stable blocks of compute is extremely valuable and commands a premium, a bullish signal for compute providers.
*   **The AI Lab Flywheel:** The primary goal for leading AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) is not to become a utility cloud provider. Their business model is to use inference revenue to fund massive capex for training the *next* generation of models to stay ahead in the race for AGI. They are incentivized to spend on R&D, not just monetize current models.
*   **Competitive Moat in Efficiency:** With compute as a scarce, expensive input, the ability to create more efficient models (achieving the same performance with less compute) becomes a major competitive advantage. A lab with a more efficient model can achieve much higher margins, solidifying its market position.

**Specific Company Commentary**

*   **Nvidia (NVDA):** As the primary supplier of AI compute, Nvidia is the most direct beneficiary of rising compute prices. The Google/SpaceX deal for Nvidia GPUs at a 2x spot price premium is a strong positive indicator. The chart of cumulative compute capacity shows Nvidia's dominant and growing market share.
*   **Google (GOOGL) / Amazon (AMZN) / Hyperscalers:** Positioned to capture value as the price of renting their GPU clusters increases. They benefit from the scarcity and demand from AI labs.
*   **TSMC (TSM) / ASML (ASML):** Identified as fundamental bottlenecks in the supply chain. Their limited capacity to produce leading-edge wafers and EUV machines, respectively, makes the 3x annual growth in compute supply difficult to accelerate, reinforcing the scarcity thesis.
