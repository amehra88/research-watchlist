# What AI Will Actually Do for Math in the Next 5 Years  - Grant Sanderson

Here are the key investment insights from the podcast episode.

### AI Sector Trends & Investment Framework

The core discussion centers on the use of AI for fundamental research and discovery, specifically in finding novel connections between disparate fields of knowledge. This points to a long-term trend of AI augmenting scientific R&D.

- **The ROI Measurement Challenge:** A key challenge for investors is measuring the value of AI-driven discoveries. The speaker distinguishes between two types of AI progress:
    1.  **Solving a Defined Problem:** This is easily measurable and allows an AI company to generate a "PR move" and a clear headline (0:54), which can directly impact public market valuation.
    2.  **Finding Abstract Connections:** This is a more profound but harder-to-measure capability. Its value is less immediate and more difficult to translate into a quarterly earnings report.

- **Investment Implication:** Investors should be cautious about valuing AI companies based on claims of abstract "understanding" or "connection-finding." Tangible, problem-solving benchmarks remain a more reliable (though potentially less visionary) indicator of near-term commercial viability and stock performance.

### The "Co-Pilot" Model for High-Value Work

- **Human-in-the-Loop is Key:** The speaker predicts that the most useful progress from AI in complex fields over the next five years will require a "human in the loop" to validate the significance of AI-generated insights (1:01).
- **Investment Implication:** This reinforces the investment thesis for companies building AI "co-pilot" tools that augment, rather than replace, high-value experts like scientists and engineers. The value is in productivity amplification of existing human capital, suggesting a strong market for specialized enterprise AI software.
