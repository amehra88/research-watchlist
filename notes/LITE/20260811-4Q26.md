## 3. Forward guidance

**Q1 FY27 (quarter ending ~Sept 2026):** revenue **$1.225–1.275B**; non-GAAP operating margin **39.5–40.5%**; non-GAAP diluted EPS **$4.05–4.35**; non-GAAP tax rate 16.5%; ~102M diluted shares (8-K Ex-99.1 Business Outlook; CFO Ali prepared remarks).

Magnitude and direction: the $1.25B midpoint is **>130% y/y growth** and a fourth consecutive quarter of >20% sequential growth. Hurlston quantified the raise bluntly — "adding nearly $250 million incrementally into the guide is pretty impressive, and we think we have the opportunity to do that more as we look out in future quarters" (Q&A, response to Cardoso/JPMorgan). Composition of the sequential step-up is roughly **half Components** (scale-out and scale-across) and **half Systems** (1.6T transceiver ramp plus accelerating OCS deliveries) (Hurlston prepared remarks).

**Change in guidance philosophy — the most important forward item.** Management is explicitly retiring its existing target model as obsolete and will **reset financial targets at the next OFC**: "we'll come out with some new financial targets probably at the next OFC. We're obviously running well ahead on almost every metric" (Hurlston, Q&A). CFO Ali pre-framed the direction: the prior model set 38–42% operating margin *at $2B quarterly revenue*, but "we're already at the midpoint of that at much lower revenue levels … think of the 42% outlier more as a midpoint, with the range probably moving up 100 to 200 basis points versus what we showed at OFC" (Q&A, response to Rakesh/Mizuho). Tone is confident and specific rather than promotional; the company is guiding above its own published framework and telling analysts the framework is the thing that is wrong.

Named drivers management says were **not** in the prior OFC framework: NPO engagements, CW lasers, and OCS upside.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** **Confirm** (with one live watch item)
- **Evidence:** Asked directly by Simon Leopold (Raymond James) about "Chinese based companies coming to market with new indium phosphide fabs," Hurlston: "we obviously haven't seen any impact on our numbers as yet, and I don't expect there to be any impact. We think we have differentiation, obviously, on EMLs … And then even in CW, we've been surprised that our ability to price up … The width of the spec is very narrow and that results in far better transceiver yields for the customers … we are able to command a nice price premium that we would expect to sustain, as these Chinese — if these Chinese guys come online. I caution people also … some of these Chinese laser suppliers are not delivering in the market today." (Q&A)
- **Implication:** Pricing power is being *demonstrated*, not just asserted — LITE is taking price in CW, the product line most exposed to commoditization, on a yield-quality argument. **But the competitive rebuttal is management assertion, not independent verification**, and it is forward-looking ("I don't expect"). Keep as a watch item into the FY26 10-K and peer prints. **Corpus correction the PM should note:** the `tier_4_ecosystem` Chinese names registered as affecting LITE split into two distinct groups that should not be treated as one threat — **Innolight, Eoptolink and Source Photonics are transceiver/module makers, i.e. LITE's *customers* for EML and CW chips**, and benefit from LITE's supply; the actual competitive threat is Chinese **InP fab** entrants upstream. This blind spot is narrower than the watchlist entry implies, but sharper where it is real.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** **Confirm**
- **Evidence:** Revenue +109.3% y/y, eighth consecutive quarter of growth, third consecutive quarter of >20% sequential growth (Hurlston prepared remarks; 8-K). Demand is running ahead of supply across multiple lines: EMLs "still shipping behind customer demand"; on ultra-high-power lasers "we are way behind our shipments … the demand signal has increased and we are very much further behind relative to our ability to supply" (Q&A, response to Sylla/Citi). Pump lasers "will remain effectively sold out for the foreseeable future despite our rapid capacity expansion," with a **fourfold shipment increase** expected over the next several quarters. Capex $167M in the quarter; inventory +$59M sequentially.
- **Implication:** LITE is capex-constrained rather than demand-constrained, which is the strongest possible version of this theme. The scarcity is the thesis. Risk symmetry to watch: this also means a demand air-pocket would be masked for several quarters by backlog, and capacity landing in 2028 (Greensboro) arrives well after the current tightness.

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** **Confirm — strongly, and this is where the call added the most new information**
- **Evidence:** Hurlston addressed "recent market noise around co-packaged and near-packaged optics" head-on: "our lead CPO customer's production plans remain very much on track and their demand signal has increased since our last update." On the intermediate architecture: "the rest of our customer base is currently prioritizing near-packaged architectures … The NPO opportunity is **completely additive** for us, significantly increasing the optical TAM … Even our largest CPO customer is looking at NPO for specific new use cases." First **ELS (external light source) module** order secured for delivery 2H CY2027. Wupen Yuen sized the mechanism: each NPO optical engine is ~6.4T of bandwidth, "equivalent to four times that of a local CC module," requiring LITE's 400mW design principle scaled to the 150–200mW range. On OCS: after doubling shipments Q3→Q4, "our guidance includes our first triple digit OCS revenue quarter," expected "meaningfully above the three digit mark," with crossover to **number one supplier at the key customer in early 2027**. Ultra-high-power lasers ~$50M by end CY2026, first triple-digit quarter in fiscal Q3 FY27.
- **Implication:** The CPO-vs-NPO debate, which had been framed by the market as a binary risk to LITE, is resolved in LITE's favour as *additive* — both architectures consume differentiated InP lasers, and LITE supplies both. Scale-up optical connectivity (in-rack, previously copper) is opening as a genuinely new TAM layer on top of scale-out and scale-across. This is the single largest upward revision to the thesis from this call.

### hyperscaler_revenue_concentration

- **Status:** **Drift** — the exposure is real and unchanged in kind, but the call reframes it and no analyst tested it
- **Evidence:** Concentration signals point both ways. Toward concentration: a single "lead CPO customer"; a single "key customer" for OCS; 3D sensing tied to "our primary customer"; and a hyperscaler illustration — "for one major hyperscaler, the network capacity connecting just two AI data center sites is double the total global backbone capacity they built over the entirety of the last decade." Toward broadening: CW laser chips shipping "to a multitude of customers"; NPO momentum "across multiple high velocity engagements"; ultra-high-power demand that "cuts across a couple of customers." Structurally new: "we have secured **multiple long-term customer agreements that help offset our planned capital expenditures**" — characterized in Q&A (Roy/Stifel) as roughly **three-year take-or-pay agreements with pricing levers**, with NEMs on the pump-laser/scale-across side.
- **Implication:** **Not resolvable this cycle, and I decline to call it worsening or diversifying.** The quantitative disclosure that would settle it — the customer-concentration table — lives in the FY26 10-K, which **has not yet been filed** (see §10). Notably, **no analyst asked a customer-concentration question on this call**, across 13 questioners; the entire Q&A was demand, supply and margin. That silence is itself a signal about where the sell-side's attention is, and a reason the PM should not expect the sell-side to surface concentration deterioration early. The take-or-pay LTAs materially improve the *durability* of concentrated revenue without reducing the concentration itself.

## 5. AI positioning signal

- **Current score:** **4** (set 2026-06-02, 71 days old — 30+ days stale and still in use; not `[STALE]`, which requires 90+)
- **Evidence from this call:** LITE is now effectively a pure-play AI optical supplier. Revenue +109% y/y described by management as "driven by sharp acceleration in AI revenue"; capex "primarily focused on manufacturing capacity to support cloud and AI customers"; inventory build explicitly "to support the expected growth in our cloud and AI related revenue." Product evidence spans every AI optical domain: 1.6T cloud transceivers in production, record 800G, OCS entering triple-digit quarterly revenue, first ELS module order, NPO/CPO laser families at 120/150/400mW, ultra-high-power CPO lasers, 200-gig EMLs at >25% of EML revenue heading to 50% of volume by mid-2027. Even the industrial line is AI-levered: ultra-fast lasers for "high density PCB via drilling that supports advanced AI XPU boards and 1.6T optical modules."
- **Recommendation:** **drift to 5** — analyst draft for operator review.
- **Reasoning:** The current note's framing ("AI datacom cycle beneficiary plus cloud/datacom recovery") understates what the FY26 print shows — this is no longer a cyclical recovery story with AI upside, it is a company whose entire revenue base and capex program is AI datacenter connectivity, positioned across all three topology layers (scale-out, scale-across, scale-up) and both contending in-rack architectures (NPO and CPO). "Beneficiary" is the wrong word for a supplier that is the binding constraint.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

_All three current values set 2026-06-02 (71 days; 30+ and still in use)._

- **Innovation rate** (current: **3**)
  - Evidence: The stated rationale for 3 was "more component than systems." That is now directly contradicted. **Systems is 35.5% of Q4 revenue and grew 122.6% y/y** — faster than Components — and FY26 Systems grew 90.7% vs Components 79.7%. Moving *up* the stack: first **ELS module** order (Hurlston: "from an ASP standpoint is meaningfully higher than the set of lasers that we ship in," margins "above corporate average, but not quite as good as the lasers — so we're trading a little bit of margin to get some revenue bump"); OCS roadmap broadening to "higher and lower port count products, including specialized in-tray offerings"; 1.6T modules in production. Moving *down* the stack simultaneously: Greensboro fab conversion from gallium arsenide to indium phosphide on track for first revenue early 2028, two InP fabs in Japan expanding, and a new **AXT indium phosphide substrate agreement** to secure feedstock. Genuine process innovation with P&L effect: CW laser die size "significantly reduced," closing the margin gap to EMLs "considerably."
  - Recommendation: **revise to 4**
- **Distribution** (current: **4**)
  - Evidence: See §4 `hyperscaler_revenue_concentration`. Designed into the AI transceiver and optical-engine supply chain at the lead CPO customer, the key OCS customer, multiple NPO engagements, and NEM pump-laser accounts under multi-year take-or-pay agreements. Concentration persists; quantitative disclosure unavailable pending the FY26 10-K.
  - Recommendation: **hold at 4**
- **Overall** (current: **4**)
  - Recommendation: **revise to 5**
  - Reasoning: The watchlist caps overall at 4 because "concentration plus legacy-telecom-segment drag cap it." **The telecom leg is empirically retired**, on positive evidence rather than non-disclosure: the legacy/telecom-heritage lines still exist inside Systems and they *improved* this quarter — "performance in industrial lasers and cable access strengthened quarter-over-quarter" (Hurlston prepared remarks) — while the telecom-heritage laser products (pump lasers, narrow-linewidth assemblies) are now sold as **scale-across** AI datacenter interconnect, +80% and +130% y/y respectively and sold out. Structurally, LITE reports in Components/Systems only, and those two lines sum exactly to total revenue ($649.4M + $356.9M = $1,006.3M), so there is no separate telecom segment left to drag the mix. **The concentration leg survives intact.** On how the two dimensions interact for *this* business model, and why the answer is not the mechanical average of 4 and 4: LITE's innovation and distribution couple through **scarcity**. Because its InP laser output is the binding constraint on its customers' transceiver and optical-engine builds, concentration in a few large buyers currently confers pricing *power* rather than pricing *exposure* — Hurlston reports commanding a sustained premium in CW against market price on the strength of spec-width consistency that lifts customers' own transceiver yields, while EML and ultra-high-power shipments both remain behind demand and LTAs are structured take-or-pay with pricing levers. That inverts the usual concentration discount. It is, however, a **cycle-contingent inversion**: it holds only while LITE is supply-short, and reverses if Chinese InP capacity, or LITE's own Greensboro/Japan expansions, close the gap faster than demand grows. **Falsifiers to watch:** the FY26 10-K concentration table; any quarter where EMLs are no longer shipping behind demand; evidence of Chinese InP fabs actually delivering qualified product.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

Thirteen analysts; InsiderScore scored zero challenging exchanges, and the tone was congratulatory throughout ("Congrats on the results," "Fantastic quarter"). Flags are therefore about emphasis and omission rather than evasion.

1. **Unusual emphasis — proactive CPO defense.** Hurlston used prepared remarks to pre-empt "recent market noise around co-packaged and near-packaged optics" before any analyst raised it, asserting the lead CPO customer's demand signal has *increased*. Unprompted rebuttals are worth tracking; this one was backed by a concrete new datapoint (first ELS module order), which supports it.
2. **Candid negative disclosure — supply shortfall.** Asked by Sylla (Citi) about the supply/demand imbalance previously quantified at "30%+," Hurlston did not requantify but conceded ultra-high-power lasers got *worse*: "we are way behind our shipments … very much further behind." **Note he declined to give an updated aggregate imbalance percentage** after having given one last quarter — a quiet reduction in disclosure granularity on the metric most useful for sizing the gap.
3. **Analyst premise corrected, gently.** Sylla's question assumed OCS "hit triple digit" in the reported quarter. It did not — Hurlston's prepared remarks said OCS *doubled* Q3→Q4 and that the **Q1 guide** contains the first triple-digit OCS quarter. Confirmed to Genovese (Rosenblatt): "in the guide, which is the first quarter, we'd expect our first triple digit OCS revenue quarter." Also note OCS is **not separately disclosed** — it sits inside Systems, so this is management-provided colour that cannot be audited from the financials.
4. **Sandbag candidate — the $400M OCS guide.** The 2H CY2026 OCS guide of $400M+ was characterized as on track rather than ahead, while nearly every other metric was described as running well ahead. Given OCS doubled sequentially and is guided to a triple-digit quarter "meaningfully above the three digit mark," with crossover to #1 supplier at the key customer in early 2027, the $400M looks conservative.
5. **Nobody asked about customer concentration.** Across 13 questioners, zero questions on customer concentration, revenue by customer, or dependency risk — despite it being the sole surviving cap on LITE's competitive-advantage score. Do not expect sell-side coverage to flag this early.

## 9. Vocabulary candidates

Surfaced for operator review; not added to the watchlist.

- **scale-across** — LITE's own coinage for DC-to-DC interconnect, distinct from scale-out (within DC) and scale-up (within/between racks). **The highest-value candidate here:** it is the linguistic vehicle by which telecom-heritage pump lasers and narrow-linewidth assemblies are reframed as AI infrastructure, which is precisely the reframing that retires the "legacy telecom drag" caveat. A `scale_across` or `optical_interconnect_tiers` theme would let the corpus track this across FN, AAOI, POET and COHR.
- **scale-up (optical)** — optical penetrating in-rack connectivity previously served by copper; management's framing of the 2028 architecture inflection.
- **NPO (near-packaged optics)** as explicitly distinct from **CPO (co-packaged optics)** — the corpus likely collapses these; the call treats NPO as the intermediate step and as "completely additive" TAM.
- **ELS module (external light source)** — the productization step from selling laser chips to selling modules; the concrete marker of LITE moving up the stack.
- **OCI standards** — emerging ASIC-to-optical-engine interface standards; Hurlston frames standardization as TAM-opening ("opens up switch opportunities … that may not be open to us before").
- **300-gig lane** — next lane-speed node beyond 200-gig, already in capacity-planning language.
- **in-tray offerings** — OCS form-factor variant.
- **equitization** (of convertible notes) — the specific capital-structure action behind the GAAP loss.
