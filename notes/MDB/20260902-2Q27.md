# MDB — Q2 FY27 earnings read

_Prepared by earnings-reviewer, 2026-09-02. Tier: T3 (watchlist). Second earnings note on file; prior note `notes/MDB/20260529-1Q27.md` (Q1 FY27)._

## 1. Headline read

Large beat against management's own guide — revenue $771.8M vs. the Q1-issued guide of $729–734M, +30% YoY, the fastest growth since FY24 — with a $70M full-year raise on top. But the composition drifts: **Atlas grew +28.9%, its fifth consecutive quarter at ~29% (flat, not accelerating); the entire acceleration to 30% came from Enterprise Advanced & other at +35.9%**, a line management had guided to *flat* in H2 just one quarter ago. Management's own guide implies both lines decelerate from here (Atlas FY27 ~27%, EA H2 flat-to-mid-single-digit), and Q3 revenue is guided **below** the Q2 actual in absolute dollars. Thesis is Confirm on all four themes — the AI-retrieval product cycle is visibly monetizing — but with a Drift flag on **growth composition**: the headline acceleration is mix-driven and, on management's own numbers, not repeatable in H2.

This is precisely what Alex Zukin (Wolfe) pressed on in the call's single flagged challenging exchange (see §8).

## 4. Thesis read by theme

_Themes per the `tier_3_watchlist` entry for MDB (see §10 on the duplicate T2 stub)._

### enterprise_ai_adoption

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Record 2,900 net new customers, total 70,600+ (8-K Ex-99.1 customer-count table); customers >$100K ARR 2,999, +17% YoY (same table); approximately 75% of the Fortune 100 now customers (8-K Ex-99.1, "About MongoDB"). Share of large Atlas customers using 2+ platform features rose to 48% from 42% PY _(paraphrase — summary only; Q1 note recorded 45% vs. 37%)_. Named production AI adopters this quarter: Financial Times, Fireflies, plus AI labs _(paraphrase — summary only)_. Search and Vector Search reached **general availability in Enterprise Advanced**, with uptake called out in financial services and public sector — regulated buyers who cannot use public cloud (8-K Ex-99.1 business highlights).
- **Implication:** Adoption is broadening on two axes at once: more customers (record adds) and deeper per-customer attach (48% multi-feature, up 6pts YoY and 3pts QoQ). The genuinely new datapoint is regulated/self-managed enterprises getting AI retrieval — that is the segment most structurally locked out of cloud-native AI tooling, and it is now addressable. Thesis intact and strengthening.

### ai_infrastructure_software

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** GA of four AI-retrieval capabilities in Atlas — Automated Embeddings powered by Voyage AI, the Atlas Embedding and Reranking API, voyage-code-4, and Vector Search in Atlas Stream Processing (8-K Ex-99.1). Launch of the **Atlas Managed MCP Server**, a hosted service connecting coding agents — explicitly Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build, and Devin by Cognition — to live Atlas data without customer-managed infrastructure (8-K Ex-99.1). Named a Leader in The Forrester Wave: Multimodel Data Platforms, Q2 2026 (8-K Ex-99.1). Voyage customers doubled QoQ for a second consecutive quarter, frequently as net-new relationships _(paraphrase — summary only)_.
- **Implication:** Confirms and upgrades the Q1 read. Q1's evidence was a LangChain partnership and an org change; Q2's is shipped, generally-available product across embeddings, reranking, streaming vector search, and an agent-connectivity layer. The MCP Server is the most strategically interesting item — MDB is positioning as the *default operational-data endpoint for third-party coding agents*, which is distribution through someone else's agent surface rather than through its own sales motion. The Q1 open question (differentiation vs. pgvector/Pinecone/native cloud vector) is partly answered by integration breadth rather than by raw retrieval quality; that remains the durable competitive question.

### ai_re_architected_incumbent

- **Status:** Confirm — **upgraded from Q1's "Confirm with drift"**
- **Evidence:** Search and Vector Search are now **GA in MongoDB Enterprise Advanced**, bringing Atlas's AI retrieval capabilities "to self-managed, private cloud, and hybrid environments—with the same platform, APIs, and technical skills across deployment models" (8-K Ex-99.1, verbatim). EA & other revenue +35.9% to $181.231M, described as the strongest EA quarter in three years, with EA ARR +11% YoY and a third consecutive quarter of double-digit ARR growth _(paraphrase — summary only)_. Desai on the "run anywhere strategy across both Atlas and Enterprise Advanced" and MDB as "the only modern data platform that extends seamlessly from self-managed to any cloud" (8-K Ex-99.1, verbatim).
- **Implication:** This closes the Q1 note's open recommendation. Q1 argued the theme label was drifting — that MDB was a *beneficiary of customers' re-architecture* rather than a re-architected incumbent itself, and suggested the operator consider re-tagging. Q2 argues for **keeping the tag**: MongoDB took its own legacy self-managed product line, retrofitted it with the AI retrieval stack, and monetized it inside two quarters (+36% revenue, FY guide from mid-single-digits to 11%). That is the incumbent re-architecting itself, literally. Management also explicitly rebutted the cannibalization read — EA is positioned as complementary to Atlas, not a substitute — which is the load-bearing claim (see §8). **Recommendation: retain `ai_re_architected_incumbent`; the Q1 re-tag question is resolved in favor of the status quo.**

### inference_compute_economics

- **Status:** Confirm, with a composition caveat
- **Evidence:** Net ARR expansion rate 122%, vs. 121% in Q1 _(paraphrase — summary only)_. Atlas run rate approaching $2.3B; consumption trends described as stable with minimal customer requests for contract renegotiation _(paraphrase — summary only)_. Desai (to Radke) detailed AI-lab inference workloads running on Atlas, and (to Keirstead) customer-facing production workloads in banking and enterprise; use cases cited as chatbots, semantic search, and document intelligence, with time-to-production measured in weeks _(paraphrase — summary only)_.
- **Implication:** The consumption flywheel is intact and marginally expanding (122% vs. 121% NRR). But the caveat is now concrete rather than theoretical: **Atlas — the consumption-metered line, and therefore the true inference-economics exposure — did not accelerate.** It held at ~29% for a fifth quarter while the *licence-like* EA line supplied all of the headline acceleration. Rising inference volume is showing up in MDB's revenue via seat-and-licence expansion at least as much as via metered consumption this quarter. That is a lower-quality flavour of the thesis than pure consumption growth, and it is the reason §1 flags composition drift rather than a clean Confirm. Watch whether Atlas breaks above 29% in Q3; the FY guide of ~27% says management does not expect it to.

  - Recommendation: **Hold at 4.**
- **Overall** (current: [empty]; Q1 note recommended **4**)
  - Recommendation: **Hold at 4, with the downside case materially weakened.**
  - Reasoning — how the two dimensions interact for *this* business model: Q1's overall 4 was set *below* what the dimensional inputs implied, on an explicit structural argument — MDB's distribution lives inside the hyperscalers it competes with (AWS DocumentDB, Azure Cosmos DB, GCP Firestore/AlloyDB), so its position depends on perpetually out-innovating native cloud database services, and that dependence made the score more fragile than the components suggested. **Q2 is a partial structural answer to exactly that critique.** "Run anywhere" — the same platform, APIs, and skills from self-managed through private cloud to any public cloud, now including AI retrieval — is a proposition no single hyperscaler can match, because matching it would mean supporting workloads on competitors' infrastructure. EA's +36% quarter is the empirical evidence that customers pay for it. So the interaction has changed sign: innovation is no longer merely *defending* distribution inside hostile channels; innovation is now *creating* a channel (self-managed and hybrid) the hyperscalers structurally cannot contest. That said, the overall stays at 4 rather than moving to 4+ for one reason — Atlas, the line most exposed to direct hyperscaler substitution, is the line that did **not** accelerate. Until Atlas breaks above ~29%, the hyperscaler-pressure hypothesis cannot be ruled out on the evidence. Q1's stated markdown trigger (a sharp Atlas deceleration, specifically on AWS) did **not** trigger this quarter; Atlas held.
