## 1. Headline read

**Confirm, and an extension of the Q1'27 read rather than a mere repeat.** The Q1'27 note (2026-06-04) called a strong Confirm on a record net-new-ARR quarter (+32% YoY) and a ~520bps FY27 net-new-ARR guidance raise; Q2 does not sustain that raise, it **extends** it — a further +630bps taking FY27 net-new-ARR growth to ~34% vs. the 22.5% initial outlook (cumulative **+1,150bps across two quarters**), on record net new ARR of $333M (+51% YoY) and a fourth consecutive quarter of ending-ARR acceleration.

**Split alert — read the EPS line carefully.** CRWD effected a **4-for-1 forward split** (booked as a 300% stock dividend; announced 2026-06-03, effective 2026-07-02). The Q1'27 note's $1.10 EPS was the as-reported pre-split figure and remains correct as of its date; FactSet has since restated Q1 to **$0.275**. The correct split-adjusted sequential comparison is **$0.275 → $0.31 (+12.7%)**, not $1.10 → $0.31. The split changes no valuation: it is a units change only, and the ~$189 share price in this note versus the higher price implied by the prior note's EPS is **not** multiple compression.

## 3. Forward guidance

Management raised FY27 net-new-ARR guidance by a further **630bps**, to ~**34% YoY growth**, an increase of **$116M versus the initial FY27 outlook** of 22.5% growth — a cumulative **+1,150bps** across the Q1 and Q2 raises. The arithmetic closes exactly and independently corroborates the guidance figures: $116M / 0.115 = **$1,008.7M** implied FY26 net-new-ARR base, which reconciles against the Q1 note's $1.279–1.303B FY27 guide (midpoint $1.291B = 28% growth on that base). Implied FY27 net new ARR at the new ~34% midpoint is **~$1.35B** _(derived, not stated by management)_.

Free cash flow margin guided to **27.5% in Q3** and **at least 30% for the full year** — the FY figure reiterated from Q1, now on a higher revenue base. Management framed the raise as underpinned by a **record Q3 pipeline** and broad-based strength, attributing demand to AI expansion accelerating cybersecurity modernization.

**Guidance philosophy:** unchanged from Q1 and still notably ambitious rather than conservative — a second consecutive in-year raise, with the summary nonetheless characterizing the outlook as "prudent." The Q1 note flagged an un-guided government/executive-order tailwind as potential upside; **that topic did not recur on this call** and its status is therefore unresolved this quarter.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### enterprise_ai_adoption

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Management quantified agentic work arriving on the endpoint — **Claude usage up more than 400%** and custom agent usage up more than 100% (prepared remarks, paraphrase from call summary). The FY27 guidance raise was explicitly attributed to "a robust demand environment driven by AI expansion, which is accelerating cybersecurity modernization." Kurtz, responding to Borges (Goldman Sachs) on customers moving from sandboxed to production AI, described the need for data access controls and comprehensive threat management (Q&A, paraphrase).
- **Implication:** The Q1 thesis that AI adoption is a demand *multiplier* for CRWD rather than a disruption risk now has a measured, quantified metric behind it rather than an assertion. Enterprise AI deployment is converting into security budget within the same fiscal year, which is what a toll-on-the-buildout thesis requires.

### ai_native_vertical

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** **AIDR ending ARR nearly tripled versus Q1** and was adopted in eight-figure Flex wins at major financial institutions; **token-based pricing has shipped inside AIDR** (moving from Q1's "Falcon Flex could accommodate it" to a live commercial model). Next-Gen SIEM **ending ARR surpassed $695M, +60% YoY**; cloud security ending ARR exceeded **$905M, +29% YoY**, with runtime security for AI workloads cited as the differentiator; Identity ending ARR **$585M, +34% YoY**, Falcon Shield ARR **+185%**, privileged account security **+35x** (prepared remarks, paraphrase).
- **Implication:** The net-new AI-native product categories flagged in Q1 as promising-but-early are now compounding off a visibly larger base, and the token-pricing shift converts an architectural claim into realized commercial mechanics. This is the theme with the most decisive quarter-over-quarter progression and it is the principal support for the ai_positioning recommendation in §5.

### cybersecurity_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Endpoint accelerated for a **fourth consecutive quarter**, with customers replacing legacy AV and next-gen EDR providers; management noted **roughly half the market still runs legacy solutions** (Gallo/Jefferies Q&A, paraphrase). Exposure management accelerated sequentially, displacing legacy vulnerability-management vendors (Owens/Piper Sandler Q&A). Asked by Hedberg (RBC) whether Frontier Labs building their own security would erode CRWD, Kurtz asserted an advantage rooted in deep data expertise and unique threat data (paraphrase).
- **Implication:** Confirm on the evidence presented, but with one untested flank — this is not a Confirm that survived adversarial probing. CRWD is still widening rather than defending, and an accelerating endpoint franchise at $5.84B ending ARR is the strongest available evidence against share erosion. The caveat is that the competitive set was barely tested: InsiderScore logged **zero challenging exchanges**, no analyst raised Microsoft Defender, Palo Alto or SentinelOne, and the disintermediation risk was addressed once and deflected.

  - Recommendation: **revise to `4+`**
  - Reasoning: The two dimensions do not average here — they **compound in a specific direction**, and this quarter shows which one is load-bearing. Distribution — not innovation — is the dominant moat for this business model, which is why `overall` tracks the distribution field rather than splitting the difference between the two components (the schema explicitly defines `overall` as operator judgment, **not** a mechanical average). **Counterweights that keep this at `4+` rather than 5:** competitive response was entirely untested on this call (zero challenging exchanges); FCF margin stepped down sequentially; exposure management remains early-stage by management's own admission; and one exceptional quarter into an AI-security demand inflection could be a land-grab rather than a durable structural re-rating. ## 8. Management Q&A flags

- **Zero challenging exchanges is itself the flag.** InsiderScore logged **0 challenging analyst exchanges** and rated every topic Positive — under a sentiment model the vendor states was *just recalibrated to be more skeptical of generic optimism*. Thirteen analysts asked about drivers, durability and product mechanics; none pressed on competitive displacement risk, margin trajectory, or the sequential FCF step-down. A print this strong that draws no pushback means the bear case was not tested on the call and must be sourced elsewhere.
- **AIDR reframed as an "incremental module" (Tindle, Raymond James).** Asked to clarify AIDR's relationship to EDR, Kurtz described it as an **incremental module with separate pricing** — a revenue-mechanics framing notably narrower than Q1's headline claim that AIDR's TAM would exceed EDR's. The bigger claim was retained elsewhere on the call (AIDR could "eventually surpass EDR" on agent proliferation), so both framings now coexist. Worth watching whether the TAM claim quietly narrows to a module story.
- **Frontier Labs disintermediation deflected (Hedberg, RBC).** Asked directly what happens if the frontier labs build their own security, Kurtz fell back on data-expertise and unique-threat-data advantages. This is the single most consequential structural risk to the AI-security thesis and it received one answer and no follow-up.
- **Exposure management is the only tempered area (Owens, Piper Sandler).** Management acknowledged the segment is early-stage relative to other lines, citing a seven-figure Flex win against legacy vulnerability-management vendors. Honest, and a useful calibration point against the otherwise uniform strength.
- **FCF margin step-down went unaddressed.** No analyst raised FCF falling from 34% of revenue in Q1 to 26% in Q2, despite the FY guide of ≥30% requiring a meaningful second-half recovery (Q3 guided 27.5%). The math is achievable but it was never put to management.
