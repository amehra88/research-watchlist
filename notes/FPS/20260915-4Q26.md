## 4. Thesis read by theme

### data_center_deployment_constraints

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Backlog $3.0B (+53% q/q) with >90% of FY27 revenue covered and the remainder scheduled into FY28 (md_2, md_4); "we already have sufficient demand visibility to justify adding manufacturing capacity beyond the expansion we recently completed" (md_2). Modular/prefabricated construction is the constraint-relief mechanism: "modular construction can compress portions of the build schedule by roughly 30% to 50%"; share of datacenter construction "10% to 20%" in the cloud era, "approximately 40%" today, "60% by the end of the decade" (md_2). Lead times: "we still do believe that we are inside the expected lead times for every product line that we have in the industry ... just like we would have been 9 months ago or 12 months ago" (qna_28). Customers are seeking longer-term capacity commitments — "meaningful dialogue going on with all types of those cohorts around anything from individual POs all the way to making longer-term commitments" (qna_10). No pushouts: "in terms of meaningful pushouts, we are not seeing that for our product set" (qna_4). Labor is the industry constraint FPS claims to have solved: "Talent is a known constraint across our industry ... has allowed us to scale rapidly without workforce constraints limiting our growth" (md_2); headcount ~1,800 → ~3,500–3,600 (qna_25; 10-K ~3,000 FT + 450 temporary).
- **Implication:** The deployment bottleneck is intact and FPS is monetizing it from both ends — equipment speed and prefabrication that removes field labor. The nuance carried from 3Q26 still holds: management again positions FPS as the relief valve, not the constraint (supply chain "nothing habitual", qna_25; lead times inside market), so the speed premium is contingent on industry lead times staying extended. The new Tijuana build is a bet that modular demand outruns the $5B footprint before FY28.
