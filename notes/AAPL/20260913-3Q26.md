## 1. Headline read

First observation for AAPL, so no prior-quarter read to anchor to. The print is a **Drift** at the thesis level: the two premises the PM used to cap the name at scoring — "decelerating iPhone growth" and "China exposure" — were contradicted this quarter (iPhone +22% YoY, Greater China +22% YoY per Ex-99.1), and Siri AI/Apple Intelligence is now in public beta rather than "slipped." But the print swaps those caps for new ones: an advanced-node supply constraint that management says will "increase significantly sequentially," a memory-cost "100-year flood" that pulls the September gross-margin guide to 47–48% (46–47% ex a 1-pt tariff refund), and Tim Cook's final call before John Ternus takes over.

## 3. Forward guidance

September quarter (fiscal 4Q26), per the InsiderScore AI summary of Parekh's prepared remarks (paraphrase — transcript body not reachable):

- **Revenue:** +9% to +11% YoY. Against the 4Q25 base of $102,466M (Store B, context file), that is ≈ $111.7B–$113.7B, midpoint ≈ $112.7B (+3% sequential). Deceleration from June's +16% is attributed to (a) supply constraints on iPhone, Mac and iPad that will "increase significantly sequentially" and (b) FX, a ~2.5-pt sequential headwind.
- **Gross margin:** 47%–48%, **including ~1 pt of tariff refund benefit** → underlying 46–47%, versus June's ~48% underlying. Driver: memory costs, which rose "significantly" June vs March and are expected to keep rising beyond September.
- **Opex:** $19.1B–$19.4B (June actual $19.075B).
- **Services:** "similar growth trends" with continued FX headwinds (i.e., low-double-digit, not re-accelerating).
- **No guidance beyond September** — Parekh declined when pressed by Reitzes (Melius).
- Philosophy: unchanged — Apple guides a YoY revenue-growth range and a GM range, no EPS guide, no unit guide. Tone was demand-confident/supply-constrained: Cook framed the constraint as an "incredibly strong" demand cycle rather than a supplier problem. No evidence of demand pull-forward per Cook (Rakers Q).

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### ai_re_architected_incumbent

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Siri AI and Apple Intelligence, announced at WWDC26, are in developer and public betas with "strong early feedback"; Cook described a "hybrid model for AI" (on-device plus cloud) when asked about capital intensity (Mohan, BofA — Q&A, paraphrase) and "hinted at future economic models to support increased usage," with iCloud+ monetization expected to rise (prepared remarks, paraphrase). Rollout in China and the EU is gated on regulatory approvals (Chatterjee, JPM — Q&A). R&D +32% YoY to $11,729M (Ex-99.1). Nine-month capex fell to $6,799M from $9,473M (Ex-99.1).
- **Implication:** The scoring premise was "Siri overhaul slipped." It is now shipping to beta with a monetization framing, so the timeline leg of the thesis has moved forward — but nothing has converted yet (iOS 27 GA is post-quarter) and the hybrid model plus falling capex says Apple is still not building frontier capacity itself. Magnitude and mechanism unchanged; timeline improved.

### silicon_architecture_competition

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Supply constraints are "primarily driven by high demand and limited flexibility in the supply chain, especially concerning the advanced nodes used for Apple's SoCs" (prepared remarks/Q&A, paraphrase); Reitzes asked directly about "potential partnerships with new silicon providers" and got no substantive answer per the summary (Q&A). Management cited on-device AI as a competitive advantage and Mac "increasingly used for AI workloads in both enterprise and education" (prepared remarks, paraphrase).
- **Implication:** Apple Silicon remains the differentiator the theme assumes, but the binding constraint has become leading-edge foundry capacity that Apple now shares with AI-accelerator demand. The theme's mechanism widens from "whose architecture wins" to "who gets the wafers" — worth a cross-read against TSM notes.

### china_us_tensions

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Greater China revenue $18,816M, +22.4% YoY (Ex-99.1). iPhone was the top-selling model in China; Mac set all-time upgrader/new-customer records in Greater China (prepared remarks, paraphrase). Tariff refunds added ~2 pts to June GM and ~1 pt to the September guide (Ex-99.1; guidance paraphrase). Siri AI is not yet approved in China (Chatterjee Q&A).
- **Implication:** The thesis treats China as a cap; this quarter it was a growth engine and the tariff regime reversed into a refund. The risk has not gone away — AI features are regulatory-gated and the refund is a one-time offset — but the "China drag" magnitude in the PII notes is overstated on current evidence.

### handset_competition

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** iPhone $54,252M, +21.7% YoY (Ex-99.1); June-quarter records in every geographic segment and for upgraders; top-selling model in the US, China, UK, France, Australia and Japan; install base at an all-time high; 99% US customer satisfaction (prepared remarks, paraphrase). Price increases on "certain products" taken without visible demand damage (Woodring Q&A). Apple Upgrade Program (leasing) launched, US-only (Ng Q&A).
- **Implication:** Apple is taking share in a strong cycle; the competitive position leg of the thesis is intact. The "decelerating iPhone growth" premise in the PII notes is stale — the deceleration ahead is supply-driven, not demand-driven, which is a different risk.

### platform_take_rate

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Services $30,739M, +12.1% YoY (Ex-99.1), a June-quarter record with records in advertising, App Store, AppleCare, Music and Video, and >1.5B paid subscriptions — but growth decelerated from the prior quarter, and Parekh attributed the deceleration to FX, softness in mobile gaming, and **"regulatory changes in the App Store"** (Woodring Q&A, paraphrase). Services GM held at 75.6% (Ex-99.1).
- **Implication:** Take-rate erosion from regulation is now a named line item in Services growth, not a hypothetical. Magnitude is still small (Services growing double digits at a flat 75.6% margin), but the direction the theme worries about is confirmed as live.

### pc_demand

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Mac $10,352M, +28.7% YoY (Ex-99.1), a June-quarter record driven by MacBook Neo and MacBook Pro; share gains globally; best quarter ever for upgraders and new customers; Mac used for AI workloads in enterprise and education; supply-constrained into September (prepared remarks, paraphrase).
- **Implication:** PC demand for Apple is the strongest in the corpus and supply-capped — the theme is confirmed with the caveat that reported growth will understate demand next quarter.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

Transcript body was not reachable via InsiderScore; all items below are paraphrased from the vendor's AI summary of the Q&A, not verbatim.

1. **Guide vs. track record (Store B):** The September revenue guide (+9–11% YoY, mid ≈ $112.7B) should be read against Apple's recorded pattern rather than tone. SALES guide hit rate 0.8 over 5 guided periods, sandbag index −0.059, average beat vs guide midpoint +0.28%; the last three guides landed in_range/above at +0.7% (3Q26), +1.8% (2Q26) and +4.2% (1Q26). Apple's guides are near-honest with a small upside bias, so the deceleration from +16% to +9–11% is a real supply cap, not a sandbag — expect a landing at or modestly above the midpoint unless constraints ease. For gross margin the INC_GROSS record (hit rate 0.75, sandbag −0.074, average beat vs guide +1.1%) says the 47–48% range more often lands at the top end than the bottom. Note: Apple guides revenue growth and GM ranges only, no EPS.
2. **Reitzes (Melius) — supply constraints and "new silicon providers" (the one exchange the vendor scored as challenging):** Cook attributed constraints to demand, Parekh declined any view beyond September, and the second-source silicon question drew no substantive answer per the summary. The dodge matters because the constraint is guided to worsen, not ease.
3. **Daryanani (Evercore) — memory inflation:** Cook called memory pricing a "100-year flood," confirmed Apple paid significantly more in June than March, and said it will keep rising beyond September; mitigation is inventory (up to $11.1B from $5.7B at FYE per Ex-99.1), other component cost-downs, and selective price increases. Unusual emphasis — this is the clearest margin warning in the call.
4. **Woodring (Morgan Stanley) — Services deceleration:** Parekh named FX, mobile-gaming softness, and App Store regulatory changes. The regulatory driver being cited on the record is the flag; it bears directly on `platform_take_rate`.
5. **Mohan (BofA) — Siri AI capital intensity / competitive landscape:** Cook's "hybrid model" answer plus nine-month capex down 28% YoY confirms Apple is opting out of the AI capex race; Ternus, not Cook, answered on competitive positioning — an early read on the incoming CEO's posture. Also note the EPS beat is ~$0.11 tariff refund; ex-refund EPS ≈ $1.91 vs $1.89 consensus, so the "29% EPS growth" headline overstates underlying progress.
