## 3. Forward guidance

**Q1 FY27 revenue guided to $2,200–2,400M, midpoint $2,300M** (FactSet `guidance` endpoint, QTR, guidanceDate 2026-08-13, input 2026-08-12). Prior consensus was **$2,135M**, so the midpoint guide is **+7.7% above the street** — the single most important number on the call, and a larger signal than the 3.07% revenue beat itself.

Longer-dated framing, all from management on the call:
- **First quarter with over $3B in revenue by the end of fiscal 2027** — an explicit, dated, checkable milestone. From $2.3B guided in Q1 FY27, that implies ~30% growth across the fiscal year.
- Confidence attributed to three named factors: growing customer demand, improved supply of critical components, and ramp of new revenue streams (optical circuit switching, co-packaged optics).
- **EPS to grow significantly faster than revenue** on continued gross margin expansion and operating leverage.
- **Gross margin target >42%**, with CFO Luther expressing confidence in "achieving and surpassing" it. Eight of the last nine quarters showed GM improvement.
- Internal indium phosphide capacity to **double by end of the current quarter** (one quarter ahead of original plan) and **double again by end of calendar 2027**.

**Guidance philosophy:** a point-range for the quarter ($200M wide, ~9% of midpoint) paired with an unusually specific and self-imposed long-dated milestone (the ">$3B quarter by end of FY27"). Management is volunteering a falsifiable target rather than hedging — that is ambitious framing, not conservative, and it is the thing to hold them to next quarter. Backlog claims support it: record bookings, orders extending into calendar 2028, LTAs through the end of the decade.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** **Drift**
- **Evidence:** Management addressed competition almost exclusively through **pricing**, not capacity or share. Ackerman (Exane BNP) asked about LTAs and volume commitments; Anderson detailed "extraordinary bookings and backlog extending into 2028." Genovese (Rosenblatt) asked about pricing visibility; Anderson answered that "LTAs provide agreed-upon pricing, offering good visibility into future pricing dynamics." The summary's own framing: LTAs "ensure a stable pricing environment despite potential competition, particularly from China." Separately, Koontz (Needham) established that the binding constraint is **indium phosphide capacity, not demand**, and Notter (Wolfe) elicited that COHR sells **no** in-sourced laser datacom chips externally because internal demand absorbs all production. **No analyst asked, and management did not volunteer, anything about Chinese transceiver capacity additions at 800G/1.6T.**
- **Implication:** The thesis is not broken — but its protective mechanism has changed, and that matters more than the direction. `config/watchlist.yaml` records the operator's own standing concern that COHR's thesis "rest[s] on transceiver supply tightness and vertical laser integration and do[es] not mention Chinese competition at all," with the explicit test: *"if Innolight adds capacity at these speeds, the tightness thesis weakens"* (tier_4_ecosystem, `innolight.cn`). This call supplies no evidence on that test. What it supplies instead is **contractual lock-in** — LTAs fixing price and volume through the end of the decade. That is a real and arguably more durable moat, but it is a **different** thesis with a different expiry and a different failure mode: tightness fails gradually as competitors add capacity, whereas an LTA book fails discretely at renewal. Recommend the PM treat "supply tightness" as no longer the load-bearing assumption and re-state the thesis around LTA coverage, duration and renewal schedule. The Chinese-capacity question remains open and untested by this call.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** **Confirm**
- **Evidence:** Data center & communications reached **79% of Q4 revenue**, +56% YoY, with FY26 data center revenue +41%. Demand described as "exceptionally strong and broad-based," with order coverage through 2027, record bookings, and customer orders extending into calendar 2028. COHR is spending against it: $556M capex in Q4 alone with a stated **~18-month payback**, backed by purchase orders and LTAs.
- **Implication:** Direct, unambiguous confirmation. COHR is now effectively a pure-play read on hyperscale AI-datacenter optical buildout — 79% concentration means the industrial segment no longer meaningfully diversifies the name. This *strengthens* the thesis while *raising* beta to any AI-capex digestion. The 18-month-payback claim is the assumption to test if capex pacing turns.

### silicon_architecture_competition

- **Status:** **Confirm**
- **Evidence:** The 6-inch indium phosphide transition is executing **ahead of plan** — internal InP output doubling by end of Q1 FY27, a quarter early, with a second doubling targeted by end of calendar 2027. Economics disclosed: 6-inch **yields exceed 3-inch**, cost per wafer **halved**, output **4x**. InP laser production +80% YoY. Anderson: *"We remain on track to more than double our internal indium phosphide capacity again to the end of calendar '27."* Manufacturing across Texas, Sweden and Zurich; *"Our Texas facility has also begun ramping our ultra high-power CW laser for CPO solutions."* Notter (Wolfe) also drew out progress on a **200-gig VCSEL** for NPO applications.
- **Implication:** This is the strongest evidence on the call and it validates the vertical-integration leg of the watchlist's innovation_rate 4 (indium-phosphide lasers → transceivers). Halved wafer cost with higher yield is a structural margin source, not a mix effect — it is the mechanical explanation for the >42% GM target and it is largely within COHR's own control. Note the counterpart: because all internal laser output is consumed internally, there is **no merchant-market price signal** from COHR's chip business, which reduces external visibility into how contested this layer actually is.

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** **Confirm**
- **Evidence:** Multiple new topology-relevant revenue streams moving to production. **OCS addressable market outlook raised from $2B to over $4B by 2030**, with revenue already rising sequentially in Q4 and growth expected "significantly through 2027"; Curtis (Jefferies) confirmed robust demand and widening applications. **CPO revenue guided to begin in fiscal Q2 2027 with no pushouts — if anything demand is being "pulled in"**; Arya (BofA) drew out that CPO for scale-up applications is expected to generate revenue in 2H 2027. **NPO/CPO engagement intensity up over the past 3–6 months**, with comparable content per unit for both. **Photon Link** integrated-optics platform to launch at ECOC in September 2026, initial revenue in the December quarter. Multi-rail systems sampling, initial revenue ramping 1H 2027.
- **Implication:** COHR is positioning across every plausible next-gen datacenter interconnect topology — pluggable, OCS, CPO, NPO, multi-rail — rather than betting on one. The doubled OCS TAM and the "pulled in" CPO commentary are the two most forward-looking datapoints on the call. Content-per-unit parity between CPO and NPO is strategically important: it means COHR is largely indifferent to which architecture hyperscalers converge on, which materially de-risks the topology bet. Watch for actual Photon Link revenue in the December quarter as the first hard test.

### thermal_management_cooling

- **Status:** **Drift** (timeline)
- **Evidence:** Thin, and the thinness is the finding. The only reference is **ThermoDit** for data center XPU cooling, mentioned as a long-term opportunity inside the **Industrial** segment — a segment that was **flat for both Q4 and the full year**, with broader industrial markets "weak" and management explicitly noting these opportunities are **"not yet contributing meaningfully to results."** No product cadence, no design wins, no revenue timing given.
- **Implication:** No evidence supports thermal/cooling as a live driver this cycle. The watchlist's ai_positioning note cites a "thermal/cooling adjacency" — this call pushes that adjacency's timeline out rather than contradicting it, hence Drift rather than Break. Practically, the PM should underwrite COHR on optics alone and treat thermal as a free option with no dated catalyst. If it stays absent from the next two calls, consider whether the theme tag is still earning its place on this ticker.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Distribution** (current: **4**)
  - Evidence: Record bookings with orders into calendar 2028 and LTAs through end of decade. Broad-based hyperscaler engagement; multiple customers increasing 2028 commitments. Communications +54% FY26 across DCI, ZR/ZR+, pump lasers, with a multi-level sales approach into telecom. **20+ US production facilities**, positioned as advantageous if optical-transceiver import restrictions materialise. Offsetting: 79% revenue concentration in one segment, and datacom customer concentration persists (the watchlist already flags this).
  - Recommendation: **hold at 4** — genuinely strengthened by LTA duration and US-footprint optionality, but concentration deepened in the same quarter, and those partly offset.
- **Overall** (current: **4**)
  - Recommendation: **hold at 4**, with the reasoning below re-stated in the PM's notes
  - Reasoning: Not a mechanical average — for this business model the two dimensions have become **mutually reinforcing in a specific, mechanical way**. Vertical integration into indium phosphide is precisely what makes it credible for COHR to commit volume *and* price through the end of the decade, because it controls the constrained input rather than buying it; and the resulting LTA book is what de-risks the $556M/quarter capex that funds the next capacity doubling. Innovation finances distribution and distribution finances innovation. That loop is worth more than either score alone. **But the same loop concentrates the risk**: it all rests on indium phosphide remaining the binding constraint industry-wide. If Chinese suppliers (Innolight, Eoptolink, Accelink — all tagged in `tier_4_ecosystem` as affecting COHR) add capacity at 800G/1.6T, the loop weakens at both ends simultaneously rather than one at a time. Also worth recording: the watchlist's current overall-4 note names "turnaround/deleveraging execution" as *the variable* — **that variable has now largely resolved** (leverage 2.0x → 0.7x, $513M repaid). The 4 is better-supported than when written, even though the number does not change.

## 8. Management Q&A flags

1. **Leopold (Raymond James) — the call's one challenging exchange. Import restrictions, US manufacturing, June '27 revenue target, gross margin.** Leopold pressed on whether US import restrictions would actually help and whether moving manufacturing to the US is feasible, then pushed for clarity on revenue and GM targets. Anderson leaned on the 20+ US facilities; Luther went further than required, expressing confidence in **"achieving and surpassing"** the >42% GM target. *Flag: unusual emphasis, not a dodge.* Volunteering "surpassing" on a target you have not yet hit is a confidence signal — and a hostage to fortune worth marking for next quarter.
2. **Notter (Wolfe) — in-sourced laser datacom chip mix and external sales.** Anderson: current demand absorbs **all** internal production, no near-term plans for external sales. *Flag: this forecloses external price discovery.* It is a genuine capacity-constraint tell, but it also means the merchant market gives no read on how contested the InP chip layer is — precisely the layer where the watchlist notes Accelink is building in-house capability.
3. **Competition answered as pricing, never as capacity or share (Ackerman, Genovese).** Both analysts probed LTAs and pricing visibility; both got confident, detailed answers about *agreed pricing*. Neither the analysts nor management engaged Chinese capacity additions at 800G/1.6T. *Flag: consistent redirection of the competition question onto the axis where COHR is strongest.* This is the single most important gap on the call and the direct driver of the `networking_competitive_landscape` Drift above.
4. **Koontz (Needham) — capacity constraints.** Anderson identified indium phosphide as *the* constraint, not assembly/test. *Flag: convenient framing.* "We are supply-constrained, not demand-constrained" is the most favourable possible framing of a growth business and is unfalsifiable from outside — it should be treated as a claim to monitor, not an established fact, particularly once the second InP doubling completes at end-2027 and the constraint story must change.
5. **Marshall (Morgan Stanley) — "customer urgency following recent reports."** Anderson confirmed increased customer engagement and active discussions about shifting manufacturing to the US. *Flag: a potentially material benefit is being hinted at without being quantified.* Management repeatedly gestures at import-restriction upside while giving no sizing, no probability and no timeline. Treat as unquantified optionality, not as pipeline.
