## 1. Headline read

## 3. Forward guidance

September-2026 quarter guide (8-K Ex-99.1, 2026-07-29): revenue $8.10B +/- $400M; non-GAAP gross margin 52.0% +/- 1%; non-GAAP operating margin 39.5% +/- 1%; non-GAAP diluted EPS $2.15 +/- $0.15; diluted share count 1.255B. GAAP-to-non-GAAP reconciling items are immaterial ($2.7M GM, $3.0M op margin, $3.3M EPS), so GAAP and non-GAAP guides are the same to the decimal shown. The guide implies >20% sequential revenue growth (call, prepared remarks per summary).

Calendar-year framing (call, prepared remarks per summary): 2026 WFE now expected at approximately $150B, up from the prior ~$140B view; 2026 expected to be Lam's third consecutive year of outperformance versus WFE (also in the CEO quote in the press release). 2027: management described an "extraordinary" / "remarkable" setup for WFE growth across all three device types with DRAM the fastest grower, citing multiyear fab projects and customers securing equipment orders for cleanroom space coming online; no numerical 2027 guidance was given despite repeated pressing (Schneider, Rasgon).

Gross margin philosophy: near-term GM framed as 51–52%; mid-50s is a multi-year target driven by revenue scale, new product introductions, Malaysia manufacturing, and pricing actions (Bettinger, Q&A per summary). Capital return: 45% of FCF returned in the June quarter, 81% fiscal-YTD, long-run commitment of at least 85% of FCF; $4B remaining on the repurchase authorization (call per summary). Guidance format is unchanged — point-with-symmetric-range, quarterly only, no annual guide; tone is the most ambitious of the four FY26 guides relative to consensus (see Section 8).

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### foundry_capacity

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Taiwan was a record 27% of revenue in the June quarter (up from 23% sequentially), driven by leading-edge foundry and advanced packaging (call, regional discussion per summary; percentages confirmed in 8-K Ex-99.1). FY26 foundry rose to 54% of equipment/upgrade revenue from 45%, "due to mature node spending as well as investments in leading-edge equipment" (10-K FY26, Item 7). Management cited customers announcing new fab projects and Lam securing equipment orders for new cleanroom space (prepared remarks per summary); Archer said new entrants in logic offer opportunities to showcase Lam's technical advancements (Muse, Cantor, Q&A per summary).
- **Implication:** Foundry capacity is currently the largest single driver of Lam's mix and the basis for the 2027 "multiyear fab projects" narrative; the thesis that foundry expansion is a durable WFE leg is intact and the tail is now leading-edge Taiwan plus new logic entrants rather than mature-node China.

### semiconductor_cycle

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** 2026 WFE raised to ~$150B from ~$140B; September-quarter guide of $8.10B implies >20% sequential growth; deferred revenue rose $213M to $2.43B on customer downpayments; management repeatedly described the industry as undersupplied (Bettinger to Schneider and Weathers, Q&A per summary). Fourth consecutive quarter of record revenue, operating margin and EPS (prepared remarks per summary; press release). 10-K FY26 Item 7: "Wafer fabrication equipment investments were strong in the 2025 calendar year, and have continued to grow in 2026 with the AI market driving higher semiconductor industry spending across both the memory and non-memory market segments."
- **Implication:** The cycle is unambiguously in an up-leg with broadening participation (foundry, DRAM, NAND all up); the thesis's cyclicality discount is not being tested yet. The Rasgon and Malik exchanges show the sell-side is already probing for the peak, which is the signal to watch — not the fundamentals.

### hbm_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** DRAM held at 23% of systems revenue, with spending on wafer additions and upgrades for 1-alpha/1-beta/1-gamma nodes enabling DDR5, LPDDR5 and HBM; management highlighted wins in both conventional and HBM DRAM and said DRAM will lead growth in 2027 (prepared remarks per summary). Advanced packaging revenue outlook was raised multiple times this year to >70% y/y growth, with stronger HBM adoption and 2.5D/3D transitions as drivers; Lam claims leadership in TSV etch and copper electroplating (prepared remarks per summary). Bettinger acknowledged taller HBM stacks raise equipment intensity but gave no figures (Rakesh, Mizuho, Q&A per summary).
- **Implication:** Lam's HBM exposure runs through two channels — DRAM front-end capacity for the HBM base die and the TSV/plating steps in the stack — and both are accelerating; the competitive-landscape question (which memory maker wins HBM share) matters less to Lam than the aggregate HBM wafer and stack count, which is rising.

### china_export_controls

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** China fell to 26% of revenue from 34% sequentially, "as expected"; within China, global multinationals grew while domestic Chinese customers declined; management sees China WFE flat to slightly up with quarterly lumpiness (Pajjuri, RBC, Q&A per summary; percentage confirmed in 8-K Ex-99.1). Full-year FY26 China was 34% of revenue, unchanged from FY25's 34%, on a revenue base that grew 26.0% (10-K FY26, Item 7). The 10-K MD&A states that "volatility ... from trade restrictions, tariffs ... have had, and in the future may have, a negative impact on our revenue and operating margin," and attributes part of the gross-margin offset to "aluminum and steel tariff-related spend."
- **Implication:** The export-control theme as a top-line drag is not visible in reported numbers — China dollars grew with the company in FY26 — so the thesis mechanism should shift from "restricted addressable market" to "mix rotation inside China (indigenous down, multinational up) plus tariff cost pressure on margin." That is a milder and more diffuse risk than the thesis assumed at scoring.

### lithography_roadmap

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** No lithography-specific commentary is present in the AI summary of the call. The relevant mechanism is stated directly in the 10-K FY26 Item 7: "semiconductor manufacturers are adopting vertical scaling approaches, including three-dimensional ('3D') architecture, more sophisticated patterning schemes, new materials, and advanced integration approaches, as traditional two-dimensional scaling is becoming more challenging," which "drives demand for our advanced semiconductor fabrication technologies." On the call, management said Lam's NAND SAM per wafer is expected to double as layer counts increase, and that next-generation AI packages will exceed 9x reticle size with more redistribution layers (prepared remarks per summary).
- **Implication:** Lam's leverage to the lithography roadmap is the inverse one — the harder 2D scaling gets, the more value migrates to etch/deposition-intensive 3D and packaging flows — and this quarter's evidence (NAND layer-count upgrades, panel-level packaging) supports that mechanism. Absence of direct litho commentary is a summary-fidelity limitation, not contrary evidence.

### nand_demand_cycle

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** NAND revenue more than doubled sequentially; non-volatile memory rose to 23% of systems revenue from 12%; spending is upgrades to 200+ and 256+ layer devices, with customers adding bit supply and running conversions; management sees no signs of NAND oversupply for 2027 and expects upgrades to continue through at least 2027 (prepared remarks and Malik/Mani Q&A per summary). AI-driven demand for flash storage was cited as the driver; SAM per wafer expected to double with layer count (prepared remarks per summary).
- **Implication:** The NAND leg has moved from "recovery" to "AI-driven upgrade cycle" — the strongest confirmation on the call. The risk is that NAND is the most cyclical of the three device types and this is the segment analysts (Malik) are already asking about oversupply on; a doubling in one quarter should be read as a step-change in run-rate, not a new steady growth rate.
