## 4. Thesis read by theme

### foundry_capacity

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** Capex raised to $60–64B with "no bottlenecks to our capacity expansion plans" (md_1); $100B Arizona addition, ~4 more fabs, plus 13 fabs in Taiwan and three new N3 fabs (md_2, qna_9); "the demand and the supply, the gap is so big. So, we are working very hard to narrow the gap" (qna_64); packaging capacity "so tight than now it's limiting my customers' growth" (qna_27); N2 capacity CAGR "now is bigger" than the ~70% shown at the symposium (qna_103). Inventory days +7 to 87 on the N2 ramp (md_1). Robert Sanders' question on whether unconstrained N3-and-below demand exceeds supply by more than 30–50% (qna_95) — Wei's answer is turn 97, one of the nine missing turns; not assessable.
- **Implication:** TSMC is supply-constrained at the leading edge and in packaging through at least 2027 by its own account, which underwrites utilization and pricing but also caps near-term upside to the volume TSMC can physically ship. Capacity intensity is rising for non-volume reasons too (tool inflation, overseas duplication).

### semiconductor_cycle

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** A bifurcated cycle, explicitly: "AI-related demand continues to be extremely robust" while "consumer and the price-sensitive end market segment are being challenged due to the impact of rising component prices and macroeconomics uncertainties" (md_2). Mature nodes: only AI-adjacent segments (PMIC at 0.18µm/90nm, CMOS image sensors) are in shortage; "other segment is not so strong demand" (qna_116). Wei on double-ordering: "every customer tell me the truth... You put all the tools together is not a truth... we are checking the AI data center's progress, the building, the location, the demand, the racks... to make sure that TSMC's chips will not be put in inventory" (qna_37). Duration hedge: strong to 2029–2030, "whether in between there's a dip or not, I'm not very sure" (qna_39).
- **Implication:** The cycle thesis for TSM is an AI-node cycle, not a broad semiconductor upcycle — smartphone (−4% QoQ, 22% of revenue) and commodity mature nodes are not participating. Management's stated demand-verification process is the right thing to hear but is unfalsifiable from outside; the "dip" hedge is the honest tell that linearity into 2028 is not guaranteed.

### china_us_tensions

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** No retrieved turn discussed export controls, tariffs, cross-strait risk, or China revenue. The geopolitical content is all on the mitigation side: $100B additional Arizona investment "with the strong collaboration and support from our leading US customers and the US federal, state and city government" (md_2); overseas dilution 2–3% widening to 3–4% (md_1); Wei on Intel's US policy support: "We also got the government support, by the way, although we don't announce it" (qna_15). Nine Q&A turns are missing, so absence of a geopolitics question in the retrieved text is not evidence that none was asked.
- **Implication:** The thesis (geopolitical risk is the cap, not a break) holds — the call added no escalation signal and showed the geographic hedge scaling up, at a quantified and widening margin cost. The Arizona expansion reduces tail risk for US customers but does not change the Taiwan concentration of leading-edge output in the 2026–2028 window.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** HPC +20% QoQ to 66% of revenue (md_1; [derived] from ~62% in 1Q26). CSPs "continue to provide us with their very strong signal and positive outlook" (md_2). AI accelerator five-year CAGR (mid-to-high-50s given in January): "not a number, but it's stronger and stronger and stronger... stronger than what we said before" (qna_18). Capex raise driver: "It's all AI related. Everything" (qna_82). Agentic AI "is leading to a resurgence in the role of CPUs in AI data centers, which drive more silicon demand in addition to AI accelerators... whether it's x86, ARM-based or RISC-V architecture, they are almost all TSMC's customers" (md_2). "We are witnessing a kind of a new industry... called AI industry" (qna_39). TSMC does not do vendor-financing arrangements with end customers (qna_56).
- **Implication:** TSMC is the cleanest read-through on hyperscaler capex and every signal was up: FY guide, capex, CAGR direction, HPC mix. The agentic-AI/CPU point widens the addressable silicon per data-center dollar beyond accelerators, which matters for the durability of the mix shift. The refusal to re-quantify the CAGR leaves consensus without an anchor (§8).

### lithography_roadmap

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** N2 at 3% of wafer revenue in 2Q, steep ramp in 3Q (3–4 pt GM dilution implies a large volume step) (md_1). A14: second-generation nanosheet, 10–15% speed or 25–30% power vs N2, ~20% density; ~90% device performance and ~90% 256Mb SRAM yield on internal vehicles; tape-outs ahead of schedule; pre-production 2027, volume 2028; A13/A12 derivatives 2029 (md_2). High-NA EUV (qna_93): "High-NA is a very good tool... But then TSMC make it clear that we work with ASML and try to make it more suitable for manufacturing in terms of the cost and in terms of the maturity" — field size "only one half" is in the cost calculus; no adoption commitment. Charlie Chan notes ASML's EUV capacity expansion for 2028 and asks whether competitors will take slots (qna_13); Wei's answer is the "no shortcuts... five years" argument (qna_15), not a tool-allocation answer.
- **Implication:** Process leadership is intact and extending (A14 family positioned as "an even larger and long-lasting node" than N2) on a cost-disciplined, low-NA-first litho path — which lowers TSMC's capex exposure to High-NA but also means the A14 shrink relies on DTCO and optical-shrink derivatives (A13's 97% shrink). No mention of A16 in the retrieved text; the thesis names N2/A16 and the call's evidence is N2/A14.

## 5. AI positioning signal

- **Current score:** 5 (scoring_date 2026-06-02, analyst-draft)
- **Evidence from this call:** Product — N2 ramping, A14/A13/A12 roadmap on track, COUPE co-packaged optics in production, CoWoS roadmap beyond 14x reticle plus a glass-substrate pilot line ~1 year from production (qna_68, qna_75, md_2). Partnership — CSPs and CPU vendors across all three ISAs engaged; Sony image-sensor preliminary agreement in the 6-K recap. Capex — $60–64B, "all AI related." Customer — HPC 66% of revenue, "a lot of new player in the AI industry." Capability gap — packaging capacity is the binding constraint on customers' growth by management's own description; TSMC welcomes Intel EMIB-T as an outlet (qna_27, qna_87).
- **Recommendation:** hold at 5; revise the notes text.
- **Reasoning:** Nothing on this call weakens TSMC's position as the indispensable AI enabler — every quantitative signal moved up. But the watchlist's "CoWoS advanced-packaging chokehold gating HBM integration" is framed as a moat, and management framed it as a bottleneck it wants relieved ("that will help TSMC's front-end wafer business growth, which is a majority part of TSMC's business"). The score is a 5 on front-end indispensability; the packaging clause should read as a constraint TSMC is actively opening rather than defending.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: 5): A14 ahead of schedule on tape-outs with A13/A12 derivatives pre-announced for 2029, COUPE in production, glass-substrate pilot line, CoWoS >14x reticle — the cadence is a full node plus two derivatives per generation, and the High-NA answer (qna_93) shows the litho path is cost-gated, not capability-gated. Recommendation: hold at 5.
- **Distribution** (current: 5): "all of them are in TSMC" for CPU/GPU/XPU (qna_72); every CPU architecture for agentic AI is "almost all TSMC's customers" (md_2); customer base broadening ("lot of new player", qna_48); no second-source disclosures. The only distribution leak discussed is packaging (EMIB-T), which management characterizes as complementary to its front-end business. Recommendation: hold at 5.
- **Overall** (current: 5): hold at 5. The two dimensions interact through lead time: Wei's "not buying milk from 7-Eleven... it takes about five years" (qna_15) is the mechanism by which process innovation becomes distribution lock-in — customers cannot re-source a node they co-developed. The risk to distribution on this evidence is not customer defection but TSMC's own inability to supply, which is exactly what management says pushes packaging volume toward Intel. That is a capacity problem, not a competitive-advantage problem, and the $100B/13-fab response is the answer to it.
