# CSCO — 4Q26 earnings read

_Prepared by earnings-reviewer, 2026-08-13. Tier: T2._

## 1. Headline read

Q4'26 (July-quarter, reported 2026-08-12) is a **confirm-and-extend** of the 3Q26 read, and the single most important disclosure is not the AI number — it is the **ex-AI growth rate**. FY27 revenue guided to $72.2–73.4B (+15.0% at midpoint off FY26's record $63.3B), with management framing **~10% growth excluding AI** against a stated long-term model of 4–6%. That reframes CSCO from "incumbent with an AI kicker" to "incumbent whose core is re-accelerating." Q4 revenue $17,252M beat consensus by +2.5% and beat the company's own Q3-issued guide ($16.7–16.9B) at the high end; EPS $1.22 beat by +4.6% and cleared the $1.16–1.18 guide. The FY26 hyperscaler AI order book closed at **$9.3B** against the $9B target set on the 3Q26 call, and the FY27 hyperscaler AI *revenue* number moves to **$7.5B** vs. the ">=$6B" floor given last quarter. Beat magnitude has widened for three consecutive quarters. Two theme reads change direction this quarter: **security drifts up to Confirm**, and the standing recommendation to deprioritize `observability_competitive_landscape` is **withdrawn** — CSCO bought its way into the theme this quarter.

## 3. Forward guidance

1. **Q1'27 (October-quarter):** Revenue $18.0–18.2B; non-GAAP EPS $1.32–1.34. The revenue midpoint ($18.1B) is +4.9% sequential off a record Q4 — no post-Q4 air pocket.
2. **FY27 (full year):** Revenue **$72.2–73.4B**, ~+15% YoY at midpoint. A full-year FY27 EPS range was not surfaced in the summary — only the Q1 figure. Operating margin expansion is guided; gross margin is guided to **slight pressure** as hardware mix rises.
3. **AI infrastructure revenue (hyperscalers), FY27: $7.5B** — up from <$2B in FY25 and ~$4B in FY26, and **+25% above the ">=$6B" floor** given only one quarter ago.
4. **Ex-AI growth ~10%**, explicitly contrasted against the 4–6% long-term model.
5. **Security:** low-single-digit growth in FY26 → **high-single-digit in FY27**.

**The ex-AI claim reconciles, and it is worth deriving rather than accepting.** Core FY26 revenue ex-AI ≈ $63.3B − $4.0B = $59.3B. Grown at 10% = $65.2B, plus $7.5B FY27 AI revenue = **$72.7B**, against a guide midpoint of $72.8B — agreement within ~$70M. The ~10% core number is arithmetically consistent with the top-line guide and is not a rhetorical flourish.

**Provenance of the $4.0B input (two different $4.0B figures are in play — do not conflate them):** the $4.0B subtracted above is **FY26 hyperscaler AI *revenue* as guided on the 3Q26 call** (source: `notes/CSCO/20260514-3Q26.md`, where management stated $4B of the $9B FY26 order book would be recognized as FY26 revenue). **FY26 actual AI revenue was not disclosed on this call**, so this is a guidance-basis input, not a reported actual — and the reconciliation inherits that basis. It is a distinct quantity from the **$4.0B of Q4 hyperscaler AI *orders*** cited in section 2, which coincidentally shares the same magnitude.

**Guidance philosophy — two metric-framing shifts on a single call, both worth flagging.** First, Patterson "outlined the shift to revenue targets for AI," moving the headline AI metric from **orders to revenue**. Second, management stated it "emphasizes operating margin as a better profitability indicator" given the high operating leverage of the AI/hyperscale business — moving the profitability anchor from **gross margin to operating margin**. Each shift is individually defensible: orders-to-revenue is the natural maturation once a backlog converts, and GM-to-OM is the honest framing for a mix-shifting hardware business where GM optics understate economics. But both shifts move the goalposts in the same direction — away from the metric under pressure and toward the metric that looks better — and both landed on the same call. Two analysts (Vogt, Reitzes) circled precisely this. Point-range format retained; no shift to point estimates. Tone escalated again from "confident-with-conviction" (3Q26) to **structural-cycle framing** — the "multiyear, multibillion-dollar super cycle" language is new and is the rhetorical center of the call.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm (strong) — but second derivative is moderating
- **Evidence:** Q4 networking product orders **+40% YoY**, the eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth, with double-digit order growth in data center switching, compute, campus switching, wireless, enterprise routing and industrial IoT, and order momentum "cited in every geography and customer segment." **More than half of customers purchased both campus and data center** solutions. Management introduced the "multiyear, multibillion-dollar super cycle" framing supported by infrastructure modernization plus AI. Ackerman (Exane BNP) asked about Cisco Cloud Control's contribution to campus growth; Robbins cited positive customer feedback and a platform approach driving demand.
- **Implication:** Thesis confirms, but honestly: **order growth decelerated from >50% in Q3 to +40% in Q4**. My 3Q26 note leaned on the "+50%+" figure as the second most important number on that call, so the step-down deserves explicit acknowledgment rather than being buried under the "super cycle" language. That said, +40% on a materially larger base with the eighth consecutive double-digit quarter, plus the >50% campus-and-data-center attach rate, is evidence of breadth rather than a single-vector AI spike. The attach-rate disclosure is new and is the strongest structural datapoint — it is the cross-sell moat showing up in a number. Read-through remains positive for ANET, CIEN, FFIV.

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm (strong) — targets cleared, but cadence of new design wins slowed
- **Evidence:** FY26 hyperscaler AI infrastructure orders **$9.3B** (4.5x prior year) vs. the $9B target set on the 3Q26 call; **Q4 orders alone $4B**; FY27 AI infrastructure revenue guided to **$7.5B** vs. the ">=$6B" floor from 3Q26; non-hyperscaler AI orders **>$1B** for the year; **three new hyperscaler design wins in Q4**, including **scale-across and scale-out** use cases; Silicon One to roll out across all high-performance networking systems **by 2029**; 60/40 systems/optics mix held. Supply chain: direct TSMC engagement, proactive memory investment with **Nanya**, no material lead-time constraints.
- **Implication:** Every quantitative gate set last quarter cleared. The $9.3B beat its target, and the FY27 revenue number came in 25% above the floor. Two caveats keep this from being an unqualified upgrade. First, **design-win cadence fell to 3 from 5 in Q3** — one lower-cadence quarter, not yet a trend, but directly relevant to the competitive-advantage gate I set last quarter (see section 6). Second, **"scale-across and scale-out" is still not "scale-up."** The gap Rakers surfaced in 2Q26 — CSCO absent from the intra-rack/NVLink-equivalent layer — is unaddressed three quarters later; scale-across is a genuine new axis but sits between racks, not inside them. The 2029 Silicon One roadmap is the most durable disclosure: it converts AI exposure from an order book into a product-architecture commitment. Read-through positive for AVGO, MRVL, COHR/LITE (40% optics mix intact, pluggables still the preference); NVDA retains the scale-up layer uncontested.

### cybersecurity_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** **Drift → Confirm** (upgraded from 3Q26's "Drift trending toward Confirm") — with a run-rate caveat
- **Evidence:** Security revenue **+14% YoY in Q4** — clearing the "approaching double-digit exiting Q4 2026" bar management set back in 2Q26 and which my 3Q26 note judged merely "achievable." Firewall orders **+30%**; **1,500+ new customers** on Secure Access/XDR/Hypershield/AI Defense in Q4 (6,400+ cumulative, up from ~5,000 at Q3 — a step up in acquisition rate); Splunk added **280+ logos**, exceeding its 1,000 FY26 target; the whole security portfolio including Splunk posted double-digit order growth. Acquisition of **Astrix Securities** announced. FY27 guided to **high-single-digit** growth. The summary notes some Q4 outperformance came from **"a few large on-prem deals, suggesting not all gains are recurring at this pace."**
- **Implication:** The three-quarter arc completes: −4% (2Q26) → order-growth-only disclosure (3Q26) → **+14% revenue (4Q26)**. Management hit a target it set two quarters earlier, which is a credibility win. But the quality-of-beat caveat matters and management effectively conceded it: **FY27 is guided to high-single-digit, i.e. below the Q4 print**, which is management telling you the +14% is not the run rate. Confirm is the right call because the customer-acquisition metrics are recurring even if the large on-prem deals are not. Read-through remains cautious for PANW, CRWD, ZS at the platform-consolidation layer.

### observability_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** **Drift (positive)** — and the standing "deprioritize this theme" recommendation is **withdrawn**
- **Evidence:** CSCO **acquired Galileo Technologies specifically to bolster observability**. Software revenue **$6.2B, +11% YoY** — a sharp acceleration from $5.7B/+1% in Q3. Total RPO $46.7B (+7%), product RPO +9%; ARR $32.1B (+3%), product ARR +5%; subscription now 48% of total revenue. **Cisco Cloud Control** launched as a software management platform with positive early customer feedback, and was credited by Ackerman's question as a campus-growth contributor. The Splunk on-prem-to-cloud transition drag, which dominated both prior calls, was no longer framed as the defining narrative.
- **Implication:** **This reverses my own recommendation from the last two notes.** Both the 2Q26 and 3Q26 notes told the operator to consider whether `observability_competitive_landscape` was over-fit for CSCO, on the grounds that two consecutive calls produced no distinct observability narrative. That recommendation is now contradicted by evidence: CSCO spent money (Galileo) to deepen the theme, shipped a platform (Cloud Control), and software revenue inflected from +1% to +11%. **The theme should be retained.** I flag this as a self-correction rather than quietly dropping it, because a repeated recommendation carries more weight than a first-time one and the operator may have been close to acting on it. The Splunk drag easing is the mechanism: the transition headwind that suppressed the software line for two quarters has largely annualized.

### enterprise_ai_adoption

- **Status:** Confirm (mid-stage → broad-based)
- **Evidence:** Enterprise product orders **+21% YoY** in Q4 with double-digit growth across all categories and geographies. Cardoso (JPM) directly probed the sustainability of enterprise IT budgets; Robbins responded that enterprises are **reprioritizing existing budgets** toward AI readiness, cybersecurity and infrastructure refresh, likening the current urgency to how cybersecurity was prioritized in recent years, with "little evidence of stretch or pause." Modernization described as ongoing in both campus and data center. Cisco Cloud Control and **AI Canvas** introduced as operational-efficiency products.
- **Implication:** Confirms and broadens from 3Q26. The mechanism Robbins described is the important nuance and it cuts both ways: enterprise AI spend is funded by **reallocation, not budget expansion**. That is durable for CSCO (it wins the reallocation because AI readiness and security both route through the network) but it is a zero-sum signal for the broader enterprise software complex — someone is losing the budget CSCO is winning. The "little evidence of stretch or pause" framing is the most direct demand-durability statement management has made across the three calls. Read-through positive for NOW, PLTR, IOT; mildly cautionary for non-AI-adjacent enterprise software line items competing for the same reallocated dollar.
