# NVDA — 2Q27 (FY27) earnings read

_Prepared by earnings-reviewer, 2026-08-27. Tier: T1 (BCTK)._

## 1. Headline read

**Confirm on demand and scale; first genuine Drift on margin.** Q2'27 revenue of $96.2B (+4.28% vs consensus, >100% YoY — a fourth consecutive quarter of YoY acceleration) and a Q3 guide of $108B extend the demand thesis, and management's new ~70% FY28 growth guide sits ~$100B / ~+17% above FactSet's FY28 consensus mean — the largest forward-revision gap this coverage has recorded. Against that, the pillar both prior notes carried as settled — "mid-70s gross margin sustainable" — has broken its own guidance: GM is guided to trough at **71-72% in Q4 and stabilize at 72-73% in FY28**, driven by *memory input costs*, not competitive pricing. That is a different mechanism than the 4Q26 Reitzes framing (margin sustainability contingent on architectural leadership), and it is the substantive new information on this call. Secondary: growth is now explicitly **supply-capped, not demand-capped** (management says demand supports ~100% but supply permits ~70%), and roughly one-fourth of next year's business is tied to companies NVDA has itself financed.

## 3. Forward guidance

- **Q3'27 revenue: $108B ±2%.** Against a pre-print FactSet consensus mean of **$104,863M** (median $103,750M, n=45, fiscalEndDate 2026-10-31, estimateDate 2026-08-26), the guide is **+$3.14B / +3.0%** above consensus — *derived*. Growth "driven primarily by ACIE," with hyperscale growth expected to accelerate in Q4 and FY28.
- **FY28 revenue growth: ~70% YoY — a new disclosure and the most consequential number on the call.** FactSet FY27 consensus is $396,229M and FY28 consensus is $573,493M (fiscalEndDate 2028-01-31, n=60), i.e. the street models **+44.7%** FY28 growth. A ~70% guide off the FY27 consensus base implies roughly **$674B, ~$100B / ~+17.5% above the FY28 consensus mean** — *derived*. Note the wide street dispersion (FY28 stdev $62.5B; high $838B, low $437.6B), so consensus is thin here.
- **Gross margin — the reversal.** GM guided to **bottom at 71-72% in Q4** and stabilize at **72-73% in FY28**, versus 75% actual this quarter and "mid-70s" guided in both prior calls. Management attributed this to **rising memory costs**, and was explicitly transparent about it. This is an *input-cost* margin story, not a competitive-pricing one.
- **Supply is the binding constraint, not demand.** Demand forecasts indicate potential ~100% growth next year; supply permits ~70%. Acute bottlenecks named: **memory (extreme pricing and scarcity), data center power and shell availability, wafer foundry capacity.** Management expects supply to remain a bottleneck **through at least the end of FY28**.
- **CPU:** Vera CPU in production; CPU revenue expected to **more than double in FY28** (base unstated — see §6).
- **Capital return:** expects to continue returning **at least 50% of free cash flow**. Consistent with the 1Q27 commitment.
- **China:** data center compute excluded from forward outlook, as in both prior quarters.
- **Ecosystem/financing:** ~$50B invested in Frontier AI labs to date; $500B of third-party capital secured with infrastructure capital providers; credit enhancements and minimum revenue guarantees for select projects (4.25 GW Portsmouth campus for OpenAI). Management stated these investments are expected to contribute **about one-fourth of business next year**.

**Guidance philosophy — a real change.** Mechanically NVDA has added a *full-year-ahead growth-rate guide* (FY28 ~70%) to the prior Kress point-plus-band quarterly format. That is a new instrument for this company and it moves the revision battleground from the near quarter to the out-year. Tone remains confident but is measurably more hedged than 1Q27: the constraint language is quantified and repeated, and the margin guide is a walk-down delivered proactively rather than extracted in Q&A.

## 4. Thesis read by theme

### ai_infrastructure_capex

- **Status:** Confirm (emphatically)
- **Evidence:** Revenue $96B (>2x YoY); Data Center $89B (+18% QoQ); Q3 guide $108B; FY28 guided ~+70%. AWS to deploy an **additional 2 million GPUs** from this quarter through Q2 FY29. Management cited a **$2 trillion cloud industry backlog** and global AI VC funding **>$400B in H1 2026**. Demand explicitly exceeds supply ("demand forecasts indicating potential growth of up to 100% next year, constraints in the supply chain limit NVIDIA's ability to meet this demand fully").
- **Implication:** The capex cycle is not decelerating; it is now rationed. The thesis is confirmed in magnitude and extended in duration — a supply-capped 70% is a *higher-quality* growth number than a demand-met 70%, because it implies backlog rather than pull-forward. The caveat that belongs here rather than in the status: ~one-fourth of next year's business is expected to come from customers NVDA has financed ($50B into AI labs, $500B third-party capital arranged, credit enhancements and minimum revenue guarantees). That is a risk to the *durability* of the capex stream, not evidence the capex isn't happening. Flagged for §8 and §9.

### silicon_architecture_competition

- **Status:** Confirm — the pre-registered break trigger did not fire
- **Evidence:** The trigger recorded at watchlist line 606 is an **AMD MI400-class merchant-accelerator ramp into 2027**. Nothing on this call evidenced it: AMD was not named, and no merchant-share loss was disclosed or implied. Positively, Vera Rubin is described as the **fastest product ramp in NVIDIA's history** with 30x higher throughput per megawatt and 35x lower token costs; revenue opportunity per gigawatt steps $18B (Hopper) → $25B (Blackwell) → $40B (Vera Rubin); Groq 3 LPX (rack-scale LPU) shipped with 4x tokens/sec vs alternatives.
- **Implication:** The pre-specified drift-down condition is not met and the theme holds at Confirm. **Kept separate on purpose:** on the Arya exchange, management acknowledged deploying capital into OpenAI and Anthropic *while those firms develop custom chips*, expressing confidence in the "durability and redeployability" of NVDA compute. That is an **ASIC-side** signal and a genuinely new admission, but it is not the merchant-accelerator trigger the operator pre-registered. Tracking them separately keeps the pre-registered condition clean; if the operator wants the ASIC axis to carry its own trigger, it needs to be written as one.

### networking_competitive_landscape

- **Status:** Confirm (with a disclosure regression)
- **Evidence:** "The networking business had another record quarter, with Spectrum-X Ethernet growing 2.6 times year-over-year." Vera Rubin platform integrates NVLink switch trays, ConnectX-class SuperNICs and Spectrum-X.
- **Implication:** Direction confirmed, but **no dollar figure was disclosed this quarter** — breaking a series that ran $11B (4Q26) → $15B (1Q27). For a line both prior notes called an independent thesis pillar, that is a real transparency step backwards and it blocks the QoQ read. I did not pull the 10-Q (iacc 43682247) for the segment figure this run; recommended as a cheap operator follow-up. The Spectrum-X 2.6x YoY is the best available proxy and remains consistent with continued Ethernet-fabric share-take — so the read-through flagged in the 1Q27 note (competitive overhang for ANET, tailwind for COHR/LITE/CIEN/GLW) stands unchanged.

### sovereign_ai_deployments

- **Status:** Confirm (re-quantified)
- **Evidence:** Sovereign AI **up 35% QoQ and tripled YoY**, explicitly called out as a "vibrant contributor." Non-hyperscaler business (sovereigns, neoclouds, enterprise edge, air-gapped data centers) is ~half of Data Center revenue and **growing ~100% annually**. Neocloud installed capacity expected to reach **8 GW by end-2026, from 3 GW at end-2025**.
- **Implication:** This reverses the transparency complaint from the 1Q27 note, where sovereign disappeared as a line item into the ACIE aggregate. It is separately quantified again, and the growth rate (tripled YoY) is faster than the corporate average. The theme is confirmed on both economics and disclosure.

### china_export_controls

- **Status:** Drift — **for the opposite reason than the prior two quarters**
- **Evidence:** "Revenue from China data center compute is now negligible (<1% of Q2 data center revenue), with ongoing US government license restrictions and continued geopolitical uncertainty. Hopper shipments to China are dilutive to gross margins. Management has excluded China data center compute from forward outlooks."
- **Implication:** 4Q26 and 1Q27 were both classified Drift because management went *silent* on China and the silence was unreadable. This quarter it is quantified: China is <1% of DC revenue, structurally excluded from guidance, and the residual Hopper flow is *margin-dilutive*. The ambiguity is resolved — China has gone from swing variable to a de-minimis, fully-excluded line, which makes any re-open a pure unguided free option with no offsetting downside in the base. The label stays Drift because the theme's *character* has changed (magnitude collapsed to zero); the substance is that this is now a resolved question rather than an open one. Recommend the operator consider whether this theme still earns a slot on NVDA specifically, or should be tracked at cluster level.

### inference_compute_economics

- **Status:** Drift
- **Evidence:** Customer-side token economics improved sharply — Vera Rubin at **30x higher throughput per megawatt and 35x lower token costs**; revenue opportunity per gigawatt $18B → $25B → $40B across Hopper/Blackwell/Vera Rubin; Groq 3 LPX at 4x tokens/sec. Huang (Reitzes exchange) framed the current phase as one where "AI generates profitable tokens." **But** NVDA's own unit economics moved the other way: GM 75% actual, guided to trough at **71-72% in Q4** and 72-73% in FY28 on **memory input costs**.
- **Implication:** This is the call's central tension and the reason this theme moves off Confirm. The inference-economics thesis has always been read as doubly favourable — customers get cheaper tokens *and* NVDA holds mid-70s margin. Those two have now decoupled: NVDA is absorbing memory cost inflation rather than passing it through, which is a **pricing-power** signal in the wrong direction and directly contradicts the "mid-70s sustainable" pillar carried in both prior notes. Note the mechanism carefully — this is *input cost*, not competitive price pressure, which is a materially better problem to have and is plausibly cyclical with the memory market. Drift, not Break: the thesis survives with a 3-point margin haircut and a new input-cost sensitivity that the model does not currently carry.

### ai_compute_topology

- **Status:** Confirm
- **Evidence:** The "full stack AI factory platform" framing is now monetized explicitly through **revenue opportunity per gigawatt** ($18B → $25B → $40B), which is a topology metric by construction — it prices the rack/row/datacenter integration rather than the chip. Management describes involvement "from securing land and power to delivering the entire AI factory platform." Vera Rubin integrates GPU + Vera CPU + NVLink + Spectrum-X + BlueField in one rack-scale unit.
- **Implication:** The topology thesis is now the company's own primary unit of account, which is about as strong a confirmation as a theme can get. The $/GW ladder is the cleanest quantification of the topology moat this coverage has seen and is the metric to track going forward — it converts architectural integration directly into dollars per unit of the actual binding constraint (power). Reinforces read-through to fabric/optics suppliers.

## 6. Competitive advantage signal

- **Innovation rate** (current: 5)
  - Evidence: Vera Rubin is the **fastest product ramp in company history**, at 30x throughput per megawatt and 35x lower token cost; revenue per gigawatt ladder $18B → $25B → $40B; Vera CPU moved to production; Groq 3 LPX rack-scale LPU shipped at 4x tokens/sec. Annual-cadence execution intact across GPU, CPU, networking and now LPU.
  - **Pre-registered test — Vera CPU at ~$20B FY27 standalone revenue visibility / $200B TAM (watchlist line 1045, sourced to the Huang/Arya 1Q27 exchange): NOT ADJUDICABLE from this call.** The two CPU datapoints disclosed are (a) **Grace** CPU revenue >$5B TTM and (b) CPU revenue expected to **more than double in FY28**. Neither resolves the claim: (a) is the *prior-generation* CPU on a *trailing* basis and so is largely pre-Vera, and is not the same measure as FY27 total or standalone-Vera CPU revenue; (b) has an **unstated base** — if the FY27 CPU base is ~$20B, ">2x in FY28" implies >$40B and is fully consistent with the watchlist claim, whereas if the base is the $5B Grace figure it is not. FY27 also has two quarters left to run with Vera only now in production. What would resolve it: a disclosed CPU segment line, or an explicit FY27 CPU dollar figure. **Recommend the operator leave line 1045 as written and re-test at the Q3'27 print** — I am specifically not recommending a strike, because the apparent contradiction dissolves under either plausible base.
  - Recommendation: **hold at 5**
- **Distribution** (current: 5)
  - Evidence: Non-hyperscaler now ~half of Data Center revenue and growing ~100% annually; ACIE $40B (+25% QoQ, +138% YoY) vs Hyperscale $49B (+13% QoQ). AWS committed to an additional 2M GPUs through Q2 FY29. Neocloud installed capacity 3 GW → 8 GW across 2026. Sovereign tripled YoY. ~20 AI-natives above $1B run-rate, from 13. New **revenue-sharing model with neoclouds** layers a recurring stream on top of one-time equipment sales.
  - Recommendation: **hold at 5** — with a quality-of-distribution caveat: breadth is unambiguously increasing, but an increasing share of it is NVDA-financed (credit enhancements, minimum revenue guarantees, ~one-fourth of next year's business from ecosystem-investee customers). Distribution *reach* is 5; distribution *independence* is lower than it was two quarters ago.
- **Overall** (current: 5)
  - Recommendation: **hold at 5**
  - Reasoning: Not a mechanical average, and the interaction has genuinely changed shape this quarter. Through 1Q27 the two legs compounded through a **technical** channel: each architectural generation widened CUDA lock-in, which widened the customer base, which funded the next generation. This call introduces a **financial** channel between them — NVDA now converts balance sheet into demand (financing labs and neoclouds), which fills the fabs, which funds the cadence that produces the innovation. In the near term that is a moat *amplifier*: it lets NVDA convert a supply-constrained market into multi-year committed volume, and it is precisely why a 70% FY28 guide is credible. Over a longer horizon it couples the two legs to a single new failure mode that did not previously exist — if AI-lab funding markets tighten, innovation and distribution now degrade *together* rather than independently, because the same capital cycle underwrites both. The 5/5/5 holds on this print; the composition of that 5 is meaningfully different from the one recorded in May, and this is the first quarter in three where I can articulate a concrete path to a 4.

## 7. Potential investor interest signal

- **Current score:** 4 (with a standing, not-yet-adopted 4+ recommendation from both prior notes)

**Direct answer to the pre-registered question (watchlist line 1048): the trajectory held on the fundamentals, but the pre-registered 4+→5 condition is NOT met — because the cap changed rather than cleared.** Leg-by-leg:

1. **Third consecutive quarter of YoY revenue acceleration — PASSES, and extends to a fourth.** +73% (Q4'26) → +85% (Q1'27) → >100% (Q2'27, "more than doubling year-over-year"). Rare at $5T of market cap and the cleanest of the three legs.
2. **~$119B buyback authorization / 25x dividend hike as a structural ~50%-of-FCF commitment — PASSES.** $26B returned this quarter ($20B repurchase + $6B dividend), a record, and management reiterated the intent to return **at least 50% of FCF**. The 1Q27 commitment was honoured in its first full quarter, which is what converts a policy statement into a structural one.
3. **Q2'27 guide ~$10B above consensus — PASSES in sign, and the locus moved.** On the like-for-like measure the premium compressed sharply: the Q3 guide of $108B is only **+$3.14B / +3.0%** over the $104.86B consensus, versus ~$10B / ~+12% last quarter — the sell-side has caught up on the near quarter. But management replaced it with a bigger instrument: the ~70% FY28 growth guide implies **~$674B against a $573.5B consensus, ~+17.5%**. The revision impulse did not decelerate; it moved out the curve, where dispersion is far wider (FY28 stdev $62.5B).

**On the stated cap.** The watchlist caps the score with "valuation caution at ~$5.4T mcap." Market cap on this print is **$5,078,174,860,000** (InsiderScore transcript payload, 2026-08-26) — roughly 6% *below* the level at which the caution was written, while revenue over the same two quarters went from $82B to $96.2B. The multiple has compressed materially. That cuts both ways for an *investor-interest* attribute: it is simultaneously "the valuation objection has eased" and "the market is paying less for more," the latter being the revealed-preference reading and directly relevant to a score that measures investor pull rather than intrinsic quality.

**Two new caps that did not exist at pre-registration:**

- **Pricing power inverted.** Gross margin guided from 75% to a 71-72% Q4 trough on memory input costs. Pricing power is explicitly on the investor-interest menu and both prior notes scored it positive; NVDA absorbing input inflation rather than passing it through is the first negative print on that factor. It also means FY28 *EPS* revisions will be materially more muted than the +17.5% revenue revision implies.
- **Quality of earnings.** ~One-fourth of next year's business tied to ecosystem investees; minimum revenue guarantees and credit enhancements; DSO extended to 60 days; inventory at $32B. This is the circular-financing critique, and it is now sourced to management's own disclosure rather than to short-seller framing — which is exactly the sort of thing that caps a multiple.

- **Recommendation: drift to 4+ (adopt the standing recommendation). Do not advance to 5 on this print.** 5 goes live again at the Q3'27 print if the 71-72% Q4 gross-margin trough proves to be a floor rather than a way-station, since the margin walk-down — not the demand picture — is what is now capping the score.
- _Note on sentiment: InsiderScore scored this call "Slightly Positive," down from "Positive." I am deliberately **not** treating that as evidence of cooling interest — the same payload discloses that the scoring model was retuned to be "more skeptical of generic optimism" and that "sentiment scores will not generally be as positive." The downgrade is confounded with the methodology change and cannot carry weight._

## 8. Management Q&A flags

1. **Rasgon (Bernstein) — the flagged challenging exchange, on FY28 composition and pricing.** Rasgon questioned the contributors to what he framed as a **$200 billion increase in fiscal 2028** and pressed on the impact of pricing changes. Huang acknowledged significant unconstrained demand and the need to expand capacity, splitting the growth between hyperscalers and enterprises and emphasising the full factory platform. Read: the *composition* question was answered at the level of customer category, not product or price. Given that the FY28 guide is the single biggest number on the call and sits ~$100B above consensus, an answer that does not decompose it is the most important soft spot in the call. (Rasgon's "$200B" is his framing, quoted as such — I have deliberately not reconciled it against a self-computed FY27 base, since the underlying text here is summary-grade.)
2. **Arya (BofA) — ecosystem investments and custom-chip competition, asked as one question.** Arya put the circularity question directly: NVDA's ecosystem investments alongside competition from customers building custom chips. Huang answered on platform breadth and confidence in continued lab partnerships; Kress added supply-commitment detail. Management conceded that capital is going to companies (OpenAI, Anthropic) developing their own silicon, and rested the defence on the "durability and redeployability" of NVDA compute. This is a **more direct engagement than the 4Q26 Muse deflection**, and the concession is new — but "redeployability" is an assertion about a counterfactual, and the exchange did not quantify what share of the ~one-fourth ecosystem-linked revenue is exposed. The key unresolved bear question on the call.
3. **Schneider (Goldman) — most acute constraint.** Schneider asked what is actually binding. Huang answered "humorously" across the whole supply chain, per the summary, before naming yields and capacity. Memory, power/shell availability and wafer capacity are all cited elsewhere in the call. Read: the levity is doing some work here — for a company whose FY28 guide is a supply number, "everything is tight" is the honest answer but also a low-information one, and it means the 70% carries whatever error the supply plan carries.
4. **Moore (Morgan Stanley) — basis for confidence in the 70% guide.** Huang grounded it in supply-chain visibility and the long lead-time planning cycle (land, power, resources). Useful: it establishes that the FY28 guide is built bottom-up off secured supply, which is the strongest available argument for its credibility and worth weighing against flag 3.
5. **Reitzes (Melius) — recursive self-improvement and AGI.** Huang predicted further demand inflection as agents run continuously, and explicitly **dismissed AGI milestones as less relevant**, redirecting to profitable token generation. Consistent with the "compute is revenue" spine from the June GTC Taipei keynote. No flag; noted for continuity of framing.

## 9. Vocabulary candidates

- **Memory cost inflation as a margin input** — HBM/DRAM scarcity is now an explicit gross-margin driver for NVDA, which makes it a *cost* channel connecting MU/SK Hynix/Samsung to NVDA margins, not just a supplier relationship. Candidate: `memory_cost_inflation`.

- **Neocloud revenue sharing** — recurring revenue layered on one-time equipment sales; a genuine business-model change. Candidate: `recurring_compute_monetization`.
- **ACIE** — carried over unadopted from the 1Q27 note; management used it as a primary reporting segment again this quarter, so it is now durable taxonomy rather than a one-off.
- **Physical AI / agentic AI compute** — carried over from both prior notes, still unadopted.
